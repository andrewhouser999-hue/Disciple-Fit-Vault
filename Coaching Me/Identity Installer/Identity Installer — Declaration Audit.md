## The Audit Standard

Each entry was held to five tests. All five come from the governing document, not from outside it.

1. **Text test.** Does the cited verse, read in its own context, say what the declaration says? *(Approach 6: "declarations must be exegetically traceable, not constructed.")*
2. **Covenant-claim test.** Can the declaration be named as one specific covenantal claim: which covenant, and which mechanism (union with Christ, New Covenant, Abrahamic blessing, and so on)? *(Advisor Verdict item 2.)*
3. **Mediation test.** If the text is Old Testament or spoken before the cross, does the New Testament apply it to the believer, or is its context named? *(Part 1 §6.)*
4. **"In Christ" test.** Is the declaration traceable to a specific "in Christ" reality? *(Approach 5: "Every declaration should be traceable to a specific 'in Christ' reality.")*
5. **Guardrail test.** Does the wording stay inside the practice's stated positions: no "unconditional" for covenants, standing-and-perseverance as written, lament left open, no manifestation or "weapon" framing, and the corporate frame respected? *(Part 1 §1, §6; Part 3; Approach 6, Approach 8.)*

**What was audited:** the four fields that carry declaration content in each entry (*What I'm Putting Off*, *Identity Statement*, *Anchor Verse*, *What I'm Putting On*), plus the declarative lines inside *Today I Will*.

**What was not audited:** *My Evidence*, *Human Witness*, and *The Felt Sense*. Those fields hold Andrew's own testimony. They are personal data, not claims about God, so they have no text to trace.

**Scope of the other Installer files:** The [[Identity Installer - Client Workbook]] carries the same Identity Statements and Anchor Verses for entries 1–22 (it leaves out 23 and 24, and its Putting Off and Putting On fields are blank for the client to fill in). Any repair made here should be carried across to the Workbook, or the two files drift apart. The Beatitudes variant is a separate tool and is not covered.

## Cross-Cutting Findings

These hold across many entries. Fixing them once is cheaper than fixing them entry by entry.

### C1. No entry carries its own audit trail

The Hinge section asks the reader to treat every statement as "in Christ." That is a blanket instruction. Approach 5 asks for more: each declaration traceable to its own "in Christ" reality. An explicit "in Christ" marker appears in the declaration text of only 6 of the 24 entries (2, 3, 9, 11, 15, 22).

**Proposed fix:** one new field per entry, *Covenant Ground*, placed under *Anchor Verse*. A template is at the end of this document. It turns this one-time audit into a standing record.

### C2. The anchor verses are mostly NIV wording

The standing rule for biblical files is ESV. By my reading, 17 of the 24 anchor verses are verbatim NIV (1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 13, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 21, 22), and entry 14 is NIV-based and cut short. Entries 2, 12, 20, 23, and 24 read as ESV, and 6 is the same in both.

This is not only a style rule. In several entries the translation changes what the verse seems to say. Examples: Jer 31:3 (ESV: "therefore I have continued my faithfulness to you," not "drawn you with unfailing kindness"), Eph 2:10 (ESV "workmanship," NIV "handiwork"), Matt 5:14 (ESV "a city," NIV "a town").

**Before anyone swaps the text in:** I checked these from memory of the two translations, with no ESV source open. Confirm each verse against the ESV text before it goes into the file.

### C3. Twenty-four entries rest on about eleven covenant claims

Reading the "Covenant claim" column above, the entries cluster:

| Covenant claim | Entries |
|---|---|
| Adoption and sonship | 1, 9, 24 |
| Justification and no condemnation | 3, 11, 20 |
| Election and purpose | 4, 12, 16 |
| Christ's victory and inseparable love | 6, 17, 19 |
| Mission and witness | 7, 10, 23 |
| The Spirit's indwelling, power, and peace | 8, 14, 21 |
| Union and security | 15, 22 |
| New creation | 2 |
| Redemption | 5 |
| Holy people, priesthood | 18 |
| Known by God | 13 |

This is a finding about design, not an error. It means entries 3, 11, and 20 make the same claim three times. The Installer's phase system (Active, Integrating, Integrated) tracks the client's lie, not the claim, so three entries on one claim can each be right. The cost is that the 24 entries look like 24 separate covenant truths when there are about 11. A coach reading the list should know that.

### C4. Declarations that predict experience, not standing

Several entries state what the client will *feel or be able to do*, not what is *true of their standing*:

- Entry 14: "no limits, no off days, and no resource constraints."
- Entry 21: a peace "circumstances cannot remove."
- Entry 22: "every storm drives my roots deeper."
- Entry 6: suffering is "the arena where identity is proven."

Scripture does not promise any of these. Jesus was "greatly distressed and troubled" in Gethsemane (Mark 14:33-34). Paul "despaired of life itself" (2 Cor 1:8). Paul's thorn was not removed, and he was told "my power is made perfect in weakness" (2 Cor 12:9).

This matters for the practice's own mechanism. The Approach 6 caution (Wood et al.; Theory of Change, Node 7) says a statement the client does not feel can deepen the gap. A declaration that promises a *feeling* is the likeliest kind to widen it. A declaration of *standing* cannot be falsified by a bad day. The lament caution in Part 3 points the same way.

**Rule for repairs:** declare what is true of the client's standing. Describe experience only in the way the text does, with weakness and suffering still inside the picture.

### C5. Four absolutes that run past the practice's perseverance position

The practice holds that a believer's standing cannot be lost through failure, sin, doubt, or fluctuating feeling. It leaves open, on purpose, whether a person who finally and willfully renounces Christ was ever truly in (Heb 6:4-6; 10:26-31). The coach does not adjudicate that.

Four phrases go further than that stated position:

- Entry 5: "fully and finally bought back."
- Entry 15: "unassailable" and "cannot be moved."
- Entry 19: "no ending point I could trigger."
- Entry 20: "cannot be appealed."

Read as "no failure, sin, doubt, or feeling of mine ends this," each is inside the position. As written, a client from a Wesleyan or Arminian tradition would be asked to say something their tradition denies. Entry 16 raises the same question from the other side: John 15:2 and 15:6 (branches taken away and thrown out) sit in the same passage the declaration quotes.

**Proposed fix:** scope each phrase with the practice's own wording ("no failure of mine…"). Low severity, but it should be deliberate.

### C6. Pre-cross and Old Testament texts: only one fails the mediation rule

| Text | Entry | Mediation result |
|---|---|---|
| Jer 29:11 | 12 | **Fails.** Exile promise; not applied to believers anywhere in the New Testament. |
| Matt 5:14 | 7 | Passes with context named. Pre-cross teaching to disciples. |
| Ps 103:12 | 11 | Passes. Heb 10:17 carries the same promise into the New Covenant. |
| Ps 139:1-2 | 13 | Passes. A creation-level psalm of personal address. |
| John 14:27; John 15:16 | 21, 16 | Pass. Upper Room teaching that points to the Spirit's coming and the New Covenant meal. |
| Jer 31:3 | 19 | Passes. It sits in Jeremiah's New Covenant section. |

### C7. Corporate frame

Approach 8 says the covenant identity markers in 1 Pet 2:9-10 are plural and "cannot be held individually." Matt 5:14 ("you are the light") and 2 Cor 5:20 ("we are ambassadors") are plural in the Greek as well. First-person singular is a legitimate design choice for self-declaration (Ps 42:5; Lam 3:21-24; see [[Covenant Identity — The Biblical Pattern of Self-Declaration — Practitioner Exegetical Reference]]). The only entry where the singular actually contradicts the cited verse is 18. The others should simply say "a people" or "a household" somewhere in the declaration.

### C8. Housekeeping found along the way

- A stray John 15:16 quotation block sits between entries 23 and 24 with no heading (just after the end of entry 23).
- Entry 3's *What I'm Putting On* has no blockquote marker and no "speak aloud" line, unlike the other entries.
- Entry 3's old pattern is labeled "the impure," but its renunciation is of "the condemned." The label and the renunciation do not match.
- Entry 22's anchor verse carries an editing leftover: "you (I) received."
- The Installer's Contents table lists 24 entries. The memory index says "23-part," and [[Covenant Identity — Category Architecture Cross-Reference]] says "22 categories." Both are stale.
- The *Holding Both* field exists only in entries 1–4 and 24. Entries 5–23 have none, although the "How to use this" phase system (Phase 1 Step 1, Phase 2, Phase 3) depends on it. This is outside the covenant audit, but it means the phase system cannot run for 19 of the 24 entries as the file stands.

## Entry-by-Entry Audit

Each entry lists: the cited text, the covenant claim, what holds, what fails, and the proposed repair. Proposed wording is a draft for Andrew to accept, change, or reject.

### 1. Beloved Child of God — Repair
**Cited text:** 1 John 3:1. **Covenant claim:** adoption into God's household through union with Christ (Gal 4:4-7; Rom 8:14-17; John 1:12-13). The governing document's own grounding for "identity before behavior" fits here: God calls Israel "my firstborn son" before Sinai (Exod 4:22), and the New Testament carries the same order (Rom 5:8).
**Holds:** "Beloved child … not because of what I do" is directly in the text. "I did not graduate into his love. I was born into it" matches John 1:13 and 1 John 4:19.
**Flags:**
- "God is not disappointed in me" is an absolute with no text behind it. The same God disciplines His children (Heb 12:5-11), and Christ rebukes churches in Rev 2–3. The verse says He loves; it does not say He is never grieved.
- "Delighted in" is carried by union with the Beloved (Eph 1:6; the words of Matt 3:17 are said of the Son). It is a sound inference, not something 1 John 3:1 says.
- "When I came to him broken, he ran toward me" is Luke 15:20, a parable, and is uncited.
**Repair:** Add Rom 8:15-17 as a co-anchor. The vault already has the ESV exegesis in [[Covenant Identity — Matched Declaration Architecture]] (Track 1). Replace "not disappointed" with a claim the text makes, for example: "His love does not wait on my performance." Cite Luke 15:20 beside the "ran toward me" line.

### 2. New Creation — Repair
**Cited text:** 2 Cor 5:17. **Covenant claim:** New Covenant heart renewal (Ezek 36:26-27) through union ("in Christ"). Paul calls himself a minister "of a new covenant" two chapters earlier (2 Cor 3:6). This is one of the best covenant ties in the set.
**Holds:** The Identity Statement is faithful to the verse.
**Flags:**
- "Christ redeemed me from **every** curse, pattern, and chain" borrows Gal 3:13, which says Christ redeemed us "from the curse **of the law**." That is the Mosaic curse (Deut 27:26; 21:23). The document's own debate table says the Mosaic curses are not applied directly to believers. Using the verse for "family patterns" stretches it.
- "The old has not faded or improved — it has passed away" runs ahead of the practice's own view of indwelling sin (Rom 7:14-25; 1 John 1:8). Positionally the old self was crucified (Rom 6:6). Experientially the believer still fights the flesh (Gal 5:17).
- "A new nature" frames the matter as two natures. That is a debated frame.
**Better ground the document already has:** Jer 31:29-30 and Ezek 18:20. Jer 31:29-30 sits one verse before the New Covenant promise itself and says the proverb of children bearing the fathers' guilt will no longer be said. That is the text that actually carries "my bloodline does not own me."
**Repair:** Change "from every curse" to "from the curse of the law" and add Jer 31:29-30 as the covenantal ground for the family-pattern line. Replace "has not faded or improved" with the Rom 6:11 reckoning language (see [[Covenant Identity — Faith, Certainty & the Reckoning Pattern]]).

### 3. The Righteousness of God — Grounded
**Cited text:** 2 Cor 5:21. **Covenant claim:** imputed righteousness. Christ is the faithful covenant partner (Rom 5:19), and His standing is credited by union (Claim 2 in the governing document).
**Holds:** "I do not stand before God in my own record — I stand in Christ's" is a clean statement of the claim. Rom 3:21-26, 5:19, and Phil 3:9 all support it.
**Flags:**
- The second renunciation line ("the old nature that was done away with") has the same two-natures problem as entry 2.
- The old-pattern label ("the impure") does not match the renunciation ("the condemned"). Pick one.
- "Not guilty" in the Putting On is a legal image. Justification also includes forgiveness of guilt (Rom 4:5-8). The image is acceptable; do not let it replace the fuller claim.
**Repair:** Align the label and the renunciation. Reword the second line as "I renounce living as if the old self still rules me (Rom 6:6)."

### 4. Chosen and Called — Re-anchor
**Cited text:** Eph 2:10. **Covenant claim:** election in Christ (Eph 1:4-5).
**Fails the text test.** The Identity Statement says "I was chosen before the foundation of the world." That is Eph 1:4, not Eph 2:10. Eph 2:10 says God prepared good works beforehand for "us" to walk in. It says nothing about being chosen before creation and nothing about a "calling" in the vocational sense.
**Flags:**
- "God prepared works **specifically for me**" individualizes a plural verse. It is a fair inference but is not stated.
- **"My life has a script written by a sovereign God — and every detour has been part of the design"** is the most serious line. It makes every detour designed, and the "detours" in Andrew's own Evidence field include abuse and trauma. The text for God's use of evil is Gen 50:20 and Rom 8:28: God works *through* and *redeems* what others intend for harm. Neither says the harm was scripted. James 1:13 says God tempts no one. This line violates the lament caution in Part 3.
- Eph 1:4 carries a live debate (individual or corporate election, and its basis). The statement takes no side, which is the right posture, but the coach should know the debate exists.
**Repair:** Anchor the Identity Statement on Eph 1:4-5 and keep Eph 2:10 for the "works prepared" line. Replace the "script" line with: "God is able to weave even my detours into His purpose (Rom 8:28; Gen 50:20)."

### 5. Redeemed — Repair
**Cited text:** Eph 1:7. **Covenant claim:** redemption by the blood of the covenant. It carries the Exodus pattern (Deut 7:8) into the New Covenant (Luke 22:20; Heb 9:12, 15).
**Holds:** "The price has been paid in full" is exactly right. Heb 9:12 calls it "eternal redemption."
**Flags:**
- "Transferred out of every domain that once owned me" uses Col 1:13 ("transferred us to the kingdom of his beloved Son"), which is not cited. "Every domain" is a stretch beyond the text's "domain of darkness."
- "Fully and finally bought back" is true of the *price*. Redemption's *application* has a future side (Eph 1:14; Rom 8:23; Eph 4:30). The governing document itself says the New Covenant is "begun now and completed at Christ's return." See C5.
- "The enemy has no legal claim on my … **mind**." The text supports no *legal* claim (Col 2:14-15; Rom 8:33-34). It does not support "no influence." Believers are still told to take thoughts captive and to stand against the devil (2 Cor 10:3-5; Eph 6:11-14; 1 Pet 5:8).
**Repair:** Add Col 1:13 as a second anchor. Keep "the price is paid in full." Drop "my mind" or change it to "no legal claim on my standing."

### 6. More Than a Conqueror — Repair
**Cited text:** Rom 8:37. **Covenant claim:** Christ's covenant love holds the believer through suffering. The governing document already uses Rom 8:35-39 as a text addressed to believers.
**Holds:** "I fight from victory already secured" is supported (John 16:33; Col 2:15).
**Flags:**
- **"Suffering … is the arena where identity is proven."** This adds a performance idea. Identity in this practice is received, not proven. Suffering *tests* faith (1 Pet 1:6-7) and produces endurance (Rom 5:3-5). It does not *establish* who the client is.
- The verse's own context lists "famine, nakedness, danger, sword" and quotes Ps 44:22 ("we are regarded as sheep to be slaughtered"). The "conqueror" is conquering *in* these, not above them. Paul quotes a lament psalm in the same breath, which supports the lament caution.
- "I do not merely survive trials" can be heard as a promise of thriving. Read it with Rom 8:36 in view.
**Repair:** Replace "the arena where identity is proven" with "the place where Christ's love holds me (Rom 8:35-39)." Note Ps 44:22 as the lament permitted inside the verse.

### 7. Light of the World — Repair
**Cited text:** Matt 5:14. **Covenant claim:** the Servant's mission as "a covenant for the people, a light for the nations" (Isa 42:6; 49:6), which Paul applies to apostolic mission in Acts 13:47. Believers share it in Christ. This is a strong covenant bridge that the entry does not use.
**Flags:**
- **"Darkness does not define me — I define the darkness."** The second half has no text. John 1:5 says the darkness has not overcome the light. It does not say the light defines the darkness. The line also sounds like self-assertion.
- Matt 5:14 is pre-cross kingdom teaching to disciples ("you," plural). The mediation rule asks that this context be named.
- "My visibility is not arrogance, it is obedience" omits the verse's own guard: Jesus says the light shines so that others "give glory to your Father" (Matt 5:16).
**Better direct anchor:** Eph 5:8, "now you are light **in the Lord**." It is a New Testament statement made *to believers* and has the "in Christ" marker that Matt 5:14 lacks.
**Repair:** Add Eph 5:8 as co-anchor. Replace "I define the darkness" with "the darkness has not overcome it (John 1:5)." End the Putting On with Matt 5:16's purpose.

### 8. Temple of the Holy Spirit — Grounded
**Cited text:** 1 Cor 6:19-20. **Covenant claim:** New Covenant indwelling (Ezek 36:27; John 14:17; Eph 2:21-22), with the "bought at a price" clause naming redemption.
**Holds:** The Identity Statement is almost word-for-word the verse. The Putting On is supported by John 14:16-17 and Ps 139:7.
**Notes:**
- The verse's own context is sexual ethics. That is worth knowing but does not weaken the claim.
- In the Greek, "temple" is singular and "you" is plural. The corporate side is in 1 Cor 3:16. A one-line corporate nod would satisfy C7.
**Repair:** None required. Optional: add Ezek 36:27 as the covenant ground.

### 9. Co-heir with Christ — Repair
**Cited text:** Rom 8:17. **Covenant claim:** the Abrahamic inheritance, fulfilled in Christ (Gal 3:29; 4:7; Rom 4:13).
**Fails by omission.** The anchor stops before the end of the verse. Rom 8:17 continues: "provided we suffer with him in order that we may also be glorified with him." The truncation removes the clause that guards against an entitlement reading. Rom 8:18 then speaks of the sufferings of this present time.
**Flags:**
- "Inheriting **everything** the Son inherits" is large. Heb 1:2 (heir of all things), 1 Cor 3:21-23, and Rev 3:21 support a broad claim. But the governing document's Approach 4 caution says the land and descendants promises are *fulfilled in Christ and the new creation* and "not repeated to the client individually." "Everything" needs that boundary.
- The inheritance is mostly future (Eph 1:14; 1 Pet 1:4). "Inheriting" in the present tense should be read as "I hold the status now."
- *Today I Will* says to ask "with the confidence of someone who has full inheritance rights." Heb 4:16 grounds bold asking in mercy and grace, not in rights. "Inheritance rights" drifts toward a prosperity reading. See 1 John 5:14 ("according to his will") and Jas 4:3.
**Repair:** Quote Rom 8:17-18 in full. Move the boldness line to Heb 4:16.

### 10. Ambassador of Christ — Repair
**Cited text:** 2 Cor 5:20. **Covenant claim:** the New Covenant ministry of reconciliation (2 Cor 3:6; 5:18-21).
**Holds:** "Ambassador" and "God making his appeal through us" are in the text.
**Flags:**
- **"With his authority"** and **"the weight and authority of the one who sent me."** The verse gives a *message* and an *appeal*, not personal authority. An ambassador speaks the sender's words and has none of his own. Paul is speaking as an apostle ("we"); extending it to every believer is common, but "authority" should be bounded to the message of reconciliation.
- For Andrew's history with spiritual abuse, authority language that licenses "I speak truth with Christ's authority" deserves extra care. It is the grammar that abusers use.
**Repair:** Replace "carrying his message with his authority" with "carrying his message of reconciliation, as though God were making his appeal through me." Keep the Putting On's "not in my own name."

### 11. Forgiven and Free — Repair
**Cited text:** Ps 103:12. **Covenant claim:** covenant forgiveness. Ps 103:7-8 echoes the Exod 34:6 name formula ("merciful and gracious … abounding in steadfast love"), the *hesed* text the governing document flags. The New Covenant promise is Jer 31:34, quoted in Heb 10:17.
**Holds:** "Fully and finally forgiven" is supported by Heb 10:12-18 ("there is no longer any offering for sin").
**Flags:**
- The anchor is Old Testament. It is not a Mosaic-law promise, so the mediation rule does not fail it, but the New Covenant text exists and is stronger.
- **Today I Will: "Refuse to rehearse one thing God has already forgiven."** This has no way to tell *accusation* from *conviction*. The Spirit convicts of sin (John 16:8), and godly grief leads to repentance (2 Cor 7:10). A client could use this line to silence a legitimate prompt to confess or make things right. It also does not touch restitution, which forgiveness does not cancel.
- "The accuser has no evidence that has not already been covered" is supported by Rom 8:33-34 and Col 2:14.
**Repair:** Add Jer 31:34 / Heb 10:17 as the New Covenant anchor. Add one discernment line to *Today I Will*: a thought that is specific and calls me to act (confess, repair) is conviction. A thought that is general and condemning is accusation (Rom 8:1).

### 12. Filled with Purpose — Re-anchor
**Cited text:** Jer 29:11. **Covenant claim:** purpose in Christ (Eph 2:10; Rom 8:28-29; Phil 1:6).
**Fails the mediation test.** Jer 29:11 is part of a letter to Judean exiles in Babylon (29:1-10). The promise is a return after seventy years (29:10) to a nation, not a personal life-plan. The New Testament never applies it to believers. The governing document names this very pattern ("Mal 3:10 tithe promises are Mosaic; applying them as direct New Covenant guarantees is exegetically weak").
**Flags:**
- "I will not die without having fulfilled what God placed me here to do" is a guarantee with no text. Phil 1:6 promises God finishes His work *in* the believer. Acts 13:36 says David "served the counsel of God in his own generation." Neither is a promise that every vocation gets completed.
- "The pain, the detours … are all threads in a purpose that preceded my birth" has the same ambiguity as entry 4: God redeeming pain is not the same as God purposing it.
- Entries 4 and 12 overlap heavily ("not born by accident," "purpose preceded my birth").
**Repair:** Re-anchor on Phil 1:6 and Rom 8:28-29. If Jer 29:11 stays, name its context ("spoken to exiles; the pattern of a God who plans for His people's welfare"). Soften the "will not die without" line to a claim about God's faithfulness to finish what He began.

### 13. Known and Seen — Repair
**Cited text:** Ps 139:1-2. **Covenant claim:** being known by God within covenant. Closest covenant parallel: Exod 33:12-17 ("I know you by name"). NT parallels: Gal 4:9; 1 Cor 13:12.
**Holds:** "Fully known by God" is the plain sense of Ps 139.
**Flags:**
- The Identity Statement says "fully known … and **fully loved without condition**." The anchor supports "known." It does not mention love. The pairing of "known" and "loved" is Rom 5:8 ("while we were still sinners") and Rom 8:35-39.
- "God knows the number of hairs on my head" is Matt 10:30 / Luke 12:7, not cited. It was said to persecuted disciples to counter fear, not to promise safety.
- "He perceives my thoughts **before I think them**" overreaches. Ps 139:2 says He discerns thoughts "from afar"; v. 4 speaks of words not yet spoken.
**Repair:** Add Rom 5:8 as the second anchor for the "loved" half. Add Matt 10:30 to the uncited line. Change "before I think them" to "from afar" or cite v. 4.

### 14. Empowered — Re-anchor
**Cited text:** Rom 8:11. **Covenant claim:** the New Covenant gift of the Spirit (Ezek 36:27; Acts 1:8).
**Fails the text test.** Rom 8:11 is about the Spirit giving "life to your mortal bodies." That is read mainly as bodily resurrection, with present renewal debated. It is not a promise of capacity for ministry or work. The anchor also drops the verse's opening "if."
**Flags:**
- **"No limits, no off days, and no resource constraints."** The *Spirit* has no limits. That does not give the *believer* unlimited output. Paul's own experience: "when I am weak, then I am strong" (2 Cor 12:9-10). See C4.
- The risk is real for Andrew's own pattern. A statement that treats limits as a failure of faith feeds the striving the Installer is meant to undo.
**Better anchors:** Eph 1:19-20 ("the immeasurable greatness of his power toward us who believe, … when he raised him from the dead"), Eph 3:20, Col 1:29, and 2 Cor 12:9-10 for the weakness side.
**Repair:** Re-anchor on Eph 1:19-20 and Eph 3:20. Add 2 Cor 12:9-10 to the Putting On, so "empowered" and "weak" are said together.

### 15. Secure and Unshakeable — Grounded
**Cited text:** Col 3:3. **Covenant claim:** union with Christ. "Your life is hidden with Christ in God" is the governing document's own example of "security as covenantal hiddenness" (Approach 5).
**Holds:** The Identity Statement is faithful to the verse. The covenant claim is explicit.
**Notes:**
- "What can be shaken will be shaken. What is in Christ cannot be moved" echoes Heb 12:27-28 ("receiving a kingdom that cannot be shaken"), a New Covenant passage (Heb 12:24). It is uncited and would strengthen the entry.
- "Unassailable" says more than "hidden." Believers are assailed (1 Pet 5:8; Eph 6:11-16). The claim is that their *standing* is not at risk. See C5.
**Repair:** Optional. Add Heb 12:27-28. Change "unassailable" to "my standing is not at stake."

### 16. Fruit-Bearer — Repair
**Cited text:** John 15:16. **Covenant claim:** Christ as the true vine, which is the Old Testament image of Israel (Ps 80:8-16; Isa 5:1-7; Hos 10:1). In the document's progressive-covenantalist reading, Christ is the true Israel and all in Him are the New Covenant people. Jesus said this the same night as the covenant meal (Luke 22:20) and in the Spirit-promising Upper Room discourse. This is one of the richest covenant ties in the set.
**Holds:** "Fruit flows from abiding, not striving" is John 15:4-5.
**Flags:**
- "Appointed" is from the anchor; "abiding" is from John 15:4-5. The anchor does not carry the abiding half. Cite both.
- **"As long as I remain connected to the vine, fruit … is inevitable."** The same passage says branches that do not bear fruit are taken away (15:2) and branches that do not abide are thrown out and burned (15:6). That is part of the perseverance debate the practice leaves open. See C5.
- "The condition … is not striving — it is abiding" sets up a false either/or. In John 15:10 abiding includes keeping Christ's commandments.
**Repair:** Cite John 15:4-5 beside 15:16. Change "inevitable" to "as I abide, He bears fruit through me." Replace "not striving" with "not self-produced."

### 17. Overcomer — Conflict
**Cited text:** Rev 12:11. **Covenant claim:** Christ's victory, shared by faith.
**Directly contradicts the governing document.** Approach 6 (revised 2026-10-18) says: "Rev 12:11 is not used as a ground for declaration. 'The word of their testimony' (*marturia*: witness, testimony, martyrdom) is the martyrs' witness to Christ under persecution, and 'weapon' language edges toward the manifestation framing the Practice Definition rejects."
The Installer entry does all of what that sentence rules out:
- Anchors on Rev 12:11.
- Reads "the word of my testimony" as the client's personal story.
- Calls testimony "a weapon" and "ammunition."
- Has the client "enforce a victory" and "speak my testimony … over one area where the enemy is lying."
**Further flags:**
- "Enforce" is not a New Testament category. Ephesians 6:11-14 says to *stand*.
- "A defeated enemy" is supported (Col 2:15; Heb 2:14; 1 John 3:8) but needs balance. Rev 12:12 says the devil is "filled with fury because he knows his time is short." Defeated and active are both true.
**Better anchors the New Testament already provides:** 1 John 5:4-5 ("this is the victory that has overcome the world — our faith … the one who believes that Jesus is the Son of God"), 1 John 2:13-14 ("you have overcome the evil one"), and John 16:33.
**Repair:** Re-anchor the whole entry on 1 John 5:4-5. Keep "the blood of the Lamb" from Rev 12:11a if wanted. Replace "weapon / ammunition / enforce" with "stand." If Andrew wants a place for sharing testimony, ground it separately on Ps 66:16 or Mark 5:19, which describe telling what God has done as witness, not as warfare.

### 18. Holy and Set Apart — Repair
**Cited text:** 1 Pet 2:9. **Covenant claim:** Israel's priestly identity (Exod 19:5-6) extended to the church in Christ. This is the passage the governing document works through most thoroughly (Part 1 §3–4), including the strongest counter-text (Exod 19:5, "if you will indeed obey") and its answer.
**Holds:** "I am holy — not because I am perfect, but because I have been set apart" matches 1 Cor 1:2, 6:11, and Heb 10:10, 14.
**Flags:**
- **Corporate frame.** Approach 8 says 1 Pet 2:9-10 is plural and "cannot be held individually." The Putting On ("I have been called out of darkness") turns it into a purely personal claim. See C7.
- The Putting Off redefines holiness as distinctiveness ("shrinks my distinctiveness to avoid standing out"). In the New Testament holiness is belonging plus moral devotion (1 Pet 1:15-16, quoting Lev 11:44), not mainly visibility. This overlaps with entry 7.
- "A vessel set apart … ready to be filled" is 2 Tim 2:21, uncited. That verse is conditional ("if anyone cleanses himself").
**Repair:** Add 1 Pet 1:15-16 and Heb 10:10. Start the Putting On with "we": "We are a holy people; I have been called out of darkness into light." Cite 2 Tim 2:21 with its condition.

### 19. Deeply and Permanently Loved — Grounded
**Cited text:** Jer 31:3. **Covenant claim:** covenant *hesed*. The verse sits in Jeremiah's "Book of Consolation" (chs. 30–33), directly before the New Covenant promise (31:31-34). The ESV renders the second half with the word "faithfulness" — the same *hesed* the governing document calls the most important term for the practitioner.
**Holds:** "Everlasting love" is the verse's own phrase. "Will outlast my death" fits Rom 8:38-39. "Preceded my birth" fits Eph 1:4-5.
**Notes:**
- The verse was addressed to Israel. The mediation path is the New Covenant itself: Gentile believers enter it by union (Eph 2:11-19). Name that context once.
- "Unconditional" is fine here if it means *not earned and not withdrawn when I fail* (Rom 5:8). It would be wrong if heard as "God has no expectations" (John 14:21; Jude 21; Heb 12:6). The Putting On already defines it correctly ("does not rise when I perform and fall when I fail").
- "No ending point I could trigger" runs past the practice's stated position. See C5.
**Repair:** Optional. Add Rom 8:38-39 as the New Testament parallel. Scope "no ending point" as "no failure of mine."

### 20. Free from Condemnation — Grounded
**Cited text:** Rom 8:1. **Covenant claim:** justification. Christ is the faithful covenant partner (Rom 5:19). Also see Rom 8:3-4, 33-34.
**Holds:** All of it. This is the governing document's own cited text.
**Notes:**
- The Identity Statement drops the verse's qualifier. Rom 8:1 says no condemnation "for those who are **in Christ Jesus**." The statement says "for me." This is the exact gap Approach 5 closes. One phrase fixes it.
- *Today I Will* has the same accusation-versus-conviction gap as entry 11.
- "The verdict is grace" is loose. The verdict is "righteous" (Rom 3:24: justified by His grace as a gift).
- "Cannot be appealed" is a legal image only. See C5.
**Repair:** Change to "In Christ Jesus there is no condemnation for me." Add the entry 11 discernment line. For the Track 4 ESV exegesis, see [[Covenant Identity — Matched Declaration Architecture]].

### 21. Carrier of Peace — Repair
**Cited text:** John 14:27. **Covenant claim:** the New Covenant "covenant of peace" (Ezek 34:25; 37:26; Isa 54:10), given through the Spirit Jesus promises in the same discourse (John 14:26). Standing peace with God is Rom 5:1.
**Holds:** The Putting On is accurate: "not the absence of trouble — a settled confidence … that coexists with trouble." John 16:33 supports that.
**Flags:**
- **The Identity Statement says more than the Putting On.** "A peace … circumstances cannot remove" can be read as a promise about the client's *felt* state. Jesus was deeply troubled (John 12:27; Mark 14:33-34). See C4.
- The Putting Off treats "the anxious" as an *identity* to renounce and "worry" as a default. Phil 4:6 does command against anxiety. But a client may have anxiety that is clinical. This entry should carry a referral note (see [[Covenant Identity — Crisis & Referral Protocol]]) before it goes to any client.
- "Mine to release into every room" has partial ground in Luke 10:5-6 (peace on a house; it returns if not received). That was instruction to the sent disciples, pre-cross.
**Repair:** Change the Identity Statement to match the Putting On: "I carry the peace Christ gave me. It does not depend on my circumstances." Add Rom 5:1 and Ezek 37:26. Add the referral note.

### 22. Rooted and Established — Repair
**Cited text:** Col 2:6-7. **Covenant claim:** union ("in him") sustained by continuing faith.
**Holds:** "Rooted" is a perfect participle (already done to the believer). The Identity Statement is fair.
**Flags:**
- The text is an *exhortation* ("continue to live your lives in him"), not a promise of stability. "Established" is a present participle (ongoing).
- **"Every storm that comes against me drives my roots deeper."** The text does not say this, and Jesus' parables say the opposite can happen. In the Parable of the Soils the plant with no root "falls away" when tribulation arises (Matt 13:20-21). In the two builders, the storm hits both houses, and the difference is hearing and doing (Matt 7:24-27). This conflicts with the Soils framework used elsewhere in the vault.
- Entries 15 and 22 overlap.
**Better anchors:** Matt 7:24-27, Jer 17:7-8, Eph 3:17.
**Repair:** Replace the storm sentence with "In the storm I stay with what I received (Col 2:6-7)." Remove the "(I)" leftover in the verse. See C4.

### 23. Called as Coach — Not a covenant-identity claim
**Cited text:** 2 Cor 1:4. **Covenant claim:** none. This is a *vocational* declaration.
**What the verse supports:** the principle that received comfort is passed on (2 Cor 1:4-7).
**What it does not support:** "I am a coach called by God." A call to a vocation rests on gifting and discernment (Rom 12:6-8; 1 Pet 4:10-11; Eph 4:11-12) and on the confirmation of others. That belongs in *Human Witness*, not in a verse about comfort.
**Flags:**
- "God uses broken vessels precisely because they don't take credit for the wine" is 2 Cor 4:7, uncited. It is the stronger anchor.
- "The deeper my own journey into healing, the deeper I can go with a client" is a pragmatic claim. It needs a boundary. Unfinished places are access points when they are named and supervised. Unattended, they are a risk to the client (see [[CIC Practitioner Blind Spots — Self-Examination & Fixes]], self-contamination of client reads).
- Not in the Client Workbook, which is correct. This one should stay personal.
**Repair:** Add 2 Cor 4:7 and 2 Cor 12:9-10 as anchors. Move the "called" claim's ground to *Human Witness*. Add a supervision line to the Putting On.

### 24. Welcomed and Wanted — Grounded
**Cited text:** Eph 2:19. **Covenant claim:** Gentiles, once "strangers to the covenants of promise" (Eph 2:12), are brought near by the blood of Christ (2:13) into God's household. This is the most explicitly covenantal text in the set, and it is the one the governing document cites (Eph 2:11-19).
**Holds:** "A place in God's household before I did anything to earn it" is Eph 2:13, 19 and Rom 5:8.
**Notes:**
- "Formed on purpose" has support in Ps 139:13-16. "Named" has support in Isa 43:1 (an Israel-address; name the context).
- "I bring belonging with me" could read as belonging being a private possession. Belonging comes *through* the household. Approach 8 would have the household in the sentence.
- The Putting Off contains a biographical claim about Andrew's parents. That is acceptable in a personal file. It would need de-identifying before it moves anywhere client-facing.
**Repair:** Optional. Add Eph 2:12-13 as the covenant ground. Add "the household" to the "bring belonging" line.

## Proposed Field: *Covenant Ground*

Add one field per entry, directly under *Anchor Verse*. It makes the audit permanent: every entry then states its own trace.

```
**Covenant Ground:**
> Text: [verse, in ESV, with its context in half a sentence]
> Covenant: [which covenant, per the sequence table in Part 1 §1]
> Mechanism: [union with Christ / New Covenant / Abrahamic blessing / other]
> In Christ: [the specific reality the declaration restates]
> Mediation: [OT or pre-cross? How does the New Testament apply it?]
> Limit: [what this text does NOT promise]
```

**Worked example — entry 24:**

```
**Covenant Ground:**
> Text: Eph 2:19 (ESV). Gentiles, once "strangers to the covenants of promise" (2:12), are now "fellow citizens … and members of the household of God."
> Covenant: the covenants of promise, entered through the New Covenant.
> Mechanism: union with Christ; brought near "by the blood of Christ" (2:13).
> In Christ: a place in the household, given before any earning.
> Mediation: New Testament text; none needed.
> Limit: belonging is received through the household of believers; it is not a private possession, and it does not promise that every human welcome will feel the same.
```

The *Limit* line is the one that does the most work. It is where overreaches like "no limits," "every detour designed," and "fully and finally" get caught before they are spoken.

## Limits of This Audit

- **Scripture was checked against my reading of the ESV and NIV, not against a source text open at the time.** Quotation wording in the ESV comparisons (C2) should be confirmed before any text is replaced.
- **Interpretive choices are mine where the debate is open.** Where I took a position (for example, reading Rom 8:11 mainly as resurrection), I said so. The commentators divide on some of these.
- **This is a textual and covenantal audit, not a clinical one.** It does not judge whether a declaration will work for a given client. That is the job of the Theory of Change and of live use.
- **No Evidence, Witness, or Felt Sense content was assessed.**
- **The audit tests fit with the governing document.** If the governing document changes (for example, the perseverance position), entries 5, 15, 16, 19, and 20 should be re-read.
