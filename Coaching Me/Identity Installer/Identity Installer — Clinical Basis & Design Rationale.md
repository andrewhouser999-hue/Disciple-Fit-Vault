# Identity Installer — Clinical Basis & Design Rationale

*Back-engineered, not newly designed: [[Identity Installer - Personal]] already cites McAdams' Narrative Identity Theory as a foundational element. This document names the packaged clinical protocol that citation points to — Michael White and David Epston's Narrative Therapy — and shows that the document's field structure isn't loosely narrative-flavored, it reproduces White's named techniques component for component. Written the same way as [[Pattern Interrupter — Clinical Basis & Design Rationale]] and [[Lie Eliminator — Clinical Basis & Design Rationale]], for consistency across the three-tool system.*

---

## The primary clinical parent: Narrative Therapy re-authoring (Michael White & David Epston)

Dan McAdams' Narrative Identity Theory is the empirical research base establishing *that* identity is fundamentally storied, and that a coherent self-narrative predicts psychological stability better than abstract self-belief. White and Epston's Narrative Therapy is the clinical protocol built on that same premise for *how* to actually change a person's operative identity story. Identity Installer's structure reproduces White's named techniques directly, not just their spirit:

| Narrative Therapy element | What it does clinically | Identity Installer field |
|---|---|---|
| **Externalizing conversation** | Separates the problem-identity from the person — "the orphan," not "I am worthless" — so it becomes something to examine and release rather than a fixed fact about the self | *What I'm Putting Off* |
| **Unique outcomes** ("sparkling moments" / exceptions — [Dulwich Centre](https://familytherapybasics.com/blog/narrative-externalizing-reauthoring)) | Moments that don't fit the dominant problem-saturated story — the actual raw material any alternative story has to be built from, not decoration for it | *My Evidence* |
| **Re-authoring conversation** | Actively constructing and speaking the preferred alternative story, using the unique outcomes as its evidentiary basis | *Identity Statement* + *What I'm Putting On* |
| **Outsider witness practices / definitional ceremony** ([White, building on Myerhoff](https://dulwichcentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/Outsider-witness-practices-1.pdf)) | Another person's testimony reflects back and *thickens* the preferred story — a structural mechanism, not merely a supportive gesture | *Human Witness* |
| **Ongoing re-storying through lived action** | Identity is extended and confirmed by what a person does next, not fixed by the declaration alone | *Today I Will* |

This is a direct structural match, not an analogy stretched to fit: "unique outcomes" and "My Evidence" ask the identical question (a moment that doesn't fit the old story), and "Human Witness" is not a generically nice idea — it is naming the specific mechanism White called definitional ceremony.

## Supporting theories — already named in the document, organized here by role

- **Claude Steele (Self-Affirmation Theory)** — affirmations only take root when believable and evidence-based; grounds why *My Evidence* is required rather than optional, and why a declaration without it "stays abstract."
- **Jeffrey Young (Schema Therapy)** — deep identity wounds are stored emotionally, not cognitively, and the deepest layer of belief is self-referential ("I am ___"); grounds why the corrective has to be pitched at the identity level (the *Identity Statement*) rather than at surface behavior, and supplies a secondary clinical parent alongside narrative therapy: schema therapy's own mode work and limited reparenting, which install a "Healthy Adult" identity to supersede a maladaptive mode — functionally the same move as put-off/put-on, from a different clinical tradition.
- **Robert Kegan & Lisa Lahey (Big Assumption)** — "I am ___, therefore ___" as the structure protecting stuck patterns; same identity-level targeting logic as Young's.
- **Steven Hayes (ACT)** — identity is built by living in alignment with stated values, not merely believing things about yourself; grounds *Today I Will* as required, not optional — you become who you act like.
- **Hebb's Law (neuroplasticity)** — identity becomes hardwired through repetition; grounds the daily/weekly/monthly rhythm and the phase-tracking system, not a one-time read.
- **Ogden & Fisher (dual awareness), Diana Fosha (transformational affect)** — the somatic mechanics of *Holding Both*; this pairing is a cross-pollination from the same reconsolidation-adjacent research used structurally in [[Lie Eliminator — Clinical Basis & Design Rationale]], not native to narrative therapy itself, which is more conversational/linguistic in its own tradition.
- **Ephesians 4:22-24** — the theological Renounce → Renew → Receive sequence the document names as mapping "almost exactly" onto the psychological structure above; the theological anchor, not the source of the psychological claim.

## Honest limitations

1. **Narrative Therapy proper is a live, collaborative conversation, not a solo written exercise.** White's techniques — externalizing questions, unique-outcome inquiry, definitional ceremony — are things a trained narrative therapist does *with* a person in real time, often surfacing unique outcomes the person wouldn't have named unprompted. A written document can prescribe the output shape ("find a unique outcome," "get an outsider witness") but can't replicate the live questioning technique that surfaces one a person can't yet see in themselves. This is the same category of gap Pattern Interrupter's "Out Loud Rehearsal" field was built to partly close — worth considering whether *Human Witness* would benefit from an equivalent "how to actually hold this as a live conversation" addition, rather than a field filled in solo. Flagging this, not building it, unless you want it added.
2. **Evidence-tier honesty:** McAdams' narrative identity research is well-established empirical psychology with real replication behind it. Michael White's Narrative Therapy is a widely-practiced clinical model, but its own evidence tradition is more case-based and qualitative than RCT-driven — a real distinction worth naming rather than presenting both citations as equally evidenced.
3. **This is a synthesis across five named models** (Narrative Therapy, Schema Therapy, Kegan & Lahey, ACT, self-affirmation theory), not one certified manual — consistent with both sibling tools.
4. **The theological framing (Ephesians 4:22-24, union with Christ) is the document's own explicit anchor, not a claim that the psychological research is theological** — same integration-layer caveat as the other two tools.
