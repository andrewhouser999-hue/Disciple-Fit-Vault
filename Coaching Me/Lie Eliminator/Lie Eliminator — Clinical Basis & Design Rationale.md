# Lie Eliminator — Clinical Basis & Design Rationale

*Back-engineered, not newly designed: [[Lie Eliminator - Personal]] already names Bruce Ecker, Robin Ticic & Laurel Hulley for nearly every field. This document does what that annotation doesn't — names the single packaged clinical protocol those citations point to, shows the field-by-field mapping explicitly, and states the honest limitations of relying on it. Written the same way as [[Pattern Interrupter — Clinical Basis & Design Rationale]], for consistency across the three-tool system.*

## The primary clinical parent: Coherence Therapy (Ecker, Ticic & Hulley)

Coherence Therapy is an empirically-derived psychotherapy built directly on memory reconsolidation neuroscience. Its central principle — **symptom coherence** — is that a person holds a symptom (here, a false belief) not despite its cost, but because it is experienced as compellingly necessary according to an implicit, emotionally-formed model of reality. That is not a loose metaphor Lie Eliminator borrows; it is the literal design principle behind the "What Does That Lie Give You?" field, and the reason that field exists before the truth-countering fields rather than after them.

| Coherence Therapy phase | What it does clinically | Lie Eliminator field |
|---|---|---|
| **Discovery** — surfacing symptom coherence: what implicit belief the lie is organized around, and what function it serves ([symptom deprivation, sentence completion](https://www.experiential-psychotherapies.com/symptom-deprivation-technique)) | Reveals the emotional learning the lie is protecting — why it persists despite being known false | *The Lie I've Believed* + *The Rule This Lie Writes* + *What Does That Lie Give You?* — this last field is symptom coherence by name |
| **Retrieval / Reactivation** | The target belief has to be made emotionally live, not merely recalled, before anything that follows can act on it | *This Activates When* — the reason the document opens on a named, specific trigger rather than a decontextualized belief statement |
| **Retrieval of disconfirming knowledge** | A truth or experience that directly contradicts the reactivated belief | *Who God Is* / *Who I Am* / *His Promise* + *Personal Declaration* |
| **Juxtaposition experience** | Holding the reactivated belief and the disconfirming knowledge in awareness *simultaneously* — the step that actually triggers reconsolidation, not the disconfirming knowledge alone | *Holding Both — In the Body* |
| **Verification** | Testing, across repeated exposure and varied contexts, that the old belief no longer produces the old response | *Therefore I Will* + *Scripture Arsenal* (spacing-effect repetition) |

This is not a resemblance I'm imposing after the fact — every one of these citations already appears verbatim in the document's own "Research Basis" annotations. What this table adds is the single name for the packaged clinical model those citations are pieces of.

## Supporting theories — already named in the document, organized here by role

- **Aaron Beck & Judith Beck (Cognitive Therapy)** — the ABC model and the three-tier core-belief/intermediate-belief/automatic-thought structure supply the vocabulary Coherence Therapy's phases operate on: "the lie" is the core belief, "the rule" is the intermediate belief, and the trigger is the activating event.
- **Miller & Rollnick (Motivational Interviewing)** — ambivalence has to be explored in the person's own words, not argued past; this is why "What Does That Lie Give You?" is a prompt to answer, not a pre-filled indictment.
- **Johari Window (Luft & Ingham)** — grounds "What Others Observe" as addressing a genuine structural blind spot, not a generic accountability step.
- **Curt Thompson, Ana-Maria Rizzuto, Todd Hall** — the *implicit* God-image is a distinct psychological structure from explicit theology, formed relationally and requiring repeated relational address rather than information — the reason "Who God Is" exists as its own field rather than being folded into general doctrine.
- **Robert Kegan & Lisa Lahey (Big Assumption)** — grounds "Who I Am" as an identity-level target, not a behavior-level one.
- **Gabriele Oettingen (Mental Contrasting) and Dan McAdams (Narrative Identity)** — grounds "His Promise" (a credible future, not just a corrected present) and "Who Has Witnessed This Truth" (truth incorporated as lived story, not abstract affirmation).
- **Claude Steele (Self-Affirmation Theory)** — the caution already embedded in the document: a declaration made without prior reactivation risks becoming "a second belief running parallel" to the old one rather than replacing it — this is the same juxtaposition requirement stated from the self-affirmation research side.
- **Pat Ogden & Janina Fisher (dual awareness), Diana Fosha (transformational affect), Eugene Gendlin (felt sense)** — the somatic mechanics of the *Holding Both* step; these are the "how" underneath Coherence Therapy's juxtaposition requirement, which names *that* it must happen but not the somatic technique for doing it.
- **Dan Allender** — wound-level lie identification: tracing a lie to the specific moment it was agreed with, in the person's own words, rather than a general category.

## Honest limitations

1. **Coherence Therapy has a smaller evidence base than CBT proper.** It is empirically informed by memory reconsolidation neuroscience (Nader, LeDoux — well-established in the underlying science; see [[Memory Reconsolidation — Ecker & Neuroscience Basis]]), but as a packaged clinical model it has less large-scale RCT validation than mainstream CBT. Worth naming plainly rather than presenting it as equally well-established.
2. **A document can't run the Discovery phase.** Coherence Therapy's discovery techniques (symptom deprivation, sentence completion, role play) are live clinical techniques for surfacing a belief the person can't yet name — Lie Eliminator's "Where This Lie Came From" archaeology section is the closest self-directed substitute, but it assumes the lie is already nameable, which isn't always true.
3. **This is a synthesis, not one certified manual** — same pattern as both sibling tools. A clinician would recognize Beck, Ecker, Miller & Rollnick, Johari, Kegan & Lahey individually; the assembly into this specific field sequence is original to this system.
4. **The theological content (Who God Is, His Promise, Scripture Arsenal) is a deliberate integration layer, not itself a clinical finding** — the psychological research explains *why* those fields need to exist and what they need to accomplish; the theological sources (Brueggemann, Ferguson, Billings, Robertson, Horton, Tripp) supply what they actually say.
