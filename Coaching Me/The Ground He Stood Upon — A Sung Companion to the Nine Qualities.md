# The Ground He Stood Upon
## A song of the nine qualities of Christ's interior

*A sung companion to [[The Inner Life of Christ — Nine Qualities]]. Nine verses, one per quality. Each verse is followed by the same two-line refrain with one word changed.*

---

## The Song

**1 · Settled**

A call at night, a doctor's word,
the borrowed calm is gone.
He slept while water filled the boat
and let the storm go on.

*Give us the ground He stood upon:*
*Beloved, then the storm.*

**2 · Undivided**

A face for work, a face for home,
a third behind the door.
They handed Him a coin, a trap:
He knew whose face He bore.

*Give us the ground He stood upon:*
*Beloved, then the trap.*

**3 · Free**

We read the room before we speak
and borrow every tone.
The crowd would crown Him. Though the pull
was strong, He climbed alone.

*Give us the ground He stood upon:*
*Beloved, then the crowd.*

**4 · Moved Without Being Swept**

We harden, or we drown in grief—
we think we have no choice.
Beside the tomb, He let it come:
He wept, then raised His voice.

*Give us the ground He stood upon:*
*Beloved, then the tomb.*

**5 · Unhurried**

We nod, and check the time, and nod,
a task or three ahead.
A dying girl, and still He turned
to find one hand instead.

*Give us the ground He stood upon:*
*Beloved, then the clock.*

**6 · Loved and Knowing It**

We spend our lives to reach the place
where He had just begun:
before He'd healed or preached, a voice
named Him Beloved Son.

*Give us the ground He stood upon:*
*Beloved, then the test.*

**7 · Truthful Without Cruelty**

We sand the truth to make it safe
or sharpen it for war.
They dropped their stones and left. He said,
"None? Nor I. Sin no more."

*Give us the ground He stood upon:*
*Beloved, then the stones.*

**8 · Purposeful Without Striving**

We shove at doors that will not give
and watch who sees us climb.
His brothers said, "Go prove yourself."
He went—but in His time.

*Give us the ground He stood upon:*
*Beloved, then the dare.*

**9 · Available**

We have so much that must be done—
who has the time for one?
His men said, "Send the children off."
He blessed them, every one.

*Give us the ground He stood upon:*
*Beloved, then the cross.*

*Beloved.*

---

## How Each Verse Is Built

- **Lines 1–2** say what we recognize in ourselves. This is the "we" half. It is the ordinary human version of the quality's opposite.
- **Lines 3–4** show the same quality in Jesus, in the Gospel scene the Inner Life doc uses. This is the "He" half.
- **The refrain** is the aspiration, phrased as a petition. It asks to be *given* a ground. It does not ask to produce a quality. That matches the Telos doc's claim that the qualities are fruit, not the entry condition.

## Why the Refrain Says "Beloved, then the …"

The Father's word at the baptism (Mt 3:16–17) comes before every scene in the song. Each pressure comes after it. The refrain states that order. The changing word is the pressure in that verse's scene: storm, trap, crowd, tomb, clock, test, stones, dare, cross.

The refrain is addressed to the Father, the One who spoke the word over the Son. The petition rests on adoption: "See what kind of love the Father has given to us, that we should be called children of God; and so we are" (1 Jn 3:1 ESV).

Two small echoes are deliberate:

- The doctor's *word* in verse 1 is set against the Father's *Word* in the refrain. One word unsettles. One word holds.
- *Borrowed* (verses 1 and 3) is set against *give* (every refrain). We live on borrowed calm and borrowed tone. We ask to be given the ground.

## The Models

**George Herbert** (*Love (III)*, *The Flower*) — the form and the turn.

- The 8/6 alternation of common meter is the hymnal's plainer cousin of the long-short lines in *Love (III)*.
- Plain speech sits inside a strict shell.
- Each verse answers its own complaint with an act. Verse 9 asks "who has the time for one?" and ends "He blessed them, every one." *Love (III)* works the same way: every objection from the soul is met with something Love does.
- The speech in verse 7 is cut to three words and left bare.
- Herbert is unusually singable for a metaphysical poet. Vaughan Williams set *Love (III)* in *Five Mystical Songs*, and several of Herbert's poems are in hymnals.

**Wendell Berry** (*The Peace of Wild Things*, the Sabbath poems) — the diction and the posture.

- Concrete nouns carry the weight: boat, door, coin, stones, ground. No ornament.
- Verse 1 opens in the night-waking dread that opens *The Peace of Wild Things*.
- Berry finds his ground in the creaturely world. This song finds it in a spoken word. The posture is the same: lie down on what holds.
- The ground-and-root vocabulary also comes from this vault. The Telos doc says "the root held." The Inner Life doc says "the ground I was standing on."

**Rich Mullins** — the voice and the setting.

- He named his band for Brennan Manning's *The Ragamuffin Gospel*, and the two were friends. Manning is a contributor in the Telos doc, so the lineage runs through the vault.
- *Hold Me Jesus* speaks as a needy person to God. Every "we" line here is a confession. None is a boast.
- The refrain is a prayer that ordinary people sing together. It is not a proclamation.
- The hammered dulcimer was a trademark of his sound, and he also played the Appalachian dulcimer. The acoustic setting suggested under "Singing It" borrows that palette. It is a suggestion, not a transcription of any of his songs.

**What was not taken.** No lines, images, or phrases from Herbert, Berry, or Mullins appear in the song. Their influence is form, diction, and posture only.

## Singing It

- **Meter.** The four verse lines are common meter (8.6.8.6). The refrain is 8.6.
- **To test the words today.** Sing the four verse lines to NEW BRITAIN (the "Amazing Grace" tune) or ST. ANNE ("O God, Our Help in Ages Past"). Then repeat the tune's last two phrases for the refrain.
- **A Mullins-style setting.** D major pentatonic (D E F♯ A B). Compound time (6/8). Slower than feels natural. Fingerpicked guitar or a dulcimer drone on D and A. Keep the verses within a sixth. Let the refrain peak on the "-lov-" of *Beloved*.
- **Who sings what.** One voice takes each verse. Everyone takes the refrain.
- **The ending.** Sing the last refrain quietly. Hold the closing "Beloved." as a word heard, not performed.
- **To sing eight, not nine.** Drop verse 9 and place the closing "Beloved." after refrain 8.
- **Weekly use.** The Inner Life doc's "Living With This" gives one quality per week. Sing or speak that week's verse and refrain after reading its section.

## Guardrails Kept

- **Portrait and petition, not a rubric.** The Telos doc's scope boundary applies. Do not use the song to rate a client against the nine. The Missed Items Log already flags that misuse.
- **Lament stays open.** Verse 4 lets Jesus weep, and the song never tells the singer to stop grieving. This keeps to the standing lament caution.
- **Scripture.** The only direct quotation is 1 Jn 3:1 (ESV). Everything else is allusion or paraphrase.

## Open Items

- **Never sung aloud.** The scansion is checked by count only. Test it before trusting it.
- **Verse 7 is the tightest compression.** "None? Nor I." stands in for Jn 8:10–11. A listener who does not know the story may stumble there.
- **Scenes come from the Inner Life doc, not the Telos doc.** The Telos doc uses other scenes for several qualities: Pilate for Settled, the woman with the hemorrhage for Undivided, the foot-washing for Available. Swapping is a small edit per verse.
- **Count mismatch in the vault.** Several index documents still say "eight qualities." Both source docs list nine.
