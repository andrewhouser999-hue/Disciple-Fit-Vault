# Pattern Interrupter

> A lie is a false belief. A false identity is a false self-concept. This is neither — it is an **inherited relational script**: an automatic sequence of action, run on another person, learned by watching it modeled rather than by choosing it. It does not live in what you think or in who you believe yourself to be — it lives in your body, activated by a specific trigger, discharging before you've decided anything. That is why believing better and even restraining the behavior has not been enough to change it. This document targets the script itself — its author, its steps, its cost, and its rehearsed replacement — not the belief underneath it (that's [[Lie Eliminator - Personal]]) and not the self-concept it's attached to (that's [[Identity Installer - Personal]]). Use all three together; each works a different layer of the same person. This document also functions as a standalone diagnostic — you do not need to have worked Lie Eliminator or Identity Installer first, though it will go deeper if you have.

**Clinical and theological basis, briefly:** built primarily on Habit Reversal Training (Azrin & Nunn, 1973) — developed for tics and body-focused repetitive behaviors, extended here by structural analogy to relational scripts, an application with no direct evidence base of its own — with supporting structure from DBT behavioral chain analysis, schema therapy mode work, Marlatt's relapse-prevention model, and a biblical-counseling diagnostic layer (functional idolatry, per Keller and Tripp). Full mapping and honest limitations in [[Pattern Interrupter — Clinical Basis & Design Rationale]].

**A note on field order:** the fields below are ordered deliberately, not just numbered for reference. Diagnosis (fields 1–7) comes first. Identity is anchored (field 8) *before* the new script is authored (field 9) — on purpose. Writing the desired behavior before securing the identity it's supposed to flow from produces willpower, not formation; if you find yourself wanting to skip ahead and script the new behavior first, that pull is worth noticing, not indulging.

**How to use this:**
- **Daily:** Read the Desired Script for the entry most live right now. Rehearse it mentally before the situation that triggers the old one is likely to occur.
- **Weekly:** Sit with one full entry — including Holding Both and the Cost fields. Update Internal/External Cost with anything new you've noticed.
- **As it happens:** When the old script fires (in whole or in part), go straight to The Repair Step. Do it within the same day if at all possible.
- **As a script shifts — same three phases as [[Identity Installer - Personal]] (Active → Integrating → Integrated):** stay in Phase 1 (full sequence) while the old script's body-signal in "This Activates When" still produces a real charge. Move to Phase 2 (Integrating) when it comes up flat in reflection but hasn't yet been tested in the live moment — spot-check rather than rehearse. Move to Phase 3 (Integrated) once the live moment itself comes up quiet, and mark the date. At Integration: *The Repair Step* shifts from a rehearsed script to past-tense testimony ("I used to run this script here; this is what actually happens now"), and *Out Loud Rehearsal* shifts from scheduled practice to occasional spot-check. A relapse in an Integrated entry is not failure — per Prochaska, it's information that a new triggering context exists; give that context its own entry rather than reverting this one.

## Before You Begin

The Spirit is the active agent here — not you. What you are doing is creating conditions: naming the script honestly, engaging memory, rehearsing an alternative. What you cannot do is manufacture the discipline of the Spirit that actually produces gentleness where a reflex used to run. That is sanctification, and it is his work, worked out through your practice, not around it. The order of the fields below is one expression of that: you are asked to receive who you already are in Christ (field 8) before you script what you will do (field 9), because the doing is meant to flow from what's already true, not manufacture it.

*Holy Spirit, show me plainly where this script runs, what I am really trusting instead of you when it fires, and grow in me the fruit this old pattern has been standing in for. In Jesus' name.*

## Distant Judge — Obedience/Consequence Reflex

**1. This Activates When:**
> My child disobeys or doesn't comply immediately — the moment the pull toward punishment-first shows up before anything relational has happened.

**2. Whose Script This Is:**
> My father's. Obedience enforced through immediate, clear punishment — spanking, grounding — with no instruction, no training-up, no loving expression attached. He was the punisher, not a gentle, drawing-out presence. This is his sequence, not mine — I inherited it by watching it, not by choosing it. *"The son shall not suffer for the iniquity of the father"* (Ezekiel 18:20, ESV) — this pattern was handed to me, but it does not have to be handed on. Breaking it is not disloyalty to my father; it is exactly what Scripture says a son is free to do.

**3. Wound or Gap?**
> Both. *Wound:* being punished rather than instructed taught me that "father" and "distant judge" are the same category — the reflex protects against ever being seen as soft, the thing my own punishment implicitly told me was dangerous to be. *Gap:* I was never shown what instructive, relational discipline actually looks like moment to moment — restraint has given me a "no," but not a procedural "instead."

**4. The Old Script — Step by Step:**
1. *(Body: name what fires first — where, what it feels like. Fill in from your own noticing.)*
2. Child disobeys → a flash of threat to my authority.
3. I move toward distributing a consequence before I've engaged relationally at all.
4. The consequence is delivered from a position of distance and judgment, not connection.
5. I disengage — the interaction closes at the consequence. No instruction, no repair, no return to warmth follows it.

**5. What This Script Gives Me:**
> A sense of control when I feel disrespected or unsteady. Proof I'm not weak or "soft." It lets me skip the harder, more exposed work of staying relationally present with a child's defiance instead of ending it by force.

**6. What Am I Trusting Instead? (Functional Savior Diagnostic)**
> The psychological payoff above (control, proof of strength) is real, but there's a theological question underneath it: when I default to control instead of instruction, what am I functionally trusting or worshiping in place of God? Answer: my own authority and image — being seen as strong, respected, in command of my household — rather than trusting God with how my children actually turn out. Control is the idol; the fear feeding it is being seen as weak, the same fear my own punishment once taught me to guard against at all costs. This is not a behavior to manage — it's a functional savior to repent of and replace with actual trust. *(If this needs more excavation than one field allows, work the full [[09 — Functional Savior Audit]].)*

**7. What This Script Costs — In Two Parts, In This Order:**

**7a. Internal Cost (to me) — Past / Present / Future:**
> *Past:* *(A specific remembered moment you saw this script cost you something — a look on your own face, a thought after the fact, a moment you didn't like who you'd just been.)*
> *Present:* Right now, it keeps me operating as one who distributes from a distance rather than one who draws out what my kids are capable of and believes the best of them — which is the opposite of who I actually want to be as a father.
> *Future:* *(Ten years from now, if this script keeps running unchanged — what does your relationship with each of your kids look like? Be specific, not general.)*

**7b. External Cost (to my child / my family) — Past / Present / Future:**
> *Past:* *(A specific moment you saw this land on your child — their face, their withdrawal, what they said or didn't say.)*
> *Present:* What the research on this exact pattern documents: obedience purchased through consequence produces compliance under surveillance, not internalized character — the reverse of what I actually want to build in them.
> *Future:* *(What does your child's felt sense of you — as father, as authority — look like at 18, if the pattern doesn't change? What relationship are you actually risking?)*

**8. Anchor Identity:**
> [[Identity Installer - Personal#1. Beloved Child of God]] — I cannot consistently draw capability out of my children from a place where my own worth is still organized around obedience and control. Belovedness, not performance, is the identity the next field has to be written *from*, not toward — write it before scripting the new behavior, not after.

**9. The Desired Script — Step by Step** *(authored from the identity above, not from willpower):*
1. *(Body: the early-warning signal you're training yourself to catch — where does the old script's opening move register, before it discharges?)*
2. Child disobeys → I notice the trigger without immediately acting on it.
3. I engage relationally first — approaching as one teaching capability, assuming the best of them, not as a judge.
4. I instruct: name what's needed and why, in a way that draws out what they're capable of rather than simply asserting authority.
5. If consequence is warranted, it's delivered from connection, not distance — paired with instruction, never substituting for it.
6. I stay engaged afterward. The interaction does not close at the consequence.

**10. Holding Both — In the Body:**
> Where does the old script — the flash of threat, the pull toward distance — live in my body, even on an ordinary day?
>
> Where does the desired script — engaged, instructive, connected, secured in belovedness — land when I picture running it instead?
>
> One hand on each place. Hold both. Breathe. What happens at the place that's braced for threat, now that engaged presence is here with it — not after it?

**11. Out Loud Rehearsal — With a Witness:**
> Basic how-to: pick a calm moment, not mid-conflict. (1) Say the trigger out loud. (2) Say the Old Script out loud, step by step, so your witness hears exactly what currently happens. (3) Say the Desired Script out loud, step by step, as if it's already happening — actually speak the instructive words you'd say to your child. (4) Ask your witness one honest question: does that sound like the father you want me to be? (5) Repeat two or three times in the same sitting — fluency comes from repetition, not a single read-through. (6) Ask your witness to tell you afterward, in real life, which script actually showed up.
>
> *If scheduling this cold feels too exposed at first, read the script aloud to yourself alone once before bringing it to your witness — that's a legitimate first rep, not a stall.*
>
> *A note on the witness: your wife is not a neutral observer here — she's watching this pattern operate on her own children too. That's not disqualifying, but name it directly: ask her to distinguish "helping you practice" from her own feelings about what's happened. If that's too enmeshed right now, use a trusted friend or your coach as the rehearsal partner, and reserve your wife's real-life observation for the Witness field below.*
>
> *(Schedule this with your wife — not as a crisis conversation, a practice session.)*

**12. The Repair Step:**
> When the old script fires anyway — name it to my child directly, same day if possible: *"I reacted like the distant judge just now, not like the dad I actually want to be with you. I'm sorry. Here's what I actually wanted you to understand..."* Owning the misstep to my child is not damage control — it is a second, more direct lesson than the original rupture: that the authority in this house is accountable, not a verdict-giver watching from a distance. *"If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us"* (1 John 1:9) — repair is what a forgiven father models, not a risk a distant judge could ever afford to take. The father in Luke 15 runs toward the returning son; he doesn't wait at a distance for the son to prove himself first. That is the authority I am repairing toward, not away from.

**13. Witness:**
> *(Your wife, named specifically — the person positioned to tell you honestly which script actually ran, since your children aren't a fair party to monitor the pattern operating on them.)*
