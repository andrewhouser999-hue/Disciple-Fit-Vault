# Pattern Interrupter

> A lie is a false belief. A false identity is a false self-concept. This is neither — it is an **inherited relational script**: an automatic sequence of action, run on another person, learned by watching it modeled rather than by choosing it. It does not live in what you think or in who you believe yourself to be — it lives in your body, activated by a specific trigger, discharging before you've decided anything. That is why believing better and even restraining the behavior has not been enough to change it. This document targets the script itself — its author, its steps, its cost, and its rehearsed replacement — not the belief underneath it (that's [[Lie Eliminator - Personal]]) and not the self-concept it's attached to (that's [[Identity Installer - Personal]]). Use all three together; each works a different layer of the same person.

**How to use this:**
- **Daily:** Read the Desired Script for the entry most live right now. Rehearse it mentally before the situation that triggers the old one is likely to occur.
- **Weekly:** Sit with one full entry — including Holding Both and the Cost fields. Update Internal/External Cost with anything new you've noticed.
- **As it happens:** When the old script fires (in whole or in part), go straight to The Repair Step. Do it within the same day if at all possible.
- **As a script shifts — same three phases as [[Identity Installer - Personal]] (Active → Integrating → Integrated):** stay in Phase 1 (full sequence) while the old script's body-signal in "This Activates When" still produces a real charge. Move to Phase 2 (Integrating) when it comes up flat in reflection but hasn't yet been tested in the live moment — spot-check rather than rehearse. Move to Phase 3 (Integrated) once the live moment itself comes up quiet, and mark the date. A relapse in an Integrated entry is not failure — per Prochaska, it's information that a new triggering context exists; give that context its own entry.

## Before You Begin

The Spirit is the active agent here — not you. What you are doing is creating conditions: naming the script honestly, engaging memory, rehearsing an alternative. What you cannot do is manufacture the discipline of the Spirit that actually produces gentleness where a reflex used to run. That is sanctification, and it is his work, worked out through your practice, not around it.

*Holy Spirit, show me plainly where this script runs, and grow in me the fruit this old pattern has been standing in for. In Jesus' name.*

## Research Basis — Why Each Field Exists

*Each field names the researchers/contributors behind it and why that finding makes the field necessary. Fields inherited from the sibling tools are noted as such rather than re-derived.*

**This Activates When:**
> **Aaron Beck** — the ABC model: a specific event triggers the response, not the situation in the abstract. Same rationale as the other two tools; the trigger has to be named precisely enough to actually recognize live.

**Whose Script This Is:**
> **Murray Bowen** — the multigenerational transmission process: relational patterns move down a family line as modeled behavior, not as chosen belief, and naming the actual source person is what converts "this is my character" into "this is inherited material running on old fuel" — the same move Dan Allender's lie-archaeology already makes for beliefs, applied here to behavior.

**Wound or Gap?**
> Design judgment (Covenant Identity Coaching diagnostic convention, established 2026-09-06) — not itself an empirical claim, but a structural choice that determines emphasis in every field after it. A wound (protective adaptation to what was done to you) calls for grief and re-securing identity before rehearsal will hold. A gap (no alternative was ever modeled) calls for concrete modeling and repetition — grief work would look for something that isn't there. Most entries will be both in different proportions; naming which is dominant changes where the coaching effort goes.

**The Old Script — Step by Step:**
> **Daniel Siegel & Mary Hartzell** — unresolved experience is stored as implicit memory: it surfaces as a felt reflex in a similar-feeling situation, producing re-enactment even against conscious values. A single "lie" statement can't capture this because the pattern is *procedural*, not propositional — it has to be named as a sequence of moves, in order, or it stays too vague to interrupt mid-run.

**What This Script Gives Me:**
> **Secondary gain** (clinical concept, cross-modality) plus **Miller & Rollnick** — a script that contradicts your own values is still running because it's still paying something out; naming the payoff in your own words is required before a replacement can compete with it. Same rationale as Lie Eliminator's "What Does That Lie Give You."

**5b-i. Internal Cost (to me) — Past / Present / Future:**
> **Paul Fonagy** — reflective functioning: the capacity to accurately read another person's internal state depends on first being able to mentalize your own. This field has to come before the external-cost field, not just alongside it, or the external assessment is being made by an under-developed instrument. **Prochaska & DiClemente** — decisional balance: the pros and cons of a behavior are tracked as genuinely separate constructs, and it's the cons of the *old* behavior — not the pros of a new one — that tips someone out of ambivalence. **Gabriele Oettingen** — mental contrasting supplies the Future slot: projecting the present trajectory forward is part of what sustains motivation, alongside (not instead of) the vivid desired future in field 6.

**5b-ii. External Cost (to the other person / relationship) — Past / Present / Future:**
> Same Prochaska/Oettingen mechanism, applied outward, run only after 5b-i. **Diana Baumrind** and **Gershoff & Grogan-Kaylor** ground what the observable cost actually is in documented developmental outcomes (lower internalized character, modeled aggression, damaged relational trust) rather than in generic guilt — this keeps the field evidential, not just emotional.

**The Desired Script — Step by Step:**
> **Bruce Ecker, Robin Ticic & Laurel Hulley** — a vague ideal can't out-compete a vivid automatic pattern; the replacement has to be at least as concrete and specific, step for step, as the old script named in field 4. **Fonagy** — the target capacity being rehearsed is reflective functioning in the moment itself: pausing long enough to mentalize the child's state instead of only reacting to your own.

**Anchor Identity:**
> Cross-reference to the relevant [[Identity Installer - Personal]] entry. **Kegan & Lahey**'s "Big Assumption" logic (already used in Identity Installer): the desired script has to be run *from* a secured identity, not from willpower alone, or it collapses back to the old script under enough pressure.

**Holding Both — In the Body:**
> Same mechanism as both sibling tools: **Ecker/Ticic/Hulley** (reconsolidation requires simultaneous holding, not sequential), **Ogden & Fisher** (dual awareness), **Diana Fosha** (transformational affect as the marker of real integration), **Eugene Gendlin** (felt sense / felt shift).

**The Repair Step:**
> **Ed Tronick** — the rupture-and-repair research (the "still-face" studies and their replications): what predicts secure attachment is not the absence of misattunement but reliable repair *after* it. This field exists because the desired script will not always win before the old one fires, and because this script — unlike a private belief or self-concept — is performed on another person in real time. A father who names his own misstep to his child directly counter-trains the "distant judge" image the old script would otherwise transmit; the repair is not damage control, it's a second, more direct lesson than the original rupture.

**Witness:**
> **Johari Window** (Luft & Ingham) plus Proverbs 12:15 / Hebrews 3:13 — same rationale as Lie Eliminator's "What Others Observe." Here the witness has to be a peer adult (a spouse), not the person the pattern acts on — a child is not a fair party to monitor the pattern operating on them.

**Rehearsal / Phase Tracking:**
> **Identity Installer**'s own Active/Integrating/Integrated design, reused directly rather than re-derived — this is exactly the "is the old pattern still firing" question the phase system was already built to track.

## Distant Judge — Obedience/Consequence Reflex

**This Activates When:**
> My child disobeys or doesn't comply immediately — the moment the pull toward punishment-first shows up before anything relational has happened.

**Whose Script This Is:**
> My father's. Obedience enforced through immediate, clear punishment — spanking, grounding — with no instruction, no training-up, no loving expression attached. He was the punisher, not a gentle, drawing-out presence. This is his sequence, not mine — I inherited it by watching it, not by choosing it.

**Wound or Gap?**
> Both. *Wound:* being punished rather than instructed taught me that "father" and "distant judge" are the same category — the reflex protects against ever being seen as soft, the thing my own punishment implicitly told me was dangerous to be. *Gap:* I was never shown what instructive, relational discipline actually looks like moment to moment — restraint has given me a "no," but not a procedural "instead."

**The Old Script — Step by Step:**
1. *(Body: name what fires first — where, what it feels like. Fill in from your own noticing.)*
2. Child disobeys → a flash of threat to my authority.
3. I move toward distributing a consequence before I've engaged relationally at all.
4. The consequence is delivered from a position of distance and judgment, not connection.
5. I disengage — the interaction closes at the consequence. No instruction, no repair, no return to warmth follows it.

**What This Script Gives Me:**
> A sense of control when I feel disrespected or unsteady. Proof I'm not weak or "soft." It lets me skip the harder, more exposed work of staying relationally present with a child's defiance instead of ending it by force.

**5b-i. Internal Cost (to me) — Past / Present / Future:**
> *Past:* *(A specific remembered moment you saw this script cost you something — a look on your own face, a thought after the fact, a moment you didn't like who you'd just been.)*
> *Present:* Right now, it keeps me operating as one who distributes from a distance rather than one who draws out what my kids are capable of and believes the best of them — which is the opposite of who I actually want to be as a father.
> *Future:* *(Ten years from now, if this script keeps running unchanged — what does your relationship with each of your kids look like? Be specific, not general.)*

**5b-ii. External Cost (to my child / my family) — Past / Present / Future:**
> *Past:* *(A specific moment you saw this land on your child — their face, their withdrawal, what they said or didn't say.)*
> *Present:* What the research on this exact pattern documents: obedience purchased through consequence produces compliance under surveillance, not internalized character — the reverse of what I actually want to build in them.
> *Future:* *(What does your child's felt sense of you — as father, as authority — look like at 18, if the pattern doesn't change? What relationship are you actually risking?)*

**The Desired Script — Step by Step:**
1. *(Body: the early-warning signal you're training yourself to catch — where does the old script's opening move register, before it discharges?)*
2. Child disobeys → I notice the trigger without immediately acting on it.
3. I engage relationally first — approaching as one teaching capability, assuming the best of them, not as a judge.
4. I instruct: name what's needed and why, in a way that draws out what they're capable of rather than simply asserting authority.
5. If consequence is warranted, it's delivered from connection, not distance — paired with instruction, never substituting for it.
6. I stay engaged afterward. The interaction does not close at the consequence.

**Anchor Identity:**
> [[Identity Installer - Personal#1. Beloved Child of God]] — I cannot consistently draw capability out of my children from a place where my own worth is still organized around obedience and control. Belovedness, not performance, is the identity this script has to be rehearsed from.

**Holding Both — In the Body:**
> Where does the old script — the flash of threat, the pull toward distance — live in my body, even on an ordinary day?
>
> Where does the desired script — engaged, instructive, connected — land when I picture running it instead?
>
> One hand on each place. Hold both. Breathe. What happens at the place that's braced for threat, now that engaged presence is here with it — not after it?

**The Repair Step:**
> When the old script fires anyway — name it to my child directly, same day if possible: *"I reacted like the distant judge just now, not like the dad I actually want to be with you. I'm sorry. Here's what I actually wanted you to understand..."* Owning the misstep to my child is not damage control — it is a second, more direct lesson than the original rupture: that the authority in this house is accountable, not a verdict-giver watching from a distance.

**Witness:**
> *(Your wife, named specifically — the person positioned to tell you honestly which script actually ran, since your children aren't a fair party to monitor the pattern operating on them.)*
