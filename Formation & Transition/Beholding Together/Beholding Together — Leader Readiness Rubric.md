# Beholding Together — Leader Readiness Rubric

**What this is:** A concrete tool replacing "someone with maturity and leadership" with a testable profile, for a local pastor to use in selecting or affirming who leads a Beholding Together group. **Who it's for:** the pastor doing the selecting, and the candidate leader doing honest self-assessment first. **How to use:** in order — Part A (self-assessment), then Part B (pastor conversation), then Part C (supervised practice round) — none skipped, and Part C is not optional even when Parts A and B go well. A leader who has completed all three is responsible for administering the Screening Instrument and the Stop-Redirect Protocol with participants.

**Status: DRAFT — adapted from a source document (Facilitator Competency — Initial Stage) that is itself scoped only to Stage 1 of a different context and has not been tested at this narrower scope. Revised 2026-08-20 to add Part C (supervised practice round), per [[Beholding Together — Theory of Change]] Node 6, item 3 — Parts A and B alone (self-report plus character corroboration) are exactly the assessment shape the training-design evidence in that document says does not predict durable skill. Treat as a strong starting point, not a validated instrument.**

---

## Part A — Candidate Leader Self-Assessment (private, before any conversation)

1. **Lived experience.** Have you personally gone through real change in an implicit or emotional pattern in your own life — not just understood it intellectually — through Scripture, prayer, and relationship with others? Can you name a specific example? (Heb. 5:12–14 — teaching this requires having been trained by it yourself first.)
2. **Regulation under others' distress.** When someone near you becomes upset or distressed, are you generally able to stay calm and present — or do you find yourself needing to fix it quickly, shutting down, or becoming distressed yourself?
3. **Holding without fixing.** Are you comfortable sitting with someone's pain or struggle without needing to resolve it, give advice, or move them toward feeling better right away?
4. **Scriptural literacy.** Can you bring relevant Scripture into a conversation accurately and humbly, without over-applying a verse to someone else's specific situation?
5. **Posture check.** Do you see this role as modeling your own ongoing growth alongside the group — or as a way to fix other people's problems? (Facilitator Competency's distinction: condition-creator, not transformation agent.)
6. **Accountability.** Are you willing to operate under your pastor's oversight for this role — including reporting concerns and stepping back if asked, without that being treated as a failure?

---

## Part B — Pastor Conversation and Affirmation

- Review Part A together, plainly.
- The pastor's own independent knowledge of the candidate matters here and isn't optional — this rubric doesn't select a leader by itself; the pastor's affirmation does. Ask directly: *"Has this person shown durable, lived change in their own life that others besides themselves could testify to?"*
- If the pastor doesn't know the candidate well enough to answer that — that's itself the answer. See decision logic.
- A candidate who clears Part B moves to Part C. Part B affirmation is provisional, not final, until Part C is complete.

---

## Part C — Supervised Practice Round (required before independent leading)

**Why this exists:** Parts A and B assess self-report and character corroboration — real prerequisites, but not evidence the candidate can actually *execute* the four-element mechanism (awareness, safety, beholding, repetition) with another person, or recognize in real time when to invoke the Stop-Redirect Protocol. [[Beholding Together — Theory of Change]] (Node 3) names the evidence directly: training-method research on relational/communicative skills (Miller, Yahne, Moyers, Martinez & Pirritano, 2004) and deliberate-practice research (Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer, 1993) both find that self-assessment and workshop-style preparation, without observed practice and feedback, do not reliably predict durable skilled behavior. Part C closes that gap directly rather than assuming Parts A and B cover it.

**Who observes:** the affirming pastor, or an already-affirmed Beholding Together leader in the same church if the pastor prefers a peer with direct experience of the exercises. Not a clinician — this stays inside the lay-competency scope the whole track is built for; the observer's job is to watch for the four elements and the stop/redirect boundary, not to run a clinical evaluation.

**What happens:** the candidate runs one full practice round of the paired-practice exercise — using a low-stakes prompt, not a real disclosure — playing the leader role while the observer and one or two others stand in as the "group." (Until the curriculum's own Session 1 material exists, use the opening script and a simple low-stakes awareness prompt from the [[Beholding Together — In-Session Stop-Redirect Protocol]] as the practice material; replace with the actual Session 1 exercise once the curriculum is built.) The observer then gives direct, specific feedback — not a pass/fail impression — against these questions:

1. Did the candidate open with the Stop-Redirect Protocol's opening script, substantially as written, establishing the self-pause signal?
2. Did the candidate's presence actually seem to lower tension in the room (safety, element 2), or did their own visible anxiety raise it?
3. When guiding the practice, did the candidate stay with awareness-then-safety-then-beholding in that order, or rush toward "fixing" or resolution (the same failure Part A, Item 3 asks about self-report — Part C tests it live)?
4. Presented with a simulated Tier 1 moment (mild emotion) and a simulated Tier 2 moment (content beyond scope, scripted by the observer in advance), did the candidate respond at the correct tier — normalizing Tier 1, pausing that pair without processing content at Tier 2 — or misjudge the boundary?

**Outcome of this round:** specific, named feedback either way — what to keep doing, and if something needs to change, exactly what and how it will be checked again. This is coaching, not a single-attempt gate; a candidate who is not yet ready is not disqualified, only not yet affirmed for independent leading.

## Decision Logic

- **All Part A items affirmed, positive pastor corroboration, and Part C observation shows the candidate executing the four elements in the correct order with an accurate stop/redirect response:** Affirmed to lead independently.
- **Gaps in Part A — especially #2 (regulation) or #3 (holding without fixing):** Not yet. Name specifically what would need to change, and revisit later. This is the same skill the group's mutual practice depends on (co-regulation) — a leader who doesn't have it yet can't model or protect it in the room. (Part C will very likely surface the same gap live, even if Part A self-report missed it.)
- **No real pastor corroboration available** (new to the church, not yet known): Not yet — recommend a season of being known first. This operationalizes "recognized, not self-appointed" as a hard requirement rather than a preference.
- **Parts A and B clear, but Part C reveals a gap** (misjudged a tier, rushed toward fixing, visibly raised rather than lowered tension): Not yet to lead independently. Give specific, actionable feedback, and require a second observed round — focused on the named gap — before affirming. This is expected to happen sometimes; it is Part C doing its job, not a failure of the candidate or the process.
- **Part C repeatedly reveals the same gap after coaching:** Not a fit for this role at this time, regardless of how strong Parts A and B were. Character and self-awareness are necessary but were never sufficient on their own — this is the case Part C exists to catch.

## Open Dependencies

- Not yet tested at this scope — the source document (Facilitator Competency) was built for a different (congregational-transition) context.
- No clinical or pastoral-training consultant review yet.
- Depends on the Screening Instrument and Stop-Redirect Protocol existing as the tools a leader affirmed here will actually administer — both exist as drafts; neither is clinically reviewed yet.
- Part C's practice material is a placeholder (borrowed from the Stop-Redirect Protocol's opening script and a generic low-stakes prompt) until the actual curriculum and Session 1 exercise are built — revisit Part C's instructions once that exists.
- Part C assumes at least one already-affirmed leader or a willing pastor is available to observe and give specific behavioral feedback, which itself assumes some baseline skill in giving that kind of feedback — not yet addressed by any tool in this track.
