# Beholding Together — In-Session Stop-Redirect Protocol

**What this is:** A scripted set of leader responses for use *during* paired/triad practice sessions in a Beholding Together group — the moment-to-moment safety layer. **Who it's for:** the local, church-selected lay leader running a group — assumes no clinical training. **How to use:** the opening script is read verbatim every session before paired practice; the three response tiers are memorized in advance, not looked up in the moment, since real-time response can't wait on consulting a document.

This protocol handles what happens live, in the room. It is a companion to, not a replacement for, the pre-group candidate screening instrument (not yet built) and a Formation & Transition–specific crisis/referral reference (not yet built — see Section E).

---

## A. Prevention — Opening Script (verbatim, every session before paired practice)

> "Before we practice together: I'm not a counselor, and this isn't therapy — I'm here to help us stay safe and grounded while we do this. If anything feels like too much at any point, you can say the word 'pause' out loud, or just raise your hand — that's a full stop, no explanation needed, and nobody will ask you to say more than you want to. If something comes up for your partner that feels bigger than what we're doing here, that's not a failure of the exercise — it just means we pause and I'll step in."

This does two things before anything goes wrong: it gives every participant their own stop signal, so safety doesn't depend entirely on the leader noticing something; and it pre-frames a stop as normal rather than a crisis, which lowers the odds of someone pushing through something they shouldn't.

---

## B. Three Response Tiers

**Tier 1 — Expected intensity.**
Signals: tears, voice breaking, visible emotion while staying present and responsive to the room. This is normal for this kind of work, not a stop condition.
*Leader response:* Stay nearby, don't intervene in the pair. If checking in: *"Take whatever time you need — there's no rush here."* The exercise continues or wraps naturally.

**Tier 2 — Beyond this group's scope.**
Signals: content suggesting something larger than an everyday implicit pattern — abuse, complex trauma, a disclosure neither the leader nor the partner is equipped to hold, or a participant who seems overwhelmed past what Tier 1 covers.
*Leader response — stop that pair, not the room.* Approach directly: *"Let's pause here — thank you both for your trust. [Name], I'd love to talk with you for a few minutes after we finish tonight."* Redirect the room's attention elsewhere (start the group's closing element early, or invite quiet reflection) so the moment isn't put on display. Do not process the disclosure content in front of the group, and do not ask the discloser to explain further right now.
*Follow-up (required, not optional):* private conversation after the session — see Section D.

**Tier 3 — Safety-critical.**
Signals: suicidality, disclosure of ongoing danger or abuse, dissociation (someone who can't reorient to the room), or a medical emergency.
*Leader response:* Stop the exercise for the entire group immediately: *"We're going to pause everything for a moment."* Do not leave the person alone. This tier hands off to the church's crisis protocol — pastor contact, emergency services, an established referral contact. The leader's job here is containment and handoff, not resolution.

---

## C. Protecting the Listening Partner

The person who *received* a Tier 2 or 3 disclosure is also carrying something now, and this gets missed if all the attention goes to the discloser.
*Leader response:* Before the session ends, check in privately with the partner too: *"That took real care on your part. How are you doing with what you heard?"* If they seem overwhelmed, they get the same private-follow-up treatment as the discloser, and can be reminded that anything they heard isn't theirs to carry or repeat.

---

## D. After the Session

- **Private follow-up conversation** with anyone flagged Tier 2 or 3: not diagnostic — just a check on their next step. Do they want to keep coming? Do they need something the group can't provide? Do they have someone (pastor, counselor) to talk to?
- **Leader reports to their supervising pastor** — not carrying it alone, and not optional. This matches the accountability posture the leader role already requires (see [[Formation & Transition — Facilitator Competency — Initial Stage]]).
- **Referral resource** — this protocol cannot supply one generically. Each church needs to attach its own actual referral contacts (pastor, local Christian counselor, crisis line) before running a group. This is a required local setup step, not something the curriculum hands them pre-filled.

---

## E. What This Protocol Does Not Do

It is not a screening tool — it handles what happens live, in the room; it does not replace the pre-group candidate screening instrument, which still needs to be built separately. It is also not a substitute for a Formation & Transition–specific crisis/referral document, which does not yet exist. (A crisis/referral protocol exists for the Covenant Identity Coaching track, but it belongs to that track and should not be cross-referenced directly here — Formation & Transition needs its own version, built from the same underlying safety knowledge, kept separately per the standing rule that the two tracks are never blended.)

**Before this is real:** this draft should be reviewed by a clinical consultant with trauma-informed assessment training, ideally familiar with memory reconsolidation work specifically (ref. Ecker, Ticic & Hulley, *Unlocking the Emotional Brain*, 2012) — it was built from general trauma-informed group-practice principles (window of tolerance, participant self-pause signals), not from the reviewer's own clinical training, and a qualified reviewer may catch what this draft can't.
