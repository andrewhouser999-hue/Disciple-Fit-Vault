# Beholding Together — Pre-Group Candidate Screening Instrument

**What this is:** A two-part fit-check completed before someone joins a Beholding Together group — not a diagnosis, not a clinical assessment, not a substitute for a real intake conversation with someone qualified to make that call if anything here is ambiguous. **Who it's for:** the local pastor or lay leader enrolling participants. **How to use:** Part A first, always completed privately and in writing before any conversation; Part B is a short leader conversation afterward, reviewing anything flagged in Part A.

**Status: DRAFT — pending clinical consultant review before use.** See Design Basis below for what is and isn't sourced from validated methodology.

---

## Design Basis & Sourcing

- **Trauma-informed framing (safety, transparency, choice)** — SAMHSA's trauma-informed care principles. *Established.*
- **Item 3 (direct safety question) sourcing** — modeled on the Columbia Protocol's core logic that brief, direct suicide-risk screening administered by a non-clinician is a real, validated category of practice, distinct from full clinical assessment. *Established approach; this instrument uses the logic, not the proprietary instrument itself — a clinical consultant should confirm the exact wording against a licensed version before deployment.*
- **Deliberately excludes ACE-style trauma-history checklists** (Felitti et al., 1998) — *design judgment.* ACE tools are built for clinical/research use with support structures around disclosure; using one here to screen people OUT would require asking people IN, which risks the exact retraumatization this instrument exists to prevent. Current functioning is screened instead of history.
- **Dissociation-awareness item (Part A, Q4)** — *design judgment, Theoretical confidence.* Not drawn from a specific validated lay-administrable dissociation scale; written in plain behavioral language a non-clinician can ask and a participant can self-report without clinical vocabulary. Flag for clinical reviewer: is there a better-sourced brief item available?

---

## Part A — Private Written Self-Check

*Completed alone, before any conversation with a leader. Framing matters: this is presented as "helping us build the right group for you," not as a gate to pass.*

1. In the last month, have you been receiving care from a therapist, counselor, or doctor specifically for trauma, abuse, or a mental health crisis?
2. Do you currently have safe, stable housing and are you free from ongoing abuse or danger in your daily life?
3. In the past two weeks, have you had thoughts of harming yourself or ending your life?
4. Do you sometimes lose track of time, feel disconnected from your body or surroundings, or find yourself somewhere without remembering how you got there?
5. When something hard comes up for you emotionally, are you generally able to keep functioning through the rest of your day — or does it tend to shut you down for hours or days at a time?
6. Are you hoping this group will give you ongoing support for one specific, significant past trauma?

---

## Part B — Leader Conversation (5–10 minutes)

After reviewing Part A privately, the leader meets briefly with the participant:

- Review any flagged answers together, plainly and without judgment: *"I saw you noted [X] — can you tell me a little about where things stand with that right now?"*
- Ask directly: *"Is there anything happening in your life right now that feels like a crisis, or that you're actively working through with a professional?"*
- Confirm understanding: *"Just so we're clear together — this group isn't therapy or counseling, and it's not meant to replace care you're already receiving. Does that match what you're looking for?"*
- Where the leader is also part of the same local church, their own independent knowledge of the person is a legitimate input here — this isn't meant to rely on self-report alone.

---

## Decision Logic

- **Any positive answer to Q3 (safety):** Stop the screening conversation. This is not a group-enrollment decision — hand off immediately to the church's crisis protocol. Do not proceed to Part B as a normal conversation.
- **Positive on Q1 (active trauma-specific clinical care) or Q5 indicating shutdown lasting hours/days:** Not a candidate right now. Frame as "not yet," not "no" — encourage continuing their current care, and revisit fit later.
- **Positive on Q2 (unsafe/unstable):** Not a candidate right now — refer to appropriate support first (safety and stability come before this kind of group work, not alongside it).
- **Positive on Q4 (dissociation) alone, with no other flags:** Not an automatic exclusion — requires the Part B conversation before enrolling, not enrollment by default.
- **Positive on Q6 (seeking trauma-specific ongoing support):** This program isn't built for that — refer toward trauma-specific care, framed honestly as a scope boundary, not a rejection.
- **No flags:** Candidate — enroll.
