## Purpose

**What it is:** The causal explanation of how Beholding Together produces transformation — from the underlying change mechanism, through how that mechanism must be taught to lay people to reach real skill (not just familiarity with the idea), to the outcome the whole design exists to produce: the skill cascading beyond the formal group into ordinary, unsupervised one-another life. Every tool built so far in this track — and every session in the curriculum still to be built — should be traceable to a specific node in this chain.

**When used:** Before building any curriculum session, workbook page, or leader-guide instruction, to verify which node it serves and whether that node's requirements are actually met by the design, not just gestured at. When a pilot group underperforms or a leader reports the group "understood it but couldn't do it," to diagnose which node broke. When scrutinizing any future addition to this track for scope creep or unearned confidence.

**Who uses it:** Andrew, as the track's designer. Not a leader-facing or participant-facing document — this is the engineering spec underneath the curriculum, parallel in function to [[Covenant Identity Coaching — Theory of Change]] for CIC, and deliberately built as a separate document rather than a shared one, per the standing rule that CIC and Formation & Transition never blend vocabulary or tooling even where they draw on the same underlying research base.

## The Causal Chain

### Node 1 — The Mechanism: What Actually Changes an Implicit Pattern

**What is happening:** Across memory reconsolidation research, affect-labeling research, polyvagal/co-regulation research, Focusing research, and habit science, the mechanisms that reliably change an automatic, non-verbal, procedural pattern converge on four recurring elements, named differently in each field but structurally the same:

1. **Awareness / naming.** The pattern has to become consciously visible before it can be touched. Affect labeling — putting felt experience into words — measurably reduces amygdala reactivity (Lieberman et al., 2007, UCLA fMRI research; *Established*). Biblical anchor: **Ephesians 5:13** — "But when anything is exposed by the light, it becomes visible" (ESV).

2. **Relational / spiritual safety.** The nervous system has to be regulated enough, in relationship, for anything new to be received rather than defended against. Siegel's window-of-tolerance framework and Porges' polyvagal theory both describe co-regulation — a calm, attuned other person's presence measurably widening what a dysregulated person can tolerate and process (*Established/Supported*). Biblical anchor: **1 John 4:18** — "There is no fear in love, but perfect love casts out fear" (ESV).

3. **Juxtaposition / beholding of something truer.** The old pattern has to be held alongside a live, felt, disconfirming experience — not just told it's wrong. This is the reconsolidation window: a schema that is emotionally activated when a mismatching experience is introduced can be rewritten at the level of the original encoding, not merely supplemented alongside it (Nader; Schafe & LeDoux, 2000; Ecker, Ticic & Hulley, *Unlocking the Emotional Brain*, 2012 — *Established/Supported*, the strongest and most load-bearing single finding in this chain). Gendlin's Focusing method (1978) and the Hendricks meta-analysis on felt-sense processing describe the same juxtaposition at the level of bodily-felt experience, not just cognitive belief. Biblical anchor: **2 Corinthians 3:18** — "And we all, with unveiled face, beholding the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another. For this comes from the Lord who is the Spirit" (ESV) — the source of this product's name.

4. **Repetition over time.** A single moment of insight does not durably rewire an implicit pattern. Wood & Neal's habit-science research finds that repetition in a stable context — not insight — is what drives lasting behavioral and procedural change (*Established*). Biblical anchor: **Hebrews 5:14** — "solid food is for the mature, for those who have their powers of discernment trained by constant practice to distinguish good from evil" (ESV) — *gegymnasmena*, trained gymnasium-style, by repeated use.

**The narrative case study:** Psalm 73 runs all four elements in a single text — Asaph names his envy and near-stumbling explicitly (awareness), enters God's sanctuary where he is safely held rather than condemned (safety), and there his perspective is reordered by beholding the wicked's actual end rather than their apparent prosperity (juxtaposition/beholding — the psalm's own turning point), producing a settled, repeatable confession ("Whom have I in heaven but you?") rather than a one-time emotional peak. This is not proof-texting a modern framework onto Scripture; the psalm's own internal structure moves through these four movements in this order.

**Mechanism status:** Each element individually is well-sourced (see confidence labels above). The claim that these four specific elements, together, are jointly sufficient and severally necessary for durable implicit-level change — as opposed to merely correlated with it — is a design synthesis, not a proven causal model. *Theoretical confidence on the joint-sufficiency claim; Established/Supported on each individual element.*

### Node 2 — Why This Doesn't Require CIC's Diagnostic Architecture

**What is happening:** CIC's five-layer architecture exists to do something Beholding Together deliberately does not attempt: diagnose *which specific false covenant object* organizes a person's false identity, with enough precision that covenant truth has something exact to land on (see [[Covenant Identity Coaching — Theory of Change]], Node 2). That precision work requires a trained practitioner because misdiagnosis at that level of specificity — naming the wrong false structure, or naming a structure formed by unprocessed complex trauma — can do real harm.

**Why Beholding Together can skip it:** The population this track is built for is people with *no or weak complex trauma* holding *unhelpful but ordinary* implicit patterns — impatience with a spouse, a reflexive defensiveness, a pattern of people-pleasing that isn't rooted in a diagnosable wound but in ordinary un-Spirit-formed habit. For this population, the four-element mechanism doesn't need to isolate the exact false structure to work; awareness + safety + beholding + repetition can operate on "this reaction isn't who I want to be and isn't who Christ is forming me to be" without naming the precise covenant object underneath it, the way two friends praying honestly together have always been able to help each other without either one running a diagnostic protocol.

**Where the boundary is enforced:** This is exactly the population the [[Beholding Together — Pre-Group Candidate Screening Instrument]] exists to protect — anyone whose pattern is rooted in active trauma, current danger, dissociation, or crisis-level risk is explicitly screened out or redirected before this simpler mechanism is ever applied to them, precisely because the simpler mechanism is not built to carry that weight safely.

**Mechanism status:** This is a scope judgment, not an empirical finding — no study directly tests "does the four-element mechanism work as well without diagnostic precision for a non-trauma population." It is inferred from the general clinical principle that intervention intensity should match presenting severity, and from the precedent programs' own implicit scoping (Celebrate Recovery, GriefShare, and Boundaries all achieve real change at lay-facilitation depth for their respective non-clinical populations). *Design judgment, reasonably grounded, not directly evidenced.*

### Node 3 — The Pedagogical Problem: Knowing the Mechanism Is Not the Same as Being Able to Do It

**What is happening:** Node 1 explains what changes an implicit pattern. It does not explain how to get an ordinary lay person to a place where they can actually *execute* that mechanism with another person — safely, reliably, without training being purely informational. This is a distinct engineering problem from the mechanism itself, and the curriculum cannot be built as if teaching the four elements were the same task as producing the skill to use them.

**Why content alone fails:** Miller, Yahne, Moyers, Martinez & Pirritano (2004) ran a randomized trial testing how clinicians actually learn Motivational Interviewing — a directly relevant precedent because it is a study of training method for a relational/communicative skill, not merely a knowledge domain. Finding: workshop-style training (teaching plus demonstration — structurally what a content-only session arc is) improved knowledge and attitudes but did **not** produce durable change in actual skilled behavior. What did produce durable skill was workshop **plus** practice **plus** feedback on that practice, sustained over time. *Established, RCT-level evidence — with an honest population caveat: the trial studied trained clinicians, not lay volunteers. The population differs; the underlying mechanism (practice-with-correction outperforms passive exposure) is a general skill-acquisition finding, not one specific to MI or to clinical training, and is corroborated independently below.*

**The general principle underneath it:** Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer's deliberate-practice research (1993) establishes that skill — as distinct from information — is produced by repeated, effortful practice with correction, not by exposure or repetition-without-feedback. *Established as a general finding; caveat: the popularized "10,000 hours" framing built on this research has been legitimately contested by later meta-analyses (e.g., Macnamara et al.) showing smaller and more domain-variable effect sizes than first claimed. The contested part is the dosage claim, not the core mechanism (feedback-corrected practice beats passive learning), which is not seriously disputed and is the only part this document relies on.*

**Implication for this track:** An 8-session content arc that teaches the four elements — even teaches them well, even grounds them richly in Scripture — is necessary but not sufficient to produce a lay person who can actually run the mechanism with a spouse or friend. The curriculum has to be engineered as a skill-training instrument, not a teaching series with practice bolted on.

### Node 4 — Structuring Sessions for Skill, Not Just Content

**What is happening:** If practice-with-feedback is the active ingredient (Node 3), the internal structure of every session that includes paired practice needs to be built around a real learning cycle, not around "teach, then try it." Kolb's experiential learning cycle (1984) — already an established source in Andrew's own evidence base — gives the structural answer: **concrete experience → reflective observation → abstract conceptualization → active experimentation.** Applied here: participants practice the skill with a partner first or early (concrete experience), then debrief specifically on what happened against the four elements, not just how it felt (reflective observation), then the leader connects what surfaced back to the mechanism and the Word (abstract conceptualization), then participants are sent to try it again between sessions in ordinary life (active experimentation) — and the next session's concrete experience begins from there.

**Why this reorders session pacing:** A pure content-delivery design would teach all four elements before any practice, on the theory that participants need the full framework first. Kolb's cycle argues the opposite: practice (even partial, even before all four elements are formally introduced) generates the reflective material that makes the later teaching land, rather than teaching landing on a blank slate. This doesn't mean unsafe practice before trust is built — see Sequencing below — but it does mean each session that includes practice needs a structured debrief step built in as a required component, not an optional close.

**Sequencing: trust precedes practice.** Kolb's cycle governs the internal structure of a practice-bearing session; it does not by itself say *when in the arc* practice may safely begin. That's a separate question, and Node 1's own element order answers it: element 2 (relational/spiritual safety) is what makes element 3 (juxtaposition/beholding) tolerable rather than re-injuring — 1 John 4:18's "perfect love casts out fear" is a precondition for 2 Corinthians 3:18's beholding to land as transformation rather than as exposure. A curriculum that asks near-strangers to practice vulnerable beholding together before safety is *functionally present in the room* — not merely explained as a concept — inverts the mechanism's own dependency order and risks producing exactly the Tier 2/3 events the Stop-Redirect Protocol exists to contain, at a rate the protocol was never meant to absorb as routine.

The practical sequencing this implies, worked out earlier in this track's design conversation and restated here as this document's governing answer to "when does practice start": early sessions build awareness (element 1) and establish safety as *lived group trust*, not just taught content (element 2), before beholding is ever practiced between two people; only once safety is functionally established does the first paired-practice session run; remaining sessions then deepen and repeat that practice (element 4), closing on integration and commissioning into unsupervised use (Node 7). A tentative shape for an 8-session cohort, illustrative rather than fixed: Orientation/Ps 73 → Awareness/Eph 5:13 → Safety/1 John 4:18 → Beholding introduced/2 Cor 3:18 → first paired practice → two deepening-practice sessions → integration/commissioning. Session count and pacing are not yet confirmed with Andrew and remain open curriculum-build decisions; the ordering principle — awareness and safety established before beholding is practiced, practice repeated before the arc closes — is what this document commits to, not the specific session count.

**Mechanism status:** Kolb's cycle is a well-established framework for adult experiential learning generally (*Established as a general pedagogical model*); its specific application to this curriculum's session design, and the sequencing claim built on Node 1's element order, are a design synthesis, not independently tested for this content.

### Node 5 — Defining "Adequate": The Competency Target

**What is happening:** "Generally adequate skill level" needs an operational meaning, or curriculum design has no way to know when training is sufficient versus when it's chasing an open-ended, unreachable standard. The Dreyfus model of skill acquisition (Dreyfus & Dreyfus, 1980) names five stages — novice, advanced beginner, competent, proficient, expert — and gives this track a real target: the design goal for both leaders and participants is the **competent** tier, not proficient or expert.

**Why this matters for this track specifically:** The entire premise of Beholding Together — that this is simpler than CIC's diagnostic architecture, and that "anyone with some maturity" can employ it — only holds if the competency bar is actually set at "competent," not silently drifting toward "proficient" or "expert" as the curriculum gets built out with more nuance and more edge cases. A curriculum that tries to produce Dreyfus-proficient lay leaders has quietly become a professional-training program again, which is the exact shape the earlier scrutiny pass eliminated when the professional-therapist-led model was reversed.

**Operational definition, competent tier, this track:** able to reliably execute the four elements with another person in a low-stakes, non-crisis context; able to recognize the edge of their own competence and stop or redirect rather than push through (this is precisely what the [[Beholding Together — In-Session Stop-Redirect Protocol]] trains); able to self-correct with occasional feedback rather than needing constant supervision. Not: able to diagnose why a technique isn't working, able to handle ambiguous or high-intensity disclosures unsupervised, able to train other leaders without further formation (that is a distinct, harder competency the cascade model needs but has not yet designed for — see Node 7).

**Mechanism status:** The Dreyfus model is a widely used, well-established framework for professional and skill-based training design (nursing's Benner adaptation, 1984, is the most field-tested instance) but is itself a theoretical/expert-opinion model rather than an empirically derived one. *Expert-opinion confidence, appropriately used here as an operational definition rather than a causal claim.*

### Node 6 — What This Requires of the Curriculum Build

**What is happening:** Nodes 3–5 aren't abstract pedagogy — they name concrete, load-bearing requirements the curriculum build must satisfy or the whole chain breaks downstream, no matter how theologically or mechanistically sound Node 1 is.

1. **A structured debrief step is mandatory after every paired-practice round**, not optional — without it, sessions produce exposure, not skill (Node 3), and the Kolb cycle (Node 4) has no reflective-observation stage to run.
2. **A post-arc reinforcement checkpoint is needed after the formal 8-session cohort ends** — a light-touch check-in a few weeks out, not a full additional session. Without it, the MI training research (Node 3) would predict skill decay, and decay directly threatens the one outcome that makes this track distinct from its precedents: the Layer 3 cascade (Node 7).
3. **The [[Beholding Together — Leader Readiness Rubric]] had an identified gap — closed 2026-08-20.** It originally certified leader readiness through self-assessment plus pastor corroboration only, with no supervised-practice-with-feedback step, even though Node 3's own evidence says exactly this kind of self-report-plus-character-witness assessment is not what predicts durable skill. The rubric now includes Part C — a supervised practice round, observed by the pastor or an already-affirmed leader, scored against the four elements and against an accurate Stop-Redirect Protocol tier response, with a coaching-not-gatekeeping decision logic (specific feedback and a second attempt, not automatic disqualification). The lived-experience and posture items in Parts A and B remain real prerequisites Part C doesn't replace — the fix was adding the missing skill check, not removing the character check.
4. **The competency target (Node 5) needs to be stated explicitly inside the leader guide and the workbook**, not left implicit — both so a leader doesn't over-reach into territory the Stop-Redirect Protocol already reserves for a Tier 2/3 handoff, and so Andrew doesn't unintentionally scope-creep the curriculum toward "proficient" while building it out in later drafts.

### Node 7 — Telos: Cascade Beyond the Group

**What is happening:** Success for Beholding Together was defined, in the scrutiny pass that shaped this track, not as "the 8-week cohort went well" but as the skill cascading beyond the formal group — a participant using awareness, safety, beholding, and repetition with their spouse or a friend, unsupervised, without a leader or a session structure present. This is "Layer 3" in the track's own working vocabulary, and it is the single largest differentiator this track claims against Celebrate Recovery, GriefShare, and Boundaries, none of which are designed around unsupervised skill transfer as the success metric.

**Why Nodes 3–6 are actually in service of this node, not a detour from it:** A curriculum that produces Dreyfus-novice participants who can recite the four elements but can't execute them without a leader present will never cascade — cascade requires actual competent-tier skill (Node 5), produced by practice-with-feedback (Node 3) structured through a real learning cycle (Node 4), reinforced past the point training normally stops (Node 6, item 2). Every pedagogical requirement named in this document exists because this telos requires it, not as generic good-training-design hygiene.

**What is not yet designed:** the cascade itself has no instrumentation — no way, currently, for the curriculum to know whether Layer 3 is actually happening versus hoped for. This is named as an open gap, not solved here.

## Where the Chain Breaks: Failure Mode Map

| Break point | What it looks like | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Node 1 applied outside its population | A trauma-rooted pattern is worked on as if it were an ordinary implicit habit | Screening Instrument exists exactly to prevent this; if bypassed, the four-element mechanism is asked to carry weight it isn't built for |
| Node 2 boundary blurred | A leader or participant starts diagnosing "why" a pattern exists, not just naming and beholding it | Drifts back toward CIC's diagnostic-precision work without CIC's training — the track-separation and scope boundary erode simultaneously |
| Node 3 skipped (content-only design) | Participants can describe the four elements accurately but can't execute them with a partner | Workshop-only training effect (Miller et al., 2004) — knowledge changes, behavior doesn't |
| Node 4 ignored (practice without structured debrief) | Paired practice happens but produces the same intensity/exposure each time without improvement | No reflective-observation stage — Kolb's cycle broken at step 2, practice repeats rather than compounds |
| Node 5 target drifts upward | Curriculum quietly adds nuance and edge-case handling until it requires more than competent-tier skill | Reintroduces the professional-led shape the earlier scrutiny pass deliberately removed |
| Node 6 items unbuilt | Leaders are certified on character and self-report alone; no post-arc reinforcement exists | Leader skill and leader readiness stay conflated; trained skill decays after Session 8 with nothing catching it |
| Node 7 uninstrumented indefinitely | The program runs cohort after cohort with no way to know if cascade is actually happening | The track's core differentiator becomes an unverified claim rather than a demonstrated outcome |

## What This Requires of the Leader

Two demands parallel CIC's Theory of Change, adapted to this track's much lower bar — this section exists to keep that bar honest, not to smuggle CIC's practitioner-formation weight back in through the side door.

**1. The leader must have actually lived the mechanism, not just learned it.** This is Hebrews 5:12–14's own logic, already the load-bearing text behind the Leader Readiness Rubric's Part A, Item 1 — someone who has not personally experienced awareness-safety-beholding-repetition producing change in their own life cannot credibly model it or recognize it happening in someone else. This is a lived-experience requirement, not a credentialing requirement, and that distinction is the whole reason a professional isn't required — but it is still a real bar, and 1 Timothy 3:6's caution against appointing the recently-formed applies here with full force even though the office in view isn't eldership.

**2. The leader must be able to recognize the edge of competent-tier skill in real time and stop, not push through it.** This is Node 5's operational definition made into a leader behavior, and it's the entire reason the Stop-Redirect Protocol exists as a memorized script rather than a reference document — the moment this skill is most needed is the moment there's no time to look anything up.

## Document Provenance

**Why this exists:** Beholding Together was built tool-forward, the same way CIC originally was: the Stop-Redirect Protocol, the Screening Instrument, and the Leader Readiness Rubric were each built as a safety gap surfaced in sequence, before the underlying mechanism and its pedagogical requirements had been written up as a single governing document. This document is that corrective, built at the point where the pedagogical question ("how do you actually educate someone to adequate skill?") could no longer be answered informally inside a single conversation turn and needed to become part of the track's permanent design record — the same trigger condition that produced CIC's Theory of Change.

**Governing question:** What is the full causal chain from the underlying change mechanism, through what it actually takes to train an ordinary lay person to competent-tier skill in that mechanism, to the outcome — unsupervised cascade — the whole track exists to produce?

**Primary contributors:**

| Contributor | Specific contribution |
|---|---|

| Matthew Lieberman et al. | Affect labeling (Node 1, element 1) |

| Eugene Gendlin | Focusing / felt sense (Node 1, element 3) |

| K. Anders Ericsson, Ralf Krampe & Clemens Tesch-Römer | Deliberate practice — feedback-corrected practice as the active ingredient in skill acquisition (Node 3) |

**Design decisions:**

- *Seven nodes, deliberately split between mechanism (1–2) and pedagogy (3–6) before telos (7):* The earlier research conversation answered "what changes an implicit pattern" well before it answered "how do you train someone to actually do that changing" — treating these as two different engineering problems, rather than assuming mechanism knowledge transfers automatically into teachable skill, is the central insight this document exists to preserve.
- *The competency target (Node 5) named explicitly rather than left as an intuition:* Without a stated target, curriculum development has no way to notice scope creep back toward the professional-led shape the earlier scrutiny pass deliberately eliminated. Naming "competent, not proficient" as the Dreyfus-tier target makes that boundary checkable, not just felt.
- *The Leader Readiness Rubric gap surfaced here, then closed the same day:* Consistent with the CIC Theory of Change's practice of naming mechanism gaps openly in a tool-anchoring table instead of presenting a falsely complete architecture — and, in this case, acting on the finding immediately rather than letting it sit as a flagged-but-unaddressed item. The rubric's Part C (supervised practice round) exists because this document named the gap first.
- *Node 1 not yet spun out into its own standalone document:* Considered and deliberately deferred — this document sources the mechanism fully enough to govern the curriculum build, and splitting it into a separate document before curriculum work begins would add a document without yet adding new content. Flagged in the mechanism-gaps list as a likely future need, not done preemptively.

**Scope boundary:** This document is the causal architecture governing curriculum design — why the training must be structured the way it is, and what "adequate" means. It is not the curriculum itself (session skeleton and content are still to be built), not the live-session safety script (that is the Stop-Redirect Protocol), and not the participant- or leader-selection instrument (those are the Screening Instrument and Leader Readiness Rubric). It answers "why should the curriculum be built this way?" not "what does Session 3 actually say?"
