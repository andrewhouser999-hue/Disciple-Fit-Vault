*All Scripture is ESV. A reference with no text beside it is a pointer to the full-text treatment in [[Covenant Identity — Biblical Foundations Index]].*

4. Check the claims ladder (Part II §D) before anything is published or spoken.

**The core claim, stated precisely:**

Covenant Identity Coaching is built on a Scripture-governed account of why a sincere believer can know the truth about who they are in Christ and still not live from it. Its sequence of work is designed to meet the conditions that account says must be met. Scripture decides what is true about identity, what has gone wrong, and what renewal is. Research is admitted only to describe how the inner life holds and changes beliefs, and only where it passes two tests. The practice does not produce the change. It arranges the conditions in which the Word, by the Spirit, does what it does. The problem the design targets is well evidenced. Each mechanism is grounded in Scripture and, at the component level, in research of varying strength. The practice as a whole has not yet been outcome-tested.

**Who this is written to** *(decided 2026-10-20: Wilderness Christian first, Sunday-Monday Christian second, following the Marketing Charter's Segment Registry)*: A born-again follower of Jesus who is already committed to growing in Christ. The first reader is the [[Wilderness Christian]], the Charter's Target Market. They have probably studied hard, because they were doing it alone. They know what they believe, and they cannot move something in themselves by effort. They distrust authority for reasons they earned, so they will test every claim against Scripture and against their own judgment. # PART I — THE VALUE CASE

### 1. The short answer

The value of Covenant Identity Coaching is not more truth. You already hold most of the truth you need. The value is that the truth you already believe reaches the place you actually live from.

That place decides what you reach for under pressure. It decides what you fear losing and what you protect when no one is watching. It can be out of step with your theology for years while you remain entirely sincere.

The practice rests on two sources, in a fixed order. Scripture comes first. It decides what is true about who you are, what has gone wrong, and what renewal looks like. Research comes second. It is allowed to describe how the inner life holds and changes deep beliefs. It is never allowed to decide where that change is going.

What you receive is a structured engagement of twelve weekly sessions. The work is built to do four things. It finds the specific belief that is governing you. It lets you feel what that belief has cost. It brings what God has actually said to that exact place. It gives the Holy Spirit room to keep working between sessions. The change itself is His. The practice exists to take away what has stood in its way.

One fact belongs near the start. The problem this addresses is well documented. The design is grounded piece by piece. The practice as a whole is new and has not been outcome-tested. Nothing here is offered as proven. It is offered as carefully built, with the reasons visible so you can judge them.

### 2. What is missing

You never stopped believing.

You kept showing up. You studied, prayed, served, and kept going, often with little company. You may have studied harder than most, because you were doing it on your own. By any honest measure you are a serious believer who wants to grow.

And there is a place in you that does not move.

It shows in the reaction that comes out the same way under pressure. It shows in a flatness you cannot explain. It shows in a truth you can teach and cannot quite rest in. You can say who God says you are. You cannot always feel it when it matters. Some part of you suspects that if this is still true after everything you have tried, the problem must be you.

That suspicion is wrong about the cause. It is not evidence that your faith is thin. It is evidence that the truth you hold and the place you live from have not yet met.

> *You know what you believe. You just can't seem to live from it.*
> *You've prayed this away more times than you can count. It keeps coming back.*
> *You're not walking away from God. You're just flat.*

*(Stage 2 recognition lines, already cleared in [[Covenant Identity Coaching — Quick Launch Plan]]. Hypothesis-level until tested against real prospect language.)*

Scripture names this gap. James describes a person who hears the Word and does not live from it:

> "For if anyone is a hearer of the word and not a doer, he is like a man who looks intently at his natural face in a mirror. For he looks at himself and goes away and at once forgets what he was like." (James 1:23–24)

Jesus quotes Isaiah about a people who honor God with their lips while their heart is far away (Matthew 15:8): *"This people honors me with their lips, but their heart is far from me"*. Paul writes the divided experience in the first person: *"For I do not understand my own actions. For I do not do what I want, but I do the very thing I hate"* (Romans 7:15).

Researchers have measured the same gap from outside the church. The REVEAL study of thirty congregations, beginning with Willow Creek, found that increased involvement in church activities did not predict long-term growth in love for God and others. George Barna's worldview research, now published through Arizona Christian University's Cultural Research Center, finds that a single-digit share of American adults hold what it calls a biblical worldview. It defines that as belief and consistent practice together (6% in 2021). These are population snapshots. They show that the gap exists. They do not show what closes it.

Nothing is missing from who you are in Christ. That is complete. Scripture states it in the past tense:

> "Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come." (2 Corinthians 5:17)

What may be missing is the felt, lived alignment of your inner life with a status that is already yours. Scripture calls that renewal: *"be transformed by the renewal of your mind"* (Romans 12:2). This practice calls it formation.

### 3. Why the usual help does not reach it

Almost everything you have tried did what it was built to do.

- Teaching delivers truth to many people at once.
- A book or a conference delivers information and a shared experience.
- A small group gives community and structure.
- Study and private discipline deepen what you know and practice.
- Accountability supplies consistency.
- Clinical counseling treats what needs treatment and builds the skills to manage it.
- Coaching clarifies goals and strategy.
- Pastoral care shepherds a whole life over time.

Each is good at its own job. None is built to find one person's specific governing belief and carry the truth to the exact place where it operates. That is a difference of scope. It is not a failure of competence. Most of them also work at the level of information, effort, or structure. For a person who already has plenty of each, more of the same does not touch what is wrong.

If you have studied hard on your own, a fact is not what is missing. A person is: someone who knows both the Word and the inner life, and who does not ask you to perform in exchange for the relationship.

Two further reasons make this hard to do alone. The first is that the heart is not easily read by its owner:

> "The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately sick; who can understand it?" (Jeremiah 17:9)

The second is that Scripture expects another person to help:

> "The purpose in a man's heart is like deep water, but a man of understanding will draw it out." (Proverbs 20:5)

Needing help here is not weakness. It is one of the ways God has ordinarily worked.

### 4. How it was designed: Scripture first, evidence second

**The order of construction.** Covenant Identity Coaching was not built by taking a coaching method and adding Bible verses to it. Its governing architecture was built from Scripture outward. It began with what Scripture says about where transformation happens. Only then did it ask what coaching method and what research could serve that claim. [[Covenant Identity Coaching — Peer Practitioner Briefing]] §1 records this order.

**The rule that governs research.** Scripture governs. It determines what identity is, what has gone wrong, and what renewal looks like. Research may describe how the inner life works at the level Scripture assumes but does not explain. It does not set the direction. Every research contribution had to pass one of two tests before it entered the design.

1. **Confirmatory.** Research independently observes what Scripture already states. It adds vocabulary and no new content.
2. **Additive.** Research describes a mechanism Scripture assumes but does not explain, such as how a belief is stored or why a truth fails to land. It enters only if it does not bring a competing account of what people are or what they are for.

A contribution that passes neither test is set aside, however useful it is clinically. The design names what it set aside. It does not adopt neural integration as the definition of flourishing. It does not treat shame as the root of identity. It does not accept a self that is whole by nature. It re-grounds that idea in the new self in Christ. It does not take over a directive counseling posture. The position behind this is what Eric Johnson calls Christian psychology: psychology developed from within Christian commitments, not secular psychology blended with Christian content. [[Covenant Identity — Integration Rationale]] holds the full record.

**Why this is not a compromise.** Scripture models the procedure. Paul cites a Cretan's own observation about Cretans and says of it, *"This testimony is true"* (Titus 1:13). He commands, *"but test everything; hold fast what is good"* (1 Thessalonians 5:21). He also warns:

> "See to it that no one takes you captive by philosophy and empty deceit, according to human tradition, according to the elemental spirits of the world, and not according to Christ." (Colossians 2:8)

Proverbs adds that *"It is the glory of God to conceal things, but the glory of kings is to search things out"* (Proverbs 25:2). The two tests try to do all four things at once. They test. They hold fast what is good. They refuse capture. They keep searching.

**What "never contradicting" means in practice.** When research and Scripture appear to disagree, Scripture governs. The research claim is dropped or held loosely. When Scripture is silent, as it is about how memory is stored, research may describe and may not prescribe. This is a procedure. It is not a hope that the two will always agree.

**An honest note on order.** Some tools began in lived formation work and were grounded in Scripture and research afterward. The Biblical Foundations Index records this in its basis line, which cites methodology-grounding research for the Lie Eliminator and Identity Installer done on 2026-06-11. For every design decision the Index also records whether the biblical case is Developed, Assumed, Contested, or still a Gap. That record is part of the design's integrity. When you say "Scripture first," say what is true. Scripture governs every design decision about who a person is, what has gone wrong, and what renewal is. Professional ethics, not Scripture, sets the rules for scope and referral, and the design says so. The architecture was built outward from Scripture.

### 5. What Scripture establishes: five claims the design stands on

**Claim 1. Identity is given before behavior is required.**

Every covenant God makes states who He is and what He has already done. The command comes after. At Sinai, before any law, He says:

> "You yourselves have seen what I did to the Egyptians, and how I bore you on eagles' wings and brought you to myself." (Exodus 19:4)

The Ten Commandments open the same way: *"I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery. You shall have no other gods before me"* (Exodus 20:2–3). Paul keeps the order:

> "For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand, that we should walk in them." (Ephesians 2:10)

Grammarians call this indicative before imperative. For this practice it means the work begins with who God has declared you to be, not with what you should do. Be ready for one counter-text. Exodus 19:5 begins, *"Now therefore, if you will indeed obey my voice and keep my covenant"*. The covenant research document answers it in Part 1 §3. Keep that answer at hand.

**Claim 2. The inner life is where God does His defining work.**

Jesus named His mission in terms of the inner life first. He was sent *"to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives"* (Isaiah 61:1, the text He reads in Luke 4:18–21). The prophets promised that the New Covenant would work at the same depth:

> "And I will give you a new heart, and a new spirit I will put within you. And I will remove the heart of stone from your flesh and give you a heart of flesh." (Ezekiel 36:26)

And: *"I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts"* (Jeremiah 31:33). Paul names the site of ongoing renewal:

> "So we do not lose heart. Though our outer self is wasting away, our inner self is being renewed day by day." (2 Corinthians 4:16)

**Claim 3. Hearing is not the same as living from.**

Scripture treats the gap between knowing and living as a category of its own. James calls it self-deception (1:22–25). Hebrews explains why a message can fail to benefit the people who hear it:

> "For good news came to us just as to them, but the message they heard did not benefit them, because they were not united by faith with those who listened." (Hebrews 4:2)

Paul commends the Thessalonians because they did more than hear:

> "And we also thank God constantly for this, that when you received the word of God, which you heard from us, you accepted it not as the word of men but as what it really is, the word of God, which is at work in you believers." (1 Thessalonians 2:13)

"Received" and "accepted" translate two different Greek verbs. The first is about taking in what was heard. The second is about welcoming it. *(Mapping this to the practice's registration-and-reception distinction is a design judgment, and both Hebrews 4:2 and this verse are new candidate anchors still to be vetted. See Part II §I.)* What Scripture supplies is the phenomenon. It does not supply the mechanism. The mechanism is where research enters (§8).

**Claim 4. Change is the Spirit's work, received in a posture Scripture names.**

> "Work out your own salvation with fear and trembling, for it is God who works in you, both to will and to work for his good pleasure." (Philippians 2:12–13)

The "for" makes God's working the ground of ours, not a supplement to it. The mechanism text is 2 Corinthians 3:18:

> "And we all, with unveiled face, beholding the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another. For this comes from the Lord who is the Spirit."

The human acts are turning and beholding. The transformation is received. Paul asks Christians a revealing question: *"Did you receive the Spirit by works of the law or by hearing with faith?"* (Galatians 3:2). He asks it again about the Spirit's continuing supply (3:5). The practice therefore treats its tools as conditions, not causes. They are the riverbed, not the water. It stands in the Wesleyan means-of-grace stream. God has bound Himself to work through appointed means such as Scripture, prayer, and fellowship. The practice is built to serve those means. It is not offered as a new one. Readers from other traditions will weigh this differently (§G, objection 9).

**Claim 5. God uses people as one of His means.**

Paul does not describe his own work as producing change. He describes cooperation:

> "Him we proclaim, warning everyone and teaching everyone with all wisdom, that we may present everyone mature in Christ. For this I toil, struggling with all his energy that he powerfully works within me." (Colossians 1:28–29)

He describes the manner as well: *"we were gentle among you, like a nursing mother taking care of her own children... we were ready to share with you not only the gospel of God but also our own selves"* (1 Thessalonians 2:7–8). Scripture commends the ordinary work of people helping people see: *"Iron sharpens iron, and one man sharpens another"* (Proverbs 27:17), and *"if anyone is caught in any transgression, you who are spiritual should restore him in a spirit of gentleness"* (Galatians 6:1). This is why a coach is neither a replacement for the Spirit nor a replacement for the church. A coach is one of the means.

### 6. The identities you are rooting your heart in

The Bible states some identities more often and more plainly than others. This practice builds on ten of them. None is new. You can find each in your own Bible, and you can check how the practice uses it there. The anchors follow [[Covenant Identity — Biblical Foundations Index]] §3.

1. **Made in God's image.** Genesis 1:26–27; Psalm 139:13–14; Colossians 3:10.
2. **Known fully and chosen.** Psalm 139:1–4; Ephesians 1:4; John 15:16; Jeremiah 31:3.
3. **A new creation.** 2 Corinthians 5:17; Romans 6:6; Philippians 1:6.
4. **An adopted child.** Romans 8:15; Galatians 4:4–7; 1 John 3:1.
5. **A fellow heir with Christ.** Romans 8:17.
6. **Indwelt by the Spirit.** John 14:16–18; Romans 8:9; 1 Corinthians 6:19.
7. **Seated with Christ, and held.** Ephesians 2:6; John 10:28–29; Romans 8:38–39.
8. **Loved as the Father loves the Son.** John 17:23; John 15:9; Romans 5:5.
9. **Sent.** 2 Corinthians 5:20; Ephesians 2:10; Acts 1:8.
10. **Being conformed to Christ's image.** Romans 8:29; 2 Corinthians 3:18; Philippians 1:6.

Take two of them in full. The adoption passage carries the Spirit's witness:

> "For you did not receive the spirit of slavery to fall back into fear, but you have received the Spirit of adoption as sons, by whom we cry, 'Abba! Father!' The Spirit himself bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God, and if children, then heirs—heirs of God and fellow heirs with Christ, provided we suffer with him in order that we may also be glorified with him." (Romans 8:15–17)

And the tenth identity names the direction of the whole:

> "For those whom he foreknew he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son, in order that he might be the firstborn among many brothers." (Romans 8:29)

Nine of the ten describe what is already true of you, and most are true because you are in Christ. The tenth is the process by which the other nine become lived. This distinction keeps the work from becoming self-persuasion. The practice does not ask you to declare a self into existence. It asks you to reckon with what is already true:

> "So you also must consider yourselves dead to sin and alive to God in Christ Jesus." (Romans 6:11)

### 7. Why the order matters for your faith

Underneath the value question there may be another one: can I trust this? For a believer the answer depends on where the trust is placed.

**The faith this builds is faith in God and His Word, not in the method or the coach.** The practice names the opposite as a failure mode: treating the tools as the cause of change. The coach creates conditions. God does the changing.

**Every step can be checked against your own Bible.** The Bereans *"received the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures daily to see if these things were so"* (Acts 17:11). The practice invites that. Part II §C lists the Scripture behind each step and marks where the biblical case is still thin. The standard is that a step with no biblical case should go.

**Faith grows by the Word, and Scripture says how.**

> "So faith comes from hearing, and hearing through the word of Christ." (Romans 10:17)

In its own context that sentence is about the gospel and saving faith. The practice therefore does not rest a believer's growth on that verse alone. It rests it on the texts that speak to believers directly. Paul asks Christians whether the Spirit's continuing supply comes *"by works of the law, or by hearing with faith"* (Galatians 3:5). He tells the Thessalonians that the word of God *"is at work in you believers"* (1 Thessalonians 2:13). Peter says God's promises are the means by which *"you may become partakers of the divine nature"* (2 Peter 1:4).

**Faith holds the hard fact and the promise together.**

> "He did not weaken in faith when he considered his own body, which was as good as dead (since he was about a hundred years old), or when he considered the barrenness of Sarah's womb. No unbelief made him waver concerning the promise of God, but he grew strong in his faith as he gave glory to God, fully convinced that God was able to do what he had promised." (Romans 4:19–21)

Abraham did not deny the reality of his body. He refused to let it have the last word. The work follows the same pattern. It does not ask you to deny what the old story says. It brings what God has said to that exact place and lets the two meet. *(This is a candidate anchor for the practice's "felt juxtaposition" step. It is a pattern, not a proof text. See Part II §I.)*

**"Effective" is a New Testament word for God's work.** The Greek word family behind "effective" and "at work" (*energeō*) describes real operation: something doing actual work. In the passages that matter here, the one at work is God, His Word, or faith acting through love. The word of God *"is at work in you believers"* (1 Thessalonians 2:13). In Philippians, *"it is God who works in you"* (2:13). Faith itself is *"working through love"* (Galatians 5:6). Paul prays *"that the sharing of your faith may become effective for the full knowledge of every good thing that is in us for the sake of Christ"* (Philemon 6). Covenant Identity Coaching takes that vocabulary at face value and borrows its humility. The effectiveness belongs to the Word and the Spirit. The practice exists to remove what has stood in the Word's way. That may be a belief that was never named, a grief that was never felt, or a truth that was heard many times and never received. *(The King James Version renders several of these passages with "effectual": Philemon 6, 1 Thessalonians 2:13, Ephesians 3:7, and James 5:16. Vet the vocabulary observation before public use.)*

Research does not prove any of this. It explains why the order of the work is wise. A truth spoken before the belief it addresses is felt tends to run alongside that belief instead of reaching it. §8 states how strong that account is.

### 8. What research adds, and where it stops

Research enters the design in six places. Each is stated in plain words first, with the research term in parentheses. Each carries an honest statement of strength.

**1. Deep beliefs defend themselves.** People tend to seek and trust information that confirms how they already see themselves, even when that view is painful (self-verification; Swann). Behind many failed attempts to change there is a competing commitment protecting an unexamined assumption (Kegan and Lahey). The stories people tell about their lives shape what they do next (narrative identity; McAdams). *What the design does with it:* it names the governing belief precisely before any truth is spoken to it. *Strength:* the self-verification pattern is well replicated, though researchers debate why it happens. Kegan and Lahey's model is case-validated and has few controlled trials. Research does not say identity always comes first. Behavior can also shape identity. It says that for deep, emotionally charged self-beliefs, work that bypasses them tends to revert.

**2. There are two ways of knowing, and they do not update the same way.** Explicit memory holds what you can state. Implicit memory holds what you have learned to expect, especially about safety and other people (Squire; Schacter; LeDoux; Kahneman on fast and slow thinking). *What the design does with it:* it treats "I know it in my head but not in my heart" as an accurate description, not a failure of faith. *Strength:* well established as general cognitive science. The step from there to a believer's felt sense of God is this model's own synthesis. Related research finds that a person's felt sense of God tends to track their early relationships (Kirkpatrick; Granqvist). That work is correlational.

**3. A belief can be updated while it is live.** In laboratory work, a reactivated emotional memory becomes briefly changeable and can be updated, not only overridden (memory reconsolidation; Nader, Schafe and LeDoux in animals, 2000). In humans the picture is mixed. A 2009 study found that a drug given before reactivation reduced fear responses (Kindt and colleagues). A well-known 2010 study reported that a behavioral version prevented the return of fear (Schiller and colleagues). A 2020 verification report and a registered replication did not confirm it (Chalkia and colleagues). A 2018 critical review found many consistent results and many inconsistent ones, and called reconsolidation a viable but contested account (Elsey, Van Ast and Kindt). A 2015 paper proposed that many kinds of psychotherapy work this way, through three ingredients: reactivating the old memory, a new emotional experience, and practice that reinforces it (Lane and colleagues). *What the design does with it:* it waits until the belief is felt before it speaks truth to it, and it assigns return, not rehearsal, between sessions. *Strength:* a respected hypothesis with real support and real dispute, applied here by reasoning. It has not been tested in coaching. The design's caution against confusing assent with reception is also common clinical experience, so the practice does not stand or fall with one laboratory paradigm.

**4. The relationship carries a large share of the change.** Across 295 studies of psychotherapy, the quality of the working relationship correlates with outcome at about r = .28 (Flückiger and colleagues 2018). A meta-analysis of coaching found r = .41 between working alliance and client outcomes (Graßmann and colleagues 2020). Early attachment shapes what a person expects from other people and, in correlational work, from God. *What the design does with it:* it treats the relationship as part of the method, and it treats the coach's own formation as a stated requirement. *Strength:* well established as correlation. The direction of cause is not settled.

**5. Coaching works on average, and faith-adapted help is not a handicap.** A meta-analysis of workplace coaching found positive effects on performance, well-being, coping, work attitudes, and goal-directed self-regulation (Hedges' g from 0.43 to 0.74; 18 studies; Theeboom and colleagues 2014). A meta-analysis of 97 therapy studies found that therapy adapted to a client's religion and spirituality was about as effective as standard approaches for psychological distress and more effective for spiritual well-being (Captari and colleagues 2018). *What the design does with it:* it uses the coaching container and does not apologize for integrating faith. *Strength:* neither study tested this practice. One is about workplace coaching. The other is about therapy.

**6. Planned practice between sessions helps.** Specific if-then plans improve follow-through (implementation intentions; d = 0.65 across 94 tests; Gollwitzer and Sheeran 2006). Spaced, repeated retrieval improves retention of what has been learned (Cepeda and colleagues 2006; Roediger and Karpicke 2006). *What the design does with it:* it sends you out with one small, specific act and a practice of returning to the truth. *Strength:* well established for goal behavior and for information. The design's further claim is an extension. It holds that returning to a received truth in stillness consolidates a felt belief better than repeating it aloud. That claim has not been tested.

**Where research stops.** No study has tested Covenant Identity Coaching. The problem is well evidenced. The components are supported at different strengths. The whole has not been outcome-tested. Research cannot test the Spirit's witness, and the design does not ask it to. Research does not decide the telos. None of these studies measures transformation as Scripture defines it. They describe conditions associated with durable change in beliefs, habits, and relationships.

### 9. The design, walked through

The engagement is twelve weekly sessions in four phases. The phase map below follows the Engagement Onboarding Document. *(It maps sessions 1–10. Sessions 11–12 are unmapped. See Part II §I.)* For each step the entry gives what happens, what Scripture says about it, what research adds, and what to look for. These are descriptions of the design's intent. None is a promise of what you will experience.

**Before session one: a conversation and a screening.**
- *What happens.* A discovery call, then a short pre-screen and a fuller intake. The aim is fit and safety. The practice asks for genuine faith in Jesus Christ, tested and not assumed. It asks for willingness to receive Scripture as authoritative, willingness to practice between sessions, and enough stability for formation work.
- *Scripture.* The tools presume the identity that already belongs to a believer (Romans 6:6; Colossians 3:3; 2 Corinthians 5:17). A person who is not yet in Christ is served by a different conversation. The gate is a branch, not softer language.
- *Research.* None. This is professional ethics: the ICF standard on referral and the practice's own Crisis and Referral Protocol.
- *What to look for.* A conversation, not a pitch. If what you carry needs clinical care first, you are told and helped to find it.

**Phase 1: Covenant Orientation (sessions 1–2).**
- *What happens.* You and the coach agree how you will work together. The gap is named in your own words. The picture of God you actually carry, which can differ from the one you confess, is surfaced.
- *Scripture.* *"Search me, O God, and know my heart!"* (Psalm 139:23). The purposes of the heart are "deep water" and need drawing out (Proverbs 20:5).
- *Research.* The working relationship correlates with outcome (well established). A person's felt sense of God tends to track early relationships (correlational).
- *What to look for.* Being heard accurately. Possibly saying something aloud for the first time.

**Phase 2: Wound Mapping (sessions 2–4).**
- *What happens.* The specific belief is found: its exact claim, what triggers it, what it promises, and what it protects. Its origin is traced. Its cost is felt, often through lament.
- *Scripture.* *"The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately sick; who can understand it?"* (Jeremiah 17:9). *"For godly grief produces a repentance that leads to salvation without regret, whereas worldly grief produces death"* (2 Corinthians 7:10). God's people forsook the fountain and dug cisterns that could not hold water (Jeremiah 2:13).
- *Research.* A belief that persists after being rejected is protecting something (Kegan and Lahey; case-validated). A belief must be live for truth to update it (mixed support).
- *What to look for.* A sentence about yourself that you recognize at once. In the practice's own account, a sign that the cost is being felt is that a person talks less, not more.

**Mid-engagement Snapshot (sessions 4–5).**
- *What happens.* Your own words from intake are set beside your words now. You rate, from one to ten, how true what God says about you feels in lived experience. The coach adds specific observations. If movement is slow, the document says so.
- *Scripture and research.* None claimed. The Snapshot is a record, not a rite.
- *What to look for.* Evidence you can check for yourself.

**Phase 3: Identity Installation (sessions 4–8).**
- *What happens.* What God has actually said is brought to the specific belief, at a moment the belief is felt, in a posture of receiving. Between sessions you carry one small act and return to the truth in prayer and stillness.
- *Scripture.* *"So you also must consider yourselves dead to sin and alive to God in Christ Jesus"* (Romans 6:11). *"The Spirit himself bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God"* (Romans 8:16). Spoken self-address inside the covenant: *"'The LORD is my portion,' says my soul, 'therefore I will hope in him'"* (Lamentations 3:24).
- *Research.* Memory-update research (mixed support). If-then planning (well supported). Spaced return (well supported for information). The preference for return over repetition is the design's own judgment and is untested.
- *What to look for.* Possibly a quiet recognition: "I have always known this, but I have never received it." It is not forced. The coach waits, because this step is the Spirit's.

**Phase 4: Integration and Close.**
- *What happens.* Consolidation, a rule of life, and a Growth Report built from your own words, with a letter to your beginning self.
- *Scripture.* *"If we live by the Spirit, let us also keep in step with the Spirit"* (Galatians 5:25). *"And I am sure of this, that he who began a good work in you will bring it to completion at the day of Jesus Christ"* (Philippians 1:6).
- *Research.* Redemptive narrative forms are associated with well-being (McAdams; correlational). Retrieval and spacing for information (well supported).
- *What to look for.* A document in your own words, and practices that continue without the coach.

**Throughout.** The coach holds three roles in this order: formation partner, covenant-identity diagnostician, coaching partner. Prayer is the coach's orienting practice. The coach does not produce the change and does not need to see it. When what surfaces exceeds coaching, the coach says so and refers.

### 10. What the investment buys

The value comes in four forms. None is promised. Each is what the design aims at.

**Inside you.** The direction of travel is a set of nine qualities of the inner life: settled, undivided, free, moved without being swept, unhurried, loved and knowing it, truthful without cruelty, purposeful without striving, and available. They are not goals assigned to you or a standard to be measured against. They are the fruit of what the work makes room for. The client-facing promise puts it in plain terms. You may be less ownable by other people's opinions. You may feel fully without being swept away. You may stop running from a deficit. It uses an image: the difference between reading a weather report and standing in the rain.

**Around you.** An inner life that is less defended is more available to other people. The ninth quality is the one that shows in a room. You arrive already carrying something for others, instead of checking whether the room is safe. The outward counterpart is the fruit of the Spirit:

> "But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control; against such things there is no law." (Galatians 5:22–23)

In marriage, parenting, work, conflict, and community, these are desired outcomes. They are not promised ones.

**Beyond the engagement.** The engagement is a concentrated season of a lifelong work. You leave with practices and a rule of life that continue without the coach. The aim is not to keep you as a client. It is to equip you for the ongoing walk with the Holy Spirit that Scripture assumes.

**Evidence you can check as you go.** At about session four or five you see your own words from intake beside your words now. At the close you receive a written record built from your own language. Nobody asks you to take the movement on trust. If movement is slow, the Snapshot says so.

**What leaving the gap alone costs.** The cost is paid in years. It looks like effort that does not compound, a private shame that grows with every sermon you already know, and a self that performs well and rests nowhere. This is recognition, not a warning.

**The terms.** Twelve weekly sessions of 50 to 60 minutes by video, over about twelve weeks. $1,680 in full ($140 per session) or three payments of $560. Sessions after the first twelve are $140 each. A founding rate may be offered privately to early clients in exchange for honest feedback. *[Current per the signed Coaching Agreement (2026-07-28) and the Quick Launch Plan (2026-09-15). Confirm before quoting.]*

**When another path is the better first step.** A good book, a pastor, a small group, a counselor, or a different coach can be the right choice. If what you carry needs clinical care, that comes first. If you want advice, accountability, or practical tips, this is not the tool. [[Covenant Identity — Comparative Modality Reference]] compares the options one by one.

### 11. What this does not promise, and who stands behind it

This is not a fast process. Formation takes the time it takes.

It does not promise that you will feel a particular way. Scripture makes room for lament, protest, and honest doubt inside the covenant relationship (Psalm 13; Psalm 88). The prophet cries, *"O LORD, how long shall I cry for help, and you will not hear?"* (Habakkuk 1:2). The work welcomes that. A feeling is never the test of whether you are in Christ.

It does not produce the change. Conditions are not causes. If a session ever feels like pressure toward a feeling, that is a signal that something is off, and you should say so.

It does not replace the church, Scripture, prayer, or community. It works alongside whatever of these you have. It is one concentrated season of a lifelong work.

It is not clinical care. If what is underneath needs a therapist or a physician, you will be told and helped to find one.

It carries no outcome data. The practice is new. Nothing in this document is offered as proven.

### 12. A way to decide

Five questions.

1. **Is what I am missing information, or is the truth I already hold not reaching where I live?** If you can name verses you cannot rest in, it is probably the second.
2. **What have I already tried, and what level did each reach?** If each added knowledge, effort, or structure, none reached the place this work targets.
3. **Am I willing to be honestly known, at a pace I can bear?** If exposure feels unsafe, that is information, and the work slows down. Nothing asks you to perform.
4. **Am I willing to do small, specific practices between sessions?** Much of the formation happens there.
5. **Is my growth itself something I am striving to prove?** If "becoming more" has turned into the way you try to earn standing, the work starts there. That is not a disqualifier. It is the first thing to look at.

If your answers are "it isn't reaching," "they did not," "yes," "yes," and "I'm not sure," a conversation is worth having.

**What to ask the coach.** Ask how the work is supervised. Ask what happens if you need clinical care. Ask what the coach does if nothing seems to move. Ask to see the Scripture behind any step. A coach worth hiring welcomes all four questions.

**The next step is a conversation.** A discovery call is a real conversation about whether this is the right next step. It is not a sales call. No decision is asked of you until you have been heard.

### 13. The answer, once more

The value of Covenant Identity Coaching is the truth you already hold reaching the place you actually live from. It is built from Scripture outward, with research admitted only where it serves what Scripture says. It cannot be promised. It can be judged: step by step against your Bible, and session by session against your own words. The change, if it comes, is God's.

### A. Evidence tags and how to speak each

| Tag | Meaning | How an author may speak it |
|---|---|---|
| **[S]** | Scripture says it directly. A plain textual claim. | "Scripture says..." Quote it in ESV. |
| **[S~]** | Scripture supports it by a cumulative pattern or an analogical use of a text. | "Scripture's pattern shows..." Name the move if pressed. |
| **[E]** | Research-established. Peer-reviewed, replicated, and relevant to the specific claim. | "Research consistently finds..." Name the finding, not the model. |
| **[M]** | Mixed. Peer-reviewed but contested, limited in scope, or correlational. | "Research suggests, and researchers still debate..." |
| **[T]** | Theory. A well-regarded account built largely from clinical or case observation. | "One well-regarded account holds..." |
| **[P]** | Practitioner or popular claim not yet traced to a primary source. | Recognition aid only. Never the basis for a claim. |
| **[D]** | CIC design judgment. An inference made for this model. | "The design assumes..." or "We chose..." Never "research shows." |

**The rule:** a tag limits the verb. [D] never takes "proves" or "shows." [T] never takes "research shows." Anything about CIC's own results carries no tag, because no data exists yet.

### G. Objections and answers

**1. "Isn't this just psychology with Bible verses added?"**
The concern is fair. Many integrated approaches let psychology set the agenda and add verses afterward. The answer is structural. Every design step is stated with its Scripture first (§C). Research enters only through two tests. Contributions that bring a competing account of human nature are set aside even when they are useful. Where the research is mixed or untested, this document says so. The standard is that a step with no biblical case should go, and where the case is still thin the Index says so.

**2. "Doesn't Scripture alone suffice?"**
Scripture is sufficient for what it claims: *"His divine power has granted to us all things that pertain to life and godliness"* (2 Peter 1:3). That is a claim about authority for what identity is, what has gone wrong, and what renewal looks like. It is not a claim that Scripture gives developmental or neurological descriptions. The design uses research for that level only, and it evaluates the research against Scripture. The remaining exposure is real. The boundary between describing a mechanism and prescribing a goal is not always clean, and a coach needs enough theological and psychological formation to see where a contributor has crossed it.

**3. "Isn't a focus on my inner life self-absorbed?"**
Scripture sends us to the heart (Proverbs 4:23). The prayer it models is addressed to God: *"Search me, O God, and know my heart!"* (Psalm 139:23). The aim is not a better self. Nine of the ten identities are already yours, and the telos ends outward, in being sent and being available to others. If growth itself has become something you strive to prove, the work begins there.

**4. "Isn't 'identity in Christ' just self-affirmation, or Word of Faith?"**
No, and the design is built to prevent it. The identities are positional: already true, not produced by speech or effort. The practice reckons with them (Romans 6:11). It does not manifest them. Truth is never spoken as technique apart from diagnosis and relationship. The step that matters most is the Spirit's witness, which no one can produce. Any piece that implies speaking produces change violates the design.

**5. "I have the Spirit and the Word. Why pay a coach?"**
You do have both. The practice is one means among others and never a replacement for either, or for the church. Scripture shows people helping one another see (Proverbs 20:5; Galatians 6:1; Colossians 1:28–29). What the practice adds is precision, structure, and a relationship built for this one task. If you already have that in a mentor, a pastor, or a group, you may not need this.

**6. "Does it work? Where is the data?"**
The honest answer has three parts. The problem is documented. The components are supported at the strengths in §B. The whole has not been outcome-tested. You can judge it in two ways: step by step against your Bible, and session by session against your own words. The practice will not tell you it is proven, because it is not.

**7. "Is this therapy?"**
No. It does not diagnose or treat clinical conditions or process trauma directly. It requires a non-clinical baseline at entry. It has a written referral protocol, and it refers when what surfaces exceeds coaching. *(The readiness gaps named in the 2026-07-27 review remain open until closed. Do not answer this question in public without checking §I.)*

**8. "Are you excusing sin by talking about wounds?"**
No. The work holds both: what was done to a person and what the person has cultivated. A tool built for the second, the witnessing-repentance sequence, exists for that reason. It follows the distinction Paul draws between godly grief, which turns toward God, and worldly grief, which does not (2 Corinthians 7:10).

**9. "What is your tradition? Reformed, Wesleyan, charismatic?"**
The covenant frame is biblical theology's covenant narrative, broadly evangelical, and explicitly not Reformed covenant theology. On how the Spirit uses means, the practice stands in the Wesleyan means-of-grace stream and says so. On whether a believer can lose salvation, it holds only that standing in Christ cannot be lost through failure, sin, doubt, or fluctuating feeling. It refers the question of final renunciation to the client's pastor. It does not raise the Israel and Church question unprompted. A reader from a stricter monergist tradition will weigh the "conditions" language more cautiously. The Conditions document addresses that fault line directly.

**10. "Will you tell me what to do?"**
No. The coach is not a directive counselor. The coach is also not neutral about who God says you are. The client is the authority on their experience. God is the authority on their identity. The coach draws out and does not prescribe.

**11. "Do I have to feel something for this to be working?"**
No. Lament and doubt are welcome. A feeling is never the test of whether you are in Christ. Felt reception is a possible fruit of the work, not a requirement for it. The mid-point Snapshot handles a lower score honestly. It can mean regression, or it can mean you now see yourself more accurately.

**12. "What if nothing changes?"**
Then it may be slower than hoped, and the Snapshot will say so. The arc can be extended. If the need is beyond coaching, you will be referred. Nothing is promised. The aim is never dependency on the coach.

**13. "Couldn't I do this alone, with the workbooks?"**
Some people can, for a time. The reason another person helps is the one in Jeremiah 17:9–10 and Proverbs 20:5: the heart is hard to read from the inside. Some of the tools are also designed for use with a coach.

**14. "Isn't 'Scripture first' just a slogan?"**
It would be if the record were hidden. The Biblical Foundations Index shows, for every design decision, whether the biblical case is Developed, Assumed, Contested, or a Gap. Where the case is weaker, the Index says so. That record is the answer.

### H. What this document does not claim

- It does not claim that neuroscience proves Scripture, or that Scripture predicted neuroscience.
- It does not claim that CIC's mechanism or outcomes are validated.
- It does not claim that every tool was built from Scripture first. It claims that Scripture governs every design decision about identity, diagnosis, and renewal, and that the governing architecture was built outward from it.
- It does not claim that the ten identities exhaust Scripture's language about who believers are.
- It does not claim that any cited study measures Christian transformation as Scripture defines it.
- It does not claim that the new candidate anchors are vetted.
- It does not claim that terms, pricing, or status lines are current beyond their dates.
