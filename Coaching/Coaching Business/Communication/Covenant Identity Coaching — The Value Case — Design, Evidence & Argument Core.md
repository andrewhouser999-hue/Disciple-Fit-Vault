# Covenant Identity Coaching — The Value Case
## Design, Evidence & Argument Core

*All Scripture is ESV. A reference with no text beside it is a pointer to the full-text treatment in [[Covenant Identity — Biblical Foundations Index]].*

**What this is:** The shared source behind every piece that answers one question: **What is the value of Covenant Identity Coaching?** It is not a page or a pitch for a prospect to read cold. It is a fully sourced reference. Smaller pieces draw from it and adapt it to their own venue and register. Part I reads straight through as one finished argument. Part II holds the apparatus: sources, evidence strength, limits on claims, objections, language, and adaptation rules.

**Who uses it:** Andrew, and any author (human or AI) writing for him. Pair it with [[CIC Value Case — Authorship Prompt (Standing)]].

**When it is used:** Whenever the value of the practice must be stated, defended, or adapted. A discovery call. A "why it is built this way" page. A pastor conversation. A talk. A podcast. An article. An email.

**How to use it:**
1. Draw the argument from Part I. Draw sources and limits from Part II.
2. Keep three layers separate in every derivative: what Scripture says, how the design applies it, and what research adds. A piece may drop the labels. It may not let a design judgment speak with the authority of a text, or a theory speak with the authority of a finding.
3. Check placement first. This is Stage 3–5 material in the Buyer's Journey. The root has already been revealed. Do not open a page, post, or conversation with it. The felt cost leads and this follows. [[Disciple Fit — Marketing Charter]] governs.
4. Check the claims ladder (Part II §D) before anything is published or spoken.

**The core claim, stated precisely:**

Covenant Identity Coaching is built on a Scripture-governed account of why a sincere believer can know the truth about who they are in Christ and still not live from it. Its sequence of work is designed to meet the conditions that account says must be met. Scripture decides what is true about identity, what has gone wrong, and what renewal is. Research is admitted only to describe how the inner life holds and changes beliefs, and only where it passes two tests. The practice does not produce the change. It arranges the conditions in which the Word, by the Spirit, does what it does. The problem the design targets is well evidenced. Each mechanism is grounded in Scripture and, at the component level, in research of varying strength. The practice as a whole has not yet been outcome-tested.

**Who this is written to** *(decided 2026-10-20: Wilderness Christian first, Sunday-Monday Christian second, following the Marketing Charter's Segment Registry)*: A born-again follower of Jesus who is already committed to growing in Christ. The first reader is the [[Wilderness Christian]], the Charter's Target Market. This is a sincere, spiritually serious believer, usually 45 to 65, who is still pursuing God, often privately, and often at a guarded distance from institutional church. They have probably studied hard, because they were doing it alone. They know what they believe, and they cannot move something in themselves by effort. They distrust authority for reasons they earned, so they will test every claim against Scripture and against their own judgment. They are deciding whether to invest time and money, and they may not know exactly what is missing. # PART I — THE VALUE CASE

### 1. The short answer

The value of Covenant Identity Coaching is not more truth. You already hold most of the truth you need. The value is that the truth you already believe reaches the place you actually live from.

That place decides what you reach for under pressure. It decides what you fear losing and what you protect when no one is watching. It can be out of step with your theology for years while you remain entirely sincere.

The practice rests on two sources, in a fixed order. Scripture comes first. It decides what is true about who you are, what has gone wrong, and what renewal looks like. Research comes second. It is allowed to describe how the inner life holds and changes deep beliefs. It is never allowed to decide where that change is going.

What you receive is a structured engagement of twelve weekly sessions. The work is built to do four things. It finds the specific belief that is governing you. It lets you feel what that belief has cost. It brings what God has actually said to that exact place. It gives the Holy Spirit room to keep working between sessions. The change itself is His. The practice exists to take away what has stood in its way.

One fact belongs near the start. The problem this addresses is well documented. The design is grounded piece by piece. The practice as a whole is new and has not been outcome-tested. Nothing here is offered as proven. It is offered as carefully built, with the reasons visible so you can judge them.

### 2. What is missing

You never stopped believing.

You kept showing up. You studied, prayed, served, and kept going, often with little company. You may have studied harder than most, because you were doing it on your own. By any honest measure you are a serious believer who wants to grow.

And there is a place in you that does not move.

It shows in the reaction that comes out the same way under pressure. It shows in a flatness you cannot explain. It shows in a truth you can teach and cannot quite rest in. You can say who God says you are. You cannot always feel it when it matters. Some part of you suspects that if this is still true after everything you have tried, the problem must be you.

That suspicion is wrong about the cause. It is not evidence that your faith is thin. It is evidence that the truth you hold and the place you live from have not yet met.

> *You know what you believe. You just can't seem to live from it.*
> *You've prayed this away more times than you can count. It keeps coming back.*
> *You're not walking away from God. You're just flat.*

*(Stage 2 recognition lines, already cleared in [[Covenant Identity Coaching — Quick Launch Plan]]. Hypothesis-level until tested against real prospect language.)*

Scripture names this gap. James describes a person who hears the Word and does not live from it:

> "For if anyone is a hearer of the word and not a doer, he is like a man who looks intently at his natural face in a mirror. For he looks at himself and goes away and at once forgets what he was like." (James 1:23–24)

Jesus quotes Isaiah about a people who honor God with their lips while their heart is far away (Matthew 15:8): *"This people honors me with their lips, but their heart is far from me"*. Paul writes the divided experience in the first person: *"For I do not understand my own actions. For I do not do what I want, but I do the very thing I hate"* (Romans 7:15).

Researchers have measured the same gap from outside the church. The REVEAL study of thirty congregations, beginning with Willow Creek, found that increased involvement in church activities did not predict long-term growth in love for God and others. George Barna's worldview research, now published through Arizona Christian University's Cultural Research Center, finds that a single-digit share of American adults hold what it calls a biblical worldview. It defines that as belief and consistent practice together (6% in 2021 and 4% in 2023). These are population snapshots. They show that the gap exists. They do not show what closes it.

Nothing is missing from who you are in Christ. That is complete. Scripture states it in the past tense:

> "Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come." (2 Corinthians 5:17)

What may be missing is the felt, lived alignment of your inner life with a status that is already yours. Scripture calls that renewal: *"be transformed by the renewal of your mind"* (Romans 12:2). This practice calls it formation.

### 3. Why the usual help does not reach it

Almost everything you have tried did what it was built to do.

- Teaching delivers truth to many people at once.
- A book or a conference delivers information and a shared experience.
- A small group gives community and structure.
- Study and private discipline deepen what you know and practice.
- Accountability supplies consistency.
- Clinical counseling treats what needs treatment and builds the skills to manage it.
- Coaching clarifies goals and strategy.
- Pastoral care shepherds a whole life over time.

Each is good at its own job. None is built to find one person's specific governing belief and carry the truth to the exact place where it operates. That is a difference of scope. It is not a failure of competence. Most of them also work at the level of information, effort, or structure. For a person who already has plenty of each, more of the same does not touch what is wrong.

If you have studied hard on your own, a fact is not what is missing. A person is: someone who knows both the Word and the inner life, and who does not ask you to perform in exchange for the relationship.

Two further reasons make this hard to do alone. The first is that the heart is not easily read by its owner:

> "The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately sick; who can understand it?" (Jeremiah 17:9)

The second is that Scripture expects another person to help:

> "The purpose in a man's heart is like deep water, but a man of understanding will draw it out." (Proverbs 20:5)

Needing help here is not weakness. It is one of the ways God has ordinarily worked.

### 4. How it was designed: Scripture first, evidence second

**The order of construction.** Covenant Identity Coaching was not built by taking a coaching method and adding Bible verses to it. Its governing architecture was built from Scripture outward. It began with what Scripture says about where transformation happens. Only then did it ask what coaching method and what research could serve that claim. [[Covenant Identity Coaching — Peer Practitioner Briefing]] §1 records this order.

**The rule that governs research.** Scripture governs. It determines what identity is, what has gone wrong, and what renewal looks like. Research may describe how the inner life works at the level Scripture assumes but does not explain. It does not set the direction. Every research contribution had to pass one of two tests before it entered the design.

1. **Confirmatory.** Research independently observes what Scripture already states. It adds vocabulary and no new content.
2. **Additive.** Research describes a mechanism Scripture assumes but does not explain, such as how a belief is stored or why a truth fails to land. It enters only if it does not bring a competing account of what people are or what they are for.

A contribution that passes neither test is set aside, however useful it is clinically. The design names what it set aside. It does not adopt neural integration as the definition of flourishing. It does not treat shame as the root of identity. It does not accept a self that is whole by nature. It re-grounds that idea in the new self in Christ. It does not take over a directive counseling posture. The position behind this is what Eric Johnson calls Christian psychology: psychology developed from within Christian commitments, not secular psychology blended with Christian content. [[Covenant Identity — Integration Rationale]] holds the full record.

**Why this is not a compromise.** Scripture does not hand down a procedure for judging psychology. It does show a pattern, and the design follows the pattern. Paul cites a Cretan's own observation about Cretans and says of it, *"This testimony is true"* (Titus 1:13). An accurate observation does not have to come from a believer to be true. Paul also warns about capture:

> "See to it that no one takes you captive by philosophy and empty deceit, according to human tradition, according to the elemental spirits of the world, and not according to Christ." (Colossians 2:8)

That warning was written about a specific teaching at Colossae, not about research in general. It shows what the danger looks like: a system that takes the place of Christ. Paul's command to *"test everything; hold fast what is good"* (1 Thessalonians 5:21) comes in a passage about prophecies, so applying it to research is an analogy. Proverbs gives the searching its own warrant: *"It is the glory of God to conceal things, but the glory of kings is to search things out"* (Proverbs 25:2).

One ground for looking closely at people is that people are made in God's image: *"So God created man in his own image, in the image of God he created him; male and female he created them"* (Genesis 1:27). Careful observation of persons is observation of what God made. It is still observation, and Scripture, not the observation, decides what it means.

The two tests are built to do four things at once. They test. They hold fast what is good. They refuse capture. They keep searching. That is a pattern from Scripture, not a command in it.

**What "never contradicting" means in practice.** When research and Scripture appear to disagree, Scripture governs. The research claim is dropped or held loosely. When Scripture is silent, as it is about how memory is stored, research may describe and may not prescribe. This is a procedure. It is not a hope that the two will always agree.

**An honest note on order.** Some tools began in lived formation work and were grounded in Scripture and research afterward. The Biblical Foundations Index records this in its basis line, which cites methodology-grounding research for the Lie Eliminator and Identity Installer done on 2026-06-11. For every design decision the Index also records whether the biblical case is Developed, Assumed, Contested, or still a Gap. That record is part of the design's integrity. When you say "Scripture first," say what is true. Scripture governs every design decision about who a person is, what has gone wrong, and what renewal is. Professional ethics, not Scripture, sets the rules for scope and referral, and the design says so. The architecture was built outward from Scripture.

### 5. What Scripture establishes: five claims the design stands on

**Claim 1. Identity is given before behavior is required.**

Every covenant God makes states who He is and what He has already done. The command comes after. At Sinai, before any law, He says:

> "You yourselves have seen what I did to the Egyptians, and how I bore you on eagles' wings and brought you to myself." (Exodus 19:4)

The Ten Commandments open the same way: *"I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery. You shall have no other gods before me"* (Exodus 20:2–3). Paul keeps the order:

> "For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand, that we should walk in them." (Ephesians 2:10)

Grammarians call this indicative before imperative. For this practice it means the work begins with who God has declared you to be, not with what you should do. Be ready for one counter-text. Exodus 19:5 begins, *"Now therefore, if you will indeed obey my voice and keep my covenant"*. The covenant research document ([[Developing a Biblical Understanding of Covenantal Relationship with God]]) answers it in Part 1 §3. Keep that answer at hand.

**Claim 2. The inner life is where God does His defining work.**

Jesus named His mission in terms of the inner life first. He was sent *"to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives"* (Isaiah 61:1, the text He reads in Luke 4:18–21). The prophets promised that the New Covenant would work at the same depth:

> "And I will give you a new heart, and a new spirit I will put within you. And I will remove the heart of stone from your flesh and give you a heart of flesh." (Ezekiel 36:26)

And: *"I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts"* (Jeremiah 31:33). Paul names the site of ongoing renewal:

> "So we do not lose heart. Though our outer self is wasting away, our inner self is being renewed day by day." (2 Corinthians 4:16)

**Claim 3. Hearing is not the same as living from.**

Scripture treats the gap between knowing and living as a category of its own. James calls it self-deception (1:22–25). Jesus tells of a sower whose seed lands in different soils. Of the good soil He says:

> "As for what was sown on good soil, this is the one who hears the word and understands it. He indeed bears fruit and yields, in one case a hundredfold, in another sixty, and in another thirty." (Matthew 13:23)

Hearing is where it starts. Understanding and fruit are where it is meant to arrive. Paul commends the Thessalonians because the word they heard did its work in them:

> "And we also thank God constantly for this, that when you received the word of God, which you heard from us, you accepted it not as the word of men but as what it really is, the word of God, which is at work in you believers." (1 Thessalonians 2:13)

Paul's contrast in that verse is between a human message and God's. His point is that the word of God is at work in those who believe. What Scripture supplies is the phenomenon: a word can be heard and still bear no fruit, and a word received as God's does work. It does not supply the mechanism for the difference. The mechanism is where research enters (§8). *(The practice's own distinction between registration and reception is a design judgment. It is not drawn from the two Greek verbs behind "received" and "accepted" in this verse.)*

**Claim 4. Change is the Spirit's work, received in a posture Scripture names.**

> "Work out your own salvation with fear and trembling, for it is God who works in you, both to will and to work for his good pleasure." (Philippians 2:12–13)

The "for" makes God's working the ground of ours, not a supplement to it. The mechanism text is 2 Corinthians 3:18:

> "And we all, with unveiled face, beholding the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another. For this comes from the Lord who is the Spirit."

The human acts are turning and beholding. The transformation is received. Paul asks Christians a revealing question: *"Did you receive the Spirit by works of the law or by hearing with faith?"* (Galatians 3:2). He asks it again about the Spirit's continuing supply (3:5). The practice therefore treats its tools as conditions, not causes. They are the riverbed, not the water. It stands in the Wesleyan means-of-grace stream. God has bound Himself to work through appointed means such as Scripture, prayer, and fellowship. The practice is built to serve those means. It is not offered as a new one. Readers from other traditions will weigh this differently (§G, objection 9).

**Claim 5. God uses people as one of His means.**

Paul does not describe his own work as producing change. He describes cooperation:

> "Him we proclaim, warning everyone and teaching everyone with all wisdom, that we may present everyone mature in Christ. For this I toil, struggling with all his energy that he powerfully works within me." (Colossians 1:28–29)

He describes the manner as well: *"we were gentle among you, like a nursing mother taking care of her own children... we were ready to share with you not only the gospel of God but also our own selves"* (1 Thessalonians 2:7–8). Scripture commends the ordinary work of people helping people see: *"Iron sharpens iron, and one man sharpens another"* (Proverbs 27:17), and *"if anyone is caught in any transgression, you who are spiritual should restore him in a spirit of gentleness"* (Galatians 6:1). This is why a coach is neither a replacement for the Spirit nor a replacement for the church. A coach is one of the means.

### 6. The identities you are rooting your heart in

The Bible states some identities more often and more plainly than others. This practice builds on ten of them. None is new. You can find each in your own Bible, and you can check how the practice uses it there. The anchors follow [[Covenant Identity — Biblical Foundations Index]] §3.

1. **Made in God's image.** Genesis 1:26–27; Psalm 139:13–14; Colossians 3:10.
2. **Known fully and chosen.** Psalm 139:1–4; Ephesians 1:4; John 15:16; Jeremiah 31:3.
3. **A new creation.** 2 Corinthians 5:17; Romans 6:6; Philippians 1:6.
4. **An adopted child.** Romans 8:15; Galatians 4:4–7; 1 John 3:1.
5. **A fellow heir with Christ.** Romans 8:17.
6. **Indwelt by the Spirit.** John 14:16–18; Romans 8:9; 1 Corinthians 6:19.
7. **Seated with Christ, and held.** Ephesians 2:6; John 10:28–29; Romans 8:38–39.
8. **Loved as the Father loves the Son.** John 17:23; John 15:9; Romans 5:5.
9. **Sent.** 2 Corinthians 5:20; Ephesians 2:10; Acts 1:8.
10. **Being conformed to Christ's image.** Romans 8:29; 2 Corinthians 3:18; Philippians 1:6.

Take two of them in full. The adoption passage carries the Spirit's witness:

> "For you did not receive the spirit of slavery to fall back into fear, but you have received the Spirit of adoption as sons, by whom we cry, 'Abba! Father!' The Spirit himself bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God, and if children, then heirs—heirs of God and fellow heirs with Christ, provided we suffer with him in order that we may also be glorified with him." (Romans 8:15–17)

And the tenth identity names the direction of the whole:

> "For those whom he foreknew he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son, in order that he might be the firstborn among many brothers." (Romans 8:29)

Nine of the ten describe what is already true of you, and most are true because you are in Christ. The tenth is the process by which the other nine become lived. This distinction keeps the work from becoming self-persuasion. The practice does not ask you to declare a self into existence. It asks you to reckon with what is already true:

> "So you also must consider yourselves dead to sin and alive to God in Christ Jesus." (Romans 6:11)

### 7. Why the order matters for your faith

Underneath the value question there may be another one: can I trust this? For a believer the answer depends on where the trust is placed.

**The faith this builds is faith in God and His Word, not in the method or the coach.** The practice names the opposite as a failure mode: treating the tools as the cause of change. The coach creates conditions. God does the changing.

**Every step can be checked against your own Bible.** The Bereans *"received the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures daily to see if these things were so"* (Acts 17:11). The practice invites that. Part II §C lists the Scripture behind each step and marks where the biblical case is still thin. The standard is that a step with no biblical case should go.

**How faith is built: the Word grounded in its whole witness, and the Spirit's quickening.**

Scripture speaks of faith being built up, and it names the Word as one of the means. Paul commends the Ephesian elders *"to God and to the word of his grace, which is able to build you up"* (Acts 20:32). He tells the Colossians to walk in Christ, *"rooted and built up in him and established in the faith, just as you were taught"* (Colossians 2:6–7). Jude tells believers to be *"building yourselves up in your most holy faith and praying in the Holy Spirit"* (Jude 20).

**A truth understood in its own setting and in the whole of Scripture.** The Bible does not treat understanding as optional. *"The unfolding of your words gives light; it imparts understanding to the simple"* (Psalm 119:130). When the Law was read to the people in Jerusalem, *"they gave the sense, so that the people understood the reading"* (Nehemiah 8:8). On the Emmaus road the risen Jesus did this with all the Scriptures: *"beginning with Moses and all the Prophets, he interpreted to them in all the Scriptures the things concerning himself"* (Luke 24:27). That is the frame the practice uses. It asks what a text means in its own setting and in the whole of Scripture, read in the light of Christ, and it teaches the truth from there. Evangelical interpretation widely shares this way of reading, and Scripture's own practice points toward it. Scripture does not state it as a rule in so many words.

**Hearing is where faith starts. Understanding and doing are where it is meant to go.** Jesus closes the Sermon on the Mount with two builders. The one who *"hears these words of mine and does them"* is *"like a wise man who built his house on the rock"* (Matthew 7:24). The parable contrasts doing with not doing. Elsewhere He describes the good soil as the one who *"hears the word and understands it"* and bears fruit (Matthew 13:23). Paul prays that believers would be *"filled with the knowledge of his will in all spiritual wisdom and understanding, so as to walk in a manner worthy of the Lord"* (Colossians 1:9–10). Understanding is meant to carry into a life.

**The Spirit's quickening.** The Spirit has a part in how the Word is received. Paul says believers have received *"the Spirit who is from God, that we might understand the things freely given us by God"* (1 Corinthians 2:12). He prays that believers would have *"the Spirit of wisdom and of revelation in the knowledge of him, having the eyes of your hearts enlightened"* (Ephesians 1:17–18). The psalmist prays, *"give me life according to your word"* (Psalm 119:25). After the Emmaus road the two said, *"Did not our hearts burn within us while he talked to us on the road, while he opened to us the Scriptures?"* (Luke 24:32). Later that day Jesus *"opened their minds to understand the Scriptures"* (24:45). Luke uses one verb, *dianoigō*, for both of those and for the opening of their eyes (24:31). This is illumination: the Spirit enables a person to understand and welcome what Scripture already says. It adds no new revelation. Scripture also gives the test for anything claimed to be from the Spirit. *"Test everything; hold fast what is good"* (1 Thessalonians 5:21) is written in a passage about prophecies, and it speaks directly to testing what is offered as the Spirit's word. A felt impression is weighed against what Scripture has already said.

**Together or apart.** Faith can be built by understanding a truth in the whole witness of Scripture. It can be built by the Spirit's quickening of a Scripture to a person's spirit, felt in the soul. It can be built by either one alone or by both together. The practice serves both. It produces neither. A coach can help a person see what a text means in its own setting and in the whole of Scripture. A coach cannot quicken it and does not try. *(Andrew's own account, from which this is drawn, is in §11.)*

**Where Romans 10:17 fits.**

> "So faith comes from hearing, and hearing through the word of Christ." (Romans 10:17)

In its own context that sentence is about the gospel proclaimed and the faith that saves (Romans 10:8–17). The practice therefore does not rest a believer's ongoing growth on that verse. It rests it on texts that speak to believers directly. The word of God *"is at work in you believers"* (1 Thessalonians 2:13). It *"is able to build you up"* (Acts 20:32). God's promises are the means by which *"you may become partakers of the divine nature"* (2 Peter 1:4).

**Faith holds the hard fact and the promise together.**

> "He did not weaken in faith when he considered his own body, which was as good as dead (since he was about a hundred years old), or when he considered the barrenness of Sarah's womb. No unbelief made him waver concerning the promise of God, but he grew strong in his faith as he gave glory to God, fully convinced that God was able to do what he had promised." (Romans 4:19–21)

Abraham looked at the facts. He did not deny the reality of his body. He refused to let it have the last word. Genesis shows that the path was not tidy. When God told him that Sarah would bear a son, *"Abraham fell on his face and laughed and said to himself, 'Shall a child be born to a man who is a hundred years old? Shall Sarah, who is ninety years old, bear a child?'"* (Genesis 17:17). Faith in this account is not closing one's eyes to what is real. The work follows the same pattern. It does not ask you to deny what the old story says. It brings what God has said to that exact place and lets the two meet. *(This is a pattern, not a proof text. Many manuscripts, and the King James Version, read "he considered not his own body now dead." The ESV follows other early manuscripts that lack the "not," and this document follows the ESV. That the meeting of the two is how change happens is the design's judgment, not the text's.)*

**"Effective" is a New Testament word for God's work.** The Greek word family behind "effective" and "at work" (*energeō*) describes real operation: something doing actual work. In the passages that matter here, the one at work is God, His Word, or faith acting through love. The word of God *"is at work in you believers"* (1 Thessalonians 2:13). In Philippians, *"it is God who works in you"* (2:13). Paul says of his own toil that he struggles *"with all his energy that he powerfully works within me"* (Colossians 1:29). The word of God is *"living and active"* (Hebrews 4:12). Faith itself is *"working through love"* (Galatians 5:6). Covenant Identity Coaching takes that vocabulary at face value and borrows its humility. The effectiveness belongs to the Word and the Spirit. The practice exists to remove what has stood in the Word's way. That may be a belief that was never named, a grief that was never felt, or a truth that was heard many times and never received. *(The same Greek family also describes sin, death, and evil powers at work (Romans 7:5; 2 Corinthians 4:12; Ephesians 2:2; 2 Thessalonians 2:7). The claim here is about the passages cited, not about the word itself. The King James Version renders 1 Thessalonians 2:13, Ephesians 3:7, and James 5:16 with "effectual." It does so at Philemon 6 as well. This document leaves Philemon 6 out because its meaning is disputed.)*

Research does not prove any of this. It explains why the order of the work is wise. A truth spoken before the belief it addresses is felt tends to run alongside that belief instead of reaching it. §8 states how strong that account is.

### 8. What research adds, and where it stops

Research enters the design in six places. Each is stated in plain words first, with the research term in parentheses. Each carries an honest statement of strength.

**1. Deep beliefs defend themselves.** People tend to seek and trust information that confirms how they already see themselves, even when that view is painful (self-verification; Swann). Behind many failed attempts to change there is a competing commitment protecting an unexamined assumption (Kegan and Lahey). The stories people tell about their lives shape what they do next (narrative identity; McAdams). *What the design does with it:* it names the governing belief precisely before any truth is spoken to it. *Strength:* the self-verification pattern is well replicated, though researchers debate why it happens. Kegan and Lahey's model is case-validated and has few controlled trials. Research does not say identity always comes first. Behavior can also shape identity. It says that for deep, emotionally charged self-beliefs, work that bypasses them tends to revert.

**2. There are two ways of knowing, and they do not update the same way.** Explicit memory holds what you can state. Implicit memory holds what you have learned to expect, especially about safety and other people (Squire; Schacter; LeDoux; Kahneman on fast and slow thinking). *What the design does with it:* it treats "I know it in my head but not in my heart" as an accurate description, not a failure of faith. *Strength:* well established as general cognitive science. The step from there to a believer's felt sense of God is this model's own synthesis. Related research finds that a person's felt sense of God tends to track their early relationships (Kirkpatrick; Granqvist). That work is correlational.

**3. A belief can be updated while it is live.** In laboratory work, a reactivated emotional memory becomes briefly changeable and can be updated, not only overridden (memory reconsolidation; Nader, Schafe and LeDoux in animals, 2000). In humans the picture is mixed. A 2009 study found that a drug given before reactivation reduced fear responses (Kindt and colleagues). A well-known 2010 study reported that a behavioral version prevented the return of fear (Schiller and colleagues). A 2020 verification report and a registered replication did not confirm it (Chalkia and colleagues). A 2018 critical review found many consistent results and many inconsistent ones, and called reconsolidation a viable but contested account (Elsey, Van Ast and Kindt). A 2015 paper proposed that many kinds of psychotherapy work this way, through three ingredients: reactivating the old memory, a new emotional experience, and practice that reinforces it (Lane and colleagues). *What the design does with it:* it waits until the belief is felt before it speaks truth to it, and it assigns return, not rehearsal, between sessions. *Strength:* a respected hypothesis with real support and real dispute, applied here by reasoning. It has not been tested in coaching. The design's caution against confusing assent with reception is also common clinical experience, so the practice does not stand or fall with one laboratory paradigm.

**4. The relationship carries a large share of the change.** Across 295 studies of psychotherapy, the quality of the working relationship correlates with outcome at about r = .28 (Flückiger and colleagues 2018). A meta-analysis of coaching found r = .41 between working alliance and client outcomes (Graßmann and colleagues 2020). Early attachment shapes what a person expects from other people and, in correlational work, from God. *What the design does with it:* it treats the relationship as part of the method, and it treats the coach's own formation as a stated requirement. *Strength:* well established as correlation. The direction of cause is not settled.

**5. Coaching works on average, and faith-adapted help is not a handicap.** A meta-analysis of workplace coaching found positive effects on performance, well-being, coping, work attitudes, and goal-directed self-regulation (Hedges' g from 0.43 to 0.74; 18 studies; Theeboom and colleagues 2014). A meta-analysis of 97 therapy studies found that therapy adapted to a client's religion and spirituality was about as effective as standard approaches for psychological distress and more effective for spiritual well-being (Captari and colleagues 2018). *What the design does with it:* it uses the coaching container and does not apologize for integrating faith. *Strength:* neither study tested this practice. One is about workplace coaching. The other is about therapy.

**6. Planned practice between sessions helps.** Specific if-then plans improve follow-through (implementation intentions; d = 0.65 across 94 tests; Gollwitzer and Sheeran 2006). Spaced, repeated retrieval improves retention of what has been learned (Cepeda and colleagues 2006; Roediger and Karpicke 2006). *What the design does with it:* it sends you out with one small, specific act and a practice of returning to the truth. *Strength:* well established for goal behavior and for information. The design's further claim is an extension. It holds that returning to a received truth in stillness consolidates a felt belief better than repeating it aloud. That claim has not been tested.

**Where research stops.** No study has tested Covenant Identity Coaching. The problem is well evidenced. The components are supported at different strengths. The whole has not been outcome-tested. Research cannot test the Spirit's witness, and the design does not ask it to. Research does not decide the telos. None of these studies measures transformation as Scripture defines it. They describe conditions associated with durable change in beliefs, habits, and relationships.

### 9. The design, walked through

The engagement is twelve weekly sessions in four phases. The phase map below follows the Engagement Onboarding Document. *(It maps sessions 1–10. Sessions 11–12 are unmapped. See Part II §I.)* For each step the entry gives what happens, what Scripture says about it, what research adds, and what to look for. These are descriptions of the design's intent. None is a promise of what you will experience.

**Before session one: a conversation and a screening.**
- *What happens.* A discovery call, then a short pre-screen and a fuller intake. The aim is fit and safety. The practice asks for genuine faith in Jesus Christ, tested and not assumed. It asks for willingness to receive Scripture as authoritative, willingness to practice between sessions, and enough stability for formation work.
- *Scripture.* The tools presume the identity that already belongs to a believer (Romans 6:6; Colossians 3:3; 2 Corinthians 5:17). A person who is not yet in Christ is served by a different conversation. The gate is a branch, not softer language.
- *Research.* None. This is professional ethics: the ICF standard on referral and the practice's own Crisis and Referral Protocol.
- *What to look for.* A conversation, not a pitch. If what you carry needs clinical care first, you are told and helped to find it.

**Phase 1: Covenant Orientation (sessions 1–2).**
- *What happens.* You and the coach agree how you will work together. The gap is named in your own words. The picture of God you actually carry, which can differ from the one you confess, is surfaced.
- *Scripture.* *"Search me, O God, and know my heart!"* (Psalm 139:23). The purposes of the heart are "deep water" and need drawing out (Proverbs 20:5).
- *Research.* The working relationship correlates with outcome (well established). A person's felt sense of God tends to track early relationships (correlational).
- *What to look for.* Being heard accurately. Possibly saying something aloud for the first time.

**Phase 2: Wound Mapping (sessions 2–4).**
- *What happens.* The specific belief is found: its exact claim, what triggers it, what it promises, and what it protects. Its origin is traced. Its cost is felt, often through lament.
- *Scripture.* *"The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately sick; who can understand it?"* (Jeremiah 17:9). *"For godly grief produces a repentance that leads to salvation without regret, whereas worldly grief produces death"* (2 Corinthians 7:10). God's people forsook the fountain and dug cisterns that could not hold water (Jeremiah 2:13).
- *Research.* A belief that persists after being rejected is protecting something (Kegan and Lahey; case-validated). A belief must be live for truth to update it (mixed support).
- *What to look for.* A sentence about yourself that you recognize at once. In the practice's own account, a sign that the cost is being felt is that a person talks less, not more.

**Mid-engagement Snapshot (sessions 4–5).**
- *What happens.* Your own words from intake are set beside your words now. You rate, from one to ten, how true what God says about you feels in lived experience. The coach adds specific observations. If movement is slow, the document says so.
- *Scripture and research.* None claimed. The Snapshot is a record, not a rite.
- *What to look for.* Evidence you can check for yourself.

**Phase 3: Identity Installation (sessions 4–8).**
- *What happens.* What God has actually said is brought to the specific belief, at a moment the belief is felt, in a posture of receiving. Between sessions you carry one small act and return to the truth in prayer and stillness.
- *Scripture.* *"So you also must consider yourselves dead to sin and alive to God in Christ Jesus"* (Romans 6:11). *"The Spirit himself bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God"* (Romans 8:16). Spoken self-address inside the covenant: *"'The LORD is my portion,' says my soul, 'therefore I will hope in him'"* (Lamentations 3:24).
- *Research.* Memory-update research (mixed support). If-then planning (well supported). Spaced return (well supported for information). The preference for return over repetition is the design's own judgment and is untested.
- *What to look for.* Possibly a quiet recognition: "I have always known this, but I have never received it." It is not forced. The coach waits, because this step is the Spirit's.

**Phase 4: Integration and Close.**
- *What happens.* Consolidation, a rule of life, and a Growth Report built from your own words, with a letter to your beginning self.
- *Scripture.* *"If we live by the Spirit, let us also keep in step with the Spirit"* (Galatians 5:25). *"And I am sure of this, that he who began a good work in you will bring it to completion at the day of Jesus Christ"* (Philippians 1:6).
- *Research.* Redemptive narrative forms are associated with well-being (McAdams; correlational). Retrieval and spacing for information (well supported).
- *What to look for.* A document in your own words, and practices that continue without the coach.

**Throughout.** The coach holds three roles in this order: formation partner, covenant-identity diagnostician, coaching partner. Prayer is the coach's orienting practice. The coach does not produce the change and does not need to see it. When what surfaces exceeds coaching, the coach says so and refers.

### 10. What the investment buys

The value comes in four forms. None is promised. Each is what the design aims at.

**Inside you.** The direction of travel is a set of nine qualities of the inner life: settled, undivided, free, moved without being swept, unhurried, loved and knowing it, truthful without cruelty, purposeful without striving, and available. They are not goals assigned to you or a standard to be measured against. They are the fruit of what the work makes room for. The client-facing promise puts it in plain terms. You may be less ownable by other people's opinions. You may feel fully without being swept away. You may stop running from a deficit. It uses an image: the difference between reading a weather report and standing in the rain.

**Around you.** An inner life that is less defended is more available to other people. The ninth quality is the one that shows in a room. You arrive already carrying something for others, instead of checking whether the room is safe. The outward counterpart is the fruit of the Spirit:

> "But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control; against such things there is no law." (Galatians 5:22–23)

In marriage, parenting, work, conflict, and community, these are desired outcomes. They are not promised ones.

**Beyond the engagement.** The engagement is a concentrated season of a lifelong work. You leave with practices and a rule of life that continue without the coach. The aim is not to keep you as a client. It is to equip you for the ongoing walk with the Holy Spirit that Scripture assumes.

**Evidence you can check as you go.** At about session four or five you see your own words from intake beside your words now. At the close you receive a written record built from your own language. Nobody asks you to take the movement on trust. If movement is slow, the Snapshot says so.

**What leaving the gap alone costs.** The cost is paid in years. It looks like effort that does not compound, a private shame that grows with every sermon you already know, and a self that performs well and rests nowhere. This is recognition, not a warning.

**The terms.** Twelve weekly sessions of 50 to 60 minutes by video, over about twelve weeks. $1,680 in full ($140 per session) or three payments of $560. Sessions after the first twelve are $140 each. A founding rate may be offered privately to early clients in exchange for honest feedback. *[Current per the signed Coaching Agreement (2026-07-28) and the Quick Launch Plan (2026-09-15). Confirm before quoting.]*

**When another path is the better first step.** A good book, a pastor, a small group, a counselor, or a different coach can be the right choice. If what you carry needs clinical care, that comes first. If you want advice, accountability, or practical tips, this is not the tool. ### 11. What this does not promise, and who stands behind it

This is not a fast process. Formation takes the time it takes.

It does not promise that you will feel a particular way. Scripture makes room for lament, protest, and honest doubt inside the covenant relationship (Psalm 13; Psalm 88). The prophet cries, *"O LORD, how long shall I cry for help, and you will not hear?"* (Habakkuk 1:2). The work welcomes that. A feeling is never the test of whether you are in Christ.

It does not produce the change. Conditions are not causes. If a session ever feels like pressure toward a feeling, that is a signal that something is off, and you should say so.

It does not replace the church, Scripture, prayer, or community. It works alongside whatever of these you have. It is one concentrated season of a lifelong work.

It is not clinical care. If what is underneath needs a therapist or a physician, you will be told and helped to find one.

It carries no outcome data. The practice is new. Nothing in this document is offered as proven.

### 12. A way to decide

Five questions.

1. **Is what I am missing information, or is the truth I already hold not reaching where I live?** If you can name verses you cannot rest in, it is probably the second.
2. **What have I already tried, and what level did each reach?** If each added knowledge, effort, or structure, none reached the place this work targets.
3. **Am I willing to be honestly known, at a pace I can bear?** If exposure feels unsafe, that is information, and the work slows down. Nothing asks you to perform.
4. **Am I willing to do small, specific practices between sessions?** Much of the formation happens there.
5. **Is my growth itself something I am striving to prove?** If "becoming more" has turned into the way you try to earn standing, the work starts there. That is not a disqualifier. It is the first thing to look at.

If your answers are "it isn't reaching," "they did not," "yes," "yes," and "I'm not sure," a conversation is worth having.

**What to ask the coach.** Ask how the work is supervised. Ask what happens if you need clinical care. Ask what the coach does if nothing seems to move. Ask to see the Scripture behind any step. A coach worth hiring welcomes all four questions.

**The next step is a conversation.** A discovery call is a real conversation about whether this is the right next step. It is not a sales call. No decision is asked of you until you have been heard.

### 13. The answer, once more

The value of Covenant Identity Coaching is the truth you already hold reaching the place you actually live from. It is built from Scripture outward, with research admitted only where it serves what Scripture says. It cannot be promised. It can be judged: step by step against your Bible, and session by session against your own words. The change, if it comes, is God's.

### C. Design cards D1–D15: Scripture first, evidence second

*Each card gives the design element, what the client experiences, the Scripture behind it, the evidence behind it, where the evidence stops, its standing in [[Covenant Identity — Biblical Foundations Index]], and one plain sentence authors can use. Anchors proposed by this document were vetted on 2026-10-20 and carry the tag that review supports.*

**D1. Identity before behavior (the covenant grammar)**
- *Client experiences:* The work begins with who God has declared them to be, not with what they should do.
- *Scripture first:* [S] Exodus 19:4 (before the "if" of 19:5); Exodus 20:2–3; Ephesians 2:8–10; Colossians 3:9–10; Romans 6:6, 11. Pointers: Deuteronomy 7:6–8; 9:4–6; Romans 5:8.
- *Evidence second:* [E] Self-verification (Swann). [M] Narrative identity (McAdams and McLean). [T] Immunity to Change (Kegan and Lahey). [T] Schema accounts (Young). The counter-direction, that behavior can seed identity, is [P].
- *Where evidence stops:* Research corroborates the sequence for deep, emotionally charged self-beliefs. It does not test the covenant claim, and it does not say identity always comes first.
- *Standing:* Developed. Contested (progressive covenantalism; the Exodus 19:5 "if"; perseverance left open on purpose).
- *Say it like this:* "In Scripture, identity is declared first and the commands follow. The work begins where Scripture begins."

**D2. The inner life is the primary terrain**
- *Client experiences:* Work at the level of the heart, not behavior alone.
- *Scripture first:* [S] Proverbs 4:23; Jeremiah 17:9–10; Matthew 15:8, 18–19; Ephesians 3:16; 2 Corinthians 4:16; Ezekiel 36:26–27; Jeremiah 31:33; Isaiah 61:1 with Luke 4:18.
- *Evidence second:* [E] Dual-process cognition (Kahneman). [E] Implicit and explicit memory (Squire; Schacter; LeDoux). Mapping "heart" onto these constructs is [D].
- *Where evidence stops:* Hebrew *lev* is a unified organ of thought, will, and emotion, so this is correspondence, not identity. Hebrews 4:12 is not a proof text for two cognitive systems. Ezekiel 36 was addressed to a nation, and the New Testament individualizes it (2 Corinthians 3; Hebrews 8).
- *Standing:* Developed ([[The Inner Man — Evidence & Argument Core]]; [[Covenant Identity — The Inner Man — Biblical Foundations and Position Paper]]).
- *Say it like this:* "Scripture treats the heart as the source of life, and as deeper than what you can say about yourself."

**D3. Hearing is not the same as living from**
- *Client experiences:* "I know it but I can't live it" is named as a real and ordinary condition, not a defect.

- *Evidence second:* [M] REVEAL and Barna show the gap at population scale. [E] Implicit versus explicit memory describes why it can occur.
- *Where evidence stops:* Scripture supplies the phenomenon, not the cause. Population studies show the gap, not its cure.
- *Standing:* Developed (Foundations Index §2; James 1:22–24 closed 2026-08-23). The verb distinction in 1 Thessalonians 2:13 is a Gap and is not used.
- *Say it like this:* "The Bible names the gap between hearing the Word and living from it. It does not blame a lack of information."

**D4. Diagnosis before declaration**
- *Client experiences:* Precise questions, and the governing belief named in their own words before any truth is spoken to it.
- *Scripture first:* [S] Proverbs 20:5; Psalm 139:23–24; Jeremiah 17:10; Hebrews 4:12–13. [S~] The Emmaus road (Luke 24:13–32): Jesus asks before He opens Scripture, and the travelers say, *"Did not our hearts burn within us while he talked to us on the road, while he opened to us the Scriptures?"* (Luke 24:32). The narrative is [S]. Using it as a coaching posture is [S~] and only partly fair. Jesus also withholds recognition, rebukes them (*"O foolish ones"*, 24:25), and teaches with authority, and the Spirit is not named in verses 13–32. It is used here for posture, not as a technique. - *Where evidence stops:* Precision does not change a person. The Spirit's witness does (D7).

**D5. The felt cost before the truth (lament)**

2. Naming the governing belief precisely increases the chance that truth lands. [D]

**Do not say until there is data.**
- "CIC works." "Proven." "Evidence-based" for the whole practice. The defensible phrase is "evidence-informed," and only with the Part I §8 caveats.
- "Research shows that CIC..."
- "Most clients..." "Clients report..." "Typical results..." No client outcomes exist. Any future client material must also follow the de-identification rule.
- Any testimonial, number, or before-and-after story that does not come from a real, consenting client.
- "Permanent" or "lasting change" as a promise.
- That the Holy Spirit will respond to the method.
- Supervision, insurance, training, or credential claims, unless true on the day.

**Never say.** No exceptions.
- Urgency, scarcity, or pressure of any kind.
- "Declare it into existence," "speak it until it is true," or any wording that implies speech produces change.
- Criticism of churches, pastors, or sermons.
- Diagnosis or treatment language: "heal your trauma," "treat," "cure."
- "Become all you were created to be," "unlock your potential," "breakthrough," and similar growth slogans.
- "Unconditional" for the Abrahamic or Davidic covenants. Say the conditions were met by Christ.
- More on perseverance than this: a believer's standing in Christ cannot be lost through failure, sin, doubt, or fluctuating feeling, and the question of final willful renunciation is left open on purpose.

- "Idol," "idolatry," and "affections" vocabulary in public copy. It is internal practice language.
- The name "Word Within."

**Scripture-use cautions.** From the 2026-10-20 /bible vetting. Each is a text or a claim that overreaches if used the usual way.
1. **Philemon 6.** The meaning of *koinōnia* ("sharing") and *epignōsis* ("full knowledge") is disputed, and so is "in us" or "in you." The King James reading, "acknowledging every good thing," lends itself to confession teaching. Do not use it.
2. **Hebrews 4:2.** The Greek text is disputed. The ESV follows manuscripts with *synkekerasmenous* ("united by faith with those who listened"). The King James reads "not being mixed with faith in them that heard it." The verse sits inside a warning passage (3:7–4:13) and can feed scrupulosity. Do not use it in public copy.
3. **1 Thessalonians 2:13.** Use the clause "at work in you believers." Do not build the registration and reception distinction on "received" and "accepted." Paul's contrast is between a human message and God's. The verb behind "accepted" (*dechomai*) is also the verb in 1 Corinthians 2:14 for the person who "does not accept the things of the Spirit of God."
4. **Romans 10:17.** In context it is about the gospel proclaimed and saving faith. Do not apply it alone to a believer's growth. Never apply it to speaking something over yourself.
5. **Matthew 7:24–27.** It contrasts hearing and doing. Do not say it teaches that understanding is the rock. Use Matthew 13:19, 23 and Colossians 1:9–10 for understanding.
6. ***Rhēma* and *logos*.** Do not say that *rhēma* means a specific word quickened to you and *logos* means the written word. New Testament usage overlaps: *rhēma* is the gospel preached in Romans 10:8, 17 and 1 Peter 1:25. The distinction is a Word-of-Faith risk. Do not say "a word from God for you," "download," or "decree."
7. **2 Peter 1:20–21 and 2 Corinthians 3:6.** The first is about the origin of prophecy. The second is about the old and new covenants. Neither is a rule for how to interpret.
8. **"The original author's intended meaning."** Say "in its own setting and in the whole of Scripture, read in the light of Christ." The New Testament's own use of the Old Testament goes beyond the original setting in places (Luke 24; 1 Peter 1:10–12; Galatians 4:21–31).
9. **1 Thessalonians 5:21, Colossians 2:8, Proverbs 25:2.** 1 Thessalonians 5:21 speaks directly to testing what is offered as the Spirit's word, and Acts 17:11 to testing teaching. For evaluating research all three are analogies at most. Colossians 2:8 was written about a specific teaching at Colossae. Its ESV wording changed from "elementary principles" (2001) to "elemental spirits" (2016).
10. **Psalm 19 and Romans 1:19–20.** These are general revelation about God, not about people. The ground for looking at people carefully is the image of God (Genesis 1:26–27) and God's kindness to all (Matthew 5:45; Acts 14:17). Say "general revelation and the image of God," not "common grace," which is a Reformed-flavored label.
11. **Luke 24 as a technique.** It is a pattern of posture. Jesus also rebukes and teaches with authority. Do not present it as a coaching procedure.
12. **Galatians 3:2, 5; Galatians 5:6; James 5:16.** The Greek phrase in Galatians 3:2, 5 (*akoē pisteōs*) can mean "hearing with faith" or "the message of faith," and the ESV footnote gives "believing what you heard." The point used here, that the Spirit comes through faith and not works, holds on either reading. The voice of the verb in Galatians 5:6 and James 5:16 is debated. The *energeō* family also describes sin, death, and evil at work (Romans 7:5; 2 Corinthians 4:12; Ephesians 2:2; 2 Thessalonians 2:7).
13. **Deuteronomy 30:14 in brand copy.** Several documents quote a shortened form as ESV (§I.3, item 6). Quote the full ESV sentence or retire the line.

Existing claims in the vault that outrun this ladder are flagged in §I.3.

### E. Language bank

**E1. Fresh formulations.** Each gives the stage and the best register. Adapt freely within the ladder. Items 3, 4, and 8 are built most directly on your brief. Item 11 is built on your account of how faith is built (§11).

**1. The design in two sentences** *(Stage 4; any register)*

> Covenant Identity Coaching starts where Scripture starts: with who God has already declared you to be, and with the inner life as the place He does His defining work. It then draws on research about how people hold and change deep beliefs, but only where that research agrees with Scripture, and only to prepare the conditions in which God's Word can do its work.

**2. The two-sources sentence** *(Stage 4; skeptical or technical readers)*

> Scripture decides what is true about who you are, what has gone wrong, and what renewal looks like. Research is allowed to describe how the inner life works. It is never allowed to decide where it is going.

**3. The faith-and-effect paragraph** *(Stage 4–5; believers)*

> Two things make this worth your trust. It is built on what God has said, so every step can be checked against your own Bible. And it is ordered by what has been observed about how people hold and change deep beliefs, so the steps meet the conditions that real change requires. The first is why it deserves your trust. The second is why it is built the way it is. Neither replaces the Holy Spirit, who does the changing.

**4. The "effective" paragraph** *(Stage 4–5; theologically literate readers; vetted 2026-10-20)*

> The New Testament's word for "effective" is the word for work that actually gets done. The word of God "is at work in you believers" (1 Thessalonians 2:13) and is "living and active" (Hebrews 4:12). God is the one who "works in you" (Philippians 2:13). Faith is "working through love" (Galatians 5:6). Covenant Identity Coaching takes that vocabulary at face value. The effectiveness belongs to the Word and the Spirit. The practice exists to remove what has stood in the Word's way: a belief that was never named, a grief that was never felt, a truth heard many times and never received.

**5. What you are paying for** *(Stage 5)*

> You are paying for a precise conversation. It is built to find the specific belief that governs your reactions, let you feel what it has cost, and bring what God has actually said to that exact place at the moment it can be received. You are also paying for the structure that keeps the work going between sessions, and for a record in your own words so you can see what moved.

**6. The ten identities** *(Stage 4; Andrew's voice)*

> The identities this work builds on are not new. They are the ones Scripture states most plainly: made in God's image, known and chosen, a new creation, an adopted child, a fellow heir, indwelt by the Spirit, seated with Christ and held, loved as the Father loves the Son, sent, and being conformed to Christ's image. You can find every one in your own Bible, and you can check how I use it there.

**7. Why a coach at all** *(Stage 4)*

> Scripture says the purposes of the heart are "like deep water," and that "a man of understanding will draw it out" (Proverbs 20:5). That is not because the Bible is insufficient. It is because most of us cannot read our own depths clearly, and God has regularly used other people to help.

**8. The honest-status line** *(Stage 5; Andrew's voice; discovery call)*

> The problem is well documented. The design is grounded piece by piece. The practice as a whole is new, and I am not going to tell you it is proven, because it isn't. What I can show you is why each step is there, what Scripture says about it, what research says about it, and how you would know within a few sessions whether it is reaching you.

**9. The decision line** *(Stage 5)*

> Before you decide, ask three things. Is what I'm missing information, or is the truth I already hold not reaching where I live? Have the things I've tried reached that place, or did they work at another level? Am I willing to do small, honest work between sessions?

**10. The one-minute value statement** *(spoken; Stage 4–5; Andrew's voice; Willard-led)*

> Most help for sincere Christians assumes the problem is a lack of something: more knowledge, more discipline, more accountability. We give them more, and the gap stays. That is not because the help was wrong. It is because it was aimed at the wrong level. The gap is between the truth a person holds and the place they live from. Scripture names that gap. It also says where God does His defining work: in the heart, the inner self, where the New Covenant writes the law. Covenant Identity Coaching is built from that starting point. It finds the specific belief that is governing you, lets you feel what it has cost, brings what God has actually said to that exact place, and gives the Spirit room to keep working between sessions. Research on how deep beliefs hold and change is used where it agrees with Scripture, and only to describe, never to set the direction. The practice is new, so I will not tell you it is proven. I can show you why each step is there, and how you would know within a few sessions whether it is reaching you.

*The last two sentences are the disclosure option. Cut them only by decision, not by default.*

**11. How faith is built** *(Stage 4–5; believers; any register; built on your account in §11 and the vetted texts in §7)*

> Two things build faith, and Scripture names both. One is the Word itself, understood in its own setting and in the whole of Scripture, read in the light of Christ, so that a person knows why a truth is true and not only that it is written. The other is the Spirit, who opens the mind and the heart to what the Word says, and who at times brings one Scripture home so that it is felt in the soul. The first can be taught. The second can only be received. The practice serves both and produces neither.

### G. Objections and answers

**1. "Isn't this just psychology with Bible verses added?"**
The concern is fair. Many integrated approaches let psychology set the agenda and add verses afterward. The answer is structural. Every design step is stated with its Scripture first (§C). Research enters only through two tests. Contributions that bring a competing account of human nature are set aside even when they are useful. Where the research is mixed or untested, this document says so. The standard is that a step with no biblical case should go, and where the case is still thin the Index says so.

**2. "Doesn't Scripture alone suffice?"**
Scripture is sufficient for what it claims: *"His divine power has granted to us all things that pertain to life and godliness"* (2 Peter 1:3). That is a claim about authority for what identity is, what has gone wrong, and what renewal looks like. It is not a claim that Scripture gives developmental or neurological descriptions. The design uses research for that level only, and it evaluates the research against Scripture. The remaining exposure is real. The boundary between describing a mechanism and prescribing a goal is not always clean, and a coach needs enough theological and psychological formation to see where a contributor has crossed it.

**3. "Isn't a focus on my inner life self-absorbed?"**
Scripture sends us to the heart (Proverbs 4:23). The prayer it models is addressed to God: *"Search me, O God, and know my heart!"* (Psalm 139:23). The aim is not a better self. Nine of the ten identities are already yours, and the telos ends outward, in being sent and being available to others. If growth itself has become something you strive to prove, the work begins there.

**4. "Isn't 'identity in Christ' just self-affirmation, or Word of Faith?"**
No, and the design is built to prevent it. The identities are positional: already true, not produced by speech or effort. The practice reckons with them (Romans 6:11). It does not manifest them. Truth is never spoken as technique apart from diagnosis and relationship. The step that matters most is the Spirit's witness, which no one can produce. Any piece that implies speaking produces change violates the design.

**5. "I have the Spirit and the Word. Why pay a coach?"**
You do have both. The practice is one means among others and never a replacement for either, or for the church. Scripture shows people helping one another see (Proverbs 20:5; Galatians 6:1; Colossians 1:28–29). What the practice adds is precision, structure, and a relationship built for this one task. If you already have that in a mentor, a pastor, or a group, you may not need this.

**6. "Does it work? Where is the data?"**
The honest answer has three parts. The problem is documented. The components are supported at the strengths in §B. The whole has not been outcome-tested. You can judge it in two ways: step by step against your Bible, and session by session against your own words. The practice will not tell you it is proven, because it is not.

**7. "Is this therapy?"**
No. It does not diagnose or treat clinical conditions or process trauma directly. It requires a non-clinical baseline at entry. It has a written referral protocol, and it refers when what surfaces exceeds coaching. *(The readiness gaps named in the 2026-07-27 review remain open until closed. Do not answer this question in public without checking §I.)*

**8. "Are you excusing sin by talking about wounds?"**
No. The work holds both: what was done to a person and what the person has cultivated. A tool built for the second, the witnessing-repentance sequence, exists for that reason. It follows the distinction Paul draws between godly grief, which turns toward God, and worldly grief, which does not (2 Corinthians 7:10).

**9. "What is your tradition? Reformed, Wesleyan, charismatic?"**
The covenant frame is biblical theology's covenant narrative, broadly evangelical, and explicitly not Reformed covenant theology. On how the Spirit uses means, the practice stands in the Wesleyan means-of-grace stream and says so. On whether a believer can lose salvation, it holds only that standing in Christ cannot be lost through failure, sin, doubt, or fluctuating feeling. It refers the question of final renunciation to the client's pastor. It does not raise the Israel and Church question unprompted. A reader from a stricter monergist tradition will weigh the "conditions" language more cautiously. The Conditions document addresses that fault line directly.

**10. "Will you tell me what to do?"**
No. The coach is not a directive counselor. The coach is also not neutral about who God says you are. The client is the authority on their experience. God is the authority on their identity. The coach draws out and does not prescribe.

**11. "Do I have to feel something for this to be working?"**
No. Lament and doubt are welcome. A feeling is never the test of whether you are in Christ. Felt reception is a possible fruit of the work, not a requirement for it. The mid-point Snapshot handles a lower score honestly. It can mean regression, or it can mean you now see yourself more accurately.

**12. "What if nothing changes?"**
Then it may be slower than hoped, and the Snapshot will say so. The arc can be extended. If the need is beyond coaching, you will be referred. Nothing is promised. The aim is never dependency on the coach.

**13. "Couldn't I do this alone, with the workbooks?"**
Some people can, for a time. The reason another person helps is the one in Jeremiah 17:9–10 and Proverbs 20:5: the heart is hard to read from the inside. Some of the tools are also designed for use with a coach.

**14. "Isn't 'Scripture first' just a slogan?"**
It would be if the record were hidden. The Biblical Foundations Index shows, for every design decision, whether the biblical case is Developed, Assumed, Contested, or a Gap. Where the case is weaker, the Index says so. That record is the answer.

**15. "Isn't 'the Spirit quickened a Scripture to me' subjective? How do you test it?"**
Scripture speaks of this work in its own language. The psalmist prays, *"give me life according to your word"* (Psalm 119:25). Paul prays that the eyes of believers' hearts would be enlightened (Ephesians 1:18). The experience is not the test of the truth. Scripture is. *"Test everything; hold fast what is good"* (1 Thessalonians 5:21) speaks directly to testing what is offered as the Spirit's word. The practice never treats a felt impression as new revelation, and it never presents one as proof that a step worked. It holds that the Spirit does not contradict what He has already said in Scripture. It does not claim to produce a quickening or to predict one.

**16. "Isn't learning theology just head knowledge?"**
It can be, and Scripture says so. A person can hear and not do (Matthew 7:26; James 1:22–25), and a person can hear without understanding (Matthew 13:19). Scripture also treats understanding as a means of growth. It asks that believers be *"filled with the knowledge of his will in all spiritual wisdom and understanding, so as to walk in a manner worthy of the Lord"* (Colossians 1:9–10). The difference is not knowledge against experience. It is a truth understood where it stands in the whole of Scripture and received, against a truth held as a data point. The practice is built on that difference. It teaches the truth in its own setting and waits for the Spirit to bring it home.

### H. What this document does not claim

- It does not claim that neuroscience proves Scripture, or that Scripture predicted neuroscience.
- It does not claim that CIC's mechanism or outcomes are validated.
- It does not claim that every tool was built from Scripture first. It claims that Scripture governs every design decision about identity, diagnosis, and renewal, and that the governing architecture was built outward from it.
- It does not claim that the ten identities exhaust Scripture's language about who believers are.
- It does not claim that any cited study measures Christian transformation as Scripture defines it.
- It does not claim that the anchors in §I.2 carry more than the status the /bible vetting gave them. Some were set aside.
- It does not claim that its way of reading Scripture, "in its own setting and in the whole of Scripture, read in the light of Christ," is commanded by a text. It claims that this is a widely shared evangelical way of reading and that Scripture's own practice points toward it.
- It does not claim that the Spirit's quickening can be produced, scheduled, or detected by any method.
- It does not claim that terms, pricing, or status lines are current beyond their dates.

*A. The six candidate anchors*

**A1. 1 Thessalonians 2:13**
- *Establishes:* Paul thanks God that the Thessalonians received his message "not as the word of men but as what it really is, the word of God, which is at work in you believers." The NET note reads Paul's focus as their attitude toward the message. They took it as God's and not as a human message.
- *Variants and debates:* The two verbs, *paralambanō* ("received") and *dechomai* ("accepted"), read as a natural pair. They do not mark two stages. *Dechomai* is also the verb in 1 Corinthians 2:14, where the natural person "does not accept the things of the Spirit of God."
- *Verdict:* The clause "at work in you believers" is [S] and Developed. Using the two verbs as a registration and reception mechanism is an overreach. It is [D], and a Gap.
- *Safe wording:* "The word of God, which is at work in you believers."

**A2. Hebrews 4:2**
- *Establishes:* The good news came to the wilderness generation, "but the message they heard did not benefit them, because they were not united by faith with those who listened."
- *Variants and debates:* The Greek has two readings. The accusative plural *synkekerasmenous* (P13, P46, A, B, C, D*, and others) is the NET's choice and the ESV's: the hearers were not united by faith with those who listened. The nominative singular (Sinaiticus and some versions) says the message was not mixed with faith by the hearers. The King James follows that line: "not being mixed with faith in them that heard it." Other variants exist (minuscules 104 and 1881, and the Majority text). The verse sits inside the warning passage in 3:7–4:13, which belongs with 6:4–6 and 10:26–31. The 2026-10-18 stance leaves the question of final renunciation open on purpose. For a sincere Wilderness reader the verse also risks feeding scrupulosity.
- *Verdict:* [S~], weak. Contested. Drop from public copy. Practitioner use only, with caution.
- *Safe wording:* None. Use James 1:22–25 and Matthew 13:19–23 instead.

**A3. Romans 4:19–21**
- *Establishes:* Abraham "considered his own body, which was as good as dead" and the barrenness of Sarah's womb, and "no unbelief made him waver concerning the promise of God."
- *Variants and debates:* The manuscripts divide. The majority reading (D, F, G, Ψ, 33, 1881, and the Majority text) has the word "not": he did not consider his body. Sinaiticus, A, B, C, minuscules 6, 81, 365, 1506, and 1739, and the Coptic lack it. The NET note calls the negative reading "probably a scribal attempt to exalt the faith of Abraham." The King James follows the negative: "he considered not his own body now dead." NA28 brackets "already" (*ēdē*). The NET cites a commentator for the point that faith does not mean closing one's eyes to reality. Genesis 17:17 shows Abraham laughing.
- *Verdict:* [S~] as a pattern, and fair under the critical text. That the meeting of the fact and the promise is how change happens is [D]. Developed as a pattern, with the variant noted.
- *Safe wording:* "Abraham looked at the facts and did not let them have the last word."

**A4. Psalm 73:16–17**
- *Establishes:* The psalmist tried to understand why the wicked prosper. It seemed to him "a wearisome task, until I went into the sanctuary of God; then I discerned their end." Verse 26 is where the psalm lands: "God is the strength of my heart and my portion forever."
- *Variants and debates:* The Hebrew *miqdash* ("sanctuary") is plural here, probably the temple precincts (compare Psalm 68:35 and Jeremiah 51:51). "Discerned their end" may refer to a temple oracle, or to the songs of confidence and wisdom psalms sung there (for example Psalms 11, 12, 1, 37, and 112).
- *Verdict:* [S~] as a pattern of a bitter heart reoriented in God's presence, worship, and instruction. It is not a mechanism. Compare Lamentations 3:24. Assumed.
- *Safe wording:* "A bitter heart was reoriented in the presence of God."

**A5. Luke 24:13–32**
- *Establishes:* The narrative is [S]. Jesus asks questions and withholds recognition. He rebukes them: "O foolish ones, and slow of heart to believe." Then, "beginning with Moses and all the Prophets, he interpreted to them in all the Scriptures the things concerning himself" (24:27). Their eyes are opened (24:31). Their hearts burn as He opens the Scriptures (24:32). Later He opens their minds to understand the Scriptures (24:45).
- *Variants and debates:* One Greek verb, *dianoigō* ("open thoroughly"), runs through the passage: eyes (24:31), the Scriptures (24:32), minds (24:45). It also appears for the opening of Lydia's heart (Acts 16:14) and for Paul explaining and proving from the Scriptures (Acts 17:3). The Spirit is not named in 24:13–32.
- *Verdict:* Using it as a coaching posture is [S~] and partly an overreach. Jesus also withholds recognition, rebukes, and teaches with authority. Its strongest use is that it holds both sources in Andrew's account: exposition across the whole of Scripture (24:27, 44–45) and the burning heart and opened mind (24:31–32, 45). Developed as a pattern. Not a technique.
- *Safe wording:* "Jesus asked first and then opened the Scriptures. Their hearts burned as He did."

**A6. The *energeō* word family**
- *Establishes:* The claim is accurate for the passages chosen. God works (Philippians 2:13; Colossians 1:29). The word works (1 Thessalonians 2:13; Hebrews 4:12, *energēs*). Faith works through love (Galatians 5:6).
- *Variants and debates:* The family also describes sin, death, and Satan at work (Romans 7:5; Ephesians 2:2; 2 Corinthians 4:12; 2 Thessalonians 2:7, 9) and miraculous powers (Matthew 14:2). The voice of the verb in Galatians 5:6 and James 5:16, middle or passive, is debated, along Protestant and Catholic lines over faith formed by love. Philemon 6 is the most uncertain text. *Koinōnia* has at least four readings: the NET lists financial giving, evangelistic sharing, fellowship with believers, and participation in the faith. *Epignōsis* spans understanding and experience. "In us" or "in you" is a manuscript variant. The NET favors fellowship with believers and reads Paul as praying that Philemon be equipped to encourage and love the saints. The King James reads "by the acknowledging of every good thing which is in you in Christ Jesus," which lends itself to confession teaching. The King James uses "effectual" at Philemon 6, 1 Thessalonians 2:13 ("effectually worketh"), Ephesians 3:7 ("the effectual working of his power"), and James 5:16 ("effectual fervent prayer").
- *Verdict:* [S~]. Keep 1 Thessalonians 2:13, Philippians 2:13, Galatians 5:6, Colossians 1:29, and Hebrews 4:12. Drop Philemon 6 from public copy and from the "effective" paragraph. Keep the caution that the family also describes sin and evil at work.
- *Safe wording:* "In the passages cited, the one at work is God, His Word, or faith acting through love."

*B. The texts behind Andrew's account of how faith is built*

**B1. Matthew 7:24–27 and Luke 6:47–49**
- *Establishes [S]:* The one who hears and does builds on the rock. The one who hears and does not do builds on the sand. Luke 6:48 adds that the wise builder "dug deep and laid the foundation on the rock."
- *Variants and debates:* The parable does not say the two builders differ in understanding. "Sand is taking Scripture in as a data point only" is close to the parable's point and not exact.
- *Better texts for understanding:* Matthew 13:19, 23; Colossians 1:9–10; Colossians 2:2–3 ("full assurance of understanding"). The hearer who only hears is self-deceived (James 1:22–25). Building language for the faith of believers: Colossians 2:6–7; Acts 20:32; Jude 20; Ephesians 2:20; 4:11–14; 2 Peter 3:18.
- *Verdict:* Use it for hearing and doing. Do not use it to teach that understanding is the rock.

**B2. Grounded understanding**
- *Establishes [S]:* Psalm 119:130: the unfolding of God's words gives light and understanding to the simple. Psalm 119:18: the psalmist asks God to open his eyes. Nehemiah 8:8: the readers "gave the sense." Luke 24:27, 44–45: a Christ-centered reading of all the Scriptures, then the opening of minds. Acts 8:30–31: a guide helps the Ethiopian understand. Acts 17:3: Paul explains and proves from the Scriptures. Acts 17:11: the Bereans test teaching by Scripture. 2 Timothy 2:15: "rightly handling the word of truth" (the range of *orthotomeō* is debated).
- *Does not establish:* 2 Peter 1:20–21 is about the origin of prophecy (verse 21). It is not a rule for reading. Nothing in these texts commands a method of interpretation.
- *Verdict:* "The original author's intended meaning" is an evangelical theological method, a grammatical-historical reading joined to a canonical one. No text commands it. The New Testament's own use of the Old Testament includes a fuller sense (Luke 24; 1 Peter 1:10–12; Galatians 4:21–31). The safe wording, which Andrew chose, is "what the text means in its own setting and in the whole of Scripture, read in the light of Christ." The practice of teaching understanding is Developed. The hermeneutic is Contested at the edges.

**B3. Quickening and illumination**
- *Establishes [S]:* Psalm 119:25, 37, 40: the psalmist asks God to give him life ("according to your word," "in your ways," "in your righteousness"). The Hebrew in verse 25 is *ḥayyēnî*, a Piel imperative of *ḥāyâ*, "make alive" or "revive." The King James renders it "quicken." Psalm 119:50: the promise gives life in affliction. John 6:63: the Spirit gives life, and Jesus' words are spirit and life. 1 Corinthians 2:12–14: the Spirit enables understanding and welcome. Ephesians 1:17–18: the eyes of believers' hearts are enlightened (the "Spirit of wisdom and of revelation" is the Holy Spirit on most readings). Acts 16:14: the Lord opened Lydia's heart. 1 Thessalonians 1:5: the gospel came in word, power, the Holy Spirit, and full conviction. Jeremiah 15:16: God's words became joy and the delight of the heart.
- *Establishes [S~]:* John 14:26 and 16:13–15 are promises made to the apostles, and their extension to believers is by analogy. 1 John 2:20, 27 (the anointing) was written about false teachers who left the community. It does not say that believers need no teachers.
- *Does not establish:* 2 Corinthians 3:6, "the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life," is about the old and new covenants. It is not a rule for reading.
- *The doctrine of illumination:* The Spirit enables a person to understand and welcome what Scripture already says. It adds no new revelation.
- *The rhēma and logos claim:* The popular claim is that *rhēma* means a specific word quickened to you and *logos* means the written word. New Testament usage overlaps. *Rhēma* is the gospel preached in Romans 10:8, 17, 18 and 1 Peter 1:25. It is also used at Ephesians 6:17, Hebrews 6:5, Hebrews 11:3, Matthew 4:4, Luke 1:37, and John 6:63 (*rhēmata*). *Logos* also names the spoken message (1 Thessalonians 2:13; Acts 4:31; 2 Timothy 4:2). The Septuagint renders the Hebrew *dābār* with both. The distinction is not supported, and it is a Word-of-Faith risk.
- *Testing an impression:* 1 Thessalonians 5:19–21 (the direct context is prophecies) and 1 John 4:1. See also [[Covenant Identity — Discerning God's Voice — Biblical Research]].
- *Verdict:* The safe wording for "the Spirit quickened a Scripture to me" does not rest on the *rhēma* and *logos* distinction. It is "the Spirit brought that Scripture home to me," anchored on Psalm 119:25, John 6:63, 1 Corinthians 2:12–14, Ephesians 1:17–18, and Luke 24:32, 45.

**B4. Romans 10:17**
- *Establishes [S]:* In 10:5–21 the subject is the proclaimed gospel and saving faith. *Rhēma* is the preached message (10:8, 17, 18).
- *Variants and debates:* "Of Christ" has strong external support. The majority text reads "of God," and one papyrus fragment may read "of the Lord." The NET reads the genitive as more likely objective, "the word about Christ," though that is debated. The King James reads "hearing by the word of God."
- *Verdict:* Safe for saving faith in its own context. For a believer's ongoing growth the support comes from other texts: 1 Thessalonians 2:13; Acts 20:32; Colossians 2:6–7; Colossians 3:16; Galatians 3:2, 5 (the phrase *akoē pisteōs* is debated); Jude 20. The client-workbook use ("hearing yourself say it") was an overreach and has been corrected.

*C. The warrant texts in Part I §4*

- **C1. Titus 1:12–13.** [S~]. Paul affirms an outsider's accurate observation about behavior. The tradition that names the Cretan as Epimenides is not in the text.
- **C2. 1 Thessalonians 5:20–21.** [S~] for evaluating psychology. Direct for testing what is offered as the Spirit's word, because the context is prophecies.
- **C3. Colossians 2:8.** [S~]. It was written about a specific syncretism at Colossae. The meaning of *stoicheia* is debated. The ESV text moved from "elementary principles" (2001) to "elemental spirits" (2016 and 2025).
- **C4. Proverbs 25:2.** [S~].
- **C5. Acts 17:11.** [S] for testing teaching by Scripture.
- **C6. Psalm 19:1–4 and Romans 1:19–20.** [S] for general revelation about God. They are not about people. The warrant for psychology is the image of God (Genesis 1:26–27; 9:6) and God's kindness to all (Matthew 5:45; Acts 14:17) [S~]. "Common grace" is a Reformed-flavored label. Say "general revelation and the image of God," or "prevenient grace" in Wesleyan idiom. The Integration Rationale uses "common grace" (§I.3, item 5).

## Document Provenance

**Why this exists:** Andrew needs to speak about the biblical and the empirical design of CIC, in many venues, to believers deciding whether to invest. He needs to do it without re-deriving the case each time, and without letting register-appropriate simplification become overclaim.

**Governing question:** What can be said about the value of this practice that holds up, claim by claim, when someone in the room knows their Bible, their psychology, or both?

**Design decisions:**
- *Three layers kept separate.* The pattern comes from [[The Inner Man — Evidence & Argument Core]]: what Scripture says, how the design applies it, and what research adds.
- *Two readers.* Part I is written to the decision-maker. Part II is written to authors.
- *Honesty before persuasion.* The claims ladder, the evidence register, and the "who stands behind it" placeholder exist because [[CIC — Evidence Case for the Model (Internal)]] sets the boundary: the problem is evidenced, and the mechanism is a working thesis.
- *Anchors labeled, then vetted.* New Scripture anchors were labeled when proposed, the same discipline the Biblical Foundations Index uses. The /bible vetting of 2026-10-20 was then applied. Some anchors were kept as patterns, some were limited, and three were set aside: Philemon 6, Hebrews 4:2, and the two-verb mapping in 1 Thessalonians 2:13.
- *Corrections applied at Andrew's direction.* The six corrections found in the verification pass were applied to the files they name on 2026-10-20 (§B). The consistency flags in §I.3 are listed and not edited.
- *Vetting reasoning kept as an appendix.* The reasoning behind the /bible verdicts is kept in §J, not in a separate file, so no new index entry is needed. A standalone reference is deferred, and the notes for building it are in §J.5.
- *Dated terms.* Pricing and status lines carry their source dates.

**Contested ground:** (1) Human memory-reconsolidation evidence is mixed (§B rows 8 to 12). (2) The attachment-to-God research is correlational. (3) "Scripture first" is true of the architecture and of authority, and partly retrospective for some tools. (4) The "conditions, not causes" frame sits on a live monergist and means-of-grace fault line. (5) The way of reading ("in its own setting and in the whole of Scripture, read in the light of Christ") is widely shared and not commanded by a text, and the Spirit's quickening is held without any technique for it.

**Client summary:** Not client-facing. Source document for Andrew's preparation and for derivative pieces. The derivatives, not this document, are what audiences see.
