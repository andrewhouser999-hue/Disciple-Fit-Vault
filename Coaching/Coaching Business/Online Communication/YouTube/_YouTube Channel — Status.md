## Retention Mechanics — What Actually Keeps Someone on the Channel

*Andrew's working theory, 2026-09-17: a strong first video is the hook; a deep content bench keeps a viewer on the channel until they've extracted what they came for; a shallow bench gets sampled once and the algorithm moves the viewer on. Directionally correct, with one guardrail-relevant correction below.*

- **Hook video → session affinity is real.** One video that names a viewer's exact experience with unusual precision (Trust-Builder #1, Marketing Charter) converts a stranger into a channel visitor who will sample more.
- **Deep bench → binge → satisfaction → move on is real, and it's the correct target.** A viewer who finds five or six more videos that each name a different angle of their own experience will stay until satisfied, then leave for another channel — that is a complete, healthy use of the content, not churn to prevent.
- **News/prophecy-update channels retain longest for a reason that doesn't transfer here.** That retention pattern runs on an anxiety-incompleteness loop — the story never resolves, so the viewer never stops checking. That directly violates the Marketing Charter's "no urgency or scarcity language" guardrail and its "pressure registers as manipulation" trust-killer. **Do not reach for serialized urgency as a retention mechanic, even though it is the single most powerful one on the platform.** The ethical substitute is serialized *depth*, not serialized *incompleteness* — see Recurring Format below.

## Content Architecture — Pillar Videos vs. Deep-Bench Fill

Two different jobs, planned separately so the channel doesn't end up all-hook or all-filler.

**Pillar videos** — cornerstone, high-production, built once and re-referenced. Carry the channel's identity and do the heaviest trust-building. Low volume, high durability.
- Video 01 (*The Mind You've Never Renewed* adaptation) — Stage 2–3
- TED Talk adaptation (script already exists — `Communication/Public Speaking/The Mind You've Never Renewed — TED Talk.md`) — Stage 2–3, once video-adapted
- A future flagship on the institutional wound, once [[My Story]]'s rewrite clears legal review — Stage 4 (Root revealed); do not publish before that review completes

**Deep-bench fill content** — shorter, faster to produce, each one a single doorway. This is the bench the retention theory above depends on: a viewer who bounces off Video 01 should find enough of these to stay satisfied for several sessions.
- The website's existing 8 recognition doors (#2, #3, #4, #6, #7, #10 — see [[CIC Website — Homepage Copy]]) — each is a ready-made video title/hook, Stage 2
- The new Failed-Solution Recognition doorways (F1–F4, added 2026-09-18 to [[Client Problem & Cost Language]]: therapy plateaued, coaching missed the deeper pattern, pastoral wisdom didn't hold, broad Christian content didn't transfer) — each is also a ready-made cold open, Stage 2, with a higher institutional-critique guardrail risk than the felt-cost doors — read the standing guardrail note on that section before scripting any of them
- The remaining ~40 unused Common Experience statements across Problems #1–17 — the same raw material already earmarked for Shorts (per [[_Shorts Channel — Status]]) — usable as long-form deep-bench fill too, not just Shorts

**Recurring format — the ethical substitute for a news/prophecy loop:** a periodic segment revisiting one mechanism (Willard's VIM, Winnicott's false self, Kegan & Lahey's competing commitments — all already sourced in [[Client Problem & Cost Language]]) through a new doorway each time. This gives the channel a "there's always something new here" pull without manufacturing urgency or incompleteness — the viewer returns for depth, not because something is left unresolved. Not yet built; first candidate once Video 01 publishes and a second pillar is chosen.

## Market Context — Content Flood

Andrew's read, 2026-09-17: the space is flooding, low-effort scalped/reaction content is rising, and the bar for likability and delivery quality keeps climbing. Assessment: correct, and it is not a threat to this channel's strategy — it is the argument for it. Scalped content has zero cost of authorship and cannot carry integrated testimony or precision of language (Trust-Builders #1 and #4, Marketing Charter) — the two things this channel is already built to lead with. **Do not respond to flood pressure by increasing volume or chasing trend formats.** Compete on precision and peer proof, which are slow to produce and cannot be copied by a reaction channel.
