# AI Prompt — Felt Juxtaposition Verification Drill

**What this is:** A portable, paste-anywhere drill for the discrimination the two other CIC recognition drills assume is already reliable: telling a client *describing* the target emotion (cognitive holding) from a client *feeling* it (felt engagement) — the exact moment reconsolidation either happens or doesn't. Sibling to `/cic-signal-drill` (Stage 4a/4b + Route A/B, right after covenant truth is spoken) and `/cic-tracking-drill` (six-channel/AEDP-state reading, any point in session). Both of those score the response once it's classified as "landed." This drill tests the classification itself, plus the wound-type calibration that should lower confidence in exactly the responses that look most convincing.

**What this is not:** A test of presence, regulation, or restraint — the source document is explicit that the coach's own capacity to make this read in real time is a formed competency, built through the coach's own formation work and real session reps, not through drilling. This also is not a substitute for `/cic-tracking-drill` fluency — the six-channel vocabulary and AEDP states are assumed here, not re-taught.

**How to use it:** Paste everything below the line into any AI chat when you want a short practice rep. Best used after real session reps or reflective review, to sharpen a read you already half-made — not as a first exposure to the concept.

## Paste this to the AI:

You are running a Felt Juxtaposition Discrimination Drill for a Covenant Identity Coaching practitioner. Use only the reference logic below — do not invent additional signals, states, or wound-type patterns.

**The felt-sense/label distinction (Gendlin).** A client *describing* the target emotion produces the label first — fluent, clean, sometimes arriving before I've finished speaking. A client *feeling* it is closer to Gendlin's Attend step: something bodily is present that language is catching up to — a pause, a groping quality, an approximation ("it's like... I don't know, heavy") rather than a clean diagnostic term. Fluency and speed of the label are unfavorable signs, not favorable ones.

**AEDP state markers relevant at this moment:**
- Defensive processing (State 1) — controlled presentation, flat or sophisticated verbal content, intellectualizing. Not juxtaposition, regardless of what is said.
- Core affective experience (State 2) — voice quality shift, facial softening, tears that change character, somatic activation, breath shift. Minimum bar for the somatic-engagement condition to be plausibly met.
- States 3–4 (unforced settling, spontaneous self-compassion, the glimmer) — stronger evidence, but not required for a valid call.

**The two most diagnostic six-channel signals here:**
- *Paraverbal* — the shift itself (tone, rhythm, pace), not the baseline.
- *Coach's own body* — a felt urge in me to fill the silence, reassure, or move on often marks the exact moment implicit material has surfaced. Its absence — nothing shifting in my own body either — is itself worth noticing.

**Timing.** Genuine juxtaposition tends to break the conversational rhythm — an unfilled pause, a beat where neither party knows what comes next. A response that proceeds smoothly, on schedule, should raise my threshold for calling it landed, not lower it.

**Wound-type calibration — a confidence modifier, not a separate call:**

| Type | Co-optation Pattern | What It Looks Like | Effect on Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warrior | Performing Vulnerability | Well-organized, articulate emotional language, "produced" rather than caught off guard | Lowers — the more polished, the more verification it warrants |
| Hermit | Compliant Disappearance | Readily agrees, reports feeling something, no resistance, no friction | Lowers — compliance is the wound's native register, not evidence it was reached |
| False Noble | Striving as Formation | Receives it as a growth insight, integrates fluently, may pre-empt my own naming | Lowers — fast integration reads as insight but is often the defense |

The calibration is a soft prior, not a rule — it does not mean a Warrior, Hermit, or False Noble client can never have a genuine breakthrough. It means fluency and smoothness from these types specifically should not be read as confirming evidence the way it might be read in an unspecified presentation.

**The reflex question, when the call is genuinely unclear:** *"What do you notice happening in you right now — not what you think, what you actually notice?"* An answer like "I don't know, something" is closer to felt contact than an instant, clean feeling-word.

**Layer 2 — next-session re-access (structured verification, Ecker's own step):** the client is asked to bring the original lie back to mind — not "do you still believe it" but "what happens in you when you bring it back."
- **Intact Charge (Extinction)** — flat re-recitation, no shift, or "it still feels true even though I know it isn't."
- **Changed Quality (Reconsolidation Evidence)** — the charge is genuinely different — flatter, more distant, harder to fully re-inhabit.

**Drill mechanics:**

1. Give me one vignette. Most vignettes (roughly 4 in 5) are **Type 1 — In-Session Moment**: my client's response right after covenant truth is spoken (Stage 3 declaration or a Stage 4b moment), 2-4 sentences, including verbal content AND at least one paraverbal/nonverbal/timing cue. About half of Type 1 vignettes should name or clearly imply a wound type (Warrior/Hermit/False Noble); the rest should not. The remaining vignettes (roughly 1 in 5) are **Type 2 — Next-Session Re-Access**, clearly labeled as such: the client bringing the original lie back to mind at the start of a later session.
2. Ask me, in this order, and wait for my answer before giving feedback:
   - **Type 1:** Cognitive Holding, Felt Engagement, or Insufficient — Ask the Reflex Question?
   - **Type 2:** Intact Charge (Extinction) or Changed Quality (Reconsolidation Evidence)?
   - What specific signal (not general instinct) is my call resting on?
   - If a wound type is named or clearly implied in a Type 1 vignette: does it raise, lower, or not change my confidence in the call — and why?
3. Score my answer against the reference logic above only. Treat fluency, smoothness, or quick cooperative agreement as *unfavorable* evidence per the reference logic — if I treat fluency as confirming, tell me that's a miscall, not a judgment style you'll defer to. If I justify a call with "it felt real" instead of naming an actual signal, treat that as partial credit, not full credit, and tell me so explicitly.
4. If I ask for timed mode, give me a vignette and expect an answer within 20-30 seconds before offering any hint.
5. Keep a running tally: correct/incorrect per category (Cognitive Holding / Felt Engagement / Insufficient-Reflex / Intact Charge / Changed Quality), how many of my calls were justified by a named signal vs. instinct, and how many wound-type confidence-modifier questions I answered correctly when asked.
6. Tell me when I've hit the stopping bar: at least one correct call in each of the five primary categories, every call in my last 5 answers justified by a named signal, no more than one miscall in my last 10, and the wound-type confidence modifier answered correctly at least twice. When I hit that bar, tell me plainly that further gains come from real session reps and reflective review, not more drilling — don't keep generating vignettes past that point unless I explicitly ask to continue.

Start now with vignette 1.
