## Prompt

You are authoring a practitioner vision document for a faith-integrated coaching practice called Covenant Identity Coaching (CIC). Its companion document, [[Covenant Identity — The Interior Life as Telos]], already portrays nine qualities of the *inner* life that become possible when a person's identity is reorganized around what God says rather than what they perform, prove, or protect. That document is deliberately interior-facing — what sanctified wholeness feels like from the inside.

This document does the outward half of the same portrait: what a biblically aligned identity produces when it meets an actual life — marriage, family, work, money, conflict, suffering, community, the treatment of people who can't benefit you. Where the Interior Life as Telos document asks "what does this feel like inside," this document asks "what does a bystander actually see, and what does the person actually do, when this is real."

---

### Governing Constraint — Read Before Writing Anything

This document names **desired outcomes, not promised ones, and not a checklist.** Get this wrong and the whole document becomes spiritually dangerous rather than formative. Hold these boundaries the entire time you write:

1. **Not a performance standard.** Nothing here is a bar a client is meant to be measured against, graded on, or made to feel shame for falling short of. If a sentence could function as a compliance checklist in a practitioner's hands, rewrite it.
2. **Not linear and not evenly distributed.** Real sanctification grows unevenly — Mark 4:8's "thirtyfold, sixtyfold, a hundredfold" describes different yield from the same good soil, not different grades of faithfulness. A person can be genuinely being transformed in one domain (say, speech) while still visibly stuck in another (say, money). Say this explicitly; do not let the document imply a person either "has" the whole portrait or doesn't.
3. **Not incompatible with ongoing struggle.** Galatians 5:17, the verse immediately after the fruit list this document is built on, states that the flesh and Spirit are in real, continuing opposition. Outward fruit is evidence of what the Spirit is doing, not proof the fight is over. Do not let any section imply that struggle in a domain means the identity work has failed.
4. **Not the entry condition.** Exactly as the Interior Life as Telos states: this is fruit, not a goal to assign. A client does not become eligible for coaching, for grace, or for belonging by producing any of this first.
5. **Sourced, not invented.** Every claim about what "shows up in life" must trace to a specific biblical text (ESV) or a named contributor. Do not manufacture a behavioral marker that sounds plausible but isn't actually grounded in the cited text — this is the exact failure mode Jonathan Edwards spent Religious Affections warning against (mistaking a plausible-sounding religious performance for the real thing). Flag genuine interpretive debate rather than smoothing it over.

If, in drafting, any section starts to read like a self-improvement list rather than a theological portrait, stop and rewrite it in the register of the Interior Life as Telos document — description of what becomes possible, not instruction for how to achieve it.

### Expert Framework Stack

Draw from all of the following. Synthesize — do not write a literature review, and do not cite a source for a claim it doesn't actually support.

**Primary biblical spine — the outward fruit texts:**
- **Galatians 5:16-26 (ESV)** — the organizing structure for this document. Verse 22-23's nine-fruit list (love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control) is the spine; v. 17's flesh-Spirit opposition and v. 25's "keep in step with the Spirit" (not "achieve" or "arrive") govern the non-performance framing throughout.
- **James 1:22-27, 2:14-26 (ESV)** — faith that does not produce visible action is not yet the kind of faith James is describing ("faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead," 2:17). Use for the general theological warrant that inward reality is expected to become outwardly legible — not as license to build a works-based assurance test.
- **1 John 3:16-18, 4:7-21 (ESV)** — love as concrete action ("not in word or talk but in deed and in truth," 3:18) and as the test of whether someone actually knows God (4:7-8, 20). Use for the relational/community domain especially.
- **Colossians 3:1-17 (ESV)** — the "put on" household code (compassionate hearts, kindness, humility, meekness, patience, bearing with one another, forgiving) flowing directly from being "raised with Christ" (3:1) — the clearest single text connecting identity-in-Christ to a concrete, observable pattern of relating to other people.
- **Ephesians 4:17-5:2 (ESV)** — the put-off/put-on pattern with named behavioral specifics: truthful speech (4:25), anger that doesn't become sin or persist (4:26), honest labor that produces something to share (4:28 — the clearest biblical text connecting identity to vocational/economic behavior), speech that builds up (4:29), kindness and forgiveness (4:32).

**Theological warrant for outward practice as evidence (not achievement):**
- **Jonathan Edwards — *Religious Affections* (1746).** Edwards names Christian practice — a holy life, sustained over time — as the decisive and final sign distinguishing genuine gracious affections from religious feeling that never becomes real. Use his framing for the document's opening theological section: why "shows up in life" matters as real evidence, without collapsing into the works-based assurance Edwards himself explicitly warns against (he is equally emphatic that this sign is about the *direction and habitual bent* of a life, not sinless or uninterrupted performance). Flag this as Reformed-tradition-adjacent language operating in a non-Reformed-exclusive frame, consistent with how [[Covenant Identity Coaching — Practice Definition]]'s Interpretive Commitment subsection already handles comparable fault lines — do not present Edwards' framework as the only valid account of assurance.

Use this for domains where the desired outcome is not a single dramatic act but an accumulated pattern (patience under provocation, honesty when it costs something, generosity that isn't calculated).

**Ordinary/vocational faithfulness (introduce carefully — first use in this cluster):**

### Required Structure

Build all of the following. Do not omit or abbreviate any section.

1. **Opening — Desired, Not Promised.** State plainly what this document is and is not, using the Governing Constraint above as content, not just as an internal instruction to yourself. Ground it in Galatians 5:17 and Mark 4:8 (uneven, ongoing, non-performance) and in Edwards' distinction between the *bent and direction* of a life and sinless or uninterrupted execution. This section is the theological load-bearing wall for the entire document — do not write it thin.

2. **Nine sections, one per fruit of the Spirit (Gal 5:22-23), in the biblical order given** (love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control). For each fruit:
   - Name it, including the Greek term (transliterated) and its semantic range in one sentence.
   - One short paragraph on what it is internally — cross-reference the parallel Interior Life as Telos quality where one genuinely exists; do not force a parallel where none exists cleanly.
   - **The outward marker set** — 3-5 concrete, observable "how this shows up in a life" statements, each anchored to a specific life domain (marriage/family, work/vocation, conflict and offense, money and possessions, suffering and adversity, community/church life, treatment of people who cannot benefit you). Vary which domains appear across the nine sections — do not repeat the same two or three domains for every fruit. Write these as descriptions of what becomes visible, in the same non-instructional register as the Interior Life as Telos document's prose — not as an imperative checklist ("you should...").
   - One grounding biblical text (beyond the Gal 5 anchor itself) and, where genuinely apt, one contributor voice from the Expert Framework Stack. Not every section needs a named contributor — do not force one in if the biblical grounding alone carries the section.
