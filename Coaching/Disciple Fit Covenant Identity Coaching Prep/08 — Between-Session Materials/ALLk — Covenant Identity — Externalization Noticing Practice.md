# Covenant Identity — Externalization Noticing Practice
*Practitioner reference with embedded client-facing practice guides.*

---

## How to Use This Document

1. **Name the pattern in session before assigning anything.** The client needs to recognize externalization as something they actually do, not a category they intellectually agree could apply to someone. Use [[Covenant Identity — Externalization of the Christian Life — Diagnostic & Formation Reference]]'s Diagnostic Question Bank to get there before handing this document over.
2. **This is a noticing practice, not a self-assessment instrument. Do not turn it into one.** There is no scoring, no checklist, no list of "signs" for the client to check off against themselves. A client working through this pattern is, by definition, prone to performing correctly for an audience — including an audience of one, themselves, with a worksheet. A gradeable instrument handed to this specific client is the pattern in a new costume. Every practice below is a single open question, held loosely, brought back into the room verbally — never submitted as a completed form.
3. **Address the discovery gently.** Most clients feel some grief or embarrassment when they first notice this in themselves — normalize it immediately. Externalization is close to universal and usually formed by good intentions (a church culture that rewarded visible output, a genuine desire to be faithful) rather than by bad faith. This is not a confession of hypocrisy; it is an ordinary formation gap.
4. **Select the phase-appropriate practice.** Phase 1 is observational only. Phase 2 deepens the noticing without yet asking for change. Phase 3 introduces the abiding practice as the actual mechanism, once [[Covenant Identity — John 15 Abide Formation Posture Reference]] has been given in session. Phase 4 sustains what has shifted.
5. **Watch for the practice itself becoming performed.** If the client reports the noticing practice with the same polished, well-organized quality named as a signal in the practitioner reference, say so in session. Performing the noticing practice well is not the same as the noticing practice working.

## Phase 1 — Noticing

*Observational only. No change is being asked for yet.*

**Client-facing:**

Once this week, notice one moment where you did something "Christian" — prayed, served, read Scripture, showed up somewhere — and ask yourself one question afterward, honestly:

> *Was that alive, or was that performed?*

Don't judge the answer. Don't try to make it come out a certain way. Just notice, in one sentence, what you find. Bring it to session — don't write more than a sentence, and don't do this more than once this week.

---

## Phase 2 — Deepening Without Fixing

**Practitioner note:** The client may want to immediately correct what they notice. Resist assigning a fix at this stage — the goal is still accurate noticing, not yet change. Premature correction re-externalizes the very insight the noticing practice just produced.

**Client-facing:**

**1. The honest audience question — once**

Think of one thing you regularly do for God — a discipline, a service, a role. Ask: *If no one else ever knew I did this, would I still do it the same way?* Sit with whatever answer comes, including "I don't know." Bring one sentence to session — not an essay, not a resolution.

**2. One unmanaged moment**

This week, if something comes up in prayer or Scripture that's unresolved, messy, or doesn't produce a tidy conclusion — let it stay that way. Don't polish it into a lesson before bringing it to session. Bring the mess, not the takeaway.

---

## Phase 3 — Abiding, Not Straining

**Precondition:** [[Covenant Identity — John 15 Abide Formation Posture Reference]] must be given in session before this practice is assigned. The client needs the abide/strive distinction in view — this practice only makes sense in light of it.

**Practitioner note:** This is the primary mechanism, not a supplementary exercise. The temptation for a client working this pattern is to turn "abide" into a new technique to perform correctly — watch for that explicitly and name it if it appears (see the practitioner reference's Error 3).

**Client-facing:**

**1. Before, not during — daily, one sentence**

Before you do the "Christian" activity you noticed in Phase 1 (prayer, service, reading, whatever it was), pause and say one sentence, out loud or silently:

> *I don't have to produce anything here. I'm just staying connected.*

Then do the activity as you normally would. You are not trying to feel a certain way during it. You are naming, before it starts, what it actually is.

**2. Belovedness anchor — daily**

> *I am loved before I do anything. Nothing I do today adds to that or subtracts from it.* (Romans 8:15-16, ESV)

Speak it once, in the morning, before any activity — not as a mood-setter, as a fact stated ahead of the day's performance pressure.

**3. Weekly return to John 15**

Once per week, read John 15:4-5 slowly. One question only: *What in my week this week was fruit, and what was strain?* Don't sort every activity — just notice one example of each if you can find them. Bring both to session.

---

## Phase 4 — Sustainability

**Client-facing:**

**Monthly, not weekly, check-in:** Once a month, ask the honest audience question again (Phase 2, practice 1) for whatever your current primary "Christian" activity is. This is not a re-audit of your whole spiritual life — one activity, one honest answer, brought to session if anything shifted.

**When the old pattern resurfaces** (it will, especially under praise, criticism, or a new role): Say it directly to God rather than managing it alone — *"I notice I'm performing this right now, not abiding in it. I don't want to fix that myself — I want to come back to You."* This is itself an abiding move, not a failure of the practice.

## Load-Bearing vs. Modifiable

**Load-bearing:**
- **No scoring, no checklist, ever.** This is the single non-negotiable design constraint on this document. Any future revision that adds a self-rating scale or a multi-item checklist has recreated the failure mode this practice was built to avoid.
- **Pattern named in session before assignment.** Same precondition discipline as every other between-session document in this library.
- **Phase 3 requires John 15 Abide given first.** The abiding practices are meaningless, and risk becoming a new performance standard, without that frame already in place.

**Modifiable:**
- Specific wording of the noticing questions and anchor sentences — these are working language, not fixed scripts
- Frequency of the Phase 4 check-in — monthly is a starting point, not a rule
