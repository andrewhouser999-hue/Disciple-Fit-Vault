# Covenant Identity — Diagnostic Calibration Log
*A running record of Stage 4a/4b and Route A/B calls, closed out against what later sessions actually show — so diagnostic judgment gets checked against evidence instead of running on unaudited confidence*

---

## What This Is Not

**Not the Self-Supervision Template.** That document tracks the coach's own countertransference, avoidance, and presence quality — what entered the room from the coach's side. This log tracks a narrower and more specific thing: was the Stage 4a-vs-4b call (and, where applicable, the Route A-vs-B call) correct? Use both — they answer different questions. A coach can have excellent presence and still be systematically misreading Stage 4a for Stage 4b, or the reverse; nothing in the Self-Supervision Template would surface that, because it isn't looking for it.

**Not the Implicit Signal Recognition Quick Reference or the Fluency/Signal Recognition Drills.** Those build and sharpen recognition speed — the Quick Reference condenses signals for fast post-session scanning, and the drills (AI Prompt — Diagnostic Sequence Fluency Drill; AI Prompt — Signal Recognition Drill) train recognition on constructed vignettes. Both are rehearsal on hypothetical or fast-recall material. This log is the opposite input: real calls, on real clients, checked against what actually happened next. Drills build the reflex; this log tells you whether the reflex is accurate.

**Not a real-time tool.** Entries are made post-session, within the same 24-hour window as the Self-Supervision Template. Never consult this log, or think about how an entry will read, while in session — that reintroduces the exact self-consciousness the Stage 4b document's Failure Mode Guards warn against.

---

## Why This Exists

The Diagnostic Lens Transition Logic names two specific, costly category errors: applying Stage 4a (CBT) tools to a Stage 4b (implicit) presentation deepens a Defectiveness/Shame pattern by teaching the client their inability to feel the truth is a personal failure; applying Stage 4b (relational presence) to a Stage 4a (active cognitive resistance) presentation leaves the distortion undisturbed and can read to a Warrior client as the coach backing down. Both errors are named clearly in that document — but naming an error in the abstract does not tell a coach whether *they specifically* are making it, how often, or in which direction.

Kahneman and Klein's research on intuitive expertise is direct on this point: confident judgment is only trustworthy in domains that provide regular, valid, and reasonably prompt feedback on whether the judgment was right. Coaching sessions are private, spaced a week apart, and slow-moving — exactly the conditions Kahneman and Klein identify as producing *unearned* confidence, because the natural feedback loop is too sparse and too delayed to correct a systematic bias on its own. This log exists to manufacture the feedback loop the work doesn't naturally provide: a place to write down the call at the time it was made, and come back to it once later sessions confirm or contradict it.

---

## When to Log an Entry

Any session where a Stage 3 → 4a or Stage 3 → 4b transition was called, or where the Stage 4b Route A/B fork was read — whether or not you deployed the corresponding tools. A borderline case you talked yourself out of calling is still worth logging; those are often the most informative entries, because "I saw the signal and didn't act on it" is itself a calibration data point.

## When to Close an Entry Out

Not immediately — that's the point. Return to open entries when a later session with the same client provides evidence one way or the other:

- **Confirms Stage 4a:** the blocking belief loosened with CBT tools, and covenant truth landed on return to Stage 3.
- **Disconfirms Stage 4a → reveals 4b:** CBT tools produced no movement across multiple attempts; the client kept intellectually agreeing with the reframe while nothing shifted felt-wise. In hindsight, the "I know it in my head but not my heart" signal was probably already present at the original call and was missed or outweighed by something else.
- **Confirms Stage 4b:** relational presence, held across sessions, eventually produced a transformation marker (the glimmer, physical stilling, tears shifting from managed to authentic).
- **Disconfirms Stage 4b → reveals 4a:** what looked like "can't feel it" turned out, on closer listening, to be a well-defended argument the client wasn't voicing directly — active resistance wearing the language of sincere agreement.
- **Confirms or disconfirms Route A/B:** did the material that surfaced turn out to be relationally enacted with the coach (Route A) or genuinely interior and non-relational (Route B), regardless of which route was initially read?

A call can also simply stay open for several sessions with no clear confirming or disconfirming evidence yet — leave it open rather than forcing a verdict.

**Column notes:**
- **Client:** use the client code convention already in use elsewhere (e.g., "SC-00"), not full names — this log will accumulate cross-client data and should not become a second confidentiality exposure point.
- **Signal(s) driving the call:** name the specific observable, not the conclusion — "flat affect, rapid topic shift on covenant truth" not "seemed defended." Citing the Quick Reference channel number (e.g., "Channel 2 — paraverbal shift") keeps this fast and keeps the vocabulary consistent across entries.
- **Verdict:** Confirmed / Revised / Still Open. Use "Revised" rather than "Wrong" — the point is what the miscall teaches, not scoring the coach.

## Worked Examples

| Date | Client | Context | Signal(s) | Call made | Confidence | Outcome that session | Later evidence | Verdict | What would have sharpened the read |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-06-02 | JR-01 | Covenant truth re: identity as beloved son spoken after false identity named (performance-based worth) | Client said "yeah, I know that's true" flatly, no pushback, topic drifted within a minute (Channel 1, Channel 5) | Stage 4b | Medium | Slowed session, invited internal attention; client noticed numbness, named it | Session 5: still numb after 3 sessions of relational presence work; separately, client mentioned in passing "my dad would've said the same thing and not meant it" — an argument, not an absence of feeling | Revised → was actually Stage 4a (distrust-of-the-source distortion) wearing 4b's clothing | The Channel 1 "flat agreement" signal was present, but I didn't check for a *specific counter-belief* underneath the flatness — flat affect can mask active distrust as easily as it can mask a genuine implicit block. Next time: ask directly, "what makes it hard to take that in," rather than assuming absence of felt response means absence of an argument. |
| 2026-06-16 | JR-01 | Same identity material, re-approached at Stage 4a with the distrust belief now named directly | Client engaged the Socratic questions, evidence surfaced ("every time someone said something good about me it was because they wanted something"), belief loosened visibly | Stage 4a | High | Covenant truth returned to at Stage 3; genuine felt shift, tears (managed → authentic quality per Channel 3) | Session 6: truth held, client referenced it unprompted the following week | Confirmed | — |

*These two entries are a single arc: a miscall at session 3, corrected at session 4 once the log forced a return to the original call instead of letting it quietly disappear. This is the pattern the log is built to produce — not zero miscalls, but miscalls that get caught and fed back into the next read.*

## Periodic Rollup

Review every 10–15 entries, or roughly quarterly. This is where the log pays for itself — a single miscall is a data point; a pattern across ten is a bias worth naming.

**Tally:**

| | Confirmed | Revised | Still Open |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stage 4a called | | | |
| Stage 4b called | | | |
| Route A called | | | |
| Route B called | | | |

**Questions to ask of the tally:**

- Is there a directional bias — do misses run mostly one way (e.g., calling 4b when it was actually 4a, more often than the reverse)? The Diagnostic Lens Transition Logic's default-to-Route-A-when-unclear rule exists because missing an enactment is the costlier error there; check whether your own miscall pattern matches that logic or cuts against it.
- Do "Revised" entries cluster around a particular wound type, God-representation type, or client presentation? A coach whose miscalls concentrate on, say, False Noble clients may be reading their externalized competence as cognitive engagement (Stage 4a) when it's actually a well-defended implicit block (Stage 4b).
- Is confidence tracking with accuracy? High-confidence Revised entries are the most valuable single data point in this log — they mark exactly where intuition is running ahead of what it can actually support, which is the specific failure mode Kahneman and Klein's research warns feedback-free judgment produces.

**Bring the rollup, not individual entries, to peer consultation or supervision** (`/cic-l6`) — the pattern is the useful unit, not any single case.

## Connection Map

**↔ Covenant Identity — Self-Supervision Template**
Sibling document, not a substitute. Complete both after a session where a diagnostic call was made — the Self-Supervision Template asks what the session did in the coach; this log asks whether the coach's read was right. A "Supervision or Consultation Flag" entry in the Self-Supervision Template and a "Still Open, Low Confidence" entry here often point at the same session — check both when something felt uncertain.

**← Covenant Identity — Implicit Signal Recognition Quick Reference**
What moves in: the six-channel signal vocabulary (Verbal, Paraverbal, Nonverbal, Coach's Own Body, Timing, Enactment), reused directly in this log's "Signal(s)" column so entries stay fast to write and comparable across sessions.

**← Covenant Identity — Diagnostic Lens Transition Logic**
What moves in: the specific category errors ("What breaks if Stage 4a and 4b are conflated") this log is designed to catch empirically in the coach's own practice, rather than leaving them as an abstract caution read once and not revisited.

**← Covenant Identity — Route B Experiencing Depth Rating**
What moves in: for Route B calls specifically, this instrument's session-by-session Level rating is the intended source for the "Later evidence" column — a Level 4–5 trend confirms the call, a persistent Level 1–2 trend is grounds to revise it.

**→ /cic-l6 (Supervision Mode)**
What moves out: the periodic rollup is the natural input to a supervision-mode session — aggregate accuracy and bias data, not a session-by-session narrative.
