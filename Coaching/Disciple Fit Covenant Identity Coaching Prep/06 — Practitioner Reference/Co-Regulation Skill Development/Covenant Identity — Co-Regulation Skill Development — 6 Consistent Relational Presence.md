# Covenant Identity — Co-Regulation Skill Development — 6 Consistent Relational Presence

*Part 6 of 7. See [[Covenant Identity — Co-Regulation Skill Development — Index]] for the full set. Unlike Documents 1–5, this is a practice-design discipline rather than a moment-to-moment interpersonal skill — it can be built any time, independent of the others.*

## Target Capacity

Structural, behavioral reliability across sessions: the same greeting ritual, the same session structure, dependable time boundaries, and visible follow-through on what was said last session. This is not a charisma or warmth skill — it is a systems discipline. Attachment research ties predictability specifically (not warmth intensity) to the formation of implicit trust; a single strong session cannot substitute for an accumulated track record of showing up the same way.

## Why This Matters in CIC

[[Covenant Identity — Practitioner Competency Framework]] and [[Exceptional Coaching Practice — What Is Missed, Meaningful & Important]] Section XXI (Tracking the Arc) both depend on this being in place — you cannot track a client's arc across sessions if the sessions themselves aren't structurally consistent enough to compare. This also directly supports the holding-environment concept already used elsewhere in CIC's Kegan & Lahey-sourced material: a holding environment requires the *container* to be stable even while the *content* inside it changes session to session.

## Mastery Requirements

- A repeatable session structure that doesn't depend on your mood or energy that day
- Reliable time-boundary behavior (starting and ending on time, communicated in advance if anything changes)
- A system for actually remembering and following through on what was said last session — not relying on memory alone

## Training Protocol

**Phase 1 — Define the fixed structure (solo, one-time build).**
Write out your own session ritual: the specific opening (what you say or do in the first minute, every time), the specific closing (per [[Exceptional Coaching Practice — What Is Missed, Meaningful & Important]] Section XXIV's ending-well guidance), and what happens between them at a structural level (not content — structure). This becomes your default; deviate deliberately, not by drift.

**Phase 2 — Build the pre-session review habit (solo, becomes ongoing once live).**
Before every session, review the prior session's notes and the client's full arc, not just the last entry — this is already named in [[Exceptional Coaching Practice — What Is Missed, Meaningful & Important]] Section XIX. Practice this now with any real or role-play conversation history you have, so the habit is automatic before it matters with a paying client.

**Phase 3 — Follow-through tracking system (solo, one-time build).**
Build a simple, checkable system (even a single running document per client) for what was committed to in each session — by the client and by you — so nothing depends on memory. Test it on a mock client scenario before using it live.

**Phase 4 — Boundary-holding rehearsal (solo, scenario-based).**
Rehearse, out loud, how you will hold a time boundary when a session runs long and a client is mid-disclosure — this is a common real failure point (over-running erodes the very predictability this document is about). Script your actual language for it in advance rather than improvising under pressure.

## Solo-Safe vs. Requires Supervision

All four phases are solo-safe — this entire document is buildable now, with no dependency on a corrector or live client contact. It's the one document in this set with no gating constraint.

## Self-Assessment Markers

- You can describe your own session structure in one sentence, from memory, without checking notes
- Your Phase 3 follow-through system has actually been tested against a real or simulated case and caught something you would have forgotten
- You have a rehearsed, specific sentence ready for holding a time boundary — not just an intention to "handle it when it comes up"

## Common Failure Modes

- **Mistaking rigidity for consistency** — the structure should be stable at the level of ritual and boundary, not so mechanical that it can't flex to what a specific session needs; predictability is about the container, not scripting the content
- **Letting follow-through depend on memory** — this is the most common quiet failure; a client who mentions something in session 2 that never gets referenced again experiences it as not having been heard, even if it was simply forgotten
- **Time-boundary erosion** — allowing "just five more minutes" to become routine undermines the exact reliability this document trains; the discipline is holding the boundary consistently, with warmth, not rigidly without warmth

## Integration

The Phase 3 follow-through system is a natural companion to [[Covenant Identity — Session Growth Notes Template]] and [[Covenant Identity — Diagnostic Calibration Log]] — build it to feed the same per-client record rather than as a separate standalone system.
