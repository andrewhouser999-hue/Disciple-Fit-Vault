## CIC-Internal Terms

#### False Covenant Object

**Also called:** functional substitute, what the heart actually trusts, the broken cistern (Jer. 2:13 frame)
The functional source of security, significance, or worth that a person's heart actually trusts and organizes around, in the place God is meant to occupy as covenant partner. Formed when a character wound (betrayal, terror, or ambivalence) damages the heart's ability to trust God as a reliable covenant partner for a specific need, causing the heart to trust something else instead — competence, approval, control, financial security, and belonging are the recurring examples in CIC practice. Named in Theory of Change Node 1 (entry) and made diagnostically precise in Node 2; surfaced in-session via Layer 4 of the Affections Discovery Process (the God-Exclusion Map).
**See also:** [[Covenant Identity — False Covenant Objects & False Identity Structures — Terms Reference]] · [[Covenant Identity Coaching — Theory of Change]] · [[Covenant Identity — Disordered Affections and the Diagnostic Entry — Mechanism]] · [[Covenant Identity — God-Exclusion Map]] · False Identity Structure

---

#### False Identity Structure

**Also called:** the false self, the protecting system, the organizing self-understanding
The behavioral and relational system a person builds to protect and serve their false covenant object. Not random — coherent and predictable once the false covenant object is known, because every part of it exists to secure or defend that object. Named with precision in Theory of Change Node 2; surfaced in-session via Layer 5 of the Affections Discovery Process (Identity Revelation) and cross-checked against wound type in the Character Wound Diagnostic Tool.
**See also:** [[Covenant Identity — False Covenant Objects & False Identity Structures — Terms Reference]] · [[Covenant Identity Coaching — Theory of Change]] · [[Covenant Identity — Disordered Affections and the Diagnostic Entry — Mechanism]] · [[Covenant Identity — Character Wound Diagnostic Tool]] · False Covenant Object

---

#### Implicit Process (Implicit Level)

**Also called:** the implicit level, the implicit architecture, the register beneath explicit belief
Not a fifth item alongside character wound, disordered affections, false covenant object, and false identity structure — the procedural, felt, pre-verbal *register* on which all four of those actually run, beneath conscious/propositional awareness (Curt Thompson/Siegel's implicit vs. explicit memory distinction). A wound, its affections, the false covenant object it produces, and the false identity structure built to protect that object are all implicit-level content; "implicit" names the plane they operate on, not a separate thing they produce. This is why a client can hold correct explicit theology while the implicit process still runs the old wound → affections → object → identity chain under pressure — the gap "I know it in my head but not my heart" describes. **Misdiagnosis pitfall:** treating "implicit process" itself as the diagnostic target — rather than the level at which the four content terms operate — is a category error; it names the register instead of reaching the organizing center running on it.

## Explanatory Terminology

#### Noetic

Relating to deep inner understanding that feels known without needing explanation; a knowing that comes from insight rather than logic. e.g. Some lessons become noetic only after you've lived through them.

---

#### Occhiolism

*oh-KEE-oh-liz-um*
The humbling realization that your view of reality is only a tiny window into something infinitely larger. e.g. Gazing into the stars, he felt occhiolism — a beautiful ache wrapped in wonder. The more we see.
**Wisdom:** "The more we realize how little we truly see."

---

#### Pernicious

Having a harmful effect, especially in a way that is subtle or gradual. e.g. The pernicious habit of procrastination slowly stole his dreams.
**Wisdom:** Just like poisons that don't kill instantly; they just change you quietly.

---

#### Nugatory

*NOO-guh-tor-ee*
Having little or no importance, value, or effect; ultimately pointless or insignificant. e.g. After years of chasing approval, he realized how many of his worries had been nugatory.

## Part I — Hebrew, Aramaic & Greek Terms

*Exegetical vocabulary cited across CIC's biblical-theological documents. Pronunciation guides are simplified, English-reader phonetic approximations (caps = stressed syllable) — not IPA and not identical to Modern Hebrew or Erasmian Greek pronunciation systems, though closest to standard seminary Erasmian/Biblical Hebrew academic convention. Alphabetized by transliteration.*

### Hebrew

**ʾabbîṭ (אַבִּיט)** — *ah-VEET* — Hiphil of *nbt*, "to gaze, regard attentively." Names God's covenantal, favorable attention toward the humble, contrite, word-trembling person — election-language, not neutral observation. Isaiah 66:2b.

**ʿānî (עָנִי)** — *ah-NEE* — "humble, poor, afflicted." The prophetic *anawim* — those without power or standing who depend wholly on God. Isaiah 61:1; Isaiah 66:2b.

**anaw (עָנָו)** — *ah-NAHV* — "meek, humble." Moses' meekness (Num. 12:3) — the fruit of identity processed through decades of anonymity, not a virtue achieved by discipline.

**anush (אָנוּשׁ)** — *ah-NOOSH* — "desperately sick, incurable." Describes the heart's self-deceiving, self-diagnosing incapacity — a structural claim about the heart's condition, not rhetorical exaggeration. Jeremiah 17:9.

**bara (בָּרָא)** — *bah-RAH* — "create" — the Genesis 1 verb, used in the Hebrew Bible only with God as its subject. David's plea for a new-creation act, not a repair of existing material. Psalm 51:10.

**berith (בְּרִית)** — *beh-REET* — "covenant, treaty, bond." A solemn, oath-bound, ceremonially ratified agreement; occurs roughly 285 times in the Old Testament. *Karat berith* — literally "to cut a covenant" — references the animal-cutting ratification ceremony (Gen. 15).

**emunah (אֱמוּנָה)** — *eh-moo-NAH* — "faithfulness, trustworthiness, reliability." Root of "amen." Names relational trustworthiness rather than mere cognitive assent — the Old Testament anchor behind the New Testament's *pistis* (Gen. 15:6, cited in Rom. 4:3).

**hagah (הָגָה)** — *hah-GAH* — "to mutter, moan, mull, ruminate." The biblical model of meditation: returning repeatedly to the same text rather than extracting a point and moving on — the image is an animal chewing cud. Psalm 1:2; Joshua 1:8.

**ḥārēd (חָרֵד)** — *khah-RAID* — "trembles," from a root meaning to shake or be seized with awe. Full phrase *ḥārēd ʿal-dĕbārî*, "trembles at my word" — a visceral, full-person response to the weight of God's word, not mild reverence. Isaiah 66:2b.

**hesed (חֶסֶד)** — *KHEH-sed* — "covenant loyalty, lovingkindness, steadfast love." Oath-bound covenant faithfulness, not temperamental affection. *Hesed ve-emet* — "lovingkindness and truth" — is a fixed word-pair (hendiadys) appearing at covenant-renewal moments. Exodus 34:6.

**kilyot (כִּלְיוֹת)** — *kil-YOTE* — "kidneys, inward parts" (KJV: "reins"). The seat of the deepest affections and moral conscience — the interior hidden even from the self. Psalm 16:7.

**lev / levav (לֵב / לֵבָב)** — *LAYV / lay-VAHV* — "heart." Not primarily the seat of emotion but of cognition, volition, and moral orientation — the whole inner person. Compound forms: *lev ha'even*, "heart of stone," and *lev basar*, "heart of flesh" (Ezek. 36:26, contrasting the unregenerate and renewed heart); *lev tahor*, "a clean heart" (Ps. 51:10).

**ma'gelei-tzedek (מַעְגְּלֵי־צֶדֶק)** — *mah-geh-LAY tseh-DEK* — "paths of righteousness." Paths of covenant-rightness that the shepherd actively leads to, not paths the sheep discover unaided. Psalm 23:3.

**nacham (נָחַם)** — *nah-KHAHM* — "comfort." A covenant-reassurance verb spoken after real judgment and exile — it presumes an actual catastrophe, not generic soothing. Isaiah 40:1.

**nasa (נָשָׂא)** — *nah-SAH* — "borne, carried." Same verb family used for bearing sin and guilt — the Suffering Servant carries grief as real, transferred weight, not sympathetic observation from a distance. Isaiah 53:4.

**nephesh (נֶפֶשׁ)** — *NEH-fesh* — "soul." The whole animating inner life, not a separable spiritual component — what is "restored" in Psalm 23:3.

**nəkēh-rûaḥ (נְכֵה־רוּחַ)** — *neh-KAY ROO-akh* — "contrite" or "stricken in spirit," from a root meaning to strike down. Names a spirit broken of self-sufficiency, not mild remorse. Isaiah 66:2b; parallel Isaiah 57:15.

**nishberey-lev (נִשְׁבְּרֵי־לֵב)** — *nish-beh-RAY LAYV* — "brokenhearted," from *shabar*, "to shatter." Names the wound's actual severity — a heart genuinely shattered, not merely disappointed. Psalm 34:18.

**qashah (קָשָׁה)** — *kah-SHAH* — "to stiffen, harden." The Old Testament root behind "hardness of heart" language, paralleling the Greek *sklērynō* — cited where Hebrews quotes Psalm 95.

**qol demamah daqah (קוֹל דְּמָמָה דַקָּה)** — *kohl deh-mah-MAH dah-KAH* — "a sound of thin/gentle silence" (traditionally rendered "still small voice"). Names God's paradigm-disrupting mode of self-revelation to Elijah — not in wind, earthquake, or fire. 1 Kings 19:12.

**ra'ah (רָאָה)** — *rah-AH* — used idiomatically for "cherished" — a particular sin knowingly held onto rather than merely committed in a moment of weakness. Psalm 66:18.

**rapha (רָפָא)** — *rah-FAH* — "to heal, restore, make whole." Covenantal before medical — "I am the LORD your healer" (*Yahweh Rapheka*, Exod. 15:26); used for physical and relational/spiritual restoration alike, with no sharp dividing line between the two senses.

**shama (שָׁמַע)** — *shah-MAH* — "to hear." Regularly carries the sense "hear-and-obey" — in these texts, not hearing and not obeying function as the same failure.

**shuv (שׁוּב)** — *SHOOV* — "to return, repent." In the prophets, healing is bound to *shuv* — a directional, relational movement back toward covenant relationship, not primarily an emotional register. Jeremiah 3:22.

**tzedaqah (צְדָקָה)** — *tseh-dah-KAH* — "righteousness." Names covenantal vindication given "from the God of his salvation," not achieved moral purity. Psalm 24:5.

**yada (יָדַע)** — *yah-DAH* — "to know." Embodied, experiential, relational knowing — the same verb used for sexual intimacy and for the Creator-Israel covenant bond. Genesis 4:1; Hosea 6:6; Jeremiah 31:34.

**yodea choli (יוֹדֵעַ חֳלִי)** — *yoh-DAY-ah khoh-LEE* — "knower of sickness/suffering," built on the *yada* root. Christ's being "acquainted with grief" (Isa. 53:3) is actual acquaintance in this sense, not conceptual awareness.

**YHWH-Yireh (יְהוָה יִרְאֶה)** — *yah-WEH yeer-EH* — "The LORD will provide." Abraham's declaration after the binding of Isaac — read in CIC practice as evidence of secure attachment: holding the gift with open hands rather than a grasping fist. Genesis 22:14.

**zakar (זָכַר)** — *zah-KHAR* — "remember." Biblical remembering reaches for specific past events, not abstract affirmation, when present experience of God feels absent. Psalm 77:11; Psalm 22:4-5.

**ehyeh asher ehyeh (אֶהְיֶה אֲשֶׁר אֶהְיֶה)** — *eh-YEH ah-SHER eh-YEH* — "I am who I am" (the divine Name given at the burning bush). The Name that grounds Moses' identity outside his own capacity, given before the mission began. Exodus 3:14.

### Aramaic

**Abba (אַבָּא)** — *AH-bah* — "Father," an intimate family address rather than formal religious language. Read in CIC practice as the Spirit-produced cry of a child, not a servant. Romans 8:15; Galatians 4:6.

### Greek

**agapē (ἀγάπη)** — *ah-GAH-pay* — "love." Contrasted with *phobos* (fear) as structurally incompatible states; *teleia agapē* is "perfect" or "complete" love. 1 John 4:18.

**anthrakia (ἀνθρακιά)** — *an-thrah-kee-AH* — "charcoal fire." The only other New Testament use of this exact word is at the courtyard fire of Peter's denial — an intentional sensory link back to the shame event. John 21:9; cf. John 18:18.

**apotithēmi (ἀποτίθημι)** — *ah-poh-TEE-thay-mee* — "put off," in aorist tense — a decisive, completed act, not an ongoing process. Ephesians 4:22.

**ananeousthai (ἀνανεοῦσθαι)** — *ah-nah-neh-OOS-thai* — "be renewed," present passive — ongoing and received, not self-generated. Ephesians 4:23.

**endusasthai (ἐνδύσασθαι)** — *en-DOO-sahs-thai* — "put on," aorist middle voice. Ephesians 4:24.

**diatheke (διαθήκη)** — *dee-ah-THAY-kay* — "covenant, will/testament." The Septuagint's chosen word over *syntheke* (a mutual agreement between equal parties) — emphasizing that God's covenant is a gracious disposition, not a negotiated contract.

**doulos (δοῦλος)** — *DOO-los* — "slave." Contrasted with *huios* (son) — a category change in standing, not an upgraded servant rank. Galatians 4:7.

**huios (υἱός)** — *hwee-OS* — "son." See *doulos* above. Galatians 4:7.

**elegchos (ἔλεγχος)** — *EL-eng-khos* — "conviction, proof" of what is unseen. Hebrews 11:1.

**hypostasis (ὑπόστασις)** — *hoo-POS-tah-sis* — "assurance, substance, underlying reality." Hebrews 11:1.

**en tō ēgapēmenō (ἐν τῷ ἠγαπημένῳ)** — *en toh ay-gah-pay-MEH-noh* — "in the Beloved." Locates the client's standing in Christ's identity, not the client's own condition. Ephesians 1:6.

**energōn (ἐνεργῶν)** — *en-er-GOHN* — "works in," a present participle sharing the root *erg-* with *katergazesthe* ("work out"). The human working is the site of the divine working, not a parallel contribution alongside it. Philippians 2:13.

**eperisseusen (ἐπερίσσευσεν)** — *eh-peh-RIS-syoo-sen* — "lavished, overflowed." Grace poured out in abundance, not measured out. Ephesians 1:8.

**gar (γάρ)** — *gahr* — "for, because." The hinge word that makes God's prior work the *ground* of the human command that follows it, not its supplement. Philippians 2:13.

**gegymnasmena (γεγυμνασμένα)** — *geh-goom-nahz-MEH-nah* — "trained," by constant practice — shares its root with "gymnasium." Names maturity as a formed capacity built by repeated practice, not accumulated insight. Hebrews 5:14.

**hamartia (ἁμαρτία)** — *hah-mar-TEE-ah* — "sin." Distinguished in Hebrews 12:1 from *ogkon* (weight/encumbrance) — creating theological space for a wound or burden that is not itself moral failure.

**hexis (ἕξις)** — *HEK-sis* — "trained disposition, formed capacity" resulting from repeated practice — what Hebrews 5:14's "trained" describes.

**homologia / homologeō (ὁμολογία / ὁμολογέω)** — *hoh-moh-loh-GEE-ah / hoh-moh-loh-GEH-oh* — "confession; to say the same thing [as another]." Covenant confession is agreeing with what God has already said about you, not self-generated affirmation. Romans 10:9-10.

**horaō (ὁράω)** — *hoh-RAH-oh* — "to see, perceive, experience." What the pure in heart do — perceive/experience God, not merely view Him someday. Matthew 5:8.

**iaomai (ἰάομαι)** — *ee-AH-oh-my* — "to heal." Matthew 13:15, quoting Isaiah 6; Hebrews 12:13.

**kaloúntos (καλοῦντος)** — *kah-LOON-tos* — "the one who calls" — a creation-language participle. Romans 4:17; echoes Genesis 1 and Psalm 33:9.

**kardia (καρδία)** — *kar-DEE-ah* — "heart." Carries the same comprehensive range as Hebrew *lev* — not restricted to emotion. *Katharos tē kardia*, "pure in heart" (Matt. 5:8).

**katergazomai (κατεργάζομαι)** — *kah-ter-GAHD-zoh-my* — "work out; prepare, produce." Present imperative *katergazesthe* in Philippians 2:12; also *katergazetai*, "is preparing," in 2 Corinthians 4:17.

**katharos (καθαρός)** — *kah-thah-ROS* — "pure" — ritually clean, morally unmixed, undivided. Matthew 5:8.

**katēgorōn (κατηγόρων)** — *kah-tay-goh-ROHN* — "accuser." Names the mechanism of using true facts to produce a false conclusion. Revelation 12:10.

**katoptrizomenoi (κατοπτριζόμενοι)** — *kah-top-trid-zoh-MEH-noy* — "beholding" — a genuinely ambiguous middle-voice verb that can also mean "reflecting as a mirror." The governing mechanism-verb for transformation-by-beholding across CIC's sanctification documents. 2 Corinthians 3:18.

**koinōnia tōn pathēmatōn (κοινωνία τῶν παθημάτων)** — *koy-noh-NEE-ah tohn pah-thay-MAH-tohn* — "participation" or "fellowship" in His sufferings. Philippians 3:10.

**logizomai (λογίζομαι)** — *loh-GID-zoh-my* — "to reckon, count, consider as established fact" — an accounting term (imperative form *logizesthe* in Rom. 6:11). Treating what God has declared as settled fact, not as aspirational hope. Romans 6:11; Romans 4:3-22.

**meno (μένω)** — *MEH-noh* — "to abide, remain." A deliberate, ongoing act, not passive drifting. John 15:4-10.

**metamorphoō (μεταμορφόω)** — *meh-tah-mor-FOH-oh* — "to be transformed" — shares its root with "Transfiguration." 2 Corinthians 3:18; Romans 12:2.

**metanoia (μετάνοια)** — *meh-tah-NOY-ah* — "change of mind" (the perceiving mind itself, *nous*) — repentance. Not remorse; the same directional movement as Hebrew *shuv* under a different covenant.

**nous (νοῦς)** — *noos* — "mind" — the faculty of perception, understanding, and moral reasoning, broader than mere intellect. Romans 12:2.

**noutheteō (νουθετέω)** — *noo-theh-TEH-oh* — "to admonish, confront with truth." Root of "nouthetic," directive biblical counseling's namesake verb.

**ochyrōma (ὀχύρωμα)** — *oh-KHOO-roh-mah* — "stronghold, fortress." 2 Corinthians 10:4-5.

**ogkon (ὄγκον)** — *ONG-kon* — "weight, encumbrance." Distinguished from *hamartia* (sin) — a burden that is not itself guilt. Hebrews 12:1.

**palaion anthrōpon (παλαιὸν ἄνθρωπον)** — *pah-lah-YON an-THROH-pon* — "old self," "old man." Ephesians 4:22.

**paraklēsis / parakaleō (παράκλησις / παρακαλέω)** — *pah-RAHK-lay-sis / pah-rah-kah-LEH-oh* — "comfort," "come-alongside" encouragement. 2 Corinthians 1:3-4.

**paraklētos (παράκλητος)** — *pah-RAHK-lay-tos* — "one called alongside" — Counselor, Helper, Advocate (the Paraclete). John 14:16.

**paralogizomenoi (παραλογιζόμενοι)** — *pah-rah-loh-gid-zoh-MEH-noy* — "deceiving themselves" — reasoning gone astray (*para-*, alongside/astray + *logizomai*). James 1:22-24.

**peripateite (περιπατεῖτε)** — *peh-ree-pah-TAY-teh* — "walk" — live, walk in step with. Galatians 5:16.

**phobos (φόβος)** — *FOH-bos* — "fear" — the relational posture of one expecting punishment. 1 John 4:18.

**phroneō (φρονέω)** — *froh-NEH-oh* — "to set the mind on; mindset, orientation" (imperative *phroneite*; noun *phronēma*). Not occasional thought-content but the whole evaluative operating system a person lives from. Colossians 3:2; Romans 8:5-6; Philippians 2:5.

**pistis / pisteuō (πίστις / πιστεύω)** — *PIS-tis / pis-TYOO-oh* — "faith/trust," noun and verb. Romans 10:9-13; Ephesians 2:8-9; John 1:12.

**pneuma douleias (πνεῦμα δουλείας)** — *PNYOO-mah doo-LAY-ahs* — "spirit of slavery." Romans 8:15.

**pneuma huiothesias (πνεῦμα υἱοθεσίας)** — *PNYOO-mah hwee-oh-theh-SEE-ahs* — "spirit of adoption [as sons]." Romans 8:15.

**pneuma tēs alētheias (πνεῦμα τῆς ἀληθείας)** — *PNYOO-mah tase ah-lay-THAY-ahs* — "Spirit of truth." John 16:13.

**rhiza pikrias (ῥίζα πικρίας)** — *REE-zah pee-KREE-ahs* — "root of bitterness." Hebrews 12:15.

**sarx (σάρξ)** — *sarks* — "flesh" — the old, unregenerate orientation, not the physical body as such. Galatians 5.

**sbennute (σβέννυτε)** — *SBEN-noo-teh* — "quench, extinguish" — negative imperative, "do not...". 1 Thessalonians 5:19.

**sklērynō (σκληρύνω)** — *sklay-ROO-noh* — "to harden." Hebrews 3:8,13,15, quoting Psalm 95.

**skubala (σκύβαλα)** — *SKOO-bah-lah* — "dung, refuse" — a deliberately coarse word. Philippians 3:8.

**splanchnizomai (σπλαγχνίζομαι)** — *splangkh-NID-zoh-my* — "moved with compassion" — a visceral, gut-level term, from *splanchna*, "inward parts/entrails." Matthew 9:36.

**stoichōmen (στοιχῶμεν)** — *stoy-KHOH-men* — "keep in step" — a marching/formation term. Galatians 5:25.

**syschematizesthe (συσχηματίζεσθε)** — *soos-khay-mah-TID-zes-theh* — "be conformed [to this world]" — negative imperative. Romans 12:2.

**telos (τέλος)** — *TEH-los* — "end, ultimate purpose" — the end toward which something is constituted, not merely its termination. A governing architectural term throughout CIC, not tied to a single verse.

**ta mē onta hōs onta (τὰ μὴ ὄντα ὡς ὄντα)** — *tah may ON-tah hoss ON-tah* — "the things that do not exist, as though existing." Romans 4:17.

**zōopoiountos tous nekrous (ζωοποιοῦντος τοὺς νεκρούς)** — *zoh-oh-poy-OON-tos toos nek-ROOS* — "gives life to the dead." Romans 4:17.

**zēteite (ζητεῖτε)** — *zay-TAY-teh* — "seek" — present imperative, ongoing directional pursuit. Colossians 3:1.

**aitousin (αἰτοῦσιν)** — *ai-TOO-sin* — "keep asking" — present tense, continuous rather than one-time. Luke 11:9-13.

**ex akoēs pisteōs (ἐξ ἀκοῆς πίστεως)** — *ex ah-koh-AYS pis-TEH-ohs* — "by hearing with faith" — as opposed to works of the law. Galatians 3:2,5.

**epichorēgōn (ἐπιχορηγῶν)** — *eh-pee-khoh-ray-GOHN* — "keeps supplying" — present participle, ongoing provision. Galatians 3:5.

### Jeffrey Young — Schema Therapy

**Early Maladaptive Schema (EMS) / Schema** — A deeply held belief and emotional pattern about self, others, or the world, formed in childhood in response to unmet core needs. Functions as a felt fact, not a recognized belief — which is why it resists direct argument. CIC identifies this with Paul's "strongholds" (2 Cor. 10:4-5).

**Schemas named in CIC documentation, sourced to Young's published taxonomy:** Defectiveness/Shame ("I am fundamentally flawed; if people knew the real me, they would reject me"); Abandonment/Instability ("The people I depend on will ultimately leave or be unavailable"); Subjugation ("My needs, wants, and feelings don't matter; I must suppress them to avoid rejection"); Failure ("I have failed, am failing, and will inevitably fail at anything important"); Emotional Deprivation ("No one will ever truly understand or meet my emotional needs"); Unrelenting Standards ("I must achieve constantly and at the highest level, or I am worthless"); Mistrust/Abuse ("Others will hurt, manipulate, or exploit me"); Self-Sacrifice ("I must meet others' needs at the expense of my own").

**CIC-extended schema labels** — several additional labels appear in intake annotation and the New Covenant Promises reference (Isolation, Conditional Worth, Deprivation/Scarcity, Vulnerability/Instability, Entitlement/Grandiosity, Emotional Inhibition, Enmeshment/Undeveloped Self, Meaninglessness/Purposelessness) that do not map cleanly onto Young's original 18-schema taxonomy. Flagged here as CIC's own extensions of the schema concept, not verbatim citations of Young — see Editorial Notes.

**Coping/Maintenance Modes — Surrender, Avoidance, Overcompensation** — The three ways a person manages an activated schema, all of which maintain it while giving the illusion of managing it: surrender (living as if the schema-belief is true), avoidance (structuring life to never test it), overcompensation (fighting against or performing over it).

**Schema activation / Schema origin / Schema interruption** — CIC's own operational vocabulary built on Young's base construct: the triggering of a schema in the moment, the formative event(s) that produced it, and the practice of consciously interrupting its automatic behavioral output.

### Kegan & Lahey — Immunity to Change (ITC)

**Immunity to Change (ITC)** — Kegan and Lahey's diagnostic methodology (2009, Harvard) for surfacing why stated commitments to change fail to produce lasting behavior: a hidden, equally or more powerful competing commitment is protecting something the person needs.

**Four-column map** — The ITC diagnostic sequence: Column 1 (stated/improvement goal) → Column 2 (behaviors that undermine the goal) → Column 3 (the competing commitment those behaviors protect) → Column 4 (the Big Assumption that makes the competing commitment feel necessary).

**Competing commitment** — The hidden priority a person's undermining behavior actually protects, uncovered by asking "What am I committed to that this behavior serves?"

**Big Assumption** — The foundational, untested belief that makes the competing commitment feel like a survival necessity rather than a choice — often rooted in formative experience and functioning as an operating law rather than a hypothesis.

**Big Assumption Experiment** — A client-designed, low-risk behavioral test constructed to gather real disconfirming evidence against the Big Assumption.

**Kegan's Orders of Mind** — A separate, related constructive-developmental framework (distinct from ITC proper): 3rd Order/Socialized Mind (identity constituted by external relationships and expectations), 4th Order/Self-Authoring Mind (identity constituted by one's own internally generated system), 5th Order/Self-Transforming Mind (capacity to hold one's own system as provisional).

**Subject vs. Object** — What a person is "subject to" is fused with their sense of self and cannot be examined; what they can hold "as object" can be looked at, questioned, and revised. Developmental growth is the ongoing conversion of subject into object.

### Richard Schwartz — Internal Family Systems (IFS)

**IFS** — A model of the mind as containing multiple distinct sub-personalities ("parts"), each with its own perspective, feelings, and protective function, organized around a core Self.

**Self (capital-S) / 8 Cs of Self-energy** — The undamaged core of a person, characterized by curiosity, calm, clarity, compassion, courage, creativity, confidence, and connectedness — accessible once parts are unblended from it.

**Parts** — The general term for the sub-personalities IFS works with.

**Protectors — Managers and Firefighters** — Parts organized to prevent pain from surfacing: Managers work proactively (control, perfectionism, caretaking); Firefighters react once pain has already broken through (numbing, distraction, impulsivity).

**Exiles** — Wounded, often young parts carrying pain, shame, fear, or grief, kept hidden by the protector parts.

**Burden** — The specific lie a part carries as if it were true — what the part learned in a formative moment, not what is actually true about the person.

**Blending / Unblending** — Blending is when a person experiences a part's content as the totality of who they are ("I am this feeling"); unblending is regaining enough distance to relate to the part rather than be it.

### Attachment Theory — Bowlby, Ainsworth, Main, Kirkpatrick

**Attachment theory / secure base** — Bowlby's foundational claim: a reliable, responsive caregiver functions as a secure base from which a child can explore the world; the resulting relational template shapes adult functioning.

**Secure / Anxious / Avoidant / Disorganized attachment** — The four classic attachment patterns, each with distinct behavioral markers under relational stress, used in CIC intake to hypothesize both human-relational and God-relational attachment style.

**Internal Working Models (IWM)** — Bowlby's term for the implicit relational templates a child forms — encoded expectations about self, others, and the availability of care, operating before and beneath conscious belief.

**Bowlby's four attachment criteria** — Proximity maintenance, safe haven, secure base, separation protest — the four observable functions that define an attachment bond.

**Strange Situation** — Ainsworth, Blehar, Waters & Wall's (1978) laboratory procedure for classifying infant attachment pattern by observing separation and reunion behavior.

**Adult Attachment Interview (AAI)** — Mary Main's (1985) narrative-based instrument assessing adult attachment pattern by the *coherence* of the telling, not its content; predicts the interviewee's child's attachment classification with roughly 75% accuracy.

**Earned security** — The finding (Siegel, Main, Mikulincer & Shaver) that adults who have processed and made coherent sense of a difficult attachment history can function with security despite that history, interrupting intergenerational transmission.

**God-attachment / attachment to God (Kirkpatrick)** — The same four-category attachment model (secure, anxious, avoidant) applied to a person's relationship with God, plus a category unique to this domain: functional deism (God believed in but not relationally engaged).

**Correspondence Hypothesis vs. Compensation Hypothesis (Kirkpatrick, 1994/2005)** — Two competing predictions about the relationship between human and God-attachment: correspondence predicts the God-relationship mirrors the human pattern; compensation predicts it offsets what the human pattern lacks. Evidence supports both, situationally.

**ECR-S (Experiences in Close Relationships – Short Form)** — Wei et al.'s (2007) short-form clinical attachment instrument, built on Brennan, Clark & Shaver's (1998) two-dimension (anxiety/avoidance) model and Bartholomew & Horowitz's (1991) four-category model. CIC's own Attachment Pattern Screener is an original, non-clinical adaptation of the same two-dimension construct — explicitly not a reproduction of the licensed ECR-S itself.

**Ultimate attachment figure** — A proposed Kirkpatrick construct explicitly *rejected* in CIC's own critique section as a "substitution framework" that risks reducing God to a psychological slot-filler rather than a covenant partner.

### Curt Thompson (with Dan Siegel) — IPNB, Memory, Shame

**Implicit memory vs. Explicit memory** — Implicit memory holds procedural, felt patterns (how safety actually feels in the body) formed before conscious recall is possible; explicit memory holds propositions and narratable facts. The two systems can diverge — a person can hold a correct explicit belief while an unchanged implicit pattern governs behavior under pressure.

**Implicit relational templates** — The felt, embodied expectations a person's nervous system formed in relationship long before language was available to name them.

**Shame as attack on being, not behavior** — Distinguishes shame (an attack on the self's legitimacy to exist and be seen) from guilt (a judgment on a specific act); shame's primary move is to hide the self from being known, and it operates below conscious thought.

**Interpersonal Neurobiology (IPNB)** — Daniel Siegel's integrative framework (extended clinically by Thompson): the brain is fundamentally a social organ, and identity is co-constructed in relationship, not developed in isolation.

**Co-regulation (Siegel)** — The mechanism by which one person's regulated nervous system helps another's regulate, through non-verbal channels — read in CIC as part of God's own pastoral pattern toward His people.

**Orbitofrontal cortex (as integration site)** — The brain region Siegel identifies as experience-dependent and malleable across the lifespan — the neurological basis for claiming change remains possible at any age.

**Neuroplasticity** — The brain's capacity to form new neural connections throughout life (Hebb's principle: "neurons that fire together wire together"), popularized clinically by Rick Hanson.

*Practitioner note: several of the terms above are explicitly listed in [[Covenant Identity — Formation Mechanism Guide]] as vocabulary to translate OUT of client-facing material — "implicit memory," "explicit memory," "neural pathways," "IPNB," "schema," "cognitive restructuring," and "attachment" are practitioner-only registers in this model, not client-facing language.*

### Stephen Porges — Polyvagal Theory

**Polyvagal Theory (2011)** — Porges' account of the autonomic nervous system as a hierarchy of three circuits (social engagement, mobilization, immobilization) that activate in a fixed order under increasing threat.

**Ventral vagal complex (VVC)** — The evolutionarily newest circuit, active when a person feels safe; governs facial expression, vocal prosody, and capacity for social engagement.

**Neuroception** — The subcortical, non-conscious process by which the nervous system continuously scans for safety or danger, before and beneath conscious appraisal.

**Sympathetic activation / mobilization** — The fight-flight response; the second circuit, activating when the ventral vagal system fails to secure safety.

**Dorsal vagal shutdown** — The oldest circuit; freeze, collapse, or dissociation, activating when mobilization also fails to secure safety.

**Vagal brake** — The parasympathetic mechanism by which extended exhale downregulates arousal, used as a concrete regulation technique.

**Interoception** — Awareness of one's own internal bodily state — a prerequisite capacity for processing material stored implicitly in the body rather than in narrative memory.

**Window of Tolerance** — The functional range of nervous system arousal within which a person can think clearly, relate, and access trained skills; above it is hyperarousal, below it is hypoarousal. Appears across dozens of CIC documents, frequently without in-text attribution, though sourced to Siegel/Porges.

### Diana Fosha — AEDP (Accelerated Experiential Dynamic Psychotherapy)

**AEDP State Model** — A four-state sequence tracking movement through defended material toward transformation: Defensive processing → Core affective experience → Adaptive action tendencies (State 3) → Transformational affects (State 4).

**Transformance** — Fosha's term for the innate, organismic drive toward healing, growth, and flourishing — the counterpart to (not merely the absence of) resistance.

**The glimmer** — A fleeting moment of genuine aliveness, authentic longing, or warmth breaking through defended material — a signal worth following, not something to rush past.

**Undoing aloneness** — Fosha's core relational stance: the practitioner's task in a hard moment is not to fix the pain but to not leave the person alone in it.

**Meta-processing / metatherapeutic processing** — Making a State 3 → State 4 transition consciously explicit in the room, so the shift itself becomes usable material rather than passing unnoticed.

### Boston Change Process Study Group (BCPSG) & Daniel Stern

**Implicit relational knowing** — Lyons-Ruth (1998) and Stern et al.'s (1998) term for procedural, non-declarative knowledge of how to be in relationship, laid down through repeated relational experience rather than taught propositionally.

**Moments of meeting** — Genuine interpersonal contact between two subjectivities that cannot be scripted in advance — a spontaneous, mutual recognition that reorganizes the implicit relational field.

**Now moments** — Stern's term for the instant the normal session frame breaks and neither party knows what happens next — the precondition for a moment of meeting.

**Enactment** — When a client's implicit relational pattern is being lived out with the practitioner in real time, in the room, rather than merely described.

### Bruce Ecker (with Nader & LeDoux, Schiller et al.) — Memory Reconsolidation

**Memory reconsolidation** — The finding that a reactivated memory briefly destabilizes into a labile state during which it can be genuinely rewritten, rather than merely suppressed by a competing new memory.

**Three conditions for reconsolidation** — Reactivation (the target belief must be emotionally live, not merely recalled) → Prediction error (a disconfirmation must register at the felt level, not only cognitively) → Juxtaposition (the old belief and the disconfirming experience must be held together during the labile window).

**Extinction (contrasted outcome)** — What happens when the three reconsolidation conditions are not met: a new association is formed alongside the old one and can temporarily suppress it, but the original returns under stress because it was never actually rewritten.

**Coherence Therapy** — Ecker's own clinical protocol built on the reconsolidation research. CIC uses the *mechanism description*, not the clinical protocol itself, which remains outside coaching scope.

**Reconsolidation failure** — Repeated reactivation of a belief without meeting all three conditions — the mechanism behind "I've worked on this for years and nothing changes."

### Eugene Gendlin (with Klein, Mathieu, Kiesler) — Felt Sense / Experiencing / Focusing

**Felt sense** — A holistic, pre-verbal, meaning-carrying bodily sense of a situation, present before language can adequately name it.

**Focusing** — Gendlin's six-step process for attending to and articulating a felt sense; explicitly non-clinical and accessible without a therapeutic frame.

**Felt shift** — The bodily release that occurs when a felt sense has been accurately touched and named — the marker that Focusing has reached its target.

**Experiencing Scale (EXP Scale)** — Klein, Mathieu, Gendlin & Kiesler's (1969/1986) seven-stage observer-rated instrument for how far a person's process has moved from external/intellectualized description toward genuine felt-sense contact. CIC's own [[Covenant Identity — Route B Experiencing Depth Rating]] is a five-level coach-safe condensation of it.

**Bio-Spiritual Focusing** — McMahon & Campbell's (1985/1991) spiritual-direction adaptation of Focusing, adding an explicit "Ask" step — bringing the felt sense to God in prayer.

### Dan Allender — Wound Archaeology / Character Wound Typology

**Wound archaeology** — Allender's diagnostic posture: a person's story, read carefully, reveals what the heart is actually organized around — the excavation is narrative, not merely propositional.

**Character Wound Diagnostic types** — Three clinical-pastoral (not empirically validated) categories, each with a core wound and coping mode: **Warrior** (betrayal/weakness wound; overcompensation coping; core statement "I am weak"); **Hermit** (terror/too-much wound; avoidance coping; core statement "I am too much / unwanted"); **False Noble** (ambivalence/not-enough wound; surrender coping; core statement "I am not enough").

**False self / true self (Allender's version)** — Allender's own usage, distinct from Winnicott's below: the false self is practiced, defended, and often loud; the true self is quieter and less rehearsed, emerging as the false self's protective work is no longer needed.

**Wound → vow → relational style → God-connection absence** — The formation-arc mechanism CIC borrows from Allender: a formative wound produces an inward vow, which produces a characteristic relational style (compliance, aggression, or withdrawal), which produces a specific way God's presence gets excluded.

*Corpus-wide caution, stated repeatedly in CIC documents themselves: the three-type typology is explicitly flagged as a clinical-pastoral construct, not an empirically validated diagnostic category — types may not be mutually exclusive or exhaustive.*

### Dan McAdams — Narrative Identity Theory

**Narrative identity theory** — McAdams' claim that people construct a personal life narrative, and behavior over time conforms to that narrative — identity is a story a person is living inside, not just a set of traits.

**Narrative contamination / contamination narrative** — A life story dominated by decline, victimhood, failure, or shame as its organizing plot.

**Redemptive narrative / redemptive self** — A story that moves through suffering toward meaning, growth, or contribution — the pattern McAdams found most robustly associated with generativity and wellbeing.

**Agency vs. subject (in narrative)** — Whether a person's telling casts them as an agent who participates in shaping outcomes, or as a subject to whom things merely happen.

**Scene-to-story collapse** — Treating one emotionally intense scene as though it rewrites the entire life narrative, rather than as one data point within it.

**Flat narrative / circling narrative** — CIC-adapted narrative-type categories used in intake diagnostics, not McAdams' own published terms.

### Prochaska & DiClemente — Stages of Change / Transtheoretical Model

Reframed throughout CIC's Discovery Call material as "readiness to *receive*, not readiness to *achieve*":

**Pre-contemplation** — The person does not yet see the pattern as their own; causes are externally attributed.

**Contemplation** — The pattern is seen and named internally, but the person remains ambivalent about change.

**Preparation** — The person is specific, ready, and beginning to resource a plan.

**Action** — The person is already actively working the change.

**Maintenance** — The change has been consolidated and is being sustained.

### Aaron Beck / Judith Beck / David Burns — Cognitive Therapy (CBT)

**CBT** — The foundational claim that thoughts, feelings, and behaviors are interconnected, such that working at the thought level produces downstream change in feeling and behavior.

**ABC model** — Activating event → Belief → Consequence — the basic cognitive-behavioral causal chain.

**Automatic thoughts** — Tier 1: situation-specific, involuntary thoughts that arise in the moment.

**Intermediate beliefs** — Tier 2: the "if-then" conditional rules that generate automatic thoughts.

**Core beliefs** — Tier 3: the deepest, most absolute-sounding, schema-level statements underlying the intermediate beliefs.

**Compensatory strategies** — The fourth element of Judith Beck's Cognitive Conceptualization Diagram: the behavioral patterns a person develops specifically to avoid activating the core belief.

**Cognitive Conceptualization Diagram** — Judith Beck's full four-element clinical formulation tool (core belief, intermediate beliefs, compensatory strategies, automatic thoughts) linking a presenting situation back to its governing belief.

**Downward Arrow Technique / laddering (Burns)** — A questioning sequence ("And if that were true, what would that mean about you?") that ladders from a surface thought down to the core belief driving it.

**Socratic questioning, thought record, behavioral experiment** — Named in CIC documents as the "coach-safe CBT toolkit" appropriate to Stage 4a work, distinguished from clinical CBT protocols that remain outside coaching scope.

**11 cognitive distortions (Burns)** — All-or-Nothing Thinking, Overgeneralization, Mental Filter, Disqualifying the Positive, Mind Reading, Fortune Telling, Magnification/Minimization, Emotional Reasoning, Should Statements, Labeling/Mislabeling, Personalization — the full taxonomy used in CIC's Cognitive Diagnostic Fluency Curriculum.

### William Swann — Self-Verification Theory

**Self-Verification Theory** — Swann's finding that people actively filter incoming information to preserve coherence with their existing self-concept, even when that self-concept is negative. In CIC, this is the mechanism explaining why covenant truth "gets filtered rather than received as evidence" when spoken to an identity that has not yet been named and revised — the mind protects the story it already has, not necessarily the story that is true.

### God-Representation Cluster — Rizzuto, Todd Hall, David Benner

**Living God vs. theologian's God (Rizzuto)** — The "living God" is the psychic object formed from a person's early relational experience, functioning implicitly; the "theologian's God" is the propositionally correct God a person can describe. The two can diverge sharply.

**Illusory transitional object (Rizzuto, drawing on Winnicott/Fairbairn)** — Rizzuto's controversial framing of the God-representation as a self-constructed transitional object — CIC engages this developmental mechanism without adopting Rizzuto's reductive theological conclusion that God is therefore merely a psychological construction.

**Implicit vs. explicit God-image (Todd Hall)** — Implicit: gut-level relational expectations of God encoded in the attachment system before language. Explicit: the consciously articulable belief a person can state about God. Hall's research finds these frequently diverge and that the implicit layer, not the explicit, predicts behavior under stress.

**Believed-in God vs. experienced God (David Benner)** — Benner's distinction between the God a person affirms propositionally and the God actually encountered relationally; Benner locates transformation specifically at the point of **surrender** — the condition under which the experienced God becomes accessible.

**Six God-Representation Types (CIC synthesis)** — Punishing, Absent/Distant, Permissive/Safe, Contingent/Transactional, Capricious/Unsafe, Overwhelming/Consuming — each cross-referenced in CIC documentation to a corresponding attachment pattern and Young schema.

### Biblical Counseling Cluster — Ed Welch, David Powlison, Paul Tripp, Timothy Keller

**Fear of man (Welch)** — Welch's diagnosis of the root lie beneath most presenting lies: an excessive concern for what others think, need, or expect, functioning as a rival to the fear of God.

**Functional gods / functional worship (Powlison)** — Powlison's diagnostic lens: the heart is always worshipping something functionally, whether or not it is worshipped confessionally; "What do you want?" is the governing diagnostic question.

**Functional theology vs. confessional theology (Tripp)** — Tripp's distinction between the theology a person actually operates from under pressure and the theology they would state if asked directly — the same functional/confessional gap that organizes CIC's own [[Covenant Identity — Functional vs. Confessional Theology — 20 Diagnostic Contrasts]].

**X-ray questions (Tripp)** — Questions designed to move past a person's surface presentation to the heart-level commitment actually driving it.

**Idol domains (Keller)** — Keller's applied categories for where functional worship most commonly concentrates: money, sex, power, approval.

### Allan Schore — Countertransference / Right-Brain Model

**Countertransference (reframed)** — In CIC's Self-Supervision materials, explicitly reframed away from "contamination to suppress" toward "neurobiological signal" — real data about what the client is transmitting relationally, worth attending to rather than eliminating.

**Right-brain model / co-regulation** — Schore's claim that a practitioner's own regulation actively co-regulates the client in real time; if the practitioner dysregulates, the co-regulating function breaks down regardless of what is said.

### Deci & Ryan — Self-Determination Theory (SDT)

**SDT motivation quality spectrum** — Four regulatory types with descending sustainability: Intrinsic/Integrated (done because it matters, aligned with identity), Identified (personally valued even if not enjoyable), Introjected (driven by internal guilt/pressure), External (driven by outside reward or punishment alone).

**Autonomy-granting vs. autonomy-support** — Autonomy-granting simply removes constraints; autonomy-support actively provides rationale, choice, and acknowledgment of the other person's perspective — CIC contrasts this with Co-Active coaching's "accept the client's No" posture as a related but distinct autonomy commitment.

### Miller & Rollnick — Motivational Interviewing (MI)

**MI Engaging Stage** — The relational-establishment phase of Motivational Interviewing, governing the conversational register of CIC's Discovery Call.

**OARS** — Open questions, Affirmations, Reflections, Summaries — MI's four core micro-skills.

**Change talk vs. sustain talk** — Client language that moves toward change versus language that defends the status quo; MI practitioners are trained to selectively reinforce change talk.

**Ambivalence exploration** — MI's stance that ambivalence about change is normal and workable material, not resistance to be overcome by argument.

### Coaching Methodology — Co-Active (Kimsey-House et al.), Bordin, Wampold

**Designed Alliance** — The co-created agreement, made explicit early in an engagement, about how coach and client will work together — including how to handle friction when it arises.

**Championing** — Seeing and holding a client's capacity even when they cannot yet see it themselves, rooted in the client's own stated values rather than the coach's aspirations for them.

**Level 3 listening** — Attending simultaneously to a client's words, affect, energy, and silence — the fullest of the three classic coaching listening levels.

**Structures** — Coach-created scaffolding (reminders, commitments, check-ins) that supports a client-owned commitment between sessions, distinct from the coach imposing accountability.

**Working Alliance model (Bordin, 1979)** — The tripartite Bond/Task/Goal account of therapeutic/coaching alliance, identified in CIC documentation as the un-named theoretical basis behind its own Alliance Pulse instrument.

**Working Alliance Inventory (WAI / WAI-SR)** — Horvath & Greenberg's licensed clinical alliance-measurement instrument, explicitly named as what CIC's own Alliance Pulse tool is *not* reproducing.

**Common-factors research (Wampold, Lambert)** — The research finding that relationship quality accounts for more outcome variance than any specific technique or modality choice — the evidentiary basis for CIC's alliance-first practitioner development priority.

**Core conditions (Rogers)** — Carl Rogers' three necessary-and-sufficient relational conditions for therapeutic change: unconditional positive regard, empathy, congruence.

### Peter Levine — Somatic Experiencing, Titration, Grounding

**Somatic Experiencing (SE)** — A body-based trauma-processing modality with its own certification track (SEP); named in CIC as a clinical referral modality, outside coaching scope.

**Titration** — Levine's principle of approaching traumatic material in small, tolerable increments — "go toward the wound and then back" — rather than full immersion.

**Pendulation** — The rhythmic movement between activation and settling that titration produces over a session.

**Grounding / somatic orienting** — Concrete body-based techniques (e.g., "feet flat on the floor") used to return a dysregulated person to present-moment safety.

### Bessel van der Kolk

**"The body keeps the score"** — Van der Kolk's summary claim that attachment wounds and trauma are stored somatically — in the body, limbic system, and brainstem — such that narrative and cognitive interventions alone cannot fully access or resolve them.

### Walter Brueggemann / Claus Westermann — Lament Theology

**Orientation → Disorientation → New Orientation (Brueggemann)** — Brueggemann's three-movement account of the Psalms' emotional arc, used in CIC to normalize disorientation (lament, complaint) as a legitimate, expected movement rather than a spiritual failure.

**Four/five-movement lament structure (Westermann, 1981)** — Address → Complaint → Petition → (Expression of Trust) → Praise/Vow — the formal shape of a biblical lament, used as a template for CIC's own Lament Practice Guide.

**Cheap grace (Brueggemann's usage)** — A brittle, performative faith that has not been tested by honest engagement with darkness — distinct from Bonhoeffer's more famous use of the same phrase, and worth not conflating with it.

### Michael White & David Epston — Narrative Therapy

**Narrative therapy / re-authoring** — The stance that a person's identity is not the problem — the story they have internalized about the problem is — and that a new, truer story can be authored.

**Externalization** — Linguistically separating the problem from the person's identity ("anxiety has been telling me..." rather than "I am anxious").

**Unique outcomes / exception events / sparkling moments** — Instances that don't fit the dominant problem-saturated story — counterevidence the story has suppressed or overlooked.

**Thickening the alternative story** — The ongoing practitioner work of adding detail, evidence, and significance to the emerging alternative narrative so it becomes as substantial as the old one.

### Steven Hayes — Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT)

**ACT** — A therapy model built on psychological flexibility rather than symptom elimination as its primary target.

**Cognitive defusion** — Creating distance between the observing self and a thought ("I notice the thought that...") rather than being fused with it as literal truth.

**Psychological flexibility** — The capacity to stay present and choose values-aligned action even in the presence of difficult internal content.

**Committed action** — Behavior undertaken in service of chosen values, independent of momentary internal state.

**Identity fusion** — The state ACT works against: being so merged with a thought or story that it functions as unquestionable fact.

**Values clarification** — The ACT process of identifying what actually matters to a person, as distinct from goals or outcomes.

### Dallas Willard

**VIM** — Vision, Intention, Means — Willard's formation architecture: a person needs a clear vision of the transformed life, a genuine intention to pursue it, and specific means (practices) suited to it. Used throughout CIC as the governing formation-design frame.

**Notional belief vs. formed belief** — Willard's distinction between a belief merely affirmed as true and a belief that has actually reorganized how a person lives.

**Will-training dimension** — Willard's insistence that the will, not only the mind, must be specifically trained for lasting change — belief alone does not produce transformation.

**Training vs. trying** — Willard's core formation principle: effort aimed at a single instance of behavior ("trying") does not produce change the way sustained practice aimed at building capacity ("training") does.

### Trauma / Attachment Neuroscience Adjacent — Yehuda, Main, Gottman, Bowen

**Epigenetic transmission of trauma (Rachel Yehuda)** — Yehuda's research finding altered cortisol profiles in children of trauma survivors matching PTSD patterns, suggesting a biological, not only behavioral, transmission pathway.

**Narrative coherence (Mary Main)** — See Adult Attachment Interview above; also names disorganized attachment (with Solomon, 1990) — attachment behavior that doesn't fit a single coherent strategy, typically associated with frightened or frightening caregiving.

**Family emotional culture transmission (John Gottman)** — Gottman's finding that emotional patterns and rules ("what we do with feelings here") transmit across a family system largely without being explicitly articulated.

**Family Systems Theory / Differentiation of self (Murray Bowen)** — Bowen's framework describing a family as an interconnected emotional unit; differentiation of self is the capacity to remain oneself (thinking and feeling both engaged) while staying emotionally connected to a reactive system, rather than fusing with or cutting off from it.

### Identity/Personality Theory — Erikson, Winnicott, Terror Management Theory

**Identity diffusion (Erik Erikson)** — The absence of a stable, coherent identity formation — one of Erikson's classic developmental-crisis outcomes.

**False self / true self (D.W. Winnicott)** — Winnicott's own version of this pairing, distinct from Allender's above: the false self is a compliance-based construction formed to manage an inadequately attuned early environment; the true self is the authentic, spontaneous self it was built to protect and hide.

**Transitional object (Winnicott)** — An object (or, in Rizzuto's extension, a God-representation) that helps a child bridge inner and outer reality during separation from the caregiver.

**Terror Management Theory / Mortality Salience (Solomon, Greenberg & Pyszczynski)** — The theory that much of identity and cultural behavior is organized around managing the anxiety produced by awareness of mortality; CIC connects this theologically to Richard Beck's "slavery of death" reading of Hebrews 2:15.

### Behavior Change / Habit Formation — Fogg, Clear, Gollwitzer, Neff

**Tiny Habits / behavior design (BJ Fogg)** — Fogg's model: small, easily achieved behaviors produce a self-perception shift ("I am someone who does this") that then supports larger change.

**Identity-Based Habits (James Clear)** — Clear's *Atomic Habits* framing: durable habit change follows from a shift in self-identity, not merely from outcome goals.

**Implementation intentions (Peter Gollwitzer)** — Pre-committing to a specific if-then behavioral plan ("when X happens, I will do Y"), shown to substantially increase follow-through over a general intention alone.

**Self-compassion (Kristin Neff)** — Neff's research finding that spoken, embodied self-compassion produces measurably stronger physiological calming effects than purely internal/silent self-compassion.

**Hedonic adaptation / growth amnesia (Brickman & Campbell)** — The tendency for both positive and negative changes in circumstance to fade from felt significance over time — relevant to why growth needs to be actively documented (Growth Report) rather than trusted to memory.

### Learning Science Cluster (Practitioner Training Design)

Sourced for CIC's own practitioner drill and curriculum design, not for client-facing use:

**Deliberate practice (Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer)** — Structured, feedback-driven, effortful repetition — shown to improve practitioner skill more reliably than raw years of experience alone.

**Testing effect / retrieval practice (Roediger & Karpicke)** — Actively retrieving information from memory produces more durable learning than re-reading or re-studying it.

**Desirable difficulties (Bjork)** — Learning conditions that feel harder in the moment (spacing, interleaving, varied practice) but produce stronger long-term retention than easier, more fluent-feeling study conditions.

**Spacing effect (Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted & Rohrer)** — Distributing practice over time produces better retention than massing it in one session.

**Interleaving (Rohrer & Taylor)** — Mixing multiple related skills or categories within a single practice session, rather than blocking one at a time, improves discrimination and transfer.

**Cognitive load theory / worked-example effect (Sweller)** — Working memory has limited capacity; studying fully worked examples before attempting novel problems reduces unnecessary load and improves learning.

**Dual coding theory (Paivio)** — Information encoded both verbally and visually is retained more durably than information encoded in only one format.

**Encoding depth via longhand note-taking (Mueller & Oppenheimer)** — Handwriting notes, which forces summarization rather than transcription, produces deeper encoding than typing verbatim.

**Expressive writing (Pennebaker)** — Structured writing about emotionally significant experience, shown to produce measurable psychological and even physiological benefit.

### Miscellaneous Named Frameworks (Secondary/Single-Occurrence)

**State vs. trait (Charles Spielberger)** — The distinction between a momentary emotional state and a stable personality trait — relevant to not misreading a triggered state as evidence about identity.

**Self-schema theory (Hazel Markus)** — Cognitive structures a person uses to organize and interpret self-relevant information, filtering what gets noticed and remembered about oneself.

**Mood-congruent memory / state-dependent processing (Gordon Bower)** — The finding that current mood biases which memories are most accessible — a person in a low state more easily recalls other low-state memories, reinforcing the mood.

**Guilt vs. shame (Tangney & Dearing)** — The empirically supported distinction between guilt (which preserves a stable, evaluable self — "I did a bad thing") and shame (which collapses the self entirely — "I am bad").

**Christian Psychology framework (Eric L. Johnson)** — Johnson's proposed discipline distinct from both secular psychology and classical "integration" models; contributes CIC's use of Imago Dei as the foundational identity category, the soul's faculties (mind, will, affections), virtue/character as the middle term between belief and behavior, and the wound/sin dual diagnostic dimension.

**Episodic vs. semantic memory (Endel Tulving)** — Episodic memory holds specific, personally experienced events; semantic memory holds general facts and knowledge. CIC's use of specific-memory "evidence anchors" targets episodic, not semantic, memory specifically.

**Spiritual bypassing** — Using spiritual practice or language to avoid processing difficult psychological or emotional material. *Attribution note: CIC documents credit this term inconsistently — one document attributes it to John Welwood (1984, its actual origin), another to Ken Wilber. Welwood is correct; see Editorial Notes.*

**"Certainty" framework (Tony Robbins)** — Seeing a desired outcome as already true and refusing to waver, reinforced by daily ritual conviction. Explicitly tested against Scripture in CIC's own research and found distinct from — not equivalent to — biblical faith; not endorsed as a CIC method.

**Generational strongholds model (Neil Anderson / Charles Kraft)** — A model of inherited spiritual/behavioral patterns passed across generations. Flagged in CIC's own documentation as contested by both cessationist and Reformed practitioners — used cautiously, not as settled doctrine.

**Social identity theory (Tajfel & Turner)** — The theory that part of a person's self-concept derives from group membership, with in-group/out-group dynamics shaping behavior independent of individual belief.

**Alexithymia (Rothschild)** — Difficulty identifying and describing one's own emotional states — used in CIC as a contraindication criterion for somatic/felt-sense work, since that work assumes some baseline capacity for interoceptive access.

### Clinical Presentation & Referral Vocabulary

*Standard clinical terms the corpus uses (chiefly in the Crisis & Referral Protocol) without always defining in-text, since a licensed audience is assumed to already know them. Definitions below are supplied for learning purposes — this is the clearest example of an "improvement" over the raw source material, which mentions these terms as referral triggers without defining them.*

**Dissociation** — A disruption in the normal integration of consciousness, memory, identity, or perception, ranging from mild detachment to severe disconnection from reality.

**Depersonalization / Derealization** — Depersonalization is a felt sense of detachment from one's own body or mental processes ("watching myself from outside"); derealization is the same detachment applied to the surrounding world ("nothing feels real").

**Psychosis / psychotic-spectrum symptoms** — A state involving impaired contact with shared reality — hallucinations, delusions, or severely disorganized thought or behavior.

**Suicidal ideation (SI)** — Thoughts of ending one's own life, ranging from passive ("I wish I weren't here") to active with plan and intent — triaged in CIC via the C-SSRS severity table.

**Self-harm** — Deliberate injury to one's own body, typically as a coping mechanism rather than with suicidal intent, though the two can co-occur and require careful triage.

**PTSD symptoms** — Flashbacks, hypervigilance, avoidance, and nightmares following exposure to a traumatic event, persisting beyond the event itself.

**Complex PTSD (C-PTSD)** — A more pervasive trauma presentation than single-incident PTSD, typically resulting from prolonged or repeated trauma (especially in childhood or captive relationships), affecting emotional regulation, self-concept, and relational capacity more broadly.

**Active substance dependency** — A pattern of substance use involving physiological or psychological dependence, requiring clinical/medical treatment beyond coaching scope.

**Eating disorder behavior** — Disordered eating patterns (restriction, bingeing, purging, compulsive exercise) tied to body image, control, or emotional regulation, requiring specialized clinical treatment.

**Bipolar presentation** — Mood-episode patterns (manic/hypomanic and depressive) requiring psychiatric evaluation and typically medication management.

**Religious scrupulosity** — An OCD-adjacent presentation involving obsessive religious or moral doubt and compulsive reassurance-seeking or ritual, distinct from ordinary conviction of conscience.

**Major depression / mood disorder** — Clinically significant, persistent depressed mood and/or loss of interest impairing daily functioning, distinct from situational sadness or discouragement.

**Complex trauma** — Trauma resulting from prolonged, repeated exposure (often relational/developmental), distinguished from single-incident trauma by its pervasive effect on identity and relational capacity.

**Trauma dysregulation / activation** — A trauma-related nervous-system state outside the Window of Tolerance, observable as hyperarousal or hypoarousal.

**Freeze response** — An immobilization stress response (dorsal vagal shutdown, in Porges' terms) — distinct from calm.

**Flooding** — A state of emotional overwhelm in which a person's capacity to think or process is temporarily exceeded by the intensity of what they're experiencing.

**Shutdown** — Behavioral/relational withdrawal and disengagement, often the visible surface of dorsal vagal activation.

## Part III — Professional, Methodological & Organizational Terms

### Credentialing / Organizational

**ICF** — International Coaching Federation. The governing professional-standards body for the coaching field; CIC's Coaching Agreement operates under the ICF Code of Ethics, and the Crisis & Referral Protocol cites ICF Standard 3.7 as the ethical basis for the referral obligation.

**ACBC** — Association of Certified Biblical Counselors. A credentialing body named alongside CCEF as an appropriate background for a scope-boundary clinical supervisor.

**CCEF** — Christian Counseling & Educational Foundation. See ACBC above; also flagged in CIC's own readiness planning as a certificate track that teaches someone else's framework rather than providing feedback on the practitioner's own diagnostic judgment — useful, but not a substitute for supervision.

**QPR** — Question, Persuade, Refer. A free, roughly one-hour suicide-prevention gatekeeper certification (qprinstitute.com) teaching how to ask about suicidal ideation competently and without escalating risk. Marked as a non-optional pre-launch requirement in CIC's readiness plan.

### Clinical Assessment Tools

**C-SSRS** — Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale. An empirically validated clinical instrument (Columbia University Psychiatry) fully operationalized in the Crisis & Referral Protocol as a four-level triage table: passive ideation → active ideation with no plan → active with plan but no intent → active with plan and intent, each with a specified response.

**ACE** — Adverse Childhood Experiences. Felitti et al.'s measure of cumulative childhood exposure to abuse, neglect, and household dysfunction, with well-documented dose-response effects on identity formation, affect regulation, relational patterns, and adult functioning; elevated ACE scores raise C-PTSD risk and require calibrated pacing in coaching work.

**C-PTSD** — see Clinical Presentation & Referral Vocabulary above.

**ECR / ECR-S** — see Attachment Theory cluster above (Part II).

**AAI** — see Attachment Theory cluster above (Part II).

### Research Methodology

**RCT** — Randomized Controlled Trial. Used throughout CIC's evidence-quality assessments to distinguish strongly supported frameworks (e.g., Self-Determination Theory, "decades of RCTs across cultures, contexts, and age groups") from more preliminary ones (e.g., Immunity to Change, "limited randomized controlled trials").

**Evidence-tier taxonomy — Established / Supported / Preliminary / Theoretical** — CIC's own recurring internal convention for grading how strongly the evidence base supports any cited framework or technique — applied consistently across the Full Model Reference and Secured in Father Design Notes rather than treating every citation as equally certain.

**Peer-reviewed (literature)** — Used as a quality marker distinguishing empirically grounded research from practitioner-theorist writing that, while valuable, has not gone through formal peer review.

**Effect size** — A statistical measure of how large a finding's practical impact is, used in CIC's evidence notes to distinguish "documented but small" from "large and consistently replicated" effects.

**MI** — see Part II, Miller & Rollnick cluster.

**OARS** — see Part II, Miller & Rollnick cluster.

**FIS** — Facilitative Interpersonal Skills (Anderson et al., 2009). The specific skill set a coaching-craft supervisor is meant to evaluate: warmth, empathic accuracy, persuasiveness, hopefulness, verbal fluency, problem-focus. The intended anchor for CIC's still-paused FIS/alliance drill deck.

**Ontological Coaching** — A coaching approach grounded in speech-act theory and phenomenology rather than empirical psychology; named once in CIC's evidence table as "low empirical / high philosophical" grounding, for contrast with more research-supported frameworks.

### Safety / Scope of Practice

**Scope of practice** — The governing concept defining what CIC coaching is and is not authorized to do; the central document is the Crisis & Referral Protocol's Section 1.

**Mandated reporter** — A legal status requiring certain professionals to report suspected abuse or neglect. CIC's own documentation notes life coaches are generally not mandated reporters under state law, but this varies by jurisdiction and must be checked locally.

**988 / 911 / Crisis Text Line (741741) / National Domestic Violence Hotline (1-800-799-7233)** — The specific crisis resources named in the Crisis & Referral Protocol, each with its own triage instructions for when to direct a client to it.

**5-4-3-2-1 grounding** — A widely used trauma-informed grounding technique (no single originating source) for acute dissociation or trauma response, engaging five senses in sequence to return a person to present-moment awareness.

**Window of tolerance** — see Part II, Porges cluster. Used in the Crisis & Referral Protocol as the clinical threshold concept for whether wound material is being safely processed in session or is destabilizing daily functioning.

**SI** — Suicidal ideation. See Clinical Presentation & Referral Vocabulary above.

**PTSD, OCD** — see Clinical Presentation & Referral Vocabulary above (PTSD); OCD is Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder, named in connection with religious scrupulosity as a referral-triggering presentation.

**EMDR** — Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing. A specialized trauma-processing therapy named once as an example of clinical treatment CIC coaching cannot provide — used for complex developmental trauma requiring dedicated trauma-focused therapy.

## Editorial Notes — What "Improvements" Means in This Pass

This expansion did more than transcribe what the source documents already say. Specific improvements made:

1. **Pronunciation guides added.** None of the source exegetical documents include pronunciation — every Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek entry above adds one, since a term a practitioner can't say aloud is a term they won't use in session or teaching.
2. **Inflected word-forms consolidated.** Several terms appear across the corpus in multiple grammatical forms tied to different verses (e.g., *logizomai* / *logizesthe*; *phroneō* / *phroneite* / *phronēma*; *katergazomai*'s two conjugations). These are merged into single headwords, since a glossary should organize by root/lemma the way a practitioner would actually look a term up — not by every inflection a Bible verse happens to use.
3. **One attribution inconsistency resolved.** "Spiritual bypassing" is credited to John Welwood in one CIC document and Ken Wilber in another. Welwood (1984) is the term's actual origin; the entry above notes this and flags the inconsistency rather than silently picking one source.
4. **CIC's own extensions distinguished from the frameworks they build on.** Several schema labels used in intake documents don't appear in Jeffrey Young's original 18-schema taxonomy. Rather than presenting them as if they were verbatim Young citations, this glossary marks them explicitly as CIC's own extensions — useful vocabulary, but not something a practitioner should cite back to Young by name.
5. **Plain definitions supplied for terms the source material assumes as background knowledge.** The Clinical Presentation & Referral Vocabulary subsection (dissociation, C-PTSD, flooding, etc.) is used constantly in the Crisis & Referral Protocol as referral triggers, but rarely defined there — the Protocol assumes a licensed audience already knows these terms. This glossary adds working definitions, since the stated purpose here is learning, not just lookup by someone who already knows the field.
6. **Cross-references used instead of duplicate definitions** wherever a term legitimately belongs to more than one part of this document (e.g., ACE, ITC, MI/OARS, IPNB, Window of Tolerance, Correspondence/Compensation Hypothesis) — each is defined fully once and pointed to from elsewhere, rather than repeated with slightly different wording in two places.
7. **Bare theorist-name citations without an independently definable term were deliberately excluded** from Part II, to keep this a glossary of *vocabulary* rather than a duplicate bibliography — that material already lives in [[Covenant Identity — Author & Resource Reference]] and [[Covenant Identity — Author-Framework Mapping, Transmission Mechanisms & Community Diagnostic]].
