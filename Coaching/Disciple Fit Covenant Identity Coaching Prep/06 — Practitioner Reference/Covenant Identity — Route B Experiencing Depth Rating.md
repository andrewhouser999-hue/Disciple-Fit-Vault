# Covenant Identity — Route B Experiencing Depth Rating
*A coach-only, post-session adaptation of Klein, Mathieu, Gendlin & Kiesler's Experiencing (EXP) Scale — for tracking whether Route B felt-sense work is actually landing, session over session*

---

## What This Is Not

**Not the published EXP Scale.** Klein, Mathieu, Gendlin & Kiesler's original is a 7-stage observer-rated instrument with a full research and training manual, used in psychotherapy process research. The 5-level adaptation below is an original condensation of the same underlying construct — depth of contact with felt, bodily experience versus external or intellectualized speech — built for fast, non-clinical coach use. It reproduces the construct's shape, not the manual's actual stage criteria or training materials.

**Not client-facing, ever.** No score, level, or label from this instrument is shown to the client, referenced in session, or used to characterize the client's capacity. It is a private post-session coach note.

**Not the AEDP State Model** (Stage 4b doc, Section VI), which this document sits alongside rather than replaces. The State Model tracks broad affective/relational state across *all* Stage 4b work — defensive processing through transformational affect — and applies to both Route A and Route B. This instrument is narrower: it tracks depth of *felt-sense contact* specifically, the construct Gendlin's Focusing method (Route B's own theoretical basis) is built around. A session can show a State Model transformation marker and a low Experiencing rating, or the reverse — they are measuring related but distinct things. Use both for a Route B session; the State Model alone will not tell you whether the felt-sense sequence itself is working.

**Not an in-session checklist.** Rate after the session, from memory and (if kept) session notes — never while attending to the client. The Stage 4b document's own Failure Mode Guards (Section IX, "Legalism") apply directly here: a coach mentally scoring the client's experiencing level in real time has already broken the unhurried, non-agenda presence Route B depends on.

---

## Why This Exists

The Response Fork (Stage 4b, Section IV) tells the coach *when* to route toward Route B. It does not tell the coach, afterward, whether the felt-sense sequence that followed actually reached felt-sense material or stayed at the surface — intellectualizing about a sensation rather than contacting it. Without a way to track that, a Route B call that isn't landing looks identical, session to session, to a Route B call that's working slowly: both look like "we did the sequence, nothing dramatic happened." This instrument exists to tell those two apart, and to give the Diagnostic Calibration Log something concrete to log as evidence for a Route B call rather than a vague impression.

---

## When to Use

After any session where the Route B sequence (Attend → Handle → Resonate → Ask → Receive) was deployed — whether it completed fully, stalled partway, or was stopped early per the Stop Rule. Rate within the same 24-hour post-session window as the Self-Supervision Template and Diagnostic Calibration Log.

---

## The Five Levels

Rate the **highest level genuinely reached** during the session's Route B work — not the average, not where the session ended if it settled back down afterward.

| Level | What It Looks Like | Rough Correspondence to the Route B Sequence |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — External** | Client talks around the topic — other people, circumstances, what happened — without reference to their own present inner state. The Attend invitation produces a story, not a sensation. | Attend step produces nothing felt; client answers with content instead |
| **2 — Self-Referenced, External** | Client talks about themselves, but in behavioral or intellectualized terms — what they think, what they usually do, a diagnosis of their own pattern — without present-moment felt contact. | Attend produces an analysis ("I think it's because...") rather than a sensation |
| **3 — Named, Not Explored** | A feeling or sensation is named ("there's tightness," "I feel anxious") but the client moves past it quickly — to explanation, to a new topic, or the coach moves on before it's stayed with. | Handle produces a word or image; Resonate is skipped or rushed |
| **4 — Actively Explored** | The client stays with the named sensation, checks whether the word or image actually fits it, lets it develop or shift under attention. Real back-and-forth between attention and the felt quality. | A genuine Handle → Resonate cycle, possibly repeated |
| **5 — Felt Shift** | Something releases, clarifies, or resolves from *within* the felt sense itself — a "that's it" quality, often with a somatic marker (exhale, settling, warmth) or new understanding that arrives from the sensation rather than from reasoning about it. | A completed Resonate → Ask → Receive cycle producing what Gendlin calls a felt shift |

**A session that never leaves Level 1–2 is not automatically a failed session.** Per the Route B document's own language, "an unanswered ask is a complete outcome" — a client who isn't ready to go deeper, correctly held at Level 2 rather than pushed, is not the same finding as a session where the coach rushed past Level 3 or filled silence that needed to stay open. Rate the level *and* note briefly why it stayed there — held appropriately, or missed.

**Reading the trend across sessions with the same client:**

- **Reaching Level 4–5 at least occasionally, with movement over time:** Route B is landing. Log this as confirming evidence in the [[Covenant Identity — Diagnostic Calibration Log]] entry for the original Route B call.
- **Consistently Level 1–2 across three or more Route B attempts:** worth reconsidering the route call itself — this may be material that reads as interior/undifferentiated on the Fork Signals table but is actually a well-defended Stage 4a distortion, or the container isn't safe enough yet for felt-sense attending (check contraindications in Secured in Father's Design Notes before pushing further). Log this as disconfirming or "Still Open, trending toward Revise" in the Calibration Log.
- **Stuck at Level 3 across multiple sessions, consistently "missed" rather than "held appropriately":** the sequence is likely being rushed — check whether Resonate is being given enough silence before moving to Ask, per the Route B table's own "Coach Discipline" column.
- **A single low-level session after a string of Level 4–5 sessions:** not a trend, just a session. Do not revise a route call or a formation read on one data point — this is exactly the single-case overreaction the Diagnostic Calibration Log's rollup guidance warns against.

## The Guard This Instrument Needs Against Itself

Tracking depth of experiencing creates an obvious risk: turning "reach Level 5" into an implicit target, which would corrupt the very posture Route B depends on — non-agenda, non-rescuing, no signal that the client should have produced something by now. This is the same corrosive dynamic named elsewhere in this system when growth or progress becomes something tracked and chased rather than something that emerges (see the Lateral Reroute Signal's caution about growth-seeking becoming compulsive). If reviewing this log starts to produce pressure to move a client toward Level 4–5 faster, that pressure is the problem the log just revealed, not a reason to work harder in session. Treat a low-but-appropriately-held session as an equally good outcome to a Level 5 session — both are documented in the same log, and both are correct uses of Route B.

## Connection Map

**→ Covenant Identity — Diagnostic Calibration Log**
What moves in: this rating is the intended evidence source for a Route B call's "Later evidence" column — a trend of Level 4–5 confirms the call; a persistent Level 1–2 trend is grounds to revise it.

**↔ Covenant Identity — Stage 4b Implicit Level Practitioner Reference (Section VI, AEDP State Model)**
Parallel tracking instrument, not a replacement. The State Model reads broad affective state across all Stage 4b work; this instrument reads felt-sense contact depth specifically within Route B. Use both when reviewing a Route B session — a State 3/4 marker without a corresponding Level 4–5 rating, or the reverse, is itself worth a second look.

**← Covenant Identity — Stage 4b Implicit Level Practitioner Reference (Section IV, Route B Sequence)**
What moves in: the five-step Attend/Handle/Resonate/Ask/Receive structure this rating maps its levels onto. This document does not alter that sequence or its Coach Discipline column — it only adds a way to read, afterward, how far into it the session actually went.
