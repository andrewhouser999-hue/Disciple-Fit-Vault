# Covenant Identity — Attachment Pattern Screener
*Practitioner Reference — early-session instrument for reading attachment pattern before Secured in Father's own self-diagnostic is reached*

**Client-facing use, coach-scored.** The 16 items below are given to the client to complete; the coach scores and interprets them. No results, scores, or category labels are shown back to the client as a diagnosis. Language mirrors the "not fixed categories" posture already established in *Secured in Father*: this names which pattern is active now, not a permanent identity.

---

## What This Is Not

This is **not** the Experiences in Close Relationships scale (Brennan, Clark & Shaver, 1998), its short form (Wei et al., 2007), or any other licensed psychometric instrument. It is not normed against a validated population, does not produce a clinical score, and is not a diagnostic determination. It is an original, coaching-safe instrument built on the same two-dimension construct model (anxiety and avoidance) those instruments measure — the same non-clinical-adaptation move already used elsewhere in this system for schema recognition. Treat its output as a coach's hypothesis to confirm against session observation, not a verdict.

---

## Purpose — the gap this closes

Three places in the CIC system currently need a read on attachment pattern before one reliably exists:

1. **Intake Addendum (Coach), Summary Baseline table** — the "God-attachment pattern" row has no instrument behind it; it is filled from coach inference alone.
2. ***Secured in Father*'s own Self-Diagnostic** — a single-pass, three-paragraph self-identification — is not reached until *after* Phase 1 diagnostic work is specific enough to assign the tool. There is a real gap between Session 1 (when the Intake Addendum needs a value) and whenever *Secured in Father* is later assigned.
3. **Stage 4b's Route A/B fork** uses wound type as a "soft prior" for predicting relational- vs. somatic-presentation material (Diagnostic Lens Transition Logic, Section IV). An early attachment read sharpens that same prediction directly, independent of whether a wound type is confirmed.

This screener fills the Session 1–2 gap with a multi-item read rather than a single inference, and — because it runs both a human-relational form and a God-directed form — it also surfaces something neither existing tool checks for: whether the client's pattern with God matches their pattern with people (**Correspondence**) or diverges from it (**Compensation**). Kirkpatrick's research finds both patterns occur; divergence is not noise, it is data worth a coach note.

---

## When to Deploy

Session 1 or 2, alongside Part B of the Intake Addendum — same timing logic as the felt-sense and present-story items already gathered there. Do not deploy during acute crisis presentation; defer until the client is stable enough for straightforward self-report.

## The Instrument

**Instructions given to the client:** *"Rate each statement from 1 (not at all like me) to 5 (very much like me). Answer with your first, honest reaction rather than how you think you should feel. There are no right answers — this just helps me understand you better."*

### Form H — Close Relationships (people)

| # | Item | Score (1–5) |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | I worry that people I'm close to don't care about me as much as I care about them. | |
| H2 | I find myself needing reassurance from people close to me more than they seem to need it from me. | |
| H3 | When someone I depend on seems distant or distracted, I can't stop thinking about it until things feel resolved. | |
| H4 | I'm afraid that if people really knew me, they'd pull away. | |
| H5 | I'd rather handle things on my own than let someone else in on how hard it is. | |
| H6 | I get uncomfortable when someone wants to get closer than I'm used to. | |
| H7 | I find it hard to ask directly for what I need from people close to me. | |
| H8 | I tend to keep some part of myself back, even in relationships I trust. | |

### Form G — God

| # | Item | Score (1–5) |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | I worry that God is disappointed in me even when I can't point to anything specific I've done wrong. | |
| G2 | I find myself needing repeated reassurance that God is actually pleased with me. | |
| G3 | When I don't sense God's presence, I assume something is wrong between us. | |
| G4 | I'm afraid that if God saw the real me, He'd pull back. | |
| G5 | I approach God more as something to figure out or perform for than someone to actually be close to. | |
| G6 | I keep a working distance from God — engaged, but not fully open. | |
| G7 | I find it hard to bring God my actual need, rather than a cleaned-up version of it. | |
| G8 | Prayer feels more like a discipline I maintain than a relationship I'm in. | |

**Item-to-subscale key** (not shown to client): items 1–4 in each form = Anxiety subscale; items 5–8 in each form = Avoidance subscale. This mirrors the two-dimension structure Brennan, Clark & Shaver established: anxiety (fear of insufficient closeness/response) and avoidance (discomfort with closeness/dependence) as the two axes that generate the named patterns, not two of several equally-weighted item clusters.

---

## Scoring & Interpretation

**Step 1 — Subscale averages.** For each form, average items 1–4 (Anxiety) and average items 5–8 (Avoidance) separately. Range: 1.0–5.0 per subscale, per form.

**Step 2 — Quadrant read.** Use 3.5 as a relative working threshold, not a normed cutoff — it marks "more true than not," nothing more. Cross the two subscale scores:

| Anxiety | Avoidance | Pattern (CIC terminology) |
|---|---|---|
| Below 3.5 | Below 3.5 | Secure-leaning |
| 3.5 or above | Below 3.5 | Anxious / Preoccupied |
| Below 3.5 | 3.5 or above | Dismissing-Avoidant |
| 3.5 or above | 3.5 or above | Fearful-Avoidant — check for the Disorganized variant below |

Run this separately for Form H and Form G. A client will often land in different quadrants on each form — that is expected, not an error.

**Threshold flagged for revisit.** 3.5 was chosen as a starting midpoint-plus-margin, not derived from any client data — this screener has no field use yet. Once it has been run with a handful of clients, check it against what session observation independently confirms: if clients who read Secure-leaning at 3.5 keep showing insecure-pattern material in session (threshold too high — screener under-flagging), or if most clients cross 3.5 on at least one subscale regardless of how they actually present (threshold too low — screener over-flagging), adjust the number. Track this in coach notes across the first several administrations rather than changing it on a single case.

**Step 3 — Correspondence or Compensation.**

- **Same quadrant on both forms (Correspondence):** the God-relationship carries the same organizing pattern as human relationships. This is the more common finding (Kirkpatrick's Correspondence Hypothesis) and confirms the human-relational pattern as the working hypothesis for the God-attachment field too.
- **Different quadrants (Compensation or divergence):** genuinely diagnostic on its own. A client Secure-leaning with people but Fearful-Avoidant toward God may be carrying a God-representation distortion (see God-Representation Deepening Tool) independent of their general relational capacity — do not assume the human-relational read explains the God-relational one. The reverse pattern — insecure with people, more secure-reading toward God — can reflect either genuine compensatory reliance on God or an idealized/performed God-relationship not yet tested by real disclosure; the two are not distinguishable from the screener alone and require session observation (God-Exclusion Map, Lament Capacity from Intake A7) to sort out.

**Step 4 — Record.** Enter the Form G quadrant in the Intake Addendum's "God-attachment pattern" row. Note the Form H quadrant and Correspondence/Compensation finding in the coach's B4 synthesis statement or session notes — the Intake Addendum table has no dedicated row for the human-relational read or the divergence finding, so it does not survive into the permanent baseline unless captured in prose.

## Freeze / Numb Presentation — Read Before Finalizing a Fearful-Avoidant Call

A client whose actual presentation is disorganized (freeze, blank, "I don't know," numbness rather than conscious oscillation) may score moderately on *both* subscales without clearly crossing 3.5 on either — the numbness suppresses the felt intensity the items are trying to capture, producing a flatter profile than a true Fearful-Avoidant reading. This is the same phenomenon *Secured in Father*'s Design Notes name under "Design note — disorganized presentation within the Fearful-Avoidant category": disorganized attachment involves collapse of approach strategies, not felt ambivalence between them, so it under-reports on instruments built around felt experience. If session observation shows freeze/blank/impossible language rather than conscious pull-both-directions language, treat that as overriding a flat or ambiguous score — route to the Disorganized variant regardless of what the numbers show.

## Practitioner Cautions

- **Confirm against observation before recording.** This screener produces a hypothesis, not a determination. Cross-check the quadrant read against Somatic Observation, Energy and Affect Tracking, and early Affections Discovery material before entering it as the Intake Addendum's baseline value. If the screener and session observation disagree, session observation governs — the screener is a first-pass instrument completed once, early, without the benefit of watching the client in the room.
- **Not a substitute for *Secured in Father*'s own Self-Diagnostic.** When that tool is later assigned, the client will re-identify their pattern experientially, working through the actual pattern descriptions rather than rating items. If the two reads disagree by then, that disagreement is itself diagnostic — either the pattern has genuinely shifted, or the earlier screener read was off and should be revised, not silently overwritten.
- **Not a clinical assessment instrument.** Do not present results to the client as a diagnosis, do not use clinical attachment-disorder language, and do not let a Fearful-Avoidant or Compensation finding substitute for the Character Wound Diagnostic Tool's own injury-vs-diffuse-conditioning check — attachment pattern and character wound type are correlated, not identical, diagnostic layers.
- **A single low-effort or defended completion will look artificially secure.** A client answering from the same self-presentation management that shows up in session (minimizing, intellectualizing, answering how they think they should) will score low on both subscales without actually being secure. Weigh this against A5/A6/A8 from the Tally intake and the felt-sense description in Intake Addendum B1 — a Secure-leaning screener result alongside globalized shame language (A8) or a performed, "clean" God-description (A5) is a mismatch worth naming, not a settled finding.

## Connection Map

**→ Covenant Identity — Intake Addendum (Coach)**
What moves in: the Form G quadrant populates the "God-attachment pattern" row in the Summary Baseline table, which currently has no instrument behind it. The Form H quadrant and any Correspondence/Compensation finding have no dedicated row and must be captured in the B4 synthesis statement or session notes to survive into the permanent baseline record.

**→ Secured in Father — Practitioner Design Notes / Secured in Father — Attachment Formation & Encounter Preparation**
What moves in: an early, provisional pattern read that can inform *when* and *which version* of *Secured in Father* to prepare for assigning, well before Phase 1 work is specific enough to trigger that tool's own deployment criteria. This screener's read is superseded, not confirmed, by the client's later self-diagnostic within that document — see Practitioner Cautions above.

**→ Covenant Identity — Diagnostic Lens Transition Logic (Stage 4b Response Fork)**
What moves in: a second, independent soft prior for the Route A/B fork alongside Character Wound type. A client scoring Anxious or Fearful-Avoidant on Form H is more likely to present Route A (relationally enacted) material at Stage 4b; a client scoring Dismissing-Avoidant is more likely to present diffuse, Route B material. This is a soft prior only — the live signal read in session (Section IV's Fork Signals table) always governs over either prior when the two conflict.
