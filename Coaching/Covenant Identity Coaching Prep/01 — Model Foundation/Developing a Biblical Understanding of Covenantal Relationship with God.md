# Developing a Biblical Understanding of Covenantal Relationship with God
## Research Output — Approaches, Framework & Coaching Integration

**What this is:** A practitioner research document surveying the best approaches to build a rigorous, deployable understanding of God's covenantally structured relationship with believers — grounded in exegesis, organized for coach development, and applicable to identity formation and client work.
**Who it's for:** Andrew as Disciple Fit practitioner and curriculum builder. Not client-facing in this form.
**Date:** 2026-04-28 (first built) · revised 2026-10-18
**Status:** Living governing reference. Where this document and the [[Covenant Identity Coaching — Practice Definition]] conflict, the Practice Definition governs. The Revision Log at the end records what changed on 2026-10-18 and why.

## Advisor Flag — Framing Check

Before answering the question asked, the framing needs a challenge.

"Covenantal Identity" as a coaching category is only as strong as the theological claim underneath it. The risk in this space: importing "covenant" as inspirational vocabulary without the exegetical structure to back it. Clients will feel the weakness — not as a theological critique but as *it doesn't hold weight when life is hard.*

The other risk runs the opposite direction: building a system so academically rigorous it can't be communicated to a 22-year-old in a coaching session.

The question is well-formed *only if* the goal is genuine, defensible, exegetically grounded covenantal theology — not a framework dressed in covenant language. Assuming that's the intent, proceeding.

## Part 1 — Biblical Research Framework

### 1. Text & Context — What "Covenant" Actually Is

The Hebrew *berith* (בְּרִית) appears ~285 times in the OT. It denotes a solemn, binding agreement ratified by oath and usually by ceremony (oath-swearing, cutting of animals, shared meal, blood). The phrase *karat berith* — "to cut a covenant" — reflects the ancient practice of cutting animals and walking between the halves (Jer 34:18-20 describes the practice most directly; cf. Gen 15), invoking self-curse: "If I break this, may what happened to these animals happen to me."

**Critical distinction:** Not all covenants are structurally identical.

- **Suzerain-vassal treaties** (Mosaic covenant) — Great King initiates; vassals obligate themselves to conditions; blessings/curses are bilateral. Parallels Hittite treaty form (Meredith Kline's seminal work, *Treaty of the Great King*, 1963). Kline wrote from within Reformed covenant theology. This document uses his treaty-form findings only, not his wider covenant-of-works framework. **Conditionally structured.**
- **Royal grant covenants** (Abrahamic, Davidic) — King grants land or dynasty to a servant whose loyalty he rewards, and the King's own oath secures the grant (Moshe Weinfeld's "covenant of grant," 1970). The granting party carries the weight of the promise. **Grant-structured, with a human side.** The Abrahamic text gives the covenant a human side: Abraham is to walk blamelessly and circumcise his household (Gen 17:1, 9-14; 18:19), and the oath and its renewal are given because of his obedience (Gen 22:16; 26:5). The Davidic grant carries conditions for David's sons (1 Kgs 2:4; Ps 132:12). "Unconditional" is therefore not an accurate label for either covenant. What the grant form secures is that God Himself carries the promise to fulfillment.

This matters enormously for identity work. God alone walked between the pieces (Gen 15:17 — a smoking pot and flaming torch, with Abraham in deep sleep). In the practice Jer 34:18-20 describes, the party who passes between the pieces takes the self-curse on himself. The most natural reading is therefore that God binds Himself to the promise. Gen 15 does not state this. It is an inference from the practice (*Strong inference*). The human conditions were then met by the covenant Seed. Rom 8:32 ("He who did not spare his own Son") echoes Gen 22:12 and 22:16 ("you have not withheld your son"), and Paul uses the same verb the Septuagint uses there.

**The New Covenant** (Jer 31:31-34; Ezek 36:25-27; Luke 22:20; Heb 8-10) — made with the house of Israel and the house of Judah, ratified by Christ's blood, and entered by Jew and Gentile alike through union with the Messiah (Gal 3:26-29; Eph 2:11-19). It replaces the Sinai covenant as the believer's governing covenant, writes God's instruction on the heart by the Spirit, fulfills the Abrahamic promise of blessing to the nations, and establishes the Davidic King's reign. God initiates it. Christ is both its mediator and its faithful covenant partner (Heb 9:15; Rom 5:19), and the Spirit works it into the believer. Faith, repentance, and Spirit-enabled obedience are the believer's response, never the ground of membership. It is the covenant under which believers now live, resting on the Abrahamic promise it fulfills.

**Standing and perseverance — the practice's position.** A believer's standing in Christ cannot be lost through failure, sin, doubt, or fluctuating feeling. It rests on Christ's faithfulness, not on the believer's performance. This is the claim the coaching work needs, and it is deliberately narrower than the full doctrine of perseverance. Whether a person who finally and willfully renounces Christ was ever truly in, or can fall out, is a disputed question (Heb 6:4-6; 10:26-31). Baptist and Reformed traditions generally hold that true believers persevere. Wesleyan and Arminian traditions generally hold that a believer can fall away through persistent unbelief. This practice leaves the question unresolved on purpose, because the formation work does not depend on settling it. The coach does not adjudicate it. A client who raises it is referred to their pastor or church tradition.

**High-leverage insight for coaching:** *Hesed* is among the most important terms for a practitioner. It names love that is committed and faithful, not temperamental: God's faithfulness to the client does not rise and fall with the client's performance or with God's mood. One caution applies, from the Theory of Change. Understanding the word registers in explicit memory. It does not by itself change how the client experiences God at the implicit level (Nodes 5-6). The word gives the practitioner a precise vocabulary and gives the client a pointer. It is not a mechanism.

### 3. Historical-Cultural Context

Ancient Near Eastern covenant context is irreplaceable for this question:

- **Suzerainty treaty structure** (Hittite, Assyrian) — Preamble (who I am), historical prologue (what I've done for you), stipulations (what you must do), deposit/public reading, witnesses, blessings/curses. The Mosaic Torah follows this structure closely (Meredith Kline, *The Structure of Biblical Authority*, 1975). The dating of the closest parallels is disputed. Kline and Mendenhall point to second-millennium Hittite treaties. Scholars who date Deuteronomy later (for example Weinfeld) stress first-millennium Neo-Assyrian ones. The historical prologue grounds the demanded loyalty in the sovereign's past benefaction, which supports "obligation follows gift." The further step to "identity comes before behavior" is made by the New Testament (Rom 5:8; Eph 2:8-10), not by the treaty form alone. The original audience would have recognized this immediately as a political document — the Great King of the universe formally binding himself to a vassal people.
- **Covenant meals** — Shared meals sealed covenants (Gen 31:54; Ex 24:11; the Passover; the Lord's Supper). The covenant is not only believed — it is embodied and enacted. This has direct coaching implications for embodied identity work.
- **Covenant name** — To be named within a covenant is to have a new identity. Abram → Abraham. Jacob → Israel. Simon → Peter. Saul → Paul. Name change = identity change = covenant installation. This maps directly to Covenant Identity work.

### 4. Intertextual Connections — The Single Covenantal Thread

The framework this practice adopts for the covenants' unity is **Progressive Covenantalism** (Gentry & Wellum, *Kingdom through Covenant*, 2nd ed. 2018 — the most detailed current academic statement of that position). It is influential and contested. Reviewers from Reformed covenant theology (for example Harrison Perkins) and from dispensationalism (for example Michael Vlach) dispute its handling of the Abrahamic covenant, typology, and Israel. The argument:

- Each covenant *advances* the one before. The Abrahamic promise is carried into every later covenant, and the Mosaic covenant is brought to its end in Christ (Gal 3:25; Heb 8:13)
- The Abrahamic covenant is the mother promise; every subsequent covenant is a development of its three components: seed, land, blessing
- The Mosaic covenant is a temporary covenant (Gal 3:19-25) — a guardian until Christ
- The Davidic covenant installs the king through whom the Abrahamic promises come
- The New Covenant is the *fulfillment* of all prior covenants in Christ

Key intertextual backbone:
- **Gen 12:1-3 → Gal 3:8** — Paul calls Gen 12 "the gospel preached in advance." The Abrahamic promise IS the gospel.
- **Ex 19:4-6 → 1 Pet 2:9-10** — "Kingdom of priests, holy nation" is applied to the church in Christ. Peter's audience is debated (Gentile or mixed churches), and Paul applies Hosea's "not my people" language to Gentiles in the same way (Rom 9:24-26). The church shares in Israel's covenant identity through Christ. It does not replace Israel (Rom 9:4; 11:17-29).
- **Jer 31:31-34 → Heb 8:6-13** — The New Covenant makes the first covenant "obsolete" (Heb 8:13). The fault lies with the people (Heb 8:8), not with the law (Rom 7:12). The Abrahamic promise is not made obsolete (Gal 3:17).
- **Ezek 36:25-27 → John 3:5; Acts 2** — The Spirit poured out is New Covenant fulfillment beginning in real time. John 3:5 ("born of water and the Spirit") is the closer echo of Ezek 36:25-27. Peter explains Pentecost with Joel 2 (Acts 2:16-21), not Ezek 36, so the Ezekiel link in Acts 2 is inferential. It rests on the shared promise of the Spirit (Ezek 36:27; Gal 3:14) and on Acts 15:8-9, where Gentiles receive the same Spirit.
- **Gen 15 → Rom 4** — Abraham's faith credited as righteousness, *before* circumcision. Paul's point: the covenant is older than the law, older than the ritual sign. It is faith-structured from the beginning.

### 5. Theological Weight

Three load-bearing theological claims that everything else rests on:

**Claim 1: The covenant is the structure of grace, not a transaction.**
God initiates. God obligates Himself. God ratifies the covenant in blood — the Son's blood (Luke 22:20; Heb 9:15-18). The believer enters by faith — not by performance. Covenant identity is received, not earned.

**Claim 2: Union with Christ is the mechanism of New Covenant participation.**
"In Christ" and its variants ("in him," "in the Lord") are Paul's most common way of locating the believer. Adolf Deissmann counted about 164 occurrences of the "in Christ" formula (1892), and counts vary with what is included. Constantine Campbell shows that the language carries several senses (union, participation, identification, and incorporation). "Covenantal location" is therefore this document's reading of one strand, not the whole of Paul's usage. On that reading, to be in Christ is to be inside the covenant, because Christ is both its mediator (Heb 9:15) and its faithful covenant partner (Rom 5:19). The believer's covenantal standing is Christ's standing, credited by union.

**Claim 3: The covenant is the ground of both identity and expectation.**
God's covenantal faithfulness (*hesed*) is the basis for both who you are and what you can count on. Covenant identity is not self-generated — it is received from the covenant relationship and confirmed by the covenant community.

**The last point is the most coaching-critical.** Many Covenant Identity frameworks borrow OT provision promises wholesale without accounting for the covenantal shift at Calvary. The safer and more defensible move is a mediation rule. A Mosaic-era promise reaches the New Covenant believer only where the New Testament itself applies it in light of Christ (Heb 13:5 applies Deut 31:6; Eph 6:2-3 applies the fifth commandment's promise). Promises the New Testament states in its own right can be used directly, with their context kept in view. Rom 8:32 and Rom 8:35-39 are addressed to believers. Phil 4:19 is addressed to a church that had supported Paul's ministry (Phil 4:14-18). Matt 6:25-34 was spoken to disciples before the cross; it is kingdom teaching and should be used with that context named. Rooting provision in the Abrahamic promise is valid for the blessing and the Spirit (Gal 3:14). It is not a guarantee of material provision, and Rom 8:35-39 itself lists famine, nakedness, and danger among the things believers face. This approach is more defensible than applying Mosaic texts directly, and each promise still needs its own check against its context.

### Approach 1 — Redemptive-Historical Biblical Theology Method
`[CURRICULUM]` `[SELF-COACH]`

**What it is:** Reading the whole Bible as one unified story, tracing the progressive unfolding of God's redemptive covenant from creation → fall → redemption → new creation. Each covenant is a chapter in one story, not isolated events.

**Why it works theologically:** This is the methodology Jesus himself uses (Luke 24:25-27, 44-47 — "beginning with Moses and all the Prophets, he explained what was said in all the Scriptures concerning himself"). Paul uses it throughout Romans and Galatians. It is the native reading strategy of the NT authors.

**Best primary resources:**
- Gentry & Wellum, *Kingdom through Covenant* (2018, 2nd ed.) — **The most detailed current academic statement of progressive covenantalism** (Baptist). Influential and contested: Reformed and dispensational reviewers dispute its handling of the Abrahamic covenant, typology, and Israel.
- Thomas Schreiner, *Covenant and God's Purpose for the World* (2017) — accessible academic entry point into progressive covenantalism.
- Greg Beale, *A New Testament Biblical Theology* (2011) — NT fulfillment of OT covenant themes; comprehensive. Reformed and amillennial: his conclusions on Israel and the church are not adopted wholesale here.
- Geerhardus Vos, *Biblical Theology* (1948) — foundational for the whole approach; dated but irreplaceable. Reformed: use him for method, not for his covenant-of-works and covenant-of-grace system.

**For Andrew:** Study *Schreiner* first (shorter, accessible), then *Gentry & Wellum* for depth. Vos for historical grounding. This approach gives Andrew the spine that holds everything else together.

**Verdict: Essential. Non-negotiable foundation for the practitioner. Cannot build a defensible Covenantal Identity framework without it.**

### Approach 2 — Covenant Word Study (Lexical-Exegetical Method)
`[CURRICULUM]` `[SELF-COACH]`

**What it is:** Deep study of the key covenant terms — *berith*, *hesed*, *emunah*, *diatheke* — tracking them through Scripture with lexicons and commentaries.

**Why it works:** The vocabulary carries the theology. When a practitioner doesn't know what *hesed* actually means, they are working with a diluted concept that won't hold psychological weight.

**Tools:**
- *HALOT* (Koehler–Baumgartner) — the current standard Hebrew lexicon. Brown-Driver-Briggs (BDB, 1906) is older but still widely used.
- BDAG (Bauer-Danker-Arndt-Gingrich) — standard NT Greek lexical reference
- Katharine Doob Sakenfeld, *The Meaning of Hesed in the Hebrew Bible: A New Inquiry* (1978) — a major monograph on *hesed*. She reads it as free acts of help within a relationship, against Nelson Glueck's covenant-obligation reading (1927). Robin Routledge (*Tyndale Bulletin*, 1995) argues obligation is primary. Read all three.
- John Oswalt, *The Bible Among the Myths* (2009) — contextualizes Israelite covenant theology against ANE background.

**For coaching deployment:** The lexical work stays at the practitioner level. But the *results* become the vocabulary of the coaching system. "God's hesed is not affection — it's oath-bound loyalty" is a coaching statement. The study behind it is what makes it land with weight.

**Verdict: High value for Andrew. Medium effort, high return.**

### Approach 3 — ANE (Ancient Near Eastern) Treaty Background Study
`[CURRICULUM]` `[SELF-COACH]`

**What it is:** Studying the Hittite suzerainty treaty structure and its parallels to the Mosaic covenant; understanding the royal grant form and its parallel to Abrahamic/Davidic covenants.

**Why it works:** The original audience heard covenant documents through this cultural grid. Understanding it unlocks the full weight of what is being claimed — the God of the universe is formally adopting the structures of international law to bind himself to a people.

**Key resource:**
- Meredith Kline, *Treaty of the Great King* (1963) and *The Structure of Biblical Authority* (1975) — foundational. Shows Deuteronomy as a treaty document structurally. Kline wrote from within Reformed covenant theology. Use his treaty-form findings, not his wider covenant-of-works framework.
- Paul Kalluveettil, *Declaration and Covenant* (1982) — broader ANE covenant comparative study.

**For Andrew:** This is practitioner-level depth, not client-facing. It answers "why does the covenant have this structure?" with exegetical precision rather than intuition.

**Verdict: High value for credibility and precision; not urgent for initial deployment. Medium priority.**

**Extended 2026-07-21 — Layer 11, "Covenant Identity Coaching — Theological-Formation Contributor Stack.md" (same folder):** That document carries this approach further, into the doctrine of canon itself and the mechanism of felt (not just asserted) authority — Kline's own words ("Biblical canon is covenantal canon"), Mendenhall's framing of the prophets as covenant-lawsuit prosecutors, and Frame/Vanhoozer/Packer on why a declaration is an act of authority, not just a report about God. It also carries a standing caution worth noting here: the treaty form's "no standing to contest" language must not be read as excluding the lament and protest Scripture itself sanctions within the covenant (Abraham, Moses, Job, the lament Psalms, Habakkuk) — read that document's caution note before using this material with a client, not just for the historical background.

### Approach 4 — Narrative Immersion (Story-Based Entry)
`[CURRICULUM]` `[BIBLE-SESSION]`

**What it is:** Entering the covenantal story as a participant rather than an observer. Framing the believer's life as a continuation of the one covenant story of Scripture — not a reader of the story but a character within it.

**Biblical grounding:** This is Paul's argument in Galatians 3-4 and Romans 4. "If you belong to Christ, then you are Abraham's seed, and heirs according to the promise" (Gal 3:29). Not metaphorically heirs — actually heirs. The covenant story is now your story.

**N.T. Wright's "5-act drama" framework** (*The New Testament and the People of God*, 1992) is useful here: Creation → Fall → Israel → Jesus → Church/New Creation. The believer lives in Act 5, improvising faithfully within the established story.

**Why it works psychologically:** Narrative identity theory (Dan McAdams, *The Stories We Live By*, 1993 — **Established**) demonstrates that identity is story-shaped. People know who they are by what story they inhabit. McAdams's research links coherent, redemptive *personal* narratives with greater resilience and wellbeing. That research is correlational and concerns personal life stories, not adopting a religious story. In this practice, entering the covenant story is the narrative side of formation into a positional identity the Spirit has already established (Practice Definition, Dimension 1). It does not create that identity.

**Coaching application:** This is highly deployable with clients. "You're not just reading Abraham's story — you're in it. In Christ you are Abraham's heir. The God who bound Himself to Abraham with an oath has bound Himself to you in Christ, whose blood ratified the promise." This is a reframe with deep roots. Two cautions. First, do not say "the same promise" without naming which part. The blessing and the Spirit (Gal 3:14) and the inheritance of the world (Rom 4:13) come to believers. The land and descendants promises are fulfilled in Christ and the new creation. They are not repeated to the client individually. Second, do not call the Abrahamic covenant "unconditional" (see Part 1, §1). The security the client can rest on is that Christ met the covenant's conditions (Rom 8:32; Heb 9:15).

**Verdict: Highest client-facing value. Directly connects to identity formation work.**

### Approach 5 — In-Depth Study of the "In Christ" Formulations (Pauline Covenant Location)
`[CURRICULUM]` `[BIBLE-FRAMEWORK]` `[SELF-COACH]`

**What it is:** Systematic study of the "in Christ / in him / in the Lord" phrases in Paul's letters (Deissmann counted about 164 of the "in Christ" formula; counts vary), reading them, in this practice's use, as covenantal location markers — not devotional language but theological precision.

**Why it's the New Covenant mechanism:** In the OT, covenant location was territorial (the land), genealogical (Abraham's seed), and ritual (circumcision, temple, Passover). In the New Covenant, the location is *personal*: in Christ. Christ is the covenant. To be in him is to have his covenantal standing.

**Key texts to study:**
- Romans 8:1 — "No condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus" — covenantal legal verdict
- Ephesians 1:3-14 — Every spiritual blessing "in the heavenly realms in Christ" — inventory of covenant benefits
- 2 Corinthians 5:17 — "In Christ, new creation" — ontological covenantal identity shift
- Galatians 2:20 — "I have been crucified with Christ; I no longer live, but Christ lives in me" — mutual indwelling as covenant union
- Colossians 3:1-4 — "Your life is hidden with Christ in God" — security as covenantal hiddenness

**Best resource:** Constantine Campbell, *Paul and Union with Christ* (2012) — comprehensive scholarly survey of the "in Christ" language. Rigorous and accessible.

**Coaching application:** This is the theological backbone of Covenant Identity declarations. Every declaration should be traceable to a specific "in Christ" reality. "I am forgiven" is sentimental without the covenantal ground: "In Christ, I have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins" (Eph 1:7). The "in Christ" anchor transforms declarations from affirmations (self-generated) to covenant claims (externally grounded).

**Verdict: Critical for the Covenant Identity system specifically. High priority.**

### Approach 6 — Declaration and Embodied Covenant Practice
`[BIBLE-SESSION]` `[CLIENT-TOOL]` `[SESSION-TOOL]`

**What it is:** Regular, repeated declaration of covenantal realities as a formational practice — not mere affirmation but covenant-speech: speaking what is true about who God has declared you to be.

**Biblical grounding:**
- Deuteronomy 6:4-9 (*Shema*) — The words are to be on the heart, taught to children, talked about through the day, and bound as signs on hand, forehead, and doorposts. Reciting the Shema morning and evening is a later Jewish practice built on 6:7. The pattern is repeated, embodied, communal covenant memory. The repetition serves memory of who God is and what He has done. It is not self-persuasion.
- Psalm 103:1-5 — "Forget not all his benefits" — deliberate covenant memory rehearsal
- Self-address patterns documented elsewhere in the vault: Ps 42:5, 11; Lam 3:21-24; Jas 1:22-24 (see [[Covenant Identity — The Biblical Pattern of Self-Declaration — Practitioner Exegetical Reference]]). Rev 12:11 is not used as a ground for declaration. "The word of their testimony" (*marturia*: witness, testimony, martyrdom) is the martyrs' witness to Christ under persecution, and "weapon" language edges toward the manifestation framing the Practice Definition rejects.
- Romans 10:9-10 — Confession (*homologeō*: confess, profess, acknowledge publicly; "say the same thing as" is an etymological gloss, not the word's main sense). Public profession of Jesus as Lord is part of covenant entry. This grounds public profession, not daily self-declaration.

**Psychological grounding:**
- **Affirmations literature caveat:** Generic positive affirmations have limited evidence. Wood et al. (2009, *Psychological Science*) found that positive self-statements made people with low self-esteem feel worse. Two later replications (Flynn & Bordieri, 2020; about 225 and 237 participants) did not reproduce the effect, so treat the backfire finding as **Contested**. The practical caution remains as a clinical hypothesis: a statement the client does not feel can deepen the sense of gap (Theory of Change, Node 7). Covenant declarations differ by design: they are *externally grounded* (in what God says), not internally generated (what the person wishes were true). That grounding is a design intention. It has not been shown to prevent the gap effect, and the client still says the words aloud.
- **Neuroplasticity** — Donald Hebb's principle ("neurons that fire together wire together") is a popular shorthand for associative learning. Hanson, *Hardwiring Happiness* (2013 — **Supported**; popular-level synthesis) applies it to savoring positive experiences, not to reciting statements. This document does not claim that repeating declarations reshapes identity circuits. The Theory of Change (Node 7) holds the opposite caution: repetition alone reaches explicit memory, and consolidation comes through receptive return.
- **Narrative therapy** (White & Epston, *Narrative Means to Therapeutic Ends*, 1990) offers a partial parallel: re-authoring a dominant story by thickening an alternative one. The parallel stops there. Narrative therapy takes a decentered stance in which the client authors the alternative story. Covenant work brings an authoritative story the client receives. Use the vocabulary for description, not as grounding.
- **ACT (Acceptance and Commitment Therapy)** — The overlap is values and committed action (Hayes, *Get Out of Your Mind and Into Your Life*, 2005 — **Established** as a therapy). ACT is values-neutral by design (the client chooses the values) and treats replacing one thought with another as another form of control, so it does not ground declaration practice. Covenant identity supplies a values content that ACT deliberately leaves open. That is a difference in design, not a borrowed mechanism.

**Coaching application:** The Identity Installer and Lie Eliminator are already in this space. The coaching refinement: declarations must be exegetically traceable, not constructed. The coach's job is helping the client locate the specific covenantal truth that counters the specific lie — not generating feel-good statements. Between-session design follows the Theory of Change (Node 7): assign receptive return (speak the declaration before the Father, then be still and ask the Spirit to make it real), not affirmation repetition. See [[Covenant Identity — Matched Declaration Architecture]].

**Verdict: Already in the system. Needs rigorous exegetical backing to remain defensible. High priority to audit existing declarations for textual grounding (no completed audit was found in a vault search on 2026-10-18).**

### Approach 7 — Covenant Promise Mapping (Systematic Inventory)
`[CURRICULUM]` `[SELF-COACH]` `[BIBLE-FRAMEWORK]`

**What it is:** Building a systematic map of New Covenant promises organized by category: identity, provision, protection, relationship, purpose, future. Each promise traced to its covenantal source (which covenant, which mechanism, how it applies under the New Covenant).

**Why it matters:** Prevents the common error of applying Mosaic promises directly to New Covenant believers without accounting for Christ's fulfillment. Builds a defensible promise inventory that survives theological scrutiny.

**Categories to map:**
1. **Identity** — Who am I in covenant (child, heir, priest, new creation, friend)
2. **Access** — My standing before God (boldness at the throne, Heb 4:16; no condemnation, Rom 8:1)
3. **Provision** — God's material/spiritual provision (Phil 4:19, Rom 8:32, Matt 6:25-34)
4. **Protection** — God's covenantal preservation (Rom 8:38-39, John 10:28-29; Ps 91 is a psalm promise that Satan quoted in Matt 4:6, so use it with care). Preservation is not exemption from suffering (Rom 8:35-39).
5. **Purpose** — Covenant calling and gifting (Eph 2:10, 1 Pet 2:9, 1 Cor 12)
6. **Community** — Life within the covenant people (Heb 10:24-25, 1 Cor 12, Eph 4)
7. **Future** — Inheritance, resurrection, new creation (Rom 8:18-25, 1 Pet 1:3-5, Rev 21-22)

**For each promise:** (a) text, (b) which covenant it belongs to, (c) how it applies under the New Covenant, (d) what it means for the client today.

**Verdict: High value as a practitioner reference and authorship resource. Built: see [[Covenant Identity — New Covenant Promise Map 1]] (2026-05-21) and [[Covenant Identity — New Covenant Promises — Comprehensive Reference]]. Each promise should still be checked against the mediation rule in Part 1, §6.**

### Approach 8 — Covenant Community Formation (Ecclesial Approach)
`[CURRICULUM]` `[BIBLE-SESSION]`

**What it is:** Understanding that covenant identity is never solo — it is always located within a covenant people. Individual identity formation happens within corporate covenant community.

**Biblical grounding:**
- The covenant is always made with a people, not just individuals. Even when God addresses Abraham, the covenant is about becoming a people.
- 1 Peter 2:9-10 — The covenantal identity markers (royal priesthood, holy nation, God's own possession) are *plural*. They cannot be held individually.
- Ephesians 4:11-16 — The body builds itself up in love. Covenant identity is formed and maintained in community.
- Hebrews 10:24-25 — "Not giving up meeting together" is a covenant community obligation, not an optional spiritual discipline.

**Psychological grounding:**
- **Attachment theory** (Bowlby, Ainsworth) — Secure attachment requires consistent, responsive, available relational figures. The covenant community functions as a human scaffold for the secure attachment God provides. **Established** (decades of empirical support).
- **Social identity theory** (Tajfel & Turner, 1979 — **Established**) — Group membership is a core component of self-concept. The covenant community provides the group identity within which personal covenant identity is formed and confirmed.
- **Interpersonal neurobiology** (Siegel, *The Developing Mind*, 2012 — **Supported**) — The brain is a social organ; identity is co-constructed in relationship.

**Coaching application:** For clients without a covenant community (local church), the identity work has a structural vulnerability. The coach is not a replacement for the covenant community — name this explicitly. Part of the formation arc should address community rootedness with the client as a covenant identity anchor. The coach names it early and explores it with the client. It is a formation need to cooperate with, not a rule the coach imposes.

**Verdict: Often overlooked in identity-focused coaching systems. Important structural piece, especially for isolated clients.**

## Part 3 — Coaching Translation

### Why an Externally Grounded Identity Holds — a Psychological Description (Subordinate)

*Scope note (2026-10-18): This section describes, in psychological terms, what the Practice Definition's five-layer mechanism produces. It does not replace that mechanism. The Spirit is the primary agent (Layer 1). The coaching relationship, diagnostic work, applied covenant truth, and between-session disciplines create conditions (Layers 2-5). The theories below describe what happens. They do not cause transformation.*

Covenant identity addresses a problem of where the self-concept is grounded:

- **Self-generated identity** — "I am who I decide I am." Fragile under threat, failure, or loss. It requires constant maintenance.
- **Externally grounded identity** — "I am who God has declared me to be, and He has bound Himself to that in covenant." Load-bearing because the ground is outside the self and outside circumstance.

Two partial parallels from psychology, offered as description only. **Self-Determination Theory** (Deci & Ryan, 2000 — **Established** as a theory) describes how people take values that originate outside them into their own sense of self, most fully when the surrounding relationships support their agency. The parallel to covenant identity is partial. SDT does not claim that stable identity requires an external source, and in SDT "autonomy" means willing endorsement, not independence. Controlling environments undermine internalization. That is why the coach's posture is non-contingent regard and not pressure.

The second parallel is **attachment theory's secure base** (Bowlby; Ainsworth — **Established**). A client who experiences God as reliably, responsively faithful (*hesed*) has the kind of base that supports exploration, resilience, and risk-taking. A client stuck in anxious or avoidant patterns may *intellectually* affirm covenant truth while the felt, implicit expectation of God (the "internal working model," in attachment terms) still runs on an older pattern. Closing that gap is the aim of Nodes 4-6 in the Theory of Change. It is formation work, not attachment repair. A client whose pattern points to trauma needing treatment is referred under [[Covenant Identity — Crisis & Referral Protocol]].

### What Must Be True for This to Land with a Client

The client needs to understand (this registers in explicit memory; it is necessary and not sufficient, see below):
1. **What a covenant is** (not a contract, not a feeling — a sworn bond in which God binds Himself)
2. **That they are inside one** (in Christ = in the covenant)
3. **What that means for who they are** (not who they might become — who they already are)
4. **That their standing is not based on their performance** (God's oath was sworn and ratified in Christ before the client could do anything, and Christ met the covenant's conditions, Rom 5:19; Heb 9:15. Failure, sin, doubt, and fluctuating feeling do not revoke it.)
5. **That the community confirms what the covenant declares** (not alone in the truth)

The client needs to *feel*:
- Security (their standing does not rise and fall with their performance)
- Belonging (inside a covenant people, not just an abstract relationship)
- Dignity (royally granted identity, not earned rank)
- Purpose (covenant calling is real; the coach explores it with the client and does not impose it)

**How understanding becomes feeling.** Not by explanation. Per the Theory of Change, the false structure has to be named and felt (Nodes 2-3), the client opened to receive (Node 4), covenant truth spoken into the live belief (Node 5), and the Spirit's witness waited for and not produced (Node 6), with receptive return between sessions (Node 7). This document supplies the content of what is understood. The Theory of Change supplies how it lands.

**Lament caution.** "Security" and "calling" language never forecloses lament, protest, or doubt, which Scripture itself sanctions within the covenant (Abraham, Moses, Job, the lament Psalms, Habakkuk). See the standing caution in [[Covenant Identity Coaching — Theological-Formation Contributor Stack]], Layer 11, and the extension note under Approach 3.
