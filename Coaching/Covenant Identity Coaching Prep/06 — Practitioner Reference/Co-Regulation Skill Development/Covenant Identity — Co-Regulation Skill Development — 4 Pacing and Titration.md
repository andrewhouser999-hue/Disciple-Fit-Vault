# Covenant Identity — Co-Regulation Skill Development — 4 Pacing and Titration

*Part 4 of 7. See [[Covenant Identity — Co-Regulation Skill Development — Index]] for the full set.*

## Target Capacity

The ability to consciously slow a session's tempo independent of content, and to introduce difficult material in manageable doses with pauses between — rather than pushing straight through to a full narrative because the coach (not the client) is uncomfortable with an unresolved moment. Levine's titration concept — small, bounded doses of activation followed by a pause to let the system settle before continuing — is the underlying idea, adapted here as a pacing discipline rather than a clinical technique.

## Why This Matters in CIC

[[Exceptional Coaching Practice — What Is Missed, Meaningful & Important]] Section III already names silence as "the most underused tool" and distinguishes processing silence from avoidance silence. This document trains the capacity underneath that observation: silence tolerance is a documented, trainable skill, and less experienced practitioners measurably fill pauses faster — which structurally prevents both integration and co-regulation from happening, since neither has time to occur. This is especially load-bearing in Witnessing Repentance and Stage 4b work, both of which are explicitly built around not rushing to resolution.

## Mastery Requirements

- Comfort sitting in silence without needing to resolve it verbally
- Ability to introduce a difficult question or reflection, then stop — rather than following it immediately with a second question or a softening qualifier
- Accurate, real-time discrimination between a pause that needs protecting and one that needs gentle entry (see [[Exceptional Coaching Practice — What Is Missed, Meaningful & Important]] Section III for the processing-vs-avoidance distinction this document trains the nerve for)

## Training Protocol

**Phase 1 — Silence-length awareness (solo).**
In ordinary conversations this week (not coaching — any conversation), practice counting silently to five after someone finishes a sentence before you respond, even when you have something ready to say. Notice how uncomfortable this is and how much you want to fill it. This builds raw tolerance before it's tested under coaching stakes.

**Phase 2 — Scripted titration drill (solo).**
Take a difficult prompt (e.g., a Witnessing Repentance script line, or a Character Wound naming question) and rehearse delivering it out loud, then stopping completely for a full 10 seconds before continuing — timed, not estimated. Most people's internal sense of "a long pause" is 2–3 seconds; timing corrects this.

**Phase 3 — Self-catch drill (solo, reflective).**
Review a recording (or written transcript, if recordings aren't available) of a past coaching conversation or role-play. Mark every place you spoke again within 2 seconds of the other person finishing, or where you asked a second question before the first had been sat with. This builds the specific self-awareness of your own filling habit.

**Phase 4 — Live titration and discrimination practice (requires a partner).**
With a role-play partner or, later, in supervision, practice: (1) introducing a piece of difficult material and stopping, (2) reading whether the resulting silence is processing (protect it) or avoidance (enter it gently — "I notice we've gotten quiet, what's happening right now?"), and (3) getting explicit feedback afterward on whether your read matched what the other person was actually experiencing.

## Solo-Safe vs. Requires Supervision

Phases 1–3 are solo-safe. Phase 4's discrimination judgment — reading processing vs. avoidance silence correctly — is a live interpersonal read that benefits from a corrector's feedback; hold structured drilling of it for the coaching-craft supervisor relationship per [[Covenant Identity — Pre-Practice Readiness Action Plan]]. Phase 1 and 2 can and should start now, since they build tolerance independent of another person's state.

## Self-Assessment Markers

- Phase 2 timed pauses reach a genuine 10 seconds without you speaking early, and stop feeling unbearable
- Phase 3 review shows the "filled within 2 seconds" count decreasing across repeated sessions
- In live practice, your read on processing-vs-avoidance silence is confirmed by the other person's report at a rate that improves over time (track this — it's the direct analog to the Diagnostic Calibration Log's confirm/disconfirm structure)

## Common Failure Modes

- **Confusing pacing discipline with passivity** — titration is about dosing and pausing, not about withholding needed structure or challenge; [[Exceptional Coaching Practice — What Is Missed, Meaningful & Important]] Section V's calibrated challenge still applies, just delivered with room around it
- **Applying one fixed pause length regardless of the moment** — the skill is discrimination (processing vs. avoidance), not a mechanical timer; Phase 2's timing drill builds raw capacity, but live work requires reading the specific person
- **Titrating your own discomfort, not the client's** — the temptation to fill silence is almost always about the coach's tolerance, not the client's need; Phase 1 and 3 exist specifically to separate these
