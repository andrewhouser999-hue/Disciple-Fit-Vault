# Covenant Identity — Covenantal Permanence vs. Felt Presence

> *"I will never leave you nor forsake you."*
> — Hebrews 13:5 (ESV)

---

## Document Purpose

This document exists to give the practitioner a precise, biblically grounded distinction for a presenting statement that recurs across almost every wound type and phase: *"I can't feel God,"* *"I used to hear from Him and now I don't,"* *"I can't get into His presence anymore."* Without a clear framework, this statement gets treated as a single problem with a single fix — usually some version of "try harder to connect." It is not a single problem. It is a family of distinct presentations that share a surface complaint and require different diagnostic responses. This document names the distinction, sorts the presentations, and gives the practitioner a governing covenant truth — **covenantal permanence** — to work from in each case.

Primary placement within the model: a **diagnostic sorting tool and formation anchor** for the presence-and-hearing complaint, sitting alongside [[Covenant Identity — What Blocks Hearing God's Voice — Biblical Research]] (which covers one specific branch of this presentation — self-protective refusal) rather than replacing it.

---

## 1. The Core Distinction

Christian usage of "getting into the presence of God to hear from Him" conflates two different realities that must be separated for diagnostic clarity:

| | **Covenantal Permanence** | **Felt Presence** |
|---|---|---|
| **Nature** | Ontological, positional fact | Subjective, phenomenological experience |
| **Basis** | The Spirit's permanent indwelling (John 14:17; 1 Cor 6:19) | Attentiveness, quietness, worship, obedience, health, circumstance |
| **Governing text** | Hebrews 13:5 — *"I will never leave you nor forsake you"* | Psalm 42:1-3 — *"my soul thirsts for God... My tears have been my food day and night, while they say to me continually, 'Where is your God?'"* |
| **Variability** | Constant — does not fluctuate with mood, effort, or circumstance | Fluctuates — can be dry for extended seasons even when the positional fact is untouched |
| **Client's error, if any** | Rarely disbelieved directly | Frequently mistaken *for* the positional fact — its absence is read as evidence the positional fact has failed |

The client's complaint is almost always about the second column. The covenant truth that needs to be recovered is almost always the first. The coaching error is treating the complaint as if it were evidence against the first column, which produces shame, striving, or a subtly performance-based theology of access.

## 2. Text & Context — Why the Language Exists

The "getting into the presence" idiom is inherited from Old Testament approach-language and applied loosely to a New Covenant reality it was never built to describe.

**Old Testament**: God's presence was localized — tabernacle, then temple, Holy of Holies — and access was mediated, restricted, and dangerous. Approaching it was not casual (2 Sam 6:6-7; Lev 10:1-2). "Seek my face" (Ps 27:8) and "enter his gates with thanksgiving" (Ps 100:4) are pilgrimage and liturgical language — *going to* a place where God had agreed to be met.

**The hinge**: at the crucifixion, "the curtain of the temple was torn in two, from top to bottom" (Matt 27:51). Hebrews reinterprets this as opened access: *"we have confidence to enter the holy places by the blood of Jesus... let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith"* (Heb 10:19-22), and *"let us then with confidence draw near to the throne of grace"* (Heb 4:16). The word behind "confidence" is Greek παρρησία (*parrhēsia*) — boldness, the right of a citizen to speak freely, not a mood to be worked up.

**The transfer**: temple language is then applied to the believer directly — *"your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit within you"* (1 Cor 6:19); *"he dwells with you and will be in you"* (John 14:17). The place is no longer approached. It is inhabited.

**The trajectory**: Eden (unmediated presence) → Fall (exile from it) → Tabernacle/Temple (mediated, localized, approach-language appropriate) → Christ, "God with us" → Spirit (permanent indwelling) → New Creation — *"Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man. He will dwell with them... and God himself will be with them"* (Rev 21:3). The arc of Scripture moves *toward* presence becoming ordinary, permanent, and unmediated — not increasingly hard to access. Approach-language surviving into ordinary Christian speech after Pentecost is an inherited idiom, not a description of the client's actual position.

## 3. Language

- **פָּנִים** (*panim*, "face/presence") — OT approach-language: *lifne YHWH*, "before the face of the LORD." Carries the sense of standing in someone's direct notice.
- **כָּבוֹד** (*kabod*, "glory") — the weighty, visible manifestation of God's presence (Ex 40:34-35); this is what departs Israel in Ezekiel 10 and what the temple-critique tradition treats as the thing ritual cannot manufacture.
- **παρρησία** (*parrhēsia*, "confidence/boldness") — the NT's replacement term for approach; a legal/relational right, not an emotional state.
- **παρουσία** (*parousia*, "presence/arrival") — used of Christ's future coming, but etymologically "presence" — worth noting because it shows the NT vocabulary for divine presence trends toward event and permanence, not atmosphere.

## 4. Theological Weight — The Risk Worth Naming

Popular worship culture sometimes implies that presence is *summoned* — through musical build, corporate emotional intensity, extended repetition, or spiritual effort — which edges toward a subtly works-based or atmospheric view of God's nearness, as though he becomes more present in response to what we do. Careful theology across traditions holds the opposite emphasis: he is already near (Ps 139:7-8, "Where shall I go from your Spirit?... If I make my bed in Sheol, you are there"; Acts 17:27, "he is actually not far from each one of us"). The discipline in view throughout Scripture is *attention*, not *acquisition*. This distinction should be held explicitly by the practitioner, because client language will consistently import the acquisition frame without realizing it — "I need to get back into His presence" treats presence as a place left behind rather than a fact currently true.

## 5. "Hearing from Him" — Where Traditions Genuinely Disagree

This is a real interpretive fault line, not a manufactured one, and the practitioner should know which stream a client (or a client's home church) is standing in before interpreting what they mean by the phrase:

- **Cessationist**: God speaks sufficiently and normatively through Scripture; ongoing prophetic-style address is not expected the way it was in the apostolic era. "I don't hear from God" in this frame usually means "Scripture isn't landing" or "my conscience feels dull," which is a different diagnostic target than felt absence.
- **Continuationist/charismatic**: God still speaks through impressions, promptings, and sometimes vivid experience, always subject to testing (1 Thess 5:19-21; 1 John 4:1).
- **Contemplative tradition** (Brother Lawrence's *practicing the presence*, Ignatian discernment of consolation/desolation): less emphasis on discrete "words," more on sustained relational attentiveness — a posture rather than a data transmission.

The practitioner does not need to resolve this fault line to coach well, but should not silently assume a continuationist frame onto a client whose theology is cessationist, or vice versa — doing so will make the diagnostic questions in Section 7 land as foreign.

---

## 6. Interpretive Debate — Dryness as Normal Formation

A second fault line, distinct from the cessationist/continuationist one: is felt absence itself evidence of a problem to be fixed, or a recognized stage of Christian maturation?

Historic contemplative tradition — John of the Cross's *dark night of the soul*, Ignatian *desolation* — treats sustained dryness as normal and sometimes *necessary*: the soul being weaned off dependence on felt-sense confirmation toward a more volitional, covenantal faith. Psalm 42 and Psalm 88 (which, unusually among the psalms, ends without resolution) give this experience scriptural legitimacy rather than treating it as a symptom of hidden sin. This stands in tension with more activist strands of American evangelical and charismatic culture, where sustained dryness is often implicitly read as a discipline or intimacy failure requiring intervention. Both strands are represented in Scripture — Isaiah 59:2 describes a genuine sin-caused breach, while Psalm 88 describes affliction without any named cause or resolution. The practitioner's job is sorting which the client is actually in, not defaulting to either reading.

## 7. Coaching Translation

**Primary layer — affections / disordered worship (Biblical Counseling diagnostic):**
The complaint "I can't feel God" is rarely only a report about God. It is a report about what is currently loudest in the client's affections. Before offering comfort or reframing, ask what is competing for trust or attention right now — fear, control, an outcome, another person's approval — that may be crowding out attentiveness. Sometimes the felt absence is accurate data about a genuinely dry season (Section 6); sometimes it is spiritual vocabulary being used to describe an unrelated anxiety, in which case the affections work described in [[Covenant Identity — Disordered Affections and the Diagnostic Entry — Mechanism]] is the correct entry point, not this document.

**Second layer — whole-person formation / the Spirit's work:**
Named as a recognizable stage (Section 6) rather than a personal failure, dryness stops carrying an automatic shame charge. This reframe alone does real work — it removes the "I'm doing something wrong" interpretation that clients otherwise stack on top of the dryness itself, before any further intervention is needed.

**Third layer — false identity structure / character wound (diagnostic, when present):**
The theologically loaded move is the client collapsing covenantal permanence into felt presence and then treating the fluctuation in the second column as evidence about their standing: *"I can't feel Him → He's withdrawn → I did something wrong / I'm not enough / I've been left again."* That collapse is frequently not a new theological error — it is an old attachment pattern wearing theological language. A client whose closeness with a caregiver was conditional (approval earned, warmth withdrawn on failure) will often transfer that exact template onto God without noticing the transfer. This is where the presentation becomes a covenant-identity issue rather than a devotional-technique issue — the operative lie is not "I'm not trying hard enough," it is closer to *"closeness must be earned"* or *"absence means abandonment."* See [[Covenant Identity — God-Representation — Research Basis & Synthesis]] and [[Covenant Identity — Attachment Pattern Screener]] for the diagnostic machinery that names which God-representation and attachment pattern is operating.

**Subordinate layer — attachment / felt-sense:**
The goal in session is not to argue the client out of the feeling of absence — it is to help them stay with the discomfort of not-feeling without the nervous system resolving it through self-condemnation or renewed performance. The felt sense of distance can be honored as real without being accepted as true data about God's actual nearness.

## 8. Diagnostic Branches — Sorting the Presentation

Five distinct causes can produce the same surface complaint. Confusing them produces the wrong intervention.

| Branch | Marker | Correct response |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Normal dry season** | No precipitating rupture; general life stability; dryness is diffuse, not tied to a specific unresolved issue | Name it as recognized formation (Section 6); redirect to attention-without-performance practices (Section 9); see [[Covenant Identity — Lament Practice Guide]] |
| **2. Disordered affection crowding attention** | Presenting anxiety or preoccupation elsewhere; "I can't feel God" surfaces alongside a specific stressor | Route to [[Covenant Identity — Disordered Affections and the Diagnostic Entry — Mechanism]] — the presence complaint is downstream of the affections work, not the primary target |
| **3. Covenantal-permanence collapse / attachment transfer** | Shame-laden interpretation of the absence ("I did something wrong," "He's left me"); pattern echoes a caregiver relationship | This document's primary target — see Sections 9-10 |
| **4. Self-protective refusal (cherished sin, hardness, divided loyalty)** | A known area of resistance the client is aware of but not naming; Isaiah 59:2 pattern | Route to [[Covenant Identity — What Blocks Hearing God's Voice — Biblical Research]] — a genuinely different mechanism, addressed non-punitively |
| **5. Clinical presentation** | Flattened affect *generally*, not only toward God — sleep, appetite, or interest changes alongside the spiritual complaint | Out of CIC's non-clinical scope — see [[Covenant Identity — Crisis & Referral Protocol]] before proceeding with any of the above |

These branches are not mutually exclusive — a client can be in genuine dry-season formation (1) while also carrying an attachment-shaped misreading of what the dryness means (3). The branches sort which mechanism is *primary*, not which is exclusively present.

## 9. Session Application

### Diagnostic entry points

- *"When did you last feel close, and what was happening around that time?"* — sorts Branch 1 (season) from a precipitated rupture.
- *"What does the absence of feeling mean to you — about God, and about yourself?"* — surfaces the implicit belief doing the interpretive work; the answer usually reveals whether Branch 3 is active.
- *"Does this pattern — closeness withdrawn when I fail, or withdrawn without explanation — show up anywhere else? A parent, a partner, work?"* — tests directly for attachment transfer (Branch 3) versus a purely theological confusion.
- *"Is there something specific you've been avoiding bringing to Him?"* — tests for Branch 4 without assuming it; a client in genuine dry-season formation (Branch 1) will answer this cleanly with "no," which itself is diagnostic.
- *"Has this flatness shown up in other areas too — sleep, interest in things you usually enjoy?"* — the Branch 5 screen; ask early enough that a clinical presentation isn't mistaken for a formation issue.

### Formation use (between-session)

Redirect away from chasing a repeat of a peak worship experience — which reinforces the acquisition frame named in Section 4 — and toward brief, low-stakes practices of attention without a required emotional output: a few minutes of silence with no goal of "getting" anything; journaling aimed at practicing trust independent of feeling rather than manufacturing feeling. See [[Covenant Identity — Scripture Saturation & Meditation Guide]] and [[Covenant Identity — Lament Practice Guide]] for the deployment library — Psalm 42 and Psalm 88 are the direct scriptural companions for Branch 1.

### Declarative / Lie Eliminator entry

Likely lie candidates for Branch 3: *"God's presence depends on my performance,"* *"distance means abandonment,"* *"if I can't feel Him, He isn't near."*

> **Lie:** *"If I can't feel God, He isn't near — His nearness depends on what I bring to it."*
> **Counter-truth (Heb 13:5 + 1 Cor 6:19):** *"I am indwelt, not visited. His nearness was never suspended by my inability to feel it — it is a settled fact I am learning to trust independent of what I sense."*

Deliberately positional language, not felt-sense language, since the felt sense is exactly the thing under repair. See [[Lie Eliminator — Client Workbook]] and [[Covenant Identity — Deep Lie Work Worksheet]].

### Scope flag

Branch 5 sits outside CIC's non-clinical scope. If the reported absence is flat across life domains rather than specific to the God-relationship, hold the referral conversation per [[Covenant Identity — Crisis & Referral Protocol]] before treating the presentation as a formation issue.
