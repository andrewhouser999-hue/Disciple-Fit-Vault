# Covenant Identity — False Covenant Objects & False Identity Structures — Terms Reference

**What this is:** A focused reference on two core CIC diagnostic terms — *false covenant object* and *false identity structure* — for lookup, teaching, and precise use. This is not a mechanism document: the causal explanation of *why* disordered affections produce these two things lives in [[Covenant Identity — Disordered Affections and the Diagnostic Entry — Mechanism]], and the explanation of *why* the false covenant object must be felt as failing before covenant truth can displace it lives in [[Covenant Identity — Felt Inadequacy and the Vacancy — Mechanism]]. This document does not re-derive either mechanism. It exists so the two terms can be defined, distinguished, and applied precisely without re-reading the full mechanism chain each time.

**When used:** Onboarding a new practitioner to the vocabulary. Any time the two terms are being used loosely or interchangeably in session notes or supervision. Teaching or training use — including the worked example below, which is built for that purpose.

**Who uses it:** Practitioner only.

---

## Definitions

### False Covenant Object

The functional source of security, significance, or worth that a person's heart actually trusts and organizes around, in the place God is meant to occupy as covenant partner. It is not what a person professes to believe about God — it is what their affections are actually oriented toward as the thing that makes their life viable. A false covenant object forms when a character wound (betrayal, terror, or ambivalence) damages the heart's ability to trust God as a reliable covenant partner for a specific need, and the heart adapts by trusting something else in that need's place — competence, approval, control, financial security, belonging, and comparable functional substitutes are the recurring examples in CIC practice. Full mechanism: [[Covenant Identity — Disordered Affections and the Diagnostic Entry — Mechanism]].

### False Identity Structure

The behavioral and relational system a person builds to protect and serve their false covenant object. If the false covenant object is *what* the heart trusts, the false identity structure is the *self* organized around securing and defending that trust — the specific patterns of behavior, relating, and self-understanding that make sense only given what the heart has organized around. A false identity structure is not random; it is coherent and predictable once the false covenant object is known, because it exists to serve that object. Full mechanism: [[Covenant Identity — Disordered Affections and the Diagnostic Entry — Mechanism]]; typology: [[Covenant Identity — Character Wound Diagnostic Tool]].

## Discrimination Test — Telling Them Apart in the Room

The two terms get conflated in practice because they are adjacent and usually surface together. They are not the same thing, and naming one where the other belongs weakens the precision Stage 2 requires.

**The object question:** *What is the heart actually trusting to make life viable?* This is what the person protects, fears losing, and reaches for under pressure — the thing itself, not the system built around it.

**The structure question:** *What self has been built to protect that trust?* This is the pattern of behavior and relating that exists because the object is being trusted — the system, not the thing.

**Applied together:** name the object first, then ask what self had to be built to keep managing it. If a practitioner can only answer one of the two questions, the naming in Stage 2 is not yet precise enough to give covenant truth something specific to land on.

**Example pairing:** Object — *control*. Structure — *the self that plans three steps ahead, cannot delegate without anxiety, and reads spontaneity in others as a threat to be managed.* The object is the noun being trusted; the structure is the whole operating system built to keep that noun functioning.

## Worked Example

*Composite, illustrative case — not a real client record. Built for teaching precision in naming, following the same composite-scenario convention used in the Worked Session Examples library.*

**Presenting material:** A client — call him David, mid-30s, a ministry staff member — reports chronic exhaustion and a nagging sense that he can never fully rest, even on vacation. He describes himself as "just a hard worker" and is mildly proud of the label. Behaviorally: he over-prepares for every meeting, cannot receive delegated tasks without silently redoing the work himself afterward, and experiences a spike of anxiety when a colleague's ministry visibly outperforms his.

**Layer 1–2 (Affections Discovery):** What David protects — his reputation for reliability. What he fears losing — being seen as replaceable. What he reaches for under pressure — more effort, more preparation, more visible output.

**Layer 3 (Displacement):** David affirms, without hesitation, that his worth is secure in Christ. His functional behavior under deadline pressure tells a different story — the security calculation is actually run through output, not through his stated theology. This is the gap between confessional and functional belief (see [[Covenant Identity — Functional vs. Confessional Theology — 20 Diagnostic Contrasts]]).

**Layer 4 (God-Exclusion Map — naming the false covenant object):** In the domain of significance, God has been functionally displaced by *competence* — specifically, competence made visible through consistent, effortful output. David is not trusting God to secure his significance; he is trusting his own production record.

**Layer 5 (Identity Revelation — naming the false identity structure):** The false identity statement: *"I am significant when I am producing more than what is expected of me; if I stop, I become replaceable and therefore worthless."* The false identity structure is the whole operating system built to protect that belief — pre-emptive over-preparation, inability to delegate without re-doing the work, and comparison-driven anxiety whenever a peer's output rises above his own. Character Wound cross-check: a Warrior-type presentation (core wound: betrayal, trust in reliable authority damaged) is consistent with this picture and would be confirmed, not assumed, using [[Covenant Identity — Character Wound Diagnostic Tool]].

**Precision test applied:** A category-level naming — "David struggles with workaholism" — gives Stage 3 nothing specific to address. The Stage 2 naming above gives covenant truth (for example, a Warrior-track declaration grounded in identity as beloved apart from output, cf. [[Covenant Identity — Matched Declaration Architecture]]) a precise structure to be addressed to.

## Scripture Anchors Per Term

The Theory of Change grounds the *mechanism* of false covenant objects in Hosea 2 and Jeremiah 2:13 (the Baals, the broken cisterns). The two anchors below are chosen specifically for the *terms themselves* — one per term — as an additional, non-duplicate layer of grounding.

**False Covenant Object — Psalm 20:7 (ESV):** *"Some trust in chariots and some in horses, but we trust in the name of the LORD our God."* The verse names the mechanism in miniature: a concrete, named alternative object of trust, set directly against trust in God's covenant name. It supplies plain, client-translatable language for the concept — *what are you trusting in the place of His name?* — without requiring the fuller Hosea/Jeremiah background.

**False Identity Structure — Genesis 3:7, 10 (ESV):** *"Then the eyes of both were opened, and they knew that they were naked, and they sewed fig leaves together and made themselves loincloths... I heard the sound of you in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was naked, and I hid myself."* This is the first false identity structure in Scripture: a self-constructed covering-and-hiding system, built in direct response to exposure, functioning to manage what the fig leaves could not actually resolve. It anchors the definition's claim that a false identity structure is a *behavioral system built to protect* — not the wound itself, and not the false covenant object itself, but the operative machinery in between.

## Misdiagnosis Pitfall

**The error:** naming the presenting behavior as if it were the false identity structure itself, rather than recognizing the behavior as the structure's downstream expression.

Larry Crabb's beneath-surface diagnostic posture — cited in [[Covenant Identity — Character Wound Diagnostic Tool]] — holds that what presents behaviorally in session is organized by what is protected from view; the coaching task is not to address the presentation but to reach what is driving it. Applied here: "David is controlling" or "David is a workaholic" names a visible behavior pattern, not the false identity structure producing it. The behavior is real and worth noting, but treating the behavior itself as the diagnostic target skips past the false covenant object and false identity structure it serves — and Stage 3 covenant truth addressed to a behavior label rather than to the structure has no precise target to land on, per the same failure mode named in the Disordered Affections mechanism document ("the presenting problem is almost never the actual problem").

**The check:** before naming a Stage 2 structure, ask — *is this the thing itself, or is this what the thing produces?* If the answer is "what it produces," keep asking the object and structure questions above until the naming reaches the organizing center, not just its visible symptom.
