# Covenant Identity — Verifying Felt Juxtaposition — Mechanism & Practitioner Reference
*Practitioner Reference — how to tell, and how to become able to tell, whether juxtaposition is happening at the level reconsolidation requires*

**What this is:** A practitioner method for distinguishing a client *describing* a target emotion (shame, fear, peace, belovedness) from a client *feeling* it — applied specifically at the moment juxtaposition is claimed to be occurring, whether at Stage 3 (declaration) or Stage 4b (relational/felt-sense work). It also states, for the first time as an explicit pair, which emotions are load-bearing for CIC's reconsolidation claim and why.

**When used:** Any time covenant truth is being introduced into an activated false-identity structure — Stage 3 declaration work (Matched Declaration Architecture), Stage 4b relational moments of meeting (Route A), and Stage 4b felt-sense work (Route B). Use it in real time as trained noticing, never as an in-session checklist (see "The Method," Layer 1, below). Use it after the session, deliberately, as structured verification (Layer 2).

**Who uses it:** Practitioner only. No client-facing content is derived from this document.

---

## The Gap This Closes

Three existing documents already name the exact failure this document addresses, but none of them gives the practitioner a stated way to catch it while — or shortly after — it happens:

- [[Covenant Identity — Felt Inadequacy and the Vacancy — Mechanism|Memory Reconsolidation — Ecker & Neuroscience Basis]] names **Failure mode 3**: "The juxtaposition was cognitively present but somatically absent. The person consciously holds both the lie and the truth but the nervous system is not engaged at the level where the encoding lives."
- [[Covenant Identity — Integration Rationale]] restates the same three reconsolidation conditions (Reactivation, Prediction error, Juxtaposition) and warns that "the session stayed cognitive... Counter-statements landed in explicit memory and suppressed the implicit belief temporarily — extinction, not reconsolidation."
- [[Covenant Identity — Stage 4b Implicit Level Practitioner Reference]], Section II, states the diagnostic marker for this exact failure: *"I know it in my head but not my heart"* — and adds that this sentence is the client *accurately describing* the outcome, not a complaint to be coached away.

What none of these documents does is tell the practitioner, in the moment or immediately after, whether the juxtaposition they just facilitated actually met the somatic-engagement condition or only produced the cognitive-holding condition. A client can say the right words, in the right order, with apparent emotional coloring, while the labile window never opened. The client will not reliably know the difference either — dual awareness in the mind and felt disconfirmation in the body can feel similar from the inside, especially to a client whose false identity structure is itself organized around performing the appropriate response (see "Wound-Type Calibration," below). Without a stated method, this failure is invisible until it resurfaces under stress in a later session — at which point it is too late to have caught it, and the practitioner may misread the relapse as a new problem rather than as evidence that Stage 3 never actually completed.

This document is that stated method. It does not introduce a new mechanism. It operationalizes a distinction the existing mechanism documents already require but do not yet equip the practitioner to make.

## The Core Claim

Juxtaposition is a claim about what is happening in the client's nervous system, not about what the client has just said. Ecker's own verification step already treats the emotional response, not the cognitive content, as diagnostic: *"Does the client access the original belief and find its emotional charge intact? Or does it feel different — flat, distant, no longer gripping? The emotional response, not the cognitive content, is the diagnostic."* This document extends that same logic backward, to the juxtaposition moment itself, and generalizes it from a Route-B-only instrument ([[Covenant Identity — Route B Experiencing Depth Rating]]) to Stage 3 declaration work broadly, where it has not previously been stated.

The practitioner's task at the point of juxtaposition is not to introduce covenant truth well. It is to introduce covenant truth well *and* to have a disciplined way of reading whether it landed into felt, activated material or into a client who is holding both propositions correctly in mind. These look similar. They are not the same event, and only one of them rewrites anything.

## Which Emotions Are "Certain Emotions"

CIC's reconsolidation mechanism requires a *specific* activated belief and a *specific* disconfirming experience — Ecker's specificity requirement, already stated in [[Covenant Identity — Matched Declaration Architecture]]'s four conditions ("Specific — The declaration must address the named lie, not a generic alternative"). That specificity extends to affect. "The client felt something" is not sufficiently precise to diagnose whether reconsolidation conditions were met. The practitioner needs to know *which* emotion was supposed to be live, and whether that particular one actually was.

Read across every wound-type and declaration-track document currently in the CIC system, one pair of emotions recurs as the content of every false identity structure, and one pair recurs as the content of every covenant-truth answer to it. No prior document states this as an explicit pair — it is implicit across five separate documents. Stating it here closes that gap.

### The False-Identity Side: Shame and Fear

Every false identity statement in [[Covenant Identity — Matched Declaration Architecture]] reduces to a shame claim about the person's worth, standing, or acceptability:

| Track | False Identity Statement | Shame-Family Core |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Abandonment | "I am the one people leave" | Unworthy of being stayed with |
| 2 — Performance | "I am hired, not beloved" | Unworthy of unconditional standing |
| 3 — Shame | "I am disqualified" | Unworthy by nature, not just action |
| 4 — Condemnation | "I am the sum of what I have done wrong" | Unworthy by record |
| 5 — Impostor | "There is no self underneath that could stand on its own" | Unworthy without ongoing construction |

Fear is the second, load-bearing member of the pair — not a separate, unrelated affect but the anticipatory register of the same shame claim. Track 1's declaration text names it directly: *"you did not receive the spirit of slavery to fall back into fear"* (Rom. 8:15, ESV) — fear and the abandonment wound's isolation are named in the same breath as the condition the Spirit's adoption answers. [[Covenant Identity — Character Wound Diagnostic Tool]] confirms fear as present under every wound type, not only the Warrior's: "Fear is present beneath the surface but carries its own shame" (Warrior); "Fear presented as uncertainty and minimizing" (Hermit); "Anxiety is the primary register — specifically the performance variety: fear of being found inadequate, exposed, or not enough" (False Noble). In each case, fear is the felt anticipation that the shame claim is about to be confirmed by exposure, abandonment, or failure.

**Shame and fear are therefore the two "certain emotions" on the false-identity side** — not because other affects never appear (see "Secondary and Derivative Affects," below), but because they are the two that are structurally present across every wound type and every declaration track, and because they are the specific content the covenant truth is answering. A practitioner who has activated *some* emotion but not shame or fear specifically has not necessarily activated the belief the declaration is built to address.

### The Covenant-Truth Side: Peace and Belovedness

[[Covenant Identity — The Interior Life as Telos]] names nine qualities of the formed interior life. Two of them answer shame and fear directly, by the document's own language, not by inference this document is imposing:

**Peace answers fear.** The "Settled" quality is defined explicitly against the threat-logic fear runs on: *"not peace as the absence of threat, but peace as the presence of a foundation that threat cannot reach."* This is precise: fear's claim is that the threat is real and unanswered; peace's claim is not that the threat is absent but that it cannot reach the foundation. Peace is therefore the specific covenant-truth affect matched to fear, in the same way Track 1's Abba declaration is matched to the abandonment wound — not a generic positive feeling substituted for fear, but its structural answer.

**Belovedness answers shame.** The "Loved and Knowing It" quality names the felt reality directly: *"Not believing it. Knowing it. The difference is the difference between reading a weather report and standing in rain."* Every shame-family false identity statement above makes a worth claim; belovedness is the covenant-truth answer to a worth claim specifically — not a general encouragement, but Nouwen's *Taken* (belovedness prior to and independent of qualification), which directly reverses the shame structure's logic that worth must be earned or proven.

**Shame and peace and fear and belovedness are therefore a matched pair of pairs, not four independent items:** shame ↔ belovedness runs on the worth axis; fear ↔ peace runs on the safety axis. This mapping is not stated as such in either source document — it is this document's synthesis of what both documents already imply when read together, in the same way [[Covenant Identity — Character Wound Diagnostic Tool]] made explicit, in its 2026-07-25 update, a correspondence to Young's coping-style triad that had been implicit in its own text for months. The synthesis is flagged as such, not presented as an independent scholarly finding.

### Why These Two Pairs, Specifically

The reconsolidation mechanism does not require *an* emotion to be live — it requires the *specific* implicit belief to be live, and belief and affect are fused at the implicit level (this is Ecker's whole point: the implicit encoding is affective, not propositional). Since every wound-type's false identity statement is a shame claim carrying a fear anticipation, and every covenant-truth answer in the Interior Life telos and the Matched Declaration texts is built to answer worth (belovedness) and safety (peace) specifically, shame/fear and peace/belovedness are the two pairs a practitioner should be listening for when verifying juxtaposition — not because CIC restricts clients to four emotions, but because these four are the ones the mechanism's specificity requirement is actually built around.

### Secondary and Derivative Affects — Not a Different Target, a Defense Over the Same Target

Anger, contempt, grief, guilt, and anxiety appear constantly in session and are not irrelevant — but [[Covenant Identity — Character Wound Diagnostic Tool]] already documents each of them as a *layer over* shame or fear, not an independent target:

- **Contempt** (Warrior): "Pride functions as a lid on shame — *'I've handled worse'* keeps the shame of weakness from surfacing." Contempt is a defense against the Warrior's own shame becoming felt.
- **Grief** (Node 3, [[Covenant Identity — Felt Inadequacy and the Vacancy — Mechanism]]): grief is the felt cost of the false covenant object's failure — a different mechanism moment (Node 3, prior to Stage 3) with its own load-bearing affect and its own address-test (godly vs. worldly grief, 2 Cor. 7:10). Grief is not a juxtaposition-moment target; treating it as one conflates two adjacent but distinct mechanism steps.
- **Anxiety** (False Noble): named explicitly as "the performance variety" of fear — a derivative, not a separate category.
- **Guilt/condemnation-anxiety** (Track 4): a shame-family variant organized around record rather than nature, not a fifth independent affect.

**Practical implication:** when a practitioner reads anger, anxiety, or grief live in the room at a juxtaposition moment, the diagnostic question is not "is this the certain emotion" but "what is this defending, or what has this cost, and is shame or fear reachable underneath it." Declaring covenant truth *into* the defensive layer (contempt, performance-anxiety) without reaching the shame or fear it protects reproduces exactly the extinction failure mode Ecker's model predicts — the truth competes with the visible affect rather than the encoded belief.

## The Diagnostic Problem: Cognitive Presence, Somatic Absence

A client at the juxtaposition moment can produce every observable marker of engagement without the labile window opening:

- The correct words, spoken with apparent conviction
- Eye contact, stillness, an appropriately serious tone
- A verbal report of feeling something ("yeah, that hits")
- Tears — which, per [[Covenant Identity — Stage 4b Implicit Level Practitioner Reference]]'s own signal vocabulary, must be read for *quality*, not presence: "Tears that shift quality — from managed to authentic" is the actual transformation marker; tears alone are not

None of these is proof of somatic engagement. All of them are producible by a client whose false identity structure is itself organized around producing the appropriate response — which is not a hypothetical edge case in this population. It is the *default* presentation for two of the three character wound types and a substantial share of blended presentations (see "Wound-Type Calibration," below). A practitioner who takes verbal correctness plus visible affect as sufficient evidence of juxtaposition will systematically over-call reconsolidation in exactly the clients most likely to be performing it — and will not find out until the pattern returns under stress, session or months later.

## The Method

The method has three layers. Layer 1 operates during the session as trained noticing. Layer 2 operates immediately after the session as structured verification. Layer 3 is not a separate step but a standing correction applied to both — what to discount, per wound type, because it is a known false-positive pattern for that client.

### Layer 1 — Real-Time Trained Noticing (Not a Checklist)

**This is not an in-session protocol to run through.** [[Covenant Identity — Stage 4b Implicit Level Practitioner Reference]]'s own Failure Mode Guard applies directly and without modification: *"A coach who is tracking through categories and checking boxes cannot also be genuinely present. Authentic presence cannot be performed."* [[Covenant Identity — Route B Experiencing Depth Rating]] states the same guard for its own instrument: *"Rate after the session... never while attending to the client."* This document's Layer 1 is the same kind of thing — formed pattern recognition, not a mid-session checklist — and it fails the same way the AEDP State Model fails if it is used as one.

What the practitioner is listening and watching for, as trained noticing rather than a list:

**The felt-sense/label distinction (Gendlin).** [[Covenant Identity — Terminology & Language Reference]] defines felt sense precisely: *"A holistic, pre-verbal, meaning-carrying bodily sense of a situation, present before language can adequately name it."* A client *describing* shame produces the label first — "I feel ashamed" — as a conclusion already reached, often fluently, sometimes before the practitioner has finished speaking. A client *feeling* shame is closer to Gendlin's Attend step: something bodily is present that language is catching up to, often with a pause, a groping quality, or language that arrives as an approximation ("it's like — I don't know, heavy, low") rather than a clean diagnostic term. Fluency and speed of the emotional label are not favorable signs at the juxtaposition moment; they are more often evidence of a well-rehearsed cognitive account arriving instead of a felt one.

**The AEDP State Model, read for the specific target affect** (full model: [[Covenant Identity — Stage 4b Implicit Level Practitioner Reference]], Section VI). Defensive processing (State 1) — controlled presentation, flat or sophisticated verbal content, intellectualization — is not juxtaposition regardless of what is being said. Core affective experience (State 2) — voice quality shift, facial softening, tears that change character, somatic activation, breath shift — is the minimum bar for the somatic-engagement condition to be plausibly met. States 3 and 4 (unforced settling, spontaneous self-compassion, the glimmer) are stronger evidence but not required for a valid juxtaposition — Ecker's own sequence does not require full transformational affect, only that both the activated belief and the disconfirming experience are genuinely live together.

**The six-channel signal vocabulary, applied to this specific question** (full reference: [[Covenant Identity — Implicit Signal Recognition Quick Reference]]). Two channels are especially diagnostic at the juxtaposition moment specifically:
- *Paraverbal* — "the change itself, not the baseline." A shift in tone, rhythm, or pace as the truth is spoken is more informative than the words that follow it.
- *Coach's own body* — a felt urge in the practitioner to fill the silence, reassure, or move on is, per that document, "often diagnostic of the exact moment implicit material has surfaced." Its *absence* — the practitioner feeling nothing shift in their own body while delivering the declaration — is itself worth noticing as a possible sign the material stayed cognitive on both sides of the exchange.

**Timing.** Genuine juxtaposition tends to break the ordinary conversational rhythm — a pause that doesn't get filled, a beat where neither party knows what comes next. A juxtaposition moment that proceeds smoothly, on schedule, with the client responding promptly and appropriately, is not disqualified by its smoothness, but smoothness should raise rather than lower the practitioner's threshold for calling it landed.

**The reflex question, not a scripted checklist item.** The single highest-yield move, already present in the corpus as Route B's Attend step and Domain 3's Stage 4b competency, generalizes cleanly to any juxtaposition moment: *"What do you notice happening in you right now — not what you think, what you actually notice?"* This is not a new tool. It is the existing Attend-step question, applied here as the practitioner's default reflex at the moment covenant truth is spoken into an activated belief, not only during Route B felt-sense work. Asked as a genuine question — held open, without supplying the expected answer — it routes the client away from the label they may already have ready and toward whatever is actually present in the body. A client with nothing more precise than "I don't know, something" is closer to felt contact than a client who answers instantly with a clean feeling-word.

### Layer 2 — Structured Verification (Ecker's Own Step, Generalized)

Ecker's fourth reconsolidation condition is verification: re-access the original belief afterward and check whether its emotional charge is intact, changed, or gone. [[Covenant Identity — Route B Experiencing Depth Rating]] already operationalizes a version of this — a 5-level scale (External → Self-Referenced External → Named, Not Explored → Actively Explored → Felt Shift) — but scopes it explicitly to Route B only. This document generalizes the same construct, and the same discipline, to any Stage 3 or Stage 4b juxtaposition moment, not only felt-sense work:

1. **Within the same 24-hour post-session window** used for [[Covenant Identity — Self-Supervision Template]] and [[Covenant Identity — Diagnostic Calibration Log]], re-access the specific moment from memory: what was the named lie, and what was the practitioner's honest read of the highest level reached — using the Route B scale's five levels as the general-purpose instrument, not a Route-B-exclusive one. A juxtaposition rated Level 1–2 (external or self-referenced-external, no present-moment felt contact) did not meet the somatic condition regardless of what was said in the room.
2. **At the start of the next session**, before reintroducing the same declaration, ask the client to bring the original lie back to mind and report what happens — not "do you still believe it" but "what happens in you when you bring it back." This is Ecker's verification step performed live: intact emotional charge (flat re-recitation, no shift, or the client reports it "still feels true even though I know it isn't") indicates extinction, not reconsolidation, per [[Covenant Identity — Integration Rationale]]'s own language. A charge that has genuinely changed quality — flatter, more distant, harder to fully re-inhabit — is the positive evidence.
3. **Log the result** in the existing [[Covenant Identity — Diagnostic Calibration Log]], using its existing Signal column and reusing the six-channel vocabulary already established there — this document does not create a new tracking instrument. A pattern of Level 1–2 verification ratings across a client's Stage 3 declaration work is the same category of finding [[Covenant Identity — Route B Experiencing Depth Rating]] already treats as grounds to revise the underlying call — here, grounds to suspect the client's presentation itself (see Layer 3) rather than the declaration content or timing.

### Layer 3 — Wound-Type Calibration: Known False-Positive Patterns

[[Covenant Identity — Character Wound Diagnostic Tool]]'s co-optation section names, per type, exactly the pattern this document is warning against — a sophisticated performance of the target state that is not the state. These are not new observations; they are the existing co-optation patterns applied specifically to the juxtaposition moment:

| Type | Co-optation Pattern at Juxtaposition | What It Looks Like Here | Practitioner Correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Warrior** | Performing Vulnerability | Receives the declaration with well-organized, articulate emotional language — "produced" rather than caught off guard | Slow down before accepting the response: *"You're doing this very well. What was happening in you before you had the words?"* |
| **Hermit** | Compliant Disappearance | Agrees readily, reports feeling something, gives no resistance — correct and cooperative, with no felt sense of having actually been reached | Name the absence without shaming it: *"I believe you're engaged — I'm not sure I've found you yet. What's actually happening in there?"* |
| **False Noble** | Striving as Formation | Receives the declaration as a growth insight, integrates it fluently, may pre-empt the practitioner's own naming of what just happened | Interrupt the packaging before it completes: *"Something in me wants to slow down before this becomes a lesson. What's underneath it, right now?"* |

**The corrective is not suspicion of the client.** Per the Character Wound Tool's own framing, these are formed survival patterns, not deception. The correction is the practitioner's calibrated default: with a confirmed or suspected Warrior or False Noble presentation especially, an unusually smooth, well-organized, or quickly-integrated juxtaposition moment should lower rather than raise the practitioner's confidence that it landed — the opposite of the naive reading, where fluency and apparent insight look like good session work. With a Hermit presentation, low resistance and ready agreement should trigger the same downgrade for a parallel reason: compliance is the wound's native register, not evidence the wound has been reached.

## Defining Practitioner Expertise in Working the Juxtaposition

This is not a seventh competency domain. It is a stated deepening of two domains [[Covenant Identity — Practitioner Competency Framework]] already names — Domain 1 (Diagnostic Reading, Implicit Layer) and Domain 3 (In-Session Implicit Engagement) — specific to the juxtaposition moment. A practitioner has this expertise when the following are true, not as a checklist to complete once but as a description of formed capacity:

**Fluency in the felt-sense/label distinction as a reflex, not a concept.** The practitioner does not need to consciously recall Gendlin's theory in the moment. They have enough formed familiarity with the difference between a client producing a label and a client contacting a felt sense that the distinction registers automatically, the way an experienced clinician hears the difference between rehearsed and spontaneous speech without analyzing syntax.

**The discipline to treat a correct verbal response as insufficient evidence, on principle, every time.** This is a standing posture, not a case-by-case judgment call the practitioner makes fresh each session. The default assumption at a juxtaposition moment is that cognitive holding and somatic engagement are two different events until the signals above indicate otherwise — not the reverse.

**Wound-type-calibrated skepticism that does not curdle into cynicism.** The practitioner holds the co-optation patterns in Layer 3 as a standing correction to their own read, without treating every fluent, cooperative, or articulate client response as automatically suspect. Per the Character Wound Diagnostic Tool's own caution about type identification generally, the calibration is a soft prior applied to a specific, ambiguous read — not a rule that fluent responses are always false.

**Restraint — specifically, not converting this document into an in-session instrument.** The clearest sign a practitioner has *not* formed this competency is reaching for a mental checklist mid-session. The Stage 4b document's guard applies here without modification: a coach auditing the client's response against these markers in real time has already broken the presence the reading depends on. Expertise looks like quieter, faster, less effortful noticing over time — not more elaborate in-session tracking.

**The reflex to ask, not assume.** *"What do you notice happening in you right now?"* asked as a genuine question, held open long enough for an answer that isn't ready yet to surface, is the single most repeatable skill this document names. A practitioner who has not built the discipline of asking this — defaulting instead to reading the client's affect and concluding on their own — will miss exactly the cases (Hermit compliance, False Noble packaging) where the visible affect is the least reliable signal.

**The coach's own formation as a precondition, not a courtesy.** [[Covenant Identity — Stage 4b Implicit Level Practitioner Reference]] already states this as clinical necessity, not virtue: *"What has not been worked in the coach will shape what the coach can hold for the client."* A practitioner who cannot reliably distinguish their own performed emotional response from their own felt one — in their own formation work, under supervision, in their own Character Wound and Lie Eliminator practice — will not reliably make this read in a client. This is the direct, practical reason [[Covenant Identity — Practitioner Competency Framework]] Domain 6 (Coach Formation) is a prerequisite for Domain 3, not a parallel enrichment: the specific competency this document names cannot be trained from outside the practitioner's own felt experience of the same distinction.

## Failure Modes

**Treating fluent, well-organized emotional disclosure as confirming evidence.** The single most common error, and the one every wound-type's co-optation pattern is specifically shaped to produce. The more polished the response, the more (not less) verification it warrants.

**Converting this document into a mid-session scoring exercise.** Produces the same collapse of presence the Stage 4b document and the Route B Rating document both warn against, for the same reason. If the practitioner notices themselves consciously running through channels or levels while the client is talking, presence has already broken; return to simply being with the client, and do the structured read afterward.

**Using Layer 3's wound-type calibration to distrust a client's genuine breakthrough.** The calibration exists to catch a specific known pattern, not to create a standing presumption that any given client's positive response is false. A practitioner who has become unable to accept a Warrior's or False Noble's real movement because it "looks too clean" has overcorrected into the opposite error — treating the heuristic as a rule rather than a soft prior, which is the exact caution [[Covenant Identity — Character Wound Diagnostic Tool]] issues about its own typology generally.

**Confusing this document's target affects with an exhaustive list.** Shame, fear, peace, and belovedness are the recurring structural pair-of-pairs across the wound-type and declaration-track corpus — not the only emotions relevant to CIC work. Grief (Node 3), contempt (Warrior defense), and performance-anxiety (False Noble defense) remain load-bearing at their own mechanism points; this document does not relocate or subordinate them, it clarifies which layer they operate at relative to the juxtaposition-moment target.

**Skipping Layer 2 because Layer 1's real-time read felt clear.** A confident in-session impression is exactly the condition under which Failure Mode 3 goes undetected — the practitioner's own read can be fooled by the same co-optation patterns the client is (unconsciously) producing. Verification is not a formality for uncertain cases; it is the check against the practitioner's own confidence being wrong.

## Anticipated Critique

**Critique — This adds another layer of practitioner apparatus to an already dense diagnostic system; at what point does tracking the tracking become the problem?**

**Steelmanned form:** CIC already asks the practitioner to hold the Diagnostic Lens Transition Logic, the Character Wound typology, the AEDP State Model, the six-channel signal vocabulary, the Route B Experiencing Depth Rating, and now a fourth layer specific to the juxtaposition moment. Each addition, individually justified, risks producing a practitioner so occupied with instruments that presence — the thing every one of these documents says is the actual precondition for the work — becomes impossible to sustain.

**Response:** This document does not add a fourth instrument; it names, for the first time, what the existing instruments were already supposed to be doing at one specific and previously unaddressed moment — the AEDP State Model was already meant to be read at juxtaposition, the six-channel vocabulary already existed, the Route B Rating already existed. The only genuinely new material is the shame/fear/peace/belovedness pairing (a synthesis, not a new instrument) and the wound-type co-optation cross-reference (a reuse of an existing table for a new purpose). Layer 1 is explicitly not a new in-session practice — it is the same formed noticing the corpus already requires, applied with a sharper question. Layer 2 reuses existing logs. The critique is correctly aimed at the general risk; it is not correctly aimed at this specific document, which consolidates rather than adds.

**Remaining exposure:** The risk is real at the level of the *practitioner's total cognitive load* across the full corpus, not this document specifically. A coach still in early formation, who has not yet internalized the six-channel vocabulary or the AEDP model to the point of reflex, may experience this document as one instrument too many rather than as the consolidation it is intended to be. The Practitioner Competency Framework's own sequencing — formation precedes and sustains technique — implies this document is appropriately read and applied only after Domains 1 and 3 are already forming, not as an entry-level tool.
