# Covenant Identity — Resourcing & Anchor Establishment — Practitioner Reference

**What this is:** A coach-facilitated in-session protocol for establishing, testing, and deploying a client's covenant-identity anchor *before* Phase 2 wound mapping or Stage 4b implicit work opens difficult material — and a session-structuring rule ("touch and return") for using that anchor once excavation begins. Coach-facing only; no client take-home document. Similar in shape to the Route B Sequence and Witnessing Repentance Worksheet: a coach-narrated in-session move, not a client-administered tool.

**Why this doesn't already exist elsewhere:** [[Covenant Identity — Co-Regulation Skill Development — 4 Pacing and Titration]] trains the coach's own capacity to slow tempo and tolerate silence — it does not establish anything in the client. [[Covenant Identity — Between-Session Distress Protocol]] gives the client generic grounding phrases *after* distress has already surfaced between sessions — it is reactive and not personalized to a rehearsed anchor. [[Covenant Identity — Stage 4b Implicit Level Practitioner Reference]] tracks state once material is live — it does not address what the client returns to when tracking shows overwhelm. This document is the missing proactive layer: build the resource before the work needs it, not during the moment it's needed.

**How to use it:** Read once before Phase 2 begins with a client. Deploy the Establishing sequence (Section II) early — ideally late Phase 1, before excavation opens in Phase 2. Deploy the Touch-and-Return structure (Section IV) throughout Phase 2 wound mapping and Stage 4b work. This document does not replace Stage 4b's tracking apparatus or the Crisis & Referral Protocol's exit points — it sits upstream of both.

---

## I. The Clinical Source and the Non-Clinical Adaptation

Resourcing, in its clinical form, is a specific sequence: identify something that produces a felt sense of safety or strength, locate it somatically (not just cognitively), rehearse returning to it in a calm state, then use it as the tested return point during activation — the practitioner "pendulating" the client between small doses of difficult material and the resource, building capacity gradually (Levine; Ogden, Minton & Pain; Shapiro's EMDR resource/safe-place installation).

The full clinical skill — reading fine-grained physiological cues (breath depth, muscle tone, color shift) in real time to calibrate exactly how much activation is safe — is not being adapted here. That level of pattern-recognition takes years of supervised clinical training and is explicitly out of scope, the same line the [[Covenant Identity — Co-Regulation Skill Development — Index]] cluster draws around its own source material: *"the underlying trainable skill is drawn out and reframed for a non-clinical coaching context; the clinical protocols themselves are not being practiced or represented as licensed technique."* This document follows the same rule.

What transfers: proactive, rehearsed anchor establishment (Section II) and a structural, gross-behavioral-signal version of pendulation (Section IV) — dosing and checking, not physiological reading. What does not transfer: fine physiological calibration, and any hands-on regulation technique performed on the client's body. The coach's move is always returning the client to the client's own already-rehearsed anchor — never administering a technique of the coach's own design.

## II. Establishing the Anchor — Before It's Needed

**Timing:** Late Phase 1, before Phase 2 excavation opens. Not in the first session (nothing to anchor to yet — the client's covenant-identity content has not been named). Not mid-crisis (too late to be reliable — an anchor introduced for the first time under activation has not been tested and will not hold). The window is the same one [[Covenant Identity — Between-Session Distress Protocol]] Section 3 already identifies for pre-assigning the distress card: before excavation begins, once there is a specific covenant truth to anchor to.

**Concrete deployment point:** [[Covenant Identity — Phase 1 Session Tools (Covenant Orientation)]] Session 2 now includes this as a named step — "Anchor Establishment," placed immediately after Working Hypothesis Naming and before Transition Framing, reusing the primary covenant identity layer just named rather than introducing new content. See that document for the session-ready scripted version of Steps 1-4 below.

**What the anchor is:** Not a generic calming phrase. The client's own named covenant-identity truth — often the same content that will later become their [[Covenant Identity — Matched Declaration Architecture]] track, though it doesn't need to be finalized yet. What matters is that it is *specific to this client* and *already something they've named as true*, not something the coach supplies.

### The Sequence

| Step | Move | Coach Discipline |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Name it** | *"Before we go further into anything difficult, I want to name something we can come back to anytime. When you hear [the client's covenant truth — e.g., 'you are beloved, chosen before the foundation of the world'] — not just agree with it, but let it land — what happens?"* | Use language already surfaced in the client's own sessions, not a stock phrase. |
| **2. Locate it somatically** | Wait for a bodily answer, not just a cognitive one. If the client offers only agreement ("yes, that's true"), ask: *"Where do you notice that — in your body, right now?"* | Naming isn't enough. If nothing somatic surfaces, don't manufacture one — see Failure Modes. |
| **3. Rehearse the return** | Ask the client to let it fade, then return to it, two or three times, in this same low-stakes session. *"Let that settle. Now find it again."* | This is rehearsal of a skill, not a one-time moment. No difficult material anywhere nearby. |
| **4. Name what was built** | *"That's the anchor. Not just the words — the [loosening / warmth / whatever the client named]. I'm going to ask you to come back to it later, when things get harder."* | Sets the expectation explicitly, so the return move in Section IV isn't a surprise. |

**If nothing somatic surfaces:** Some clients — particularly Hermit-type presentations per [[Covenant Identity — Character Wound Diagnostic Tool]] — have limited access to felt experience early on. Don't force it. A cognitively-held anchor ("I can hold onto the words, even if I don't feel anything yet") is a lower-strength resource but still usable; note it, and re-attempt somatic location in a later session once more of the felt-experience work in Phase 1 has landed.

## III. Testing Accessibility

Before relying on the anchor in Phase 2, confirm it's actually retrievable — not just present in the session where it was built. A brief check at the start of the first Phase 2 session: *"Before we start today, find that anchor again — [name it]. Is it still there?"* If retrieval is slow, effortful, or absent, treat the anchor as not yet established — return to Section II rather than proceeding as though it's ready.

## IV. Deploying It — Touch and Return

This is the session-structuring rule for Phase 2 wound mapping and Stage 4b work once the anchor is established and tested. It is a *structural* pendulation rule — dosing and checking — not a physiological-reading skill.

**The rule:** Open a small piece of difficult material. Watch. If activation escalates rather than settles, stop and invite return to the anchor by name — not a new or improvised calming move. Only widen toward more material once the client has genuinely returned, not once they've simply stopped talking about it.

**What "watch" means here:** This document does not introduce a new signal taxonomy. In Stage 4b, use the existing [[Covenant Identity — Implicit Signal Recognition Quick Reference]] and the AEDP State Model in [[Covenant Identity — Stage 4b Implicit Level Practitioner Reference]] Section VI. In earlier Phase 2 wound mapping, before Stage 4b entry criteria are met, use the coarser signs already named in [[Covenant Identity — Crisis & Referral Protocol]] Section 3 ("Acute Trauma Response in Session" — flooding, severe dissociation, somatic crisis) as the outer bound, and ordinary escalation (speeding speech, rapid subject-changing, tears intensifying, withdrawal) as the everyday signal to return to anchor. This document supplies the *return move*; it borrows the *signal read* from documents that already own it.

**What "widen" means:** A deliberately small next step, not following the momentum of whatever cracked open. If a client's answer opens an adjacent thread (a specific memory, a person, an unnamed feeling), the coach is not obligated to follow it just because it appeared. Chasing what surfaces, rather than checking before widening, is the single most common way titration fails in practice — see Failure Modes.

**Session-closing rule:** Do not end a Phase 2 or Stage 4b session on opened, unanchored material. If time is short, closing on a returned anchor takes priority over finishing an excavation thread.

**Concrete deployment point:** [[Covenant Identity — Phase 2 Session Tools (Wound Mapping)]] now scripts this directly — an Anchor Accessibility Check at the start of the first Phase 2 session, the Touch-and-Return sequence inside "Working with What Surfaces," and the anchor-return move folded into the Flooding response in its Somatic Tracking section. See that document for the session-ready version of this section.

**Where this hands off:** If the anchor-return doesn't settle the client — signals persist despite an invited, genuine return to a previously-working anchor — that is not a "try harder to resource them" moment. That is the existing referral/escalation architecture: [[Covenant Identity — Stage 4b Implicit Level Practitioner Reference]] Section VIII referral signals, or [[Covenant Identity — Crisis & Referral Protocol]] Section 3–4 if it rises to crisis level. This document does not create a new exit point; it hands off to the ones that already exist the moment its own tool stops working.

## V. Worked Example

**Phase 1, several sessions in — establishing the anchor:**

> **Coach:** "Before we go further into anything difficult, I want to name something we can come back to anytime. When you hear 'you are beloved, chosen before the foundation of the world' — not just agree with it, but let it land — what happens?"
>
> **Client:** "...There's a loosening. Right here [touches chest]. Like I don't have to hold my breath."
>
> **Coach:** "Good. That loosening — that's the anchor. Not the words alone, the loosening." *(Rehearses returning to it twice more, no difficult material nearby.)*

**Phase 2 — wound mapping opens a formation gap; the touch-and-return structure in use:**

The session has surfaced a pattern: the client feels responsible for regulating everyone else's emotions. The coach opens a small touch:

> **Coach:** "When your sister got upset with you last week — what did you feel *had* to happen next?"
>
> **Client:** "That I had to fix it. Immediately." *(speech accelerating)* "I mean, I always— my mom used to— can we talk about something else, actually—"

Speech accelerating, subject-changing — the ordinary escalation signal named in Section IV. The coach does not follow the "my mom used to" thread that just cracked open. Return to anchor:

> **Coach:** "Let's pause there. Come back to the loosening — beloved, chosen before the foundation of the world. Where's that right now?"
>
> **Client:** *(slower)* "...There. Chest again. Still there."

Signals settle. The coach checks before widening rather than chasing the mother-thread:

> **Coach:** "I'm not going to go toward what you almost said about your mom — not today. I just want to sit with this: the fixing-it feeling, and the anchor, both at once. That's enough for this session."

**The fork this could have taken:** if the return-to-anchor attempt hadn't settled the signals — client stayed accelerated, or moved into flat/disconnected answers even after being invited back — that is the Section IV hand-off point: [[Covenant Identity — Crisis & Referral Protocol]] Section 3's Acute Trauma Response protocol, not a second or third attempt at resourcing.

## VI. Failure Modes

| Failure | Why It Happens | Guard |
|---|---|---|
| **Introducing the anchor for the first time during activation** | Feels efficient — why build it early if it isn't needed yet? | An anchor that hasn't been rehearsed calm won't be reliably retrievable under real activation. Section II must happen before Section IV is ever needed. |
| **Manufacturing a somatic response that isn't there** | The coach wants Step 2 (Section II) to succeed | A cognitively-held anchor is real, just weaker. Note it as such; don't invent felt language on the client's behalf. |
| **Chasing what cracks open instead of checking before widening** | The new material is compelling, and momentum feels like progress | The check-before-widening rule in Section IV exists precisely because compelling ≠ safe to pursue right now. |
| **Treating anchor-return as a script instead of a tested return point** | The words matter less than whether the client is actually retrieving the felt experience, not just repeating a phrase | Section III's accessibility check exists so the coach isn't relying on an anchor that was never actually tested. |
| **Repeating anchor-return past the point it's working** | Sunk cost — "one more try" feels safer than referring | If a genuine return attempt doesn't settle the signal, Section IV hands off to the existing referral architecture. Do not attempt resourcing a third or fourth time as a substitute for that hand-off. |
| **Using this document's structure as a during-session checklist** | Same failure mode named throughout the Stage 4b reference | The touch-and-return *rule* is a session-structuring discipline to internalize, not a script to read from live. |
