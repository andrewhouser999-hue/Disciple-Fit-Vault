# Covenant Identity — Directing Identity Work to a Specific Sphere — Practitioner Reference

*Practitioner reference (not a session tool, not client-facing on its own): a companion to [[Covenant Identity — Cross-Domain Identity Congruence — Psychological Frameworks Reference]] that operationalizes its findings into concrete guidance for using the existing Lie Eliminator and Identity Installer tools — both the personal versions in `Coaching Me/` and the client-workbook versions used in coaching engagements — to direct identity work at a specific low-congruence sphere (work, extended family, a specific relationship) rather than working identity generically.*

---

## The Reframe This Assumes

Per the parent document's Formation Implications section: the aim is not to eliminate the disparity between spheres — that overclaims what Scripture promises this side of glory and drifts toward a correction posture the Practice Definition's Dimension 2 guards against. The aim is increasing the capacity to recognize where the gap opens, understand what that specific sphere's conditions are doing, and practice truth calibrated to it. Everything below serves that aim — it is a set of *targeting* adjustments to tools that already exist, not a new tool and not a promise of total congruence.

---

## The Five Moves

### 1. Name the sphere and its specific conditions — don't leave the trigger generic

Both tools already have a field for this — Lie Eliminator's excavation questions and "The situation, thought, or moment that brings this lie to the surface," and Identity Installer's "This activates when." As currently used, these fields are usually filled with a *situation type* ("when I feel criticized") rather than a *sphere*. The sphere-specific version asks one layer deeper: *what's different about the relational conditions in this sphere specifically* — who's watching, what's at stake if fully seen, what does this sphere require performed or proven. That answer (evaluative threat, contingent regard, or low relatedness — the Sheldon/Ryan and Deci/Ryan need-support gap) is what determines which move below actually helps.

**Tool adaptation:** add an explicit **Sphere:** line immediately under the trigger field in both tools, distinct from the situational description already there. "This activates when: I'm asked a direct question in a status meeting" is a trigger; "Sphere: Work — reporting to my manager" is the sphere. Naming both separately makes the entry searchable by sphere later (Move 5) and keeps the coach or the personal-use reflection from collapsing "what happens" into "where it happens."

### 2. Build relational reinforcement inside that sphere, not just outside it

Per Stryker's role-salience/commitment mechanism (parent document, Framework 5): identity is more available where someone relationally reinforces it. If no one in the low-congruence sphere relates to the person through their covenant identity, that sphere stays the collapse point regardless of how much work happens elsewhere. This is not a tool-field change — it's a coaching-conversation and between-session move: identify one person inside (or reachable from) that sphere who can know and relate to the client as who they actually are in Christ, even briefly.

### 3. Practice truth *before* entering the sphere, not only in session or in prayer removed from it

Both tools' "Therefore I Will" / "Today I Will" fields are currently written as same-day or general-week commitments. For a sphere-linked pattern, retime this field specifically: a short reckoning practice done immediately before entering the sphere — before the Monday meeting, before the call with the parent, before the room — rather than a generic morning discipline. This is the same "touch and return" resourcing logic already built into [[Covenant Identity — Resourcing & Anchor Establishment — Practitioner Reference]]; applied here as a pre-sphere ritual rather than only a pre-session one.

### 4. Address what the sphere actually withholds, where that's genuinely possible

If a sphere is structurally low on autonomy-support or relatedness (a punitive supervisor, a family system that only relates to a fixed old role), the honest move is sometimes outside the tools entirely: reducing unnecessary exposure, a boundary conversation, or deliberately building one need-supportive relationship inside an otherwise need-starved sphere. This isn't a tool field — it's a coaching judgment call, and it's the one move on this list that is sometimes structural rather than internal.

### 5. Track the pattern per sphere over time — don't judge from one encounter

Identity Installer already has a three-phase tracking convention (Active → Integrating → Integrated) with an explicit rule for what happens when an old pattern resurfaces in an already-Integrated entry: *"that's not a failed integration — it's information that a new activating context exists. Give that context its own entry."* This is precisely the sphere-linked mechanism the parent document describes (Kegan & Lahey's domain-uneven subject-object shift), and the tool's own architecture already anticipates it — it just doesn't yet name "sphere" as the thing that changed. **Tool adaptation:** when the same identity truth is Integrated in one sphere and still Active in another, don't treat the Active sphere as a regression on the Integrated entry — give it its own Sphere-tagged sub-entry and run the full sequence for that sphere specifically, exactly as the tool's existing "new activating context" rule already directs. For CIC client use, feed sphere-tagged entries into the existing [[Covenant Identity — Diagnostic Calibration Log]] and [[Covenant Identity — Self-Supervision Template]] rather than treating a single sphere's stall as evidence the whole engagement isn't working.

## Concrete Tool Adaptation Summary

| Field | Current form | Sphere-specific adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger field (Lie Eliminator's excavation prompt / Identity Installer's "This activates when") | Situation-type description | Add a distinct **Sphere:** line naming the social domain, not just the situation |
| Lie Eliminator's "What Does That Lie Give You?" (secondary gain) | One payoff, asked once per lie | Ask whether the payoff differs by sphere — the same lie can pay out differently at work (avoiding visibility) than at home (maintaining control) |
| "Therefore I Will" / "Today I Will" | Same-day or general-week action | Retime as a pre-sphere-entry practice when the pattern is sphere-linked — done immediately before entering that specific sphere |
| Identity Installer's Phase tracking (Active/Integrating/Integrated) | One phase per identity entry | Track phase **per sphere-tagged sub-entry** when the same identity holds at different phases in different spheres — this already follows the tool's own "new activating context" rule, just applied deliberately rather than incidentally |

This is a targeting change, not a rebuild — none of the four fields above require restructuring either workbook. The Sphere line and the per-sphere phase convention are the only two literal additions; the rest is a deployment discipline (asking the existing questions with a sphere specifically in view).

## Worked Example

Using an existing entry from *Identity Installer — Client Workbook*, "1. Beloved Child of God" (old pattern: the orphan), for a client whose covenant identity holds at church and coaching but collapses at work:

- **Sphere:** Work — performance review conversations with a supervisor
- **This activates when** (existing field, kept as-is): being asked to account for a missed deadline
- **What's different about this sphere** (per the parent document's follow-up questions): evaluative exposure with real consequences (pay, standing); no one in this sphere relates to the client through their covenant identity; the sphere structurally resembles the original wound's conditions (a performance-contingent authority figure)
- **Secondary gain, sphere-specific:** staying braced/defensive here avoids the risk of being fully seen and then dismissed by someone whose approval has income attached to it — a different payoff than the same lie's payoff at home
- **Today I Will (retimed):** thirty seconds before walking into the review — not that morning, not the night before — reckon aloud: *"I am not a servant earning access to the Father's house. I live in it. This conversation cannot revoke that."*
- **Phase tracking:** this entry may already read Integrated for the *church* sphere while staying Active, sphere-tagged "Work," here — that is not a regression, per the tool's own existing rule
