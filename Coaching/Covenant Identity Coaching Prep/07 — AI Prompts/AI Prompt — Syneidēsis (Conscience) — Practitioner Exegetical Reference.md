## Purpose

Build a single-term practitioner exegetical reference on *syneidēsis* (conscience) — structured as the direct sibling to [[Covenant Identity — Isaiah 66 2b — Practitioner Exegetical Reference]], not as a new diagnostic system. [[Covenant Identity — The Inner Man — Biblical Foundations and Position Paper]] §6 surveyed ~20 NT occurrences and concluded the term carries a distinct theological payload — chiefly Hebrews 9:9,14 and 10:22's claim that conscience is not merely examined but *cleansed*, by Christ's blood specifically — that no existing CIC document treats on its own terms. That survey was breadth-first, confirming the term clears the bar for its own document. This prompt is for the depth-first pass: full exegesis, the model-integration decision, and — this is the part the survey deliberately left undone — the specific design question of where conscience work fits without duplicating what [[Covenant Identity — Why the Heart Does Not Heal — Biblical Research]] and the affections diagnostic already do.

**Why this matters enough to build:** three of the twenty occurrences are not restatements of heart-opacity material CIC already has. (1) 1 Corinthians 8:7-12's "weak conscience" — genuinely defiled by an action even when the belief driving the guilt is factually mistaken — is the closest NT analog to a conditioned, implicit moral response overriding known propositional truth CIC currently has no dedicated text for. (2) Hebrews 9:9,14/10:22's cleansing claim is a distinct divine action (not searching, not testing — cleansing, by a specific priestly-atoning act) paired with the same "draw near" language already anchoring [[Covenant Identity — Conditions for the Spirit's Sanctifying Work]]. (3) 1 Timothy 1:19's "good conscience... shipwreck of the faith" makes conscience load-bearing for faith itself, not a peripheral faculty.

## The Design Question This Prompt Exists to Force — Not to Answer in Advance

**Conscience material pulls toward moral-compliance/guilt-management framing.** This is exactly the terrain [[Covenant Identity — Conditions for the Spirit's Sanctifying Work]]'s Legalism guard and [[Sanctification — Teacher's Manual]]'s failure-mode architecture exist to keep CIC out of. A document about conscience that reads as "here is how to help a client have better moral self-monitoring" would be a category error against the model's own governing frame — CIC diagnoses through affections and functional belief, not through behavioral/moral compliance.

**The resolving move, already implicit in the source texts, is to require this document to hold conscience *inside* the affections/identity frame rather than beside it:** 1 Corinthians 8's weak-conscience dynamic is not really about the eating-meat behavior — it is diagnostic of what the person's conscience was *formed by* (habituated association with idols) prior to and independent of what they now believe propositionally. Read this way, a miscalibrated or seared conscience is not a separate moral-monitoring problem CIC has to add a new tool for — it is *further diagnostic evidence of the same functional/confessional gap* [[Covenant Identity — Functional vs. Confessional Theology — 20 Diagnostic Contrasts]] already names, now sourced to a different NT vocabulary. The document must make this connection explicit and structural, not incidental — it is what keeps the document inside CIC's existing architecture instead of building a parallel one.

**Test every section against this question before finalizing:** does this read as "diagnostic data about what the person's affections have formed" (in-model) or "a checklist for better moral behavior" (legalism drift)? If a sentence could be lifted into a behavior-modification program without alteration, rewrite it.

## Document Structure

Match [[Covenant Identity — Isaiah 66 2b — Practitioner Exegetical Reference]]'s ten-section structure exactly, adapted for a term study rather than a single-verse study:

1. **Document Purpose** — two functions (rigorous exegetical reference + model integration note), practitioner-only, not client-facing. State up front that this document exists because the breadth-first survey in the Inner Man paper found real, non-duplicative content — name that finding in one sentence rather than re-arguing it.
2. **Text & Context** — *syneidēsis* is absent from the OT (no single Hebrew equivalent; the concept surfaces functionally via *lev*-condemnation language, per 1 John 3:19-21 above) and is a Hellenistic term Paul adopts and redirects, not a native biblical-anthropology category the way *lev* is. State this plainly — it changes the term's evidentiary weight relative to *lev*/*kardia* and should inform the Historical-Cultural Context section, not just this one.
3. **Language** — *syneidēsis* itself (co-knowledge, joint-knowing — literally "knowing-with"), plus *kekautēriasmenōn* (1 Tim 4:2, "seared/branded/cauterized" — the scar-tissue metaphor, distinct from *sklērunō*'s hardening metaphor already used for the heart in [[Covenant Identity — Why the Heart Does Not Heal — Biblical Research]] §2 — name the distinction, don't collapse the two metaphors into one), and *katharizō* (Heb 9:14, "cleanse, purify" — the term this document's central claim rests on).
4. **Historical-Cultural Context** — Stoic and popular Hellenistic moral-philosophy background for *syneidēsis* as an internal moral witness predates Paul; what Paul does distinctively with an already-available term (Rom 2:14-15's application to Gentiles without the law is the clearest evidence of this move — conscience as evidence of universal moral awareness even absent revelation).
5. **Intertextual Connections** — 1 John 3:19-21 (heart-language doing the same work); the Hebrews 9/10 cleansing texts read against the whole sacrificial-system argument of Hebrews (What could the old sacrifices not reach, and why can Christ's blood reach it); Acts 24:16 read against Paul's own self-description elsewhere (2 Cor 1:12, 2 Tim 1:3) as a consistent practice, not a one-off claim.
6. **Theological Weight** — three-layer structure matching Why the Heart Does Not Heal's convention (or a structure the exegesis itself suggests, if three layers doesn't fit the material): what conscience *reveals* (universal moral witness, Rom 2:15), what can go *wrong* with it (weak/miscalibrated, seared — two distinct failure modes, not one), and what *repairs* it (cleansing by Christ's blood specifically, not merely forgiveness of the person generally — name what's distinctive about that claim).
7. **Interpretive Debate** — at minimum: whether *syneidēsis* names a genuinely distinct faculty or is simply *kardia*'s self-judging function under different (Hellenistic-sourced) vocabulary — state a position, don't leave it open, since [[Covenant Identity — The Inner Man — Biblical Foundations and Position Paper]] §6 already committed to the aspectival reading and this document needs to either confirm that fit or name a genuine exception if the exegesis forces one.
8. **Coaching Translation** — this is where the design question above gets answered, not merely gestured at. Explicit statement: weak/seared conscience as functional/confessional-gap diagnostic data (cross-reference, don't rebuild); the cleansing claim as covenant-truth/declaration content (candidate Lie Eliminator or Identity Installer anchor, matching the format Isaiah 66:2b §8 uses for its own counter-truth entry); explicit statement of what this document does NOT authorize (a moral-compliance checklist, a new stage in the diagnostic sequence, a self-examination discipline framed as effort rather than Spirit-dependent means of grace).
9. **Session Application** — diagnostic use (what a weak or seared conscience presentation looks like in session, read as affections/formation data), declarative/identity use (the cleansing claim as counter-truth, following Isaiah 66:2b §8's Lie/Counter-truth entry format), and — only if the exegesis and the legalism test both support it — formation use (Acts 24:16, framed correctly or not included at all).
10. **Model Integration — Where This Text Fits**, matching Isaiah 66:2b §9's exact format: a stated verdict (not a menu), primary address, secondary addresses table, and a closing **"What This Document Does NOT Add"** subsection — this is the load-bearing section for keeping the document inside the model's existing architecture. State explicitly that this is not a new diagnostic instrument, not a new discipline unless justified above, and names its relationship to Why the Heart Does Not Heal, Functional vs. Confessional Theology, and the Inner Man position paper without duplicating any of them.

Close with a **Document Provenance** section (matching this vault's standing convention) and a full **Document Links** section matching Isaiah 66:2b §10's format (load-bearing connections, covenant truth/identity work, formation connections, sanctification module, broader biblical framework, practitioner infrastructure).
