## Purpose

Build a single practitioner-reference position paper answering one governing question: **what does Scripture actually establish about the inner man — the site CIC treats as its primary terrain — and where does that knowledge stop?**

This is CIC's most load-bearing anthropological claim, spread thin across at least six documents (Peer Practitioner Briefing, Why the Heart Does Not Heal, Whole-Person Identity Framework, Terminology & Language Reference, Isaiah 66 2b, the Implicit Throughline) and, as of this session, a research thread in the Biblical Foundations Index and Central Verses file that has outgrown a footnote. No document currently states the whole case in one place, with its limits stated as plainly as its claims. This paper is that document.

**Why now:** CIC coaches work with the person across a range from conscious cognition to what the model calls implicit — patterns the client enacts but cannot report or feel directly (see [[Covenant Identity — The Implicit Throughline — Practitioner Orientation]]). That range is currently grounded in psychology (Thompson, Ecker, Nader & LeDoux, Siegel) with a documented, honestly-flagged gap: no standalone document establishes what Scripture itself claims about that same range, on its own terms, before psychology supplies a mechanism. This paper closes that gap — as a *base understanding* coaches can reason from, not a new session tool.

## The Unresolved Tension This Paper Must Resolve, Not Merely Flag

Identified in this session's /scrutinize pass as the single most dangerous gap in the current synthesis: every text this thread has assembled (Jer 17:9-10, Ps 19:12, Prov 20:5, kelayot pairing) shows the heart as opaque, self-inaccessible, requiring external disclosure. None of them have been tested against Scripture's equally-canonical *transparency* tradition:

- **Proverbs 4:23** — "Keep your heart with all vigilance, for from it flow the springs of life." Assumes the heart is within the person's own direct, guardable reach.
- **Deuteronomy 8:2** — "that he might know what was in your heart" — the wilderness testing is framed as making heart-content knowable, to the person, through experience.
- **1 Kings 8:38** — "each knowing the affliction of his own heart" — assumes ordinary self-knowledge of the heart as a working premise, not an achievement requiring special disclosure.

This is the wisdom-literature-optimism vs. prophetic-literature-pessimism tension named in OT theology for a century (Wolff, von Rad) — not new territory, but currently absent from every CIC document touching the heart's opacity, including this session's own research. **The paper must state this tension explicitly and resolve it** — the working hypothesis to test (not assume): vigilance and opacity are not contradictory if vigilance is read as ongoing, Spirit-assisted practice rather than a claim of total self-transparency; Proverbs 4:23's imperative doesn't claim complete self-access, only that what is guarded — imperfectly, partially — matters most. If a better resolution emerges from the source reading, use that instead. Do not simply omit the transparency texts because they complicate the thesis.

## Position Paper Structure

Organize into nine sections. Each claim in Sections 2-6 gets a status flag (Developed / Assumed / Contested / Gap) matching the Biblical Foundations Index's own convention — this paper should be citable back into that Index once complete, not run as a parallel, inconsistent standard.

### 1. Purpose & Governing Question
State the question this paper answers and why now (see Purpose above). One page maximum.

### 2. What We Know — Biblically Affirmed Claims
Each claim stated individually with its status flag and primary text(s):
- Inner man and heart are not two faculties but two names for one site (Eph 3:16-17's grammar — Developed, this paper is the write-up)
- The heart is the whole-person control center — cognition, volition, moral orientation — not primarily emotion (lev/kardia range, already Developed in Why the Heart Does Not Heal and Terminology Reference)
- The heart holds content not transparent to ordinary self-examination (Jer 17:9-10, Ps 19:12, Prov 20:5, kelayot pairing — Assumed, becoming Developed here)
- The heart is nonetheless addressable, guardable, and formable by the person themselves (Prov 4:23, Deut 8:2, 1 Kings 8:38 — new to CIC documentation entirely; state as its own claim, not a footnote to the claim above)
- God fully knows and searches what the person cannot (Jer 17:10, Ps 139:23-24, Rev 2:23 — Developed)
- The Word reaches what ordinary self-examination cannot (Heb 4:12 — Assumed, new)
- The Spirit indwells and transforms the inner man/heart, definitively at new birth and progressively in sanctification (Ezek 36:26-27, 2 Cor 3:18, Eph 3:16-17 — Developed, drawing on existing Sanctification Module and Why the Heart Does Not Heal grounding)
- Christ names the inner condition first, at the outset of His public mission (Isaiah 61:1-2, Luke 4:18-21 — Developed, drawing on Peer Practitioner Briefing)

### 3. What We Cannot Claim — Explicit Limits
Stated with the same specificity as Section 2, not as a shorter afterthought:
- Scripture does not describe a *mechanism* (encoding, procedural memory, reconsolidation) — this is psychology's contribution, not Scripture's
- "Heart" is not a single technical term with one stable sense across 850+ occurrences — the range is real and not always separable
- The container-vs-aspectival question is not self-evidently settled by the text — state the paper's own position here (see below) and name it as a position, not a finding
- The biblical authors cannot be shown to have meant "implicit memory" in the clinical-neuroscientific sense — correlation of referent, not identity of concept
- The heart is not *only* opaque — Section 2's guardable-heart claim means unqualified opacity-claims currently used elsewhere in CIC documentation (including this session's own prior turns) overstate the textual case and should be corrected going forward

### 4. Man's Relationship with His Own Inner Man
Synthesize Sections 2-3's tension into a working, stateable position: real but limited self-access; responsibility to guard (Prov 4:23) alongside genuine inability to fully self-diagnose (Jer 17:9); the psalmist's own request to be searched (Ps 139:23-24) as evidence that biblical piety already assumes partial self-opacity *without* concluding total inaccessibility.

### 5. God's Relationship with the Inner Man
Creates it (imago Dei), knows it exhaustively, searches and tests it, promises and enacts its transformation (new heart/new covenant), indwells it, reaches it through Word and Spirit — organized as a single coherent divine posture, not a list.

### 6. Wider Vocabulary Survey — Beyond Heart and Inner Man
Test each term individually against the same question (what does *this* word, specifically, establish about the explicit/implicit range) — do not assume they all say what *lev*/*kardia* says:
- *nous* (mind) — Rom 7:23, 12:2, Eph 4:23
- human *pneuma* (spirit) — 1 Cor 2:11, Rom 8:16, 1 Thess 5:23
- *psychē* / *nephesh* (soul) — caution first: OT *nephesh* often names the whole living being (Gen 2:7, "living creature"), not an interior compartment — this term may argue *against* a faculty-component reading more than for one
- *syneidēsis* (conscience) — Rom 2:15, 1 Tim 4:2 ("seared conscience") — a plausible biblical analog for something like conditioned, below-conscious moral response; test rather than assume
- *splanchna* (bowels/inward parts — NT affections) — Phil 1:8, 2:1, Col 3:12 — visceral/gut-level language, structurally parallel to OT *kelayot*
- *sarx* / body as encoding site — Rom 7, and cross-reference [[Covenant Identity — Whole-Person Identity Framework]]'s "Physical" dimension (body communicates what is held implicitly) for where this paper's textual claim and that document's psychological claim do or don't align

### 7. Wider Theological-Psychological Literature
Organized as Confirmed / Verify-Before-Citing per the Source Cluster list above. State plainly which authors do the specific textual move and which are adjacent-but-different, per this session's /scrutinize findings — do not let confidence bleed from confirmed to unconfirmed sources.

### 8. Base Understanding for CIC Coaches
What a coach can say with confidence in a peer conversation; what must be flagged as model-language or inference rather than direct textual claim; explicit statement that this paper supplies theological warrant and vocabulary, not a replacement for the existing implicit/explicit diagnostic mechanism (Thompson/Ecker/Siegel) that Diagnostic Lens Transition Logic and the Implicit Throughline already own.

### 9. Open Questions / Recommended Next Research
