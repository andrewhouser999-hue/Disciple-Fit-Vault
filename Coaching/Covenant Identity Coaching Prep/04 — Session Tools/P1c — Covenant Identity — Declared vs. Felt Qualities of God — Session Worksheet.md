# P1c — Covenant Identity — Declared vs. Felt Qualities of God — Session Worksheet

*Coach-facilitated, in-session tool. Not a stand-alone diagnostic — a short opening/surfacing move that produces a concrete, client-generated example of the declared/felt gap the model already names theoretically, then routes onward to the tools that actually work the gap.*

## Where This Fits

Deployment window: Phase 1, alongside the God-representation surfacing step already named in [[P1a — Covenant Identity — Phase 1 Session Tools (Covenant Orientation)]]. Use this when you want that surfacing to produce something concrete and felt rather than staying at the level of "tell me about your view of God." Can be re-deployed later in the engagement if a client's *stated* theology shifts (often quickly, from declaration work) while the felt layer visibly hasn't caught up yet — the worksheet gives you a fresh, dated comparison point rather than relying on memory of where things stood at intake.

**Design note:** the three-word naming-then-comparing structure is Andrew's own synthesis, built to be a single short exercise a client can do inside one session. The theoretical distinction it operationalizes and the attending technique it uses are both sourced (below) — the packaging of the two together into this specific short form is not itself a citation.

## Mechanism — Why This Works

Explicit belief (what a client can state about God) and implicit God-image (what a client's nervous system actually expects from God) are two separate systems that don't automatically move together — this is the core finding across Rizzuto, Hall, and Benner in [[Covenant Identity — God-Representation — Research Basis & Synthesis]]. A client can hold theologically correct content about God's character while their felt, moment-to-moment expectation of God runs on an entirely different, earlier-formed template. Naming this abstractly ("there's a gap between what you believe and what you feel") rarely lands with force; producing one concrete instance of it — in the client's own words, in the room, right now — does. Gendlin's Focusing method (already CIC's mechanism for Stage 4b Route B; see [[Covenant Identity — Stage 4b Implicit Level Practitioner Reference]]) supplies the actual technique for Movement 3's "get quiet and notice" step, rather than leaving it as unstructured introspection a client won't know how to do on their own.

## Contraindications / Scope

Same referral logic as every other CIC in-session tool — see [[Covenant Identity — Crisis & Referral Protocol]]. This is an attending exercise, not trauma processing:

- Do not deploy in a session where the client is already dysregulated or in acute distress; settle first, or hold the exercise for another session.
- If Movement 3 surfaces content connected to a specific injury (abuse, acute grief, dissociative material), stop the exercise itself and follow standard Stage 4b / Crisis Protocol tracking rather than pushing through all three qualities as planned.
- This tool does not replace [[Covenant Identity — Character Wound Diagnostic Tool]] or [[Covenant Identity — God-Representation Deepening Tool]] if a full wound-type or God-representation-type picture is what's actually needed — it's a short entry move that may point toward needing one of those, not a substitute for either.

## Materials

Paper and pen, or the fillable worksheet table below. No between-session component — this is a single in-session exercise (contrast with the still-unbuilt solo Focusing between-session practice flagged in [[contributor_gendlin_focusing.md|Gendlin/McMahon & Campbell contributor note]]; that gap remains open and is not what this tool addresses).

## Facilitation Sequence

**Movement 1 — Name**
Ask the client to write the first three words or short phrases that come to mind when they think of God — a quality, a title, an image. Coach note: don't let the client self-edit toward "the right answer"; the value is in the first, unfiltered response. If a client freezes on "what am I supposed to say," gently redirect: "there's no correct answer here — whatever actually comes first."

**Movement 2 — Settle**
Lead 30–60 seconds of quiet before starting Movement 3. A few slow breaths together, named out loud, works well. This isn't filler — moving straight from writing into attending skips the state shift Focusing depends on.

**Movement 3 — Attend** *(repeat once per quality, in order)*
Walk the client through Gendlin's sequence, condensed for a single short pass:

a. **Say it personally.** Client silently completes: "God is [word] toward me" — first person, not a general theological statement.
b. **Attend.** Ask the client to notice what's present in their body without judging or explaining it yet — chest, stomach, shoulders, throat. Give real silence here; don't fill it.
c. **Handle.** Ask for a word or short phrase that names the sensation itself — which will often *not* be the same word as the one they started with. ("What would you call what's there?")
d. **Resonate.** Ask the client to check the handle against the original word: does it match, does it not, is there a third thing neither word quite captures? This is the comparison move — hold it lightly, no pressure toward a tidy answer.
e. **Ask** *(optional — offer only if the client seems receptive, not as a required step)*: "Is there anything that's connected to, or anything God might be showing you here?"
f. **Receive.** Whatever surfaces, receive it without forcing resolution. Write the Handle and the Match/Gap/Unclear read on the worksheet before moving to the next quality.

**Movement 4 — Compare & Name the Gap**
Once all three qualities have a Handle, reflect the pattern back to the client using the model's own vocabulary: "Here's what you named, and here's what you actually found when you sat with it." A full match across all three, a partial match, and a full gap are all simply data — this movement should not communicate that convergence is success and divergence is failure. Per the God-Representation Research Basis synthesis, a gap here is the expected, common condition this model exists to work with, not a diagnostic red flag on its own.

**Movement 5 — Route**
Based on what surfaced, decide the next move rather than treating this worksheet as complete in itself:

- Gap traces to a nameable relational injury or wound pattern → [[Covenant Identity — Character Wound Diagnostic Tool]] or [[Covenant Identity — God-Representation Deepening Tool]].
- Gap centers on fear-substitution around a specific attribute of God (e.g., stated "God is faithful," felt anxious uncertainty) → [[Covenant Identity — Faith or Fear — Diagnosing Vain Imagination and the Attributes of God]].
- No clear route yet, or the client needs more time to sit with what surfaced → hold it, note it for a future session, and don't force a diagnostic conclusion the material doesn't yet support.

## Relationship to Other God-Image Tools

This worksheet sits upstream of, and routes into, two existing tools rather than duplicating either:

- **[[Covenant Identity — Faith or Fear — Diagnosing Vain Imagination and the Attributes of God]]** works a fixed set of seven specific attributes one at a time through a fear-substitution question bank, deployed as a diagnostic-plus-intervention sequence. This worksheet instead starts from whatever three qualities the *client* names first, uses Focusing rather than a question bank as the attending mechanism, and is a short surfacing move rather than a full deployed sequence — the two are complementary entry points, not competing versions of the same tool.
- **[[Covenant Identity — God-Representation Deepening Tool]]** works a client's full presenting God-representation against six named types once one is already visible. This worksheet is what can produce that first visible instance in-session; it doesn't attempt the six-type mapping itself.
