# "Let God Order Your Steps" — Sermon Sketch

**Format:** Full-length sermon (companion piece to "Sin as Missing the Mark" — same delivery voice, longer form). Working sketch — bracketed notes mark where Andrew inserts personal illustration before this is delivery-ready.

---

I want to ask you something intensely personal. Not "do you go to church." Not "do you read your Bible sometimes." As a child of God — are you doing the will God has laid out for you? Do you know the will of God for your life?

Here's why I'm asking it that directly. Paul prayed over the church at Colossae that they would be "filled with the knowledge of his will in all spiritual wisdom and understanding" (Col 1:9). Filled. Not guessing at it. Not circling it. Filled with the knowledge of it.

But everywhere I go, when I ask people this question, I get the same answer: "I think so." "Kind of." "Somewhat." "Maybe." That is not the standard God set. God didn't say I want you groping toward my will. He said I want you filled with the knowledge of it — and doing it.

Write this down: Proverbs 3:5-6. *"Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and do not lean on your own understanding. In all your ways acknowledge him, and he will make straight your paths."*

If you do this — all of it, not part of it — God says: I will direct your path. Not I might. Not I'll consider it. I will direct your path. God either means that, or His Word isn't trustworthy. His Word is trustworthy. So He means it.

He backs that promise up everywhere in Scripture. Isaiah 58:11: *"the LORD will guide you continually... like a spring of water, whose waters do not fail."* Psalm 32:8: *"I will instruct you and teach you in the way you should go; I will counsel you with my eye upon you."* Think about that picture for a second — guiding you with His eye. That's not distant supervision. That's the most intimate leadership language there is. Any parent in this room knows exactly what that means — you can move your child across a crowded room with nothing but a look, because you know them and they're watching you that closely. That's the posture God says He takes toward you.

Ephesians 2:10: *"we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand, that we should walk in them."* Psalm 37:23: *"The steps of a man are established by the LORD, when he delights in his way."*

Can you say that about last week? Can you say, "God ordered my steps, and I knew it while it was happening"?

[ANDY: insert personal story here — one time you didn't listen and it cost you, one time you did and watched God confirm it. The contrast is the point; let the two stories do the work rather than explaining the lesson afterward.]

Here's Satan's master strategy, and it's so simple I've fallen for it more times than I want to admit. He doesn't need you to rebel outright. He just needs you to give it your best shot. Do the best you can, and ask God to kick in and help where you fall short. That sounds humble. It isn't. It's sovereignty with a little room made for God on the side.

We say it without even hearing ourselves say it: "God, I've got real experience here, real talent. I'll do the very best I can, and what I need from You is some help along the way. You and me — we'll be a dynamic duo." God's answer to that offer is no. He's not interested in that partnership. He says, "I want to order your steps. I want you filled with the knowledge of My will" — not consulted on it after you've already decided.

So if you actually want the will of God for your life, here's the first thing you have to do.

**1. Let God choose the way.**

Not just the destination — the way. What if He wants something you don't want? What if He wants to send you somewhere you'd never pick? What if He wants you to give up something you don't want to give up? You could try to play a little game with Him — "just show me what You've got in mind, and I'll let You know if I'm interested." That sounds reasonable. It will never get you the perfect will of God, because it isn't trust. It's evaluation.

"In all your ways acknowledge Him" — all of them, not the ones you've already vetted.

Jeremiah 10:23: *"I know, O LORD, that the way of man is not in himself, that it is not in man who walks to direct his steps."* We don't get to direct our own steps and expect them to land right. Left to ourselves, our steps lead us astray. That's not pessimism — that's the diagnosis Scripture gives for why we need Him to lead at all.

Underneath that is a trust problem. And here's the thing — you're already trusting Him with the biggest thing there is. It was His will for you to be saved. 2 Peter 3:9: He is *"not wishing that any should perish, but that all should reach repentance."* You trusted Him with eternity. Now He says: trust Me with Tuesday. He loves you too much to hand you something that isn't for your good. Somewhere in the middle of that — trusting Him with eternity, but negotiating with Him over the everyday — I get caught. Maybe you do too.

**2. Let God confirm your steps.**

God never asks you to move on a hunch. He confirms His will three ways.

*First, His Word.* Get into it and let it talk to you — not just to check your plans against it after the fact, but let it be the thing that surfaces what you should do. God will never confirm something that contradicts what He's already said in Scripture. If it contradicts the Word, it isn't Him, no matter how strong the impression feels.

[ANDY: insert your own example of a decision the Word confirmed or blocked.]

*Second, prayer.* Jeremiah 33:3: *"Call to me and I will answer you, and will tell you great and hidden things that you have not known."* When's the last time you actually said, "God — talk to me"? Not recited words at Him. Asked, and then waited for an answer.

Mark 11:22-24, Jesus says: *"Have faith in God... whoever says to this mountain, 'Be taken up and thrown into the sea,' and does not doubt in his heart, but believes that what he says will come to pass, it will be done for him... whatever you ask in prayer, believe that you have received it, and it will be yours."* Faith in God — not in your own talent or ability. Whatever experience and skill you're leaning on, it's nothing next to actually knowing the will of God and asking Him for it. When you've got a mountain in front of you, call on Him. Ask Him to show you. And once He confirms it, He expects you to move — not sit on the confirmation and keep asking.

*Third, wisdom.* James 1:5: *"If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask God, who gives generously to all without reproach, and it will be given him."* Knowledge tells you what to do. Wisdom tells you how and when to do it. You can be educated and still be derelict — knowing the facts and never arriving at the truth, because you're clinging to your own bias, and knowledge without wisdom just puffs a person up. In a world this complex, knowledge alone leaves you confused. Wisdom is what God supplies when you ask Him for it in faith.

That's the same faith Jesus describes in Matthew 17:20 — faith the size of a mustard seed can move a mountain. It takes something like twenty-two to twenty-four thousand mustard seeds to weigh a single ounce. That's how small the seed is. And Jesus says that's the size of faith it takes. Not manufactured confidence. Not "I've done this before, so I know it'll work." Just enough real faith to ask, and to expect Him to answer.

Here's something worth sitting with about that seed. Matthew 17:20 doesn't say your faith has to be big. It says faith as small as a mustard seed — the smallest seed there is — is enough, because the power was never in the size of your faith. It's in who your faith is aimed at. Faith in God, not in yourself. Small faith is not a disqualification. It's a starting point He already accounted for.

But don't stop at small. Jesus uses that same tiny seed again in Matthew 13:31-32, and there He tells you what happens to it over time: *"It is the smallest of all seeds, but when it has grown it is larger than all the garden plants and becomes a tree, so that the birds of the air come and make nests in its branches."* Put those two pictures together the way the image itself invites you to: small faith, aimed at Him, planted in the soil of Him ordering your steps — that seed doesn't stay small. Keep it aimed at Him long enough, keep letting Him direct it instead of you, and you don't just end up with a seed. You end up with a mustard plant. A life so given over to His ordering that it stops being just yours — it grows until it takes up all of you, and then it grows past you, until there's room in your branches for other people to rest.

That doesn't happen by trying harder to have more faith. It happens the way Paul describes in 2 Corinthians 3:18 — you keep your eyes fixed on Him, beholding Him, and the beholding itself is what grows you. You don't grow the plant. You keep the seed aimed at the light, and He does the growing. Hebrews 12:2 says to run "looking to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of our faith, who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross." Small faith isn't even the category for Him — but sustained faith, eyes fixed on the Father the whole way through, is exactly what He modeled for you to follow. Aim your faith at Him ordering your steps, and don't be surprised if what started this small ends up sheltering people you haven't even met yet.

So here's where that leaves us. Do you know His will, and are you walking in His steps? Both. Not one or the other. Knowledge without walking it out is just information. Walking without knowledge is just guessing with confidence.

Leaning on your own understanding, you will always find a reason to do something else. It'll sound wise. It'll sound responsible. It just won't be trust.

So tell Him: any time, anywhere, any cost.

*"Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and do not lean on your own understanding. In all your ways acknowledge him, and he will make straight your paths."*

## Scrutiny notes (for revision before distribution)

- **Book misattribution corrected:** the raw sketch cited "the apostle who wrote to Ephesians" for "filled with the knowledge of his will" — that line is Colossians 1:9, addressed to the church at Colossae, not Ephesus. Corrected throughout.
- **Verse number corrected:** "guide thee with mine eye" is Psalm 32:8, not 32:6 as in the raw sketch (32:6 is a different verse about the time of distress). Corrected.
- **Translation drift corrected:** several quotations in the raw sketch used KJV-era phrasing (Jer 33:3 "great and mighty things" → ESV "great and hidden things"; Ps 37:23 "steps of a good man are ordered" → ESV "steps of a man are established"; James 1:6 "ask in faith, nothing wavering" → ESV "ask in faith, with no doubting"). All quotations above are now ESV per standing translation rule; if you deliver this from memory in KJV cadence, that's a stylistic choice to make consciously, not an accuracy gap to fix later.
- **Mustard-seed text corrected:** the raw sketch attaches the mustard-seed detail to Mark 11:22–24 (the mountain/faith/prayer passage). The "faith like a grain of a mustard seed" line is actually Matthew 17:20, a separate passage with a similar mountain image. Both are kept above, cited separately, so the mountain-moving image doesn't get credited to the wrong text if this is ever put in writing or quoted directly.
- **Stephen Olford attribution — needs your verification:** the raw sketch names "Steven Olfred... the great London preacher who then came to NY and pastored... Calvary Baptist Church." This is very likely Stephen F. Olford (1918–2004), the well-known expositor who did pastor Calvary Baptist Church in Manhattan (1959–1973) — but I could not verify the exact "find it, follow it, finish it"-style quote or confirm your secondhand source (Curtis Hudson) said it as reported. I softened the biographical claim rather than asserting an unverified quote as settled fact. Before you preach this, confirm the quote and attribution against whatever source you first heard it from — don't let an unverified secondhand citation go out as a stated fact.
- **Mustard-seed growth paragraph — intentional two-text synthesis, flagged per the practitioner reference's own caution:** [[Covenant Identity — Faith as a Mustard Seed (Matthew 17 20) — Practitioner Exegetical Reference]] is explicit that Matthew 17:20 (faith *as* a mustard seed, a single-moment comparison of quantity/quality with no growth verb in the Greek) and Matthew 13:31-32 (the mustard seed *parable*, which does contain the growth-to-tree-to-nesting-birds trajectory) are two distinct sayings, and that reading 17:20 through 13:31-32's growth lens is a common but exegetically imported move — acceptable for teaching only if presented explicitly as a canonical synthesis across two texts, not as what 17:20 says on its own. The added paragraph above does exactly that synthesis on purpose (small-faith-aimed-at-God from 17:20, growth-into-a-sheltering-tree from 13:31-32) and names both references separately so the two texts stay distinguishable if this is ever quoted in writing.
- **2 Peter 3:9 framing:** the sketch uses this verse (God "not wishing that any should perish") to argue "you already trust Him with the biggest thing — salvation." That's a legitimate application move but worth naming as application, not exegesis: the verse's own context is about the Lord's patience regarding the timing of Christ's return, not directly about the mechanics of trusting God's guidance day to day. The sermon's use of it is a bridge you're building, not what the text is itself about.
