# "The Word for Sin Doesn't Mean What You Think" — Video Script

**Format:** 3–4 min spoken video content. Foundational/trust-building teaching piece — same core content is reusable for CIC and Formation & Transition with medium-specific structural adaptation only (see practice-architecture note below).

---

There's a word in the New Testament we translate one way, and it's cost us. The Greek word is *hamartia* — ἁμαρτία. And it doesn't mean "to break a rule." It's an archery term. It means to miss the mark — to aim, and fall short.

So Paul writes, in Romans 3:23: *"For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God."*

Now here's the question almost nobody asks: fall short of *what*? The target isn't a moral checklist. The target is the glory of God — the full weight of who He is, and the full weight of what He made you to be. Genesis 1 says you were made in His image. You were shaped to carry His glory the way an arrow is shaped to fly true. That's the target.

So when Scripture says you've sinned, it's not first saying "you broke a rule." It's saying: you were designed for something, and you're not hitting it.

That's why sin in the Bible never sounds like a courtroom violation only. It sounds like blindness — John 9. It sounds like slavery — John 8. It sounds like death — Romans 6. Because something hasn't just gone wrong in what you've *done*. Something has gone wrong in what you're *aimed at*.

Augustine called it "disordered loves." Your heart always moves toward whatever it's pointed at. And sin, at its root, is a life pointed at the wrong target — chasing approval, control, comfort, self — when it was built to be pointed at God.

Here's what makes this good news, not despair: an arrow that misses isn't just guilty. It's *lost*. And you don't fix a lost arrow by trying harder to fly straight. You need someone to reset the aim entirely.

Only one person in history never missed. Hebrews 4:15 says Jesus was tempted in every way, *"yet without sin"* — without hamartia. Not one miss. Every moment of His life hit the mark He was made for.

And 2 Corinthians 5:21 says this: *"For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God."* Read that through the archery picture: the One who never missed became the miss itself — so that in Him, you could be re-aimed. Not just forgiven. Restored. Reborn pointed at the target you were made for.

That changes the question you ask yourself. It's not just "what did I do wrong today?" It's "what is my life aimed at?" Because you can be disciplined, moral, even impressive — and still be an arrow flying straight at the wrong target.

One more thing, because people get this backwards. Isaiah 42:8 says God will not give His glory to another. That's true — you cannot take God's glory. It isn't yours to seize, no matter what you accomplish. But His glory was always meant to be given, not stolen. Second Corinthians 3:18 says we are being *changed into His likeness, from one degree of glory to another*. You don't take His glory by achievement. You receive it by walking in step with His Spirit.

So the real question isn't "how do I stop messing up?" It's "am I aimed at Him?" Because in Christ, for the first time, you were made to fly true.

## Scrutiny notes (for revision before distribution)

- Archery etymology of ἁμαρτία is a real root but not the NT's settled sense by Koine usage (root-fallacy risk, per James Barr) — frame on-camera as illustration, not definition.
- "Glory of God" in Rom 3:23 as imago-dei/vocational glory is a defensible interpretive stance (companions: N.T. Wright, Second Temple Adam-glory tradition), not the only reading — fine for teaching content but not to be stated as uncontested exegesis if adapted into a practitioner-facing or written form.
- The Heb 4:15 "every breath aimed at the Glory of God" line and the 2 Cor 5:21 "miss the mark" phrasing are devotional extrapolation/paraphrase, not translation — acceptable for spoken teaching, should stay clearly distinguishable from Scripture quotation if this becomes written/quotable content.
