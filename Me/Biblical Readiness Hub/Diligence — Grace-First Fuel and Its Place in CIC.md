# Diligence — Grace-First Fuel and Its Place in CIC

> *For this very reason, make every effort to supplement your faith with virtue.* — 2 Peter 1:5 (ESV)

---

## Use

**Function:** Guides posture (primary) and know-how (secondary). It orients how Andrew frames the between-session assignment and how he responds when a client has not done it. It is not something to recite to a client.

**What Andrew does differently because this exists:**
- He frames the between-session commitment from grace, not from obligation.
- He reads a lapse as information about the client's priorities and load, not as failure.
- He knows what to say at the commitment moment, in the intake, and after a missed week.

**Companions:** [[Doers of the Word — A Register of What the New Testament Asks of the Believer]] (what is asked, and why reception comes first) · [[Matthew 6.33 — Seek First the Kingdom]] (priority and anxiety) · [[Revelation 3.14-22 — Hot, Cold, or Lukewarm]] (genuine vs. counterfeit zeal) · CIC's John 15 Abide Formation Posture Reference (the identity-level verb used in Section 5).

All Scripture references are ESV.

---

## 1. The Claim in One Paragraph

Diligence in the faith is the response of someone who has already been given what they need. It is not the price of getting it. In CIC, diligence is the way a client protects their access to the Word and the Spirit while the Word reaches their implicit beliefs. It is a practice-layer expression of a received identity. It does not belong inside the identity statements, because then every lapse would feel like an identity breach.

---

## 2. What Diligence Is (and Isn't)

English "diligence" translates several words that do not mean the same thing. The distinctions below are the ones that matter for coaching.

| Term | Literal picture | What it contributes |
|---|---|---|
| **חָרוּץ** `ḥārûṣ` (Prov 10:4; 12:24; 21:5) | To cut, sharpen, decide | Decisive, resolute, planning-minded. Not simply long hours. |
| **שָׁנַן** `šānan` (Deut 6:7) | To sharpen by repetition | Formation by repetition. This is the Hebrew basis for between-session practice. |
| **שָׁמַר** `šāmar` + intensifier (Deut 4:9; Prov 4:23) | To guard | Guard against forgetting. Diligence here is protective, not heroic. |
| **σπουδή / σπουδάζω** `spoudē / spoudazō` (2 Pet 1:5, 10; Heb 4:11; 6:11; Eph 4:3) | From *speudō*, "to hasten" | Eagerness, earnestness, making an effort. Mostly aimed at spiritual pursuit in the NT. |

**Two corrections to common assumptions:**
- Much of the KJV's "diligently" is a translation of a Hebrew intensifier (Deut 11:13). The intensity is real, but the word "diligence" is not always in the Hebrew.
- NT `spoudē` is rarely about secular work. Work is addressed by other texts (Eph 4:28; Col 3:23; 2 Thess 3:10–12). Importing a general "work ethic" into `spoudē` reads a later cultural overlay into the text.

**Diligence is not scrupulosity.** Scrupulosity is anxious, fear-driven over-conscientiousness that is never satisfied. Diligence that is biblical is earnest and settled. This document does not use "scrupulosity" for what Andrew wants clients to do (see Section 9).

---

## 3. The Source — Grace Comes First

The biblical order is received, then responded to. Three texts carry it.

- **2 Peter 1:3–5.** "His divine power has granted to us all things that pertain to life and godliness... **For this very reason**, make every effort." The effort is a response to supply already given.
- **Titus 2:11–14.** "The grace of God has appeared... training us to renounce ungodliness... a people for his own possession who are zealous for good works." Grace is the one doing the training. The zeal is the result.
- **Philippians 2:12–13.** "Work out your own salvation... for it is God who works in you, both to will and to work for his good pleasure." The client works because God is already at work.

This matches the reception-before-performance order in James 1:21–25: receive the implanted word, then be a doer. The man who forgets the mirror did not lack effort. Nothing took root.

## 4. What Fuels Diligence

Two kinds of fuel must be kept distinct.

**Foundational fuel: what is already true.** This fuel does not depend on how the client feels this week.
- Identity received in truth and love.
- God's character, including that He is safe to trust with the work.
- The promise that the implanted word "is able to save your souls" (James 1:21).
- The Spirit's enabling (Phil 2:13; Eph 2:10).

**Confirming fuel: what is experienced.** This fuel is real and valuable, but it is not the foundation.
- Freedom the client has felt.
- Inner change they can name.
- Fruit others have noticed.

**Why the distinction matters.** If experienced benefit is the only fuel, the loop is: diligence produces change, the change is felt, the feeling fuels more diligence. It works until it stalls. In a dry season, the loop fails exactly when the client most needs fuel. Worse, the client may start striving to get the feeling back, which is the mixed motive CIC is trying to remove.

Scripture models diligence before sight:
- Hebrews 11:6: whoever draws near to God must believe that He exists and that He rewards those who seek Him. The seeking comes before the reward.
- Hebrews 6:11–12: earnestness is joined to "faith and patience," not to felt progress.

**Practitioner rule:** confirming fuel is welcomed and named, but the client's commitment is never made contingent on it. A flat season is not evidence that the work has stopped working. (Lament and dry seasons keep the space Scripture gives them. See Section 9.)

## 5. Where Diligence Sits in CIC

**Identity layer: abiding.** The identity-level verb is receptive: "Abide in me, and I in you... apart from me you can do nothing" (John 15:4–5). It is relational and received.

**Practice layer: diligence.** Diligence is how abiding is protected against drift. Its job is to guard access to the Word and prayer so they are not crowded out (Deut 4:9; Prov 4:23).

**Why diligence is not placed in the identity statements.** If a client's identity statement includes "I am diligent," every missed week becomes a question about whether they are who they say they are. That re-imports performance. The cleaner line is: *I am loved, and diligence is how that love moves.*

**Trade-off Andrew should know about.** Some will argue that Scripture does describe diligence as characteristic of the believer (Titus 2:14, "zealous for good works"; Eph 2:10). That is true. The point here is placement: it describes the fruit of the identity, not a condition of holding it. This is a design judgment.

## 6. The Allocation Diagnostic — What the Heart Treats as Survival-Critical

Many clients are diligent where survival feels at stake (work), where there is enjoyment, and in some personal growth. The same clients often go long stretches without diligence in the Word, prayer, and fellowship. **Where diligence goes reveals what the heart treats as necessary and what it treats as optional.**

Scripture names this pattern:
- **Mark 4:18–19 (thorny soil).** The word is choked by "the cares of the world and the deceitfulness of riches and the desires for other things." This maps to the Soils framework in [[A-Parable of the Soils — Kingdom Coaching Deep Framework]].
- **Luke 10:38–42.** Martha is "anxious and troubled about many things." Jesus says "one thing is necessary." She was diligent about the wrong thing, not lazy.
- **Heb 2:1.** "Pay much closer attention... lest we drift away from it." The verb is drift, not rebellion. Lapses are usually slow and unchosen.
- **Rev 2:4.** The Ephesians had labor and endurance and still "abandoned the love you had at first." Diligence everywhere else can coexist with a cooling center.

**The implicit belief to look for:** "Faith practice is what I do when everything else is handled."

### Two layers inside "I have no energy left"

These can both be true. The coach's job is to find out which one is operating, not to assume.

- **Real finitude.** Capacity is limited, and Scripture does not shame that. "The spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak" (Matt 26:41). He "knows our frame" (Ps 103:14). Jesus took the disciples away to rest (Mark 6:31). An exhausted client is not morally failing.
- **A priority belief inside the exhaustion.** Scripture's pattern is giving first and best: Prov 3:9 (firstfruits); Ps 5:3; Mark 1:35. A client may give their freshest hours to work without ever having examined it.

This is a question for the session, not a conclusion to bring into it.

## 7. Two Client Types, and a Third

The entry point differs.

| Type | What it looks like | What they need | Entry point |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Under-diligent** | Long stretches without Word, prayer, or fellowship. Often work-heavy. | A reason that is not guilt, and a structure small enough to start. | Allocation diagnostic (Section 6). Then a minimal rhythm. |
| **Striving-mixed** | Consistent practice, but driven by fear or by earning acceptance. | Unmixing the motive. Seeing the practice as received, not earned. | Ask what happens, inside them, when they miss a day. |
| **Sincere but dry** | Faithful practice, little felt result. | Foundational fuel named back to them. Permission for the dry season. | Section 4. Lament space is preserved. |

The Matthew 25 servant is a useful reference for the first two. His inaction came from a picture of the master: "I knew you to be a hard man... so I was afraid." A client who will not engage may be reasoning from the same picture of God. That belongs to the God-Representation work (see CIC's God-Representation Deepening Tool), not to a discipline problem.

## 8. Three Touchpoints

These are drafted language and framing, not tested scripts. Adapt them to the client's voice.

### 8.1 Commitment framing (at the start of between-session work)

**Purpose:** the client commits knowing where diligence comes from and what fuels it.

**Draft language:**
- *"The work between sessions isn't about earning anything. It's how you keep the Word within reach while it reaches the places in you that haven't heard it yet."*
- *"You aren't doing this to become loved. You're doing this because you are, and this is what keeps that close."*
- *"Some weeks will feel like nothing is happening. That isn't a sign it stopped working. We'll plan for those weeks now."*

**What Andrew does not say:** that following the process guarantees a result. The activity is trustworthy because of what God has promised (Rom 12:2; 2 Cor 3:18; James 1:21), not because the method cannot fail.

### 8.2 Allocation question (intake or Soils work)

**Purpose:** surface what the client's heart treats as survival-critical.

**Draft question:** *"Where does your best energy go in a normal week, and what feels non-negotiable?"*

**Follow-up if needed:** *"What would happen if the Word, prayer, or fellowship got that same priority?"* Listen for the belief underneath the answer (fear, scarcity, "faith is for when I have time").

### 8.3 Lapse script (when the assignment was not done)

**Purpose:** respond to a missed week with curiosity, not exhortation.

**Sequence:**
1. Ask what was competing. Listen for load first.
2. Ask what the client thought or felt when they realized they had not done it. Listen for shame, relief, or indifference.
3. Name the pattern back without fixing it.
4. Only then, if appropriate, renegotiate the rhythm to something smaller.

**Draft language:** *"What got in the way this week?"* — *"When you noticed you hadn't done it, what happened inside you?"*

**What Andrew does not say:** "you need to be more disciplined."

## 9. Cautions

- **Scrupulosity.** Do not use the word with clients. It names a clinical pattern and is outside Andrew's non-clinical scope. If a client shows religious anxiety that escalates with the assignment (checking, repeating, inability to feel the work is "enough"), stop adding structure and apply CIC's Crisis & Referral Protocol. More homework is the wrong response.
- **Method-guarantee.** Never imply that the process guarantees the result. A coach who does sets the client up for collapse when it does not.
- **Spiritual-attack framing.** Eph 6:10–18 and 1 Pet 5:8 are real. If every lapse is framed as attack, clients can become hypervigilant. The response Scripture gives is dependence ("be strong in the Lord," Eph 6:10) with sober watchfulness. It is not heightened self-monitoring.
- **Lament and dry seasons.** Scripture's own space for protest and silence is preserved. A flat season is not a failure of diligence. See the covenant-authority lament caution.
- **Assurance-pressure.** Heb 6:11 and 2 Pet 1:10 should not be used on a client prone to doubting their standing. Pair them with the covenant-permanence material.

## 10. Interpretive Fault Lines and Design Judgments

**Interpretive debates (name, do not resolve):**
- **Proverbs and prosperity.** Proverbs states general patterns, not guarantees. Prov 13:23, Eccl 9:11, and Job all qualify the link between diligence and wealth.
- **Effort and grace in sanctification.** Reformed, Wesleyan, and Keswick traditions describe the cooperation differently (Phil 2:12–13; Col 1:29). All affirm real human action.
- **2 Peter 1:10 ("make your calling and election sure").** Read as assurance-seeking in Reformed circles and as perseverance language in Arminian ones. The text is open to both.
- **Work ethic.** Whether NT `spoudē` supports a general work-ethic reading is contested. This document's position is that it mostly does not. That is a judgment, not a consensus.

**Design judgments in this document (not directly stated in Scripture):**
- Placing diligence at the practice layer and abiding at the identity layer.
- The foundational vs. confirming fuel distinction.
- The three-type client table and the three touchpoints.

**Not verified:** the Hellenistic honorific-decree background of `spoudē`; the LXX renderings of `ḥārûṣ`; the claim that the capacity/priority pattern generalizes beyond Andrew's own experience. Test the allocation diagnostic with real clients before treating it as established.
