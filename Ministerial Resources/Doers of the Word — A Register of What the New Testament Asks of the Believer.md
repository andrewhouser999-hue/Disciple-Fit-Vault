# Doers of the Word — A Register of What the New Testament Asks of the Believer

> *But be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves.* — James 1:22 (ESV)

---

## Use

Reference for teaching, preaching, and personal formation review — a single place to see, in one prioritized register, what Scripture actually asks of a believer's life. Built for spiritual formation use: not a performance checklist to hand to anyone, but a portrait of the shape obedience takes across the New Testament, organized so the weightiest commands stay weightiest. Companion to [[James 1.21 — Meekness and the Implanted Word]] and [[The Word Reaching the Heart — Biblical Case for Implicit-Level Transformation]] — both of which supply the theological guardrail this document depends on (see Section 2 below).

---

## 1. Purpose & Method

James's charge — "be doers of the word, and not hearers only" (1:22) — is the seed of this register, but James's own list in his letter is not exhaustive. He is a wisdom/ethics letter addressed to a scattered, suffering, economically stratified community; he never touches marriage, civil government, evangelism, or corporate worship. Treating his list as the whole of what is asked of a believer would flatten the canon to one letter's occasion.

So the method here is layered:

- **The governing principle** is not mine to assign — Jesus supplies it. Asked for the greatest commandment, He named two: love God with all heart, soul, mind, strength; love your neighbor as yourself (Mark 12:29–31; Matt 22:37–40). Every item below is an outworking of one or both. Paul and James agree: "love is the fulfilling of the law" (Rom 13:8–10; Gal 5:14); James himself calls neighbor-love "the royal law" (2:8).
- **James's own itemization is prioritized** within that frame — where he names something, it is marked and placed early in its category, since that is the concrete text this register grew out of.
- **The wider New Testament fills what James does not cover** — clearly separated below (Tiers 14–19) so the boundary of his letter stays visible rather than absorbed into a false comprehensiveness.

All references ESV.

## 2. Reading "Doing" Rightly — The Warning Built Into the Text Itself

Before the register, a caution the text itself supplies. James does not open with the list of behaviors. He opens with *reception*:

> *Put away all filthiness and rampant wickedness and receive with meekness the implanted word, which is able to save your souls.* — James 1:21

Only after that does he say "be doers" (1:22). Then he explains, with a specific image, what happens when the order is reversed:

> *For if anyone is a hearer of the word and not a doer, he is like a man who looks intently at his natural face in a mirror. For he looks at himself and goes away and at once forgets what he was like.* — James 1:23–24

The man in the mirror is not lacking information. He saw clearly — the text says he "looks intently." What he lacks is retention: the seeing never became anything durable. James's own diagnosis of *why* people fail to do the word is not laziness or ignorance — it is that the word only reached the surface. Hearing and having something implanted are not the same event.

Jesus makes the identical point at the close of the Sermon on the Mount, almost certainly the tradition James is drawing on:

> *Everyone then who hears these words of mine and does them will be like a wise man who built his house on the rock. And the rain fell, and the floods came, and the winds blew and beat on that house, but it did not fall, because it had been founded on the rock. And everyone who hears these words of mine and does not do them will be like a foolish man who built his house on the sand.* — Matt 7:24–26

The two houses look identical until pressure arrives. That is the point: hearing-only and hearing-that-has-taken-root are indistinguishable from the outside, and often indistinguishable to the person themselves, until the storm exposes which one was actually built. This is also James's argument in his other major "doing" passage:

> *Faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead... You believe that God is one; you do well. Even the demons believe—and shudder!* — James 2:17, 19

Right belief that produces nothing is not partial obedience — James calls it dead. Abraham's faith "was completed by his works" (2:22) — not supplemented by them, *completed*, the way a seed is completed by the plant it was always oriented toward becoming.

**What this means for the register that follows:** every item below is what a formed life produces, not a list of things to perform in order to become formed. The list is a portrait, read backward from the doing to what must be true of the interior for that doing to be real and durable rather than a mirror-glance that fades by the time you reach the door. Presented as a checklist, this document would produce exactly what James warns against — assent that evaporates. Read as a picture of the formed life, and returned to as a mirror in the better sense (something to look into repeatedly, honestly, and let register), it does what James intends.

## 3. The Register

### Tier 1 — The Supreme Commands
1. **Love God** with all heart, soul, mind, strength — Mark 12:30.
2. **Love your neighbor as yourself** — Mark 12:31. *James 2:8 — "the royal law."* Confirmed as the summary of the whole law in Rom 13:8–10; Gal 5:14; 1 John 4:20–21.

### Tier 2 — Foundational Posture (before doing, receiving)
3. *James 1:21* — Receive the implanted word with meekness.
4. Repent — Mark 1:15; Acts 2:38.
5. Believe/trust Christ — John 6:29; Acts 16:31.

### Tier 3 — Speech
6. *James 1:19* — Be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger.
7. *James 1:26* — Bridle the tongue ("this person's religion is worthless" without it).
8. *James 3:1–12* — Tame the tongue as a life discipline; teachers judged more strictly.
9. *James 4:11* — Do not speak evil against one another; do not judge a brother.
10. *James 5:9* — Do not grumble against one another.
11. *James 5:12* — Let your yes be yes, no be no; do not swear oaths (echoing Matt 5:37).
12. Let no corrupting talk come out of your mouth, only what builds up — Eph 4:29. Put away falsehood, speak truth — Eph 4:25. Put away slander and obscene talk — Col 3:8. Speech seasoned with salt — Col 4:6.

### Tier 4 — Purity / Holiness
13. *James 1:21* — Put away all filthiness and rampant wickedness.
14. *James 1:27* — Keep oneself unstained from the world.
15. *James 4:8* — Cleanse your hands, purify your hearts (double-mindedness named as the problem).
16. Do not be conformed to this world; be transformed — Rom 12:1–2. Be holy as He is holy — 1 Pet 1:15–16. Walk by the Spirit, not the flesh — Gal 5:16–24. Do not love the world — 1 John 2:15–17. Strive for the holiness without which no one sees the Lord — Heb 12:14.

### Tier 5 — Humility, Submission to God
17. *James 4:6–10* — Submit to God, resist the devil, draw near to God, humble yourself before the Lord.
18. *James 4:16* — Do not boast arrogantly.
19. Clothe yourselves with humility — 1 Pet 5:5–6. Have the mind of Christ; count others more significant — Phil 2:3–8.

### Tier 6 — Care for the Vulnerable, Generosity, Mercy
20. *James 1:27* — Visit orphans and widows in their affliction — James's own definition of "pure religion."
21. *James 2:1–7* — Show no partiality to the rich; do not dishonor the poor.
22. *James 2:14–17* — Meet physical need directly, food and clothing, as the proof that faith is alive.
23. *James 5:1–6* — Do not defraud laborers of wages; do not hoard.
24. Feed the hungry, clothe the naked, welcome the stranger, visit the sick and imprisoned — Matt 25:31–46. Share possessions with those in need — Acts 2:44–45; 4:32–35; 1 John 3:17–18. Give generously and cheerfully — 2 Cor 8–9. Remember the poor — Gal 2:10. Do not neglect to do good and share — Heb 13:16.

### Tier 7 — Church Unity, Impartiality, Peace
25. *James 2:1, 9* — Show no partiality among believers, named twice as its own category.
26. *James 4:1–2* — Do not let quarrels and fights arise from your passions.
27. *James 3:13–18* — Pursue wisdom from above: pure, peaceable, gentle, open to reason, full of mercy, impartial, sincere. Peacemakers sow a harvest of righteousness.
28. Maintain unity in the bond of peace — Eph 4:1–3. Live in harmony — Rom 12:16. Blessed are the peacemakers — Matt 5:9. Reconcile before worship — Matt 5:23–24. Confront and restore through process — Matt 18:15–17.

### Tier 8 — Wisdom
29. *James 1:5* — Ask God for wisdom when lacking it.
30. *James 3:13* — Show wisdom by conduct, not by claims.
31. Walk as wise, understanding the Lord's will — Eph 5:15–17. Walk in wisdom toward outsiders — Col 4:5.

### Tier 9 — Endurance Under Trial
32. *James 1:2–4, 12* — Count trials as joy; let steadfastness have its full effect. Blessed is the one who remains steadfast under trial.
33. *James 5:7–11* — Be patient until the Lord's coming; take the prophets and Job as your model.
34. Suffering produces endurance, character, hope — Rom 5:3–5. Do not be surprised at fiery trial — 1 Pet 4:12–13. Run with endurance — Heb 12:1–3.

### Tier 10 — Prayer
35. *James 1:5–6* — Ask in faith, without doubting.
36. *James 4:2–3* — You do not have because you do not ask, or you ask wrongly.
37. *James 5:13–18* — Pray in suffering, sing in joy; elders pray over and anoint the sick; confess sins to one another and pray for one another for healing; the prayer of a righteous person has great power. James's most concentrated instruction on prayer practice, and one of the more contested passages in the letter (see Section 4).
38. Pray without ceasing — 1 Thess 5:17. Be anxious for nothing; pray with thanksgiving — Phil 4:6. Pray at all times in the Spirit — Eph 6:18.

### Tier 11 — Restoration of the Wandering
39. *James 5:19–20* — Bring back one who wanders from the truth; it covers a multitude of sins and saves a soul from death. James's closing charge.
40. Restore in a spirit of gentleness; bear one another's burdens — Gal 6:1–2. Warn as a brother, not an enemy — 2 Thess 3:14–15.

### Tier 12 — Obedience as the Proof of Faith
41. *James 2:14–26* — Faith without works is dead, shown in Abraham's obedience and Rahab's action, not mental assent. James's central thesis.
42. *James 4:17* — Whoever knows the right thing to do and fails to do it, for him it is sin. Easy to miss; arguably the most practically confronting line in the letter.
43. The dominical parallel underneath James's whole argument — not everyone who says "Lord, Lord," build your house on the rock by doing — Matt 7:21–27. Keep His commandments as the evidence of love — John 14:15; 1 John 2:3–6. Created for, zealous for, good works — Eph 2:10; Titus 2:14; 3:8, 14.

### Tier 13 — Money, Work, Presumption
44. *James 4:13–17* — Do not presume on tomorrow; "if the Lord wills."
45. *James 5:1–6* — Warning against wealth gained by fraud or hoarded in luxury.
46. Contentment; love of money as a root of evils; the rich to be generous, ready to share — 1 Tim 6:6–10, 17–19. Guard against covetousness — Luke 12:15–21. Work quietly, earn your own living — 2 Thess 3:10–12. Do honest work so as to have something to share — Eph 4:28.

---

### Outside James entirely

James never addresses the following. Included for comprehensiveness, marked clearly as supplement rather than extension of his letter.

### Tier 14 — Marriage, Family, Household
Mutual submission; husbands love wives as Christ loved the church; wives respect — Eph 5:21–33 (see Section 4). Children obey; fathers do not provoke — Eph 6:1–4; Col 3:18–21; 1 Pet 3:1–7.

### Tier 15 — Civil Authority
Submit to governing authorities; pay taxes — Rom 13:1–7. Honor the emperor, honor everyone — 1 Pet 2:13–17. Pray for kings and all in authority — 1 Tim 2:1–2.

### Tier 16 — Mission and Witness
Make disciples, baptize, teach obedience — Matt 28:18–20. Be my witnesses — Acts 1:8. Always ready to give a defense for your hope — 1 Pet 3:15. Preach the word — 2 Tim 4:2.

### Tier 17 — Corporate Worship and Ordinances
Do not neglect meeting together; stir one another to love and good works — Heb 10:24–25. Observe the Lord's Supper — 1 Cor 11:23–26. Sing to one another — Eph 5:19. Let all things be done for edification, decently and in order — 1 Cor 14:26, 40.

### Tier 18 — Enemies and Persecution
Love your enemies, pray for persecutors — Matt 5:38–48. Bless; do not repay evil for evil; overcome evil with good — Rom 12:14, 17–21; 1 Pet 3:9.

### Tier 19 — Readiness for Christ's Return
*James 5:9* — the one place James touches this, tucked inside his speech-ethic: "the Judge is standing at the door." Stay awake, be ready — Matt 24:42–44; 1 Thess 5:6. Be found without spot or blemish — 2 Pet 3:11–14.

## 4. Interpretive Fault Lines Worth Naming

- **James 2:14–26 and Pauline justification (Rom 3–4; Gal 2–3).** The historic fault line — Luther famously called James "an epistle of straw" over it. The two are answering different questions rather than contradicting each other: Paul addresses how a person is declared righteous before God (faith alone, apart from works of law); James addresses what kind of faith actually saves (a faith that produces nothing is not the kind Paul means either — cf. Gal 5:6, "faith working through love"). The tension is real enough that credible traditions still weigh how much doctrinal load James 2 can bear.
- **James 5:14–16, anointing with oil.** Read as medicinal (oil as first-century medicine, prayer accompanying ordinary care), symbolic/consecratory, or sacramental (Catholic extreme unction traces here). Continuationist and cessationist readers also divide on whether "the prayer of faith will save the sick" describes a normative expectation or one situated in the apostolic era.
- **Eph 5:21–33 / 1 Pet 3:1–7 submission language.** Complementarian and egalitarian readings diverge on whether the wife's "submission" and the husband's "headship" describe a fixed role structure or a mutual-submission ethic (5:21) applied asymmetrically to a specific cultural situation.
- **James 5:1–6, wealth.** Some read this as unconditional condemnation of riches as such; most read it as conditional — condemning wealth gained by fraud or by defrauding laborers, not wealth per se, since James elsewhere assumes believers will have goods to share (2:15–16, implicitly).

## 6. Closing Formation Note

The register above is long enough to be discouraging read as a task list — which is exactly the reading James spent 1:19–25 warning against before he ever gave the list. The man who forgets what he saw in the mirror did not fail from insufficient effort; he failed because nothing was implanted. The right use of this document is closer to what it warns against misusing it as: a mirror to look into slowly and repeatedly, not a ledger to work through. What it is asking, underneath every tier, is the same two things Jesus named first — whether love for God and love for neighbor have actually taken root, or only been seen.
